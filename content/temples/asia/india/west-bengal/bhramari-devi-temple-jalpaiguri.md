Behind a rice mill near the much larger and better-documented Jalpesh Temple in Jalpaiguri district, West Bengal, stands a modest local shrine known as Bhramari Devi — one of the more thinly documented entries among the Shakti Peethas covered in this directory. By tradition, this is where a fragment of Sati's body (her left leg, per one cross-referenced Shakti Peetha listing) fell after her corpse was cut apart, giving the site its name and its identification as a Shakta pitha; in the paired Shakti-Bhairava structure common to these sites, the presiding Shiva form is held to be Jalpesh, worshipped at the neighboring, much more prominent Shiva temple on the Jarda river.

Unlike Jalpesh Temple — whose own founding (1524, under the Koch-dynasty ruler Bishwa Singha) and subsequent rebuildings are well documented — no comparable historical record could be located for the Bhramari Devi shrine itself; it appears in the sources checked only as a brief, secondary mention rather than as the subject of its own sustained account. This entry is accordingly written with considerable caution: the temple's antiquity, architecture, and precise history remain effectively undocumented in accessible sources, even as its identity as a Shakti Peetha is consistently, if briefly, affirmed alongside its Jalpesh neighbor.
