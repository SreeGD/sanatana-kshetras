The Aiyarappar Temple in Thiruvaiyaru, on the Thanjavur outskirts, is a major but still under-catalogued Paadal Petra Sthalam, praised across all three of the Tevaram's principal Nayanar poet-saints — Sambandar, Appar, and Sundarar. Shiva is worshipped here as Aiyarappar, also known as Panchanadeeswarar ("lord of the five rivers"), a name tied to the belief that five sacred waterways converge at the site; his consort is worshipped as Aram Valartha Nayagi. The temple's oldest surviving masonry dates to the 9th-century Chola period, with the complex — spanning roughly 60,000 square metres across five successive prakarams — expanded as late as the 16th century under the Thanjavur Nayaks.

The temple's most distinctive living tradition is the Saptha Stanam festival each April, in which the Nandi (Shiva's bull-mount) images of seven regional temples are carried in procession to reenact the celestial wedding of Nandikeswara; part of that cycle culminates at the nearby Thirumazhapadi Vaidyanathaswami Temple. Thiruvaiyaru is also, separately, the site of the Thyagaraja Aradhana, the major annual Carnatic music festival held on the banks of the Kaveri at the samadhi of the 18th/19th-century composer-saint Thyagaraja — a coincidence of name with this temple's presiding deity (also called Thyagarajaswami in some regional usage) that is worth noting but does not reflect a shared administration; the two observances are documented as distinct.
