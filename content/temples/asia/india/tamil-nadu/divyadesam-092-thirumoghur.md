Thirumoghur (Thirumohoor, also called Mohanapuram or Mohanakshetram), in the Pandya country near Madurai, enshrines the Lord as Kalamega Perumal, his form likened to a dark rain-laden cloud whose gracious bounty showers upon devotees, standing with his consort Mohanavalli Thayar in a panchayudha kolam; it is counted among the 108 Divya Desams glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Inscriptions date the temple's construction to the reign of the Pandya king Sadaiya Varman Sundara Pandiyan around 1259 CE, with later additions under the Madurai Nayaks and the Marudu brothers; local tradition holds the site to be more than 2,000 years old and it is referenced in the classical Tamil epic Silappatikaram. Built in Dravidian style across roughly 2.5 acres, the temple has a five-tiered rajagopuram, four prakarams, the distinctive Kethaki Vimanam, and the Kshirabtha Pushkarini as its temple tank. A celebrated feature of this temple is its association with Sudarshana, the discus of the Lord: the Sudarshana Chakra shrine here enshrines Chakratalvar, with an image of Narasimha Perumal set amid 48 apsaras and surrounded by sixteen weapons inscribed with 154 letters traditionally held to represent the Bhijakshara Mantra, and Thirumoghur is renowned as a kshetra where the protective grace of the Lord's weapon is especially invoked. The shrine is hymned by Nammalwar, the foremost of the Alwars, whose verses extol the Lord of Thirumoghur, and by Thirumangai Alwar, who likewise sings its glory in the Prabandham. As the Lord of compassion who, dark and majestic as a monsoon cloud, pours out his mercy and ushers the faithful toward liberation, Kalamega Perumal of Thirumoghur is cherished as a refuge and a guide.

## Festivals

At Thirumoghur, where the Lord is revered as Kalamega Perumal, the principal annual observance is the ten-day Brahmotsavam in the Tamil month of Vaikasi (May-June), with the deity taken in procession on the traditional vahanas. Gajendra Moksham is marked on Masi Poornima (February-March), and the two-day Panguni Thirukalyanam (March-April) celebrates the Lord's sacred wedding. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi (December-January) is kept with special darshan, and Krishna Jayanthi is also observed. The Sudarshana shrine here is especially venerated, and festival days draw devotees seeking protection.

## Location & Access

Kalamega Perumal Temple is located at Thirumoghur (Tirumogoor), near Melur in the Pandya Nadu region, about 20 km north-east of Madurai, which serves as the nearest major city and transport hub with rail, road and air links. Buses and taxis from Madurai reach the village. The temple follows six daily rituals: Ushathkalam (7 AM), Kalasanthi (8 AM), Uchikalam (12 PM), Sayarakshai (6 PM), Irandamkalam (7 PM) and Ardha Jamam (10 PM). It is maintained by the Hindu Religious and Endowment Board of the Government of Tamil Nadu. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
