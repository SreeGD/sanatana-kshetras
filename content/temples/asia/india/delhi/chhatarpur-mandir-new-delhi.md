On the southwestern outskirts of New Delhi, near Mehrauli and the Qutub Minar, the Chhatarpur Mandir complex -- formally Shri Aadya Katyayani Shakti Peeth -- is one of the capital's largest and most visited temple complexes, dedicated to the goddess Katyayani, one of the nine Navadurga forms worshipped especially during Navratri. Spread across roughly 60 to 70 acres, it is commonly described as the second-largest temple complex in Delhi after the Swaminarayan Akshardham Temple, and it draws especially large crowds during both the spring (Chaitra) and autumn (Sharad) Navratri festivals, when a dedicated shrine to Katyayani opens specially to devotees.

The complex was established by Baba Sant Nagpal ji, a spiritual teacher who died in 1998 and whose samadhi (memorial shrine) stands within the grounds; sources are not entirely consistent on the exact founding year, with figures of both 1970 and 1974 appearing. Built in the Chola style associated with South Indian temple architecture and constructed largely of white marble, the complex has grown to encompass more than twenty individual shrines beyond its main Katyayani sanctum, including a Shiv-Gauri-Nageshwar Mandir and a Lakshmi Vinayaka temple, along with a sacred tree near the entrance where visitors tie threads in hope of wishes fulfilled. As a large, actively expanding 20th-century foundation rather than an ancient site, Chhatarpur Mandir's significance rests less on antiquity than on its scale, its role as a major contemporary center of Shakti worship in the capital, and its place alongside Jhandewalan and Kalkaji Mandir as one of Delhi's principal Devi shrines.
