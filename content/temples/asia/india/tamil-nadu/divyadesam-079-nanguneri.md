The Vanamamalai temple at Nanguneri, hailed in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham as Thiruvaramangai, enshrines the Lord as Thothadrinathan (Vanamamalai Perumal), one of the 108 Divya Desams sacred to Sri Vaishnavas and counted among the foremost shrines of the Pandya Nadu. The Moolavar stands in a majestic standing posture, gracing devotees together with His consort, who is revered here as Sirumamai Nachiyar (Varamangai Thayar), the divine mother from whom the place takes its Tamil name Thiruvaramangai. According to tradition, the Lord is celebrated as Vanamamalai, "the hill that is a garland of the forest," a name evoking His abundant grace and the verdant abode in which He resides. The shrine is sung by Nammalwar, the chief among the Alwars, whose verses in the Divya Prabandham consecrated this place within the eternal canon of Tamil Vaishnava devotion; through his outpouring the Lord of Thiruvaramangai is established among the abodes that the saint experienced as living presences of the Supreme. The temple is especially renowned for the sacred unguent of fragrant oil (thailam) that is poured over the Lord and never diminishes, a wonder cherished by pilgrims as a token of His inexhaustible compassion; the prasadam of this divine oil is treasured for its healing and auspicious power. As one of the great Pandya Nadu Divya Desams, Vanamamalai has long been a center of Sri Vaishnava worship and the seat of an esteemed Jeeyar matham, drawing devotees who seek the protection of Thothadrinathan and the maternal grace of Varamangai Thayar.

## Festivals

The temple at Nanguneri, sacred to Thothadrinathan (Vanamamalai Perumal) of Thiruvaramangai, traditionally keeps the full Srivaishnava festival calendar. A grand annual Brahmotsavam, spanning several days with processions of the utsava deity, the Garuda Vahanam and other vahanas, the chariot festival and Theerthavari, is the chief celebration. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is observed with great devotion, with the recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham during the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu and the opening of the Paramapada Vasal. The temple is also renowned for its annual Pavithrotsavam, performed for the purification and sanctification of the year's worship. The Theppotsavam (float festival) on the temple tank, Andal's Aadi Pooram, Krishna Jayanthi, Navaratri and the monthly observances tied to the Lord's asterism are also traditionally marked with special abhishekam and alankaram.

## Location & Access

The temple stands in the town of Nanguneri (Thiruvaramangai) in the Tirunelveli region of the old Pandya Nadu, in far southern Tamil Nadu, set roughly between the city of Tirunelveli and the southern coast. Tirunelveli serves as the nearest major city and rail and road hub, with onward bus and taxi connections to Nanguneri. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday closure, and remains open longer on festival days.
