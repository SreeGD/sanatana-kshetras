The Oppiliappan Temple at Thiruvinnagar, near Kumbakonam in the Chola Nadu, enshrines the Lord as Oppiliappan, also revered as Thiruvinnagarappan and Venkatachalapathi, and stands among the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alwars. The presiding Moolavar is a majestic standing form of Vishnu, worshipped here with his consort Bhumi Devi, who at this kshetra is venerated as Bhoomidevi or Bhargavi. According to the sthala purana, the sage Markandeya undertook penance desiring the Lord himself as his son-in-law, and the Lord, accepting his young daughter, promised that the offerings made to him at this shrine would forever be prepared without salt — for which reason the naivedyam at Oppiliappan is traditionally offered saltless to this day. From this distinctive vow comes the name Oppiliappan, the "Lord without compare," matchless among all. The kshetra is closely associated in tradition with Tirupati, the Lord being regarded as a form of Srinivasa, so that worship here is held to confer the grace of the Lord of the seven hills upon the Tamil plains. The temple's glory is celebrated in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, having been hymned by Pey Alwar, by Nammalwar, and by Thirumangai Alwar, whose verses praise the Lord of Thiruvinnagar and his abode. Devotees revere Oppiliappan as a bestower of well-being and prosperity, and the saltless offering remains the temple's most celebrated and beloved distinction.

## Festivals

The grandest observance at Thiruvinnagar is the annual Brahmotsavam for Oppiliappan, traditionally celebrated with great splendour, when the Lord is taken in procession on the garuda, hanumantha, horse and chariot vahanas through the temple streets. Sravana, the asterism associated with Oppiliappan, is marked as a special monthly occasion, and the Aippasi Sravanam is held with particular reverence. Vaikunta Ekadasi is observed in the cold month of Margazhi with the opening of the Paramapada Vasal and the recitation of the Tiruvaymozhi. Pavithrotsavam, the purificatory festival, and a Float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank are also traditionally conducted, along with the recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham during the Adhyayana Utsavam.

## Location & Access

The temple stands at Thiruvinnagar, also called Oppiliappan Koil, in the Chola Nadu country of the Kaveri delta, a short distance from Kumbakonam in the Thanjavur region of Tamil Nadu. Kumbakonam, the nearest major town and railway junction, serves as the convenient base for reaching the shrine, with Tiruchirappalli the closest large city and airport. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday closure.
