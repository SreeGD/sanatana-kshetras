Mangeshi Temple, in Priol village in Goa's Ponda taluka, is one of Goa's largest and most visited Hindu temples and serves as the kuladevata (family deity) shrine for Goud Saraswat Brahmin families of the Vatsa and Koundinya gotras, who worship Shiva here under the name Manguesh. The lingam now enshrined at Priol was not always here: its original home was Kushasthali, in what is now Cortalim, on Goa's Mormugao peninsula, and it was carried inland by its devotees to escape the religious persecution that followed the spread of Portuguese colonial rule from 1510 onward, reaching the comparative safety of Priol -- then outside Portuguese-controlled territory -- by around the mid-sixteenth century. The temple's own legend explains the deity's unusual name differently: it holds that after losing a game of dice to Parvati, Shiva left Kailasa in irritation and, when Parvati went searching for him in the forest, appeared to her as a tiger to test her; her frightened cry, "Mam Girisha" ("save me, Lord of the mountains"), is said to have become "Manguesh" over time.

The temple standing today took shape gradually rather than all at once, with the main structure developed under later Maratha-era patronage roughly a century and a half after the deity's relocation, a further building phase in 1890, and a major renovation completed in 1973. Architecturally it follows the distinctive Goan/Saraswat temple idiom that blends Hindu temple form with European-influenced elements picked up under colonial-era building practice -- domes, pilasters, and balustrades sit alongside a seven-storey deepastambha lamp tower and a large pillared hall capable of seating several hundred worshippers. The temple remains an active center of daily ritual and large annual observances, including Maha Shivaratri and its own Magha Poornima jatra, and has enforced a formal dress code for visitors since 2011.
