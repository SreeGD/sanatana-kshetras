Unusually among the 108 Divya Desams, this shrine to Vishnu stands not as a freestanding temple but within the outer precincts of the great Thillai Nataraja Temple at Chidambaram, one of Shaivism's most important sites - making it, alongside a handful of other such shrines, one of the few Divya Desams to share its compound with a major Shiva temple. Worshipped here as Govindaraja Perumal with his consort Pundarikavalli Thayar, the deity is traditionally said to have been installed by the Pallava king Nandivarman II in the 8th century, and the temple's foundation legend links it to the very origin of Chidambaram itself, recounting a dance contest between Shiva and Parvati witnessed and blessed by Vishnu in his role as an impartial referee to the gods.

The temple's later history is one of the more dramatically documented episodes among the Chola Nadu Divya Desams: in the 12th century, the Chola king Kulothunga II, a committed patron of Shaivism, is recorded by tradition as having had the image of Govindaraja removed from its shrine in an assertion of Shaiva primacy at the site. Sri Vaishnava tradition holds that the philosopher-saint Ramanuja, then active in the region, carried the processional image to safety, eventually depositing it for protection at Tirupati, where a Govindaraja shrine remains prominent to this day. The Chidambaram image itself is said to have remained without a home for centuries until the Vijayanagara-era Thanjavur Nayak governor Krishnappa Nayak reinstalled Govindaraja here in the 16th century, over continued objections from segments of the local Shaiva establishment - an episode that, in its broad outline, is corroborated across multiple independent historical accounts even where individual hagiographical details remain a matter of sectarian tradition. Kulasekara Alvar and Thirumangai Alvar between them contributed some two dozen hymns to this shrine in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and the temple's Chittirai festival today remains one of the more prominent Vaishnava celebrations held within the wider Chidambaram temple complex.
