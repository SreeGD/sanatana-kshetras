Maheshwar sits on the Narmada's right bank in what is now Khargone district, Madhya Pradesh, on a site traditionally identified with ancient Mahishmati, the city Hindu epic tradition associates with the Haihaya king Kartavirya Arjuna. Its present character, though, owes most directly to the Holkar queen Ahilyabai, who in the late 18th century made Maheshwar her dynasty's seat and embellished it with a fort-palace, broad stone riverfront ghats, and — by local reckoning — more than a hundred temples, giving the town's waterfront the layered, temple-crowded skyline it retains today. Among the named shrines are the Sahastar Arjun Temple close by the river, the Rajrajeshwar Mandir, the Kashi Vishwanath Mandir, and the Chaturbhuj Narayan Mandir, together with a temple to Vindhyavasini Maheshwari that is counted among the Shakta pithas associated with the goddess — so that, across the complex, Shaiva, Vaishnava, and Shakta worship all have a place on the same stretch of ghats.

The ghats remain a living devotional and civic space rather than a museum piece: daily bathing and worship continue much as they have for generations, and the site's best-known annual observance, the Mahaamrityunjaya Rath Yatra, processes through the town on the Sunday immediately before Makar Sankranti each year, alongside the wider Hindu calendar's Maha Shivaratri and Nag Panchami observances kept at the individual Shaiva shrines. As with many such multi-temple riverfront complexes, the deep history of any one shrine's founding is harder to pin down than the fact of the whole ensemble's growth under Ahilyabai's patronage, which is why this entry treats Maheshwar's ghats and temples together as a single pilgrimage site rather than singling out one structure.
