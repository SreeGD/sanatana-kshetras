Sri Maha Mariamman Temple was founded in 1879 by Vaithi Padayatchi, a leader of the Tamil Hindu community that had begun settling in Bangkok roughly a decade earlier as traders and laborers, part of a wider wave of Tamil migration following the expansion of British colonial administration in India. Built in South Indian Dravidian style, with a brightly sculpted gopura tower rising about 6 meters over its entrance, the temple is dedicated to Mariamman (worshipped here as Maha Uma Devi) and remains the oldest and most prominent Tamil Hindu temple in Thailand.

Popularly known to Bangkok residents as "Wat Khaek" (literally "the guest/foreigner's temple"), the temple has long drawn worshippers well beyond its founding Tamil community, including large numbers of Thai Buddhist and Chinese devotees who treat Hindu and Buddhist devotional practice as closely related rather than separate. Its most visible public event is an annual procession during Navaratri through the surrounding Silom neighborhood, alongside Deepavali celebrations, both of which remain major fixtures of Bangkok's religious calendar.
