Few Hindu temples in North America trace as far back as this one: founded in 1957 by Sivaya Subramuniyaswami as the Palaniswami Sivan Temple in a San Francisco residence, it is counted among the earliest traditional Hindu temples established on the continent. The community relocated to its present Concord site in 1988, was renamed Shiva Murugan Temple and formally incorporated as a nonprofit in 1996, and — after decades of gradual development — completed its full Kumbhabhishekham consecration ceremony on 9 May 2025.

Built in classical Chola-style South Indian architecture, the temple is notable as the first panchavarna (five-color) South Indian-tradition temple built in the United States, its 48-foot gopuram gateway tower and 47-foot vimanam over the main sanctum finished in the traditional five colors of yellow, red, green, black, and white, with hand-carved granite murtis, sanctums, and pillars. The temple, dedicated to Shiva and his son Murugan, serves the wider Bay Area Hindu community and is the destination of an annual Thaipusam pilgrimage — a 21-mile walk undertaken by devotees from San Ramon to the temple.
