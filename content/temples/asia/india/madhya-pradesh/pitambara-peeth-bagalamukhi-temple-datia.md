Pitambara Peeth in Datia is a twentieth-century foundation with an origin story that is, by the standards of most entries in this directory, unusually recent and unusually thinly documented at the same time. It was established in the 1920s by a wandering Dandi Swami monk remembered simply as "Swamiji" or "Maharaj," about whom little biographical detail survives even in modern accounts — an unusual gap for a site that has since grown into one of North India's most prominent centers of Shakta and tantric practice. The ashram is dedicated to Bagalamukhi, one of the ten Mahavidyas (wisdom goddesses) of Shakta tradition, associated with the power to stun, silence, and paralyze one's adversaries, and it also houses a shrine to Dhumavati, another of the ten Mahavidyas. Subsidiary shrines within the complex honor Parshuram, Hanuman, and Kal Bhairav.

Local tradition holds that the site was already sacred long before Swamiji's arrival, functioning as a tapasthali, or place of meditation and penance, associated with figures from the Mahabharata era, and that a lingam on the grounds, worshipped as Vankhandeshwar, dates to that period — a claim of deep antiquity that belongs to devotional tradition and could not be independently verified for this entry. What is more firmly documented about the peeth's modern character is its emphasis on Vedic and Sanskrit learning: the ashram runs a free Sanskrit school for children, maintains a Sanskrit library, hosts annual Sanskrit debates, and — notably for a major pilgrimage site — accepts no monetary offerings from visitors. Today it draws devotees from across India, particularly on Saturdays, seeking the goddess's protection against enemies, legal troubles, and obstacles.
