Manimahesh Kailash, also called Chamba Kailash, is a 5,656 m peak of the Pir Panjal range overlooking Manimahesh Lake in the Bharmour subdivision of Chamba district — one of the four Indian peaks, alongside Adi Kailash, Kinnaur Kailash, and Shrikhand Mahadev, venerated in the Pancha Kailash tradition as an abode of Shiva. Gaddi pastoral community tradition holds that Shiva created the lake after his marriage to Parvati, worshipped locally as Mata Gorja; the name itself, "jewel (mani) on Shiva's (Mahesh's) crown," is tied to a belief that on a clear full-moon night, moon-rays reflecting off a jewel on the peak are visible from the lake's surface.

At the edge of the lake stands a marble Chaumukha (four-faced) image of Shiva, and nearby a small shikhara-style temple houses a brass image of Lakshmi Devi in her Mahishasuramardini form. Pilgrims traditionally take a ritual dip (locally called "naun"), up to four times, in the lake's two sections — the larger, cold "Shiv Karotri" and the smaller, warmer "Gauri Kund" reserved for women.

The Manimahesh Yatra, declared a state-level pilgrimage by the Himachal Pradesh government, is held annually in the Hindu month of Bhadon (roughly August/September), timed around Janmashtami and continuing to Radhastami. Most pilgrims approach via Chamba-Bharmour-Hadsar, trekking roughly 13-14 km from Hadsar with an overnight halt at Dhanchho meadow before reaching the lake; the full round trip from Chamba is traditionally reckoned at about six days. The lake itself is reported frozen from roughly October through June, so both the yatra and general trekking access are effectively confined to the June-October window.
