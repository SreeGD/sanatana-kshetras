Madan Kamdev, a cluster of ruined medieval temples near Baihata Chariali in Assam's Kamrup district, is one of the more architecturally distinctive heritage sites documented in this directory: stone carvings scattered across roughly 500 metres of excavated ground depict Shiva, Ganesha, Vishnu, Saraswati, and Bhairava, alongside images of Kamdev (the god of love) and an embraced Uma-Maheshwar (Shiva-Parvati) sculpture that gives the site its distinctive character. Stylistic dating places the carvings in the 9th-10th century CE, attributed to the Pala dynasty, with archaeological excavations begun in 1977 identifying successive temple-building phases running from the 10th into the 12th century -- making Madan Kamdev, alongside Assam's Basistha, Umananda, and Sivadol temples already documented elsewhere in this directory, part of the state's substantial medieval Hindu temple-building record.

Unlike those other Assam temples, however, Madan Kamdev survives today only as ruins: the Archaeological Survey of India manages and has renovated the site for public visitors, but the source consulted for this entry is explicit that it functions as a heritage and archaeological destination rather than a place of ongoing daily worship, and worship_status is recorded here as heritage accordingly. A main temple and the remains of twelve further shrines are spread across the site, with a separate Gopeshwar Shiva temple and a feature called Parvati Cave nearby, giving visitors a sense of what was evidently once a substantial temple complex serving the medieval Kamrup region.
