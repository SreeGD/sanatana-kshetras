Founded in 1896 on a ridge north of the Umgeni River in Inanda, near Durban, the Narainsamy Temple takes its name from its founder, who established a family trust that has governed the institution ever since — a form of temple governance found among several of the older Tamil Hindu temples of colonial-era Natal. Now recognised as a provincial heritage site, it serves a predominantly Tamil Hindu congregation and remains an active place of worship well over a century after its founding.

The temple's elevated position gives it visual prominence in the landscape, topped by a distinctive white spire visible from some distance. Its architecture combines classical pedestals, stucco-decorated domes, mandala motifs at multiple levels, three arched entrance panels, and decorative animal statues (a vole and a peacock), with interior walls displaying brightly coloured lithographs of the Hindu pantheon. Inside, three image cells house Narainsamy, Ganesha, and Subrahmanya (Murugan), with a separate cell dedicated to Nataraja, the dancing form of Shiva, alongside an ablution platform for ritual use.
