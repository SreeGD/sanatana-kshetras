At Thenthiruperai in Pandya Nadu, the Lord is worshipped as Magara Nedungkuzhaikkaadhar (Makara Nedunkuzhaikathar), and the shrine is venerated as one of the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alwars. The unusual name of the presiding deity refers, in tradition, to the great fish-shaped earrings (makara kundalas) that adorn the Lord's long ears, an iconographic distinction from which Thenthiruperai draws its special renown; the standing Moolavar is flanked by Sridevi and Bhudevi. His consort is named in sources as Thiruperai Nachiyar (also given locally as Thirukkurugur Nachiyar/Senthamarai). Built in Dravidian style with a three-tiered gopuram rising to roughly 36 metres within a granite boundary wall, the temple is reckoned a Sukra (Venus) sthalam within the Nava Tirupathi's Navagraha associations. Notably, the same site also hosts the Sri Kailasanathar Temple, the seventh shrine of the separate Nava Kailasam Shiva circuit, making Thenthiruperai a joint Vaishnava-Shaiva pilgrimage location. Set among the southern Divya Desams of the Thamiraparani belt, about 5 km from Alwarthirunagari, the birthplace of Nammalwar, this temple shares in the deep devotional heritage that the Alwars wove around the rivers and villages of the far Tamil south. It was Nammalwar, the chief of the Alwars, who alone consecrated this place in song within the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, the four thousand sacred verses; through his hymns the form of the Lord wearing the makara earrings entered the perpetual liturgy of the Srivaishnava tradition. The serene setting of Thenthiruperai, like its sister shrines along the river, has long been a station on the pilgrim path that winds through the nine Divya Desams of the region.

## Festivals

Thenthiruperai, sung in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, traditionally keeps the ten-day Vaikuntha Ekadasi in Margazhi (December-January) as a major festival, accompanied by the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitals customary at the Thamiraparani temples. The Garuda Sevai Utsavam in Vaikasi (May-June) is a notable event when festival idols from all nine Nava Tirupathi temples are paraded together. The annual Brahmotsavam is also kept, with Magara Nedungkuzhaikkaadhar drawn in procession on successive vahanas amid Veda parayanam and Divya Prabandham recitation. Pavithrotsavam, the festival of purification, and a float festival (Theppotsavam) are held where local usage maintains them. The Tamil months of Purattasi, sacred to Vishnu, and Margazhi draw special worship, while the temple's consort is honoured with her own observances during the festival cycle.

## Location & Access

This Divya Desam is located at Thenthiruperai in Thoothukudi district of Pandya Nadu, about 5 km from Alwarthirunagari on the Tiruchendur-Tirunelveli route, set among the Thamiraparani river temples close to Thirukkolur. The nearest major towns and transport hubs are Tirunelveli and Thoothukudi, from which the village is reached by road.
