Panj Tirath, in the Hashtnagri quarter of Peshawar, is one of the oldest documented Hindu pilgrimage sites in what is now Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: an ancient complex of five sacred bathing pools, believed to be over a thousand years old, that historically also included two temples and a large tree-shaded gathering area. Pilgrims traditionally converged here for ritual bathing during the Hindu month of Kartik, with organized annual gatherings continuing until the mid-20th century — though the sources consulted for this entry do not identify the specific deity or deities to whom the site's original temples were dedicated, describing it instead primarily through its distinctive five pools.

Religious activity at Panj Tirath declined sharply after the 1947 Partition, as Peshawar's Hindu population dwindled, and the site fell into a long period of disuse and deterioration. That decline began to reverse only recently: the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa provincial government formally designated Panj Tirath a protected heritage site under its 2016 Antiquities Act in January 2019, and a 2023 memorandum of understanding between the Evacuee Trust Property Board and the province's archaeology directorate set out shared land allocation and conservation responsibilities for its ongoing protection — recognition of the site's significance even as it functions today primarily as a heritage monument rather than a living center of daily worship.
