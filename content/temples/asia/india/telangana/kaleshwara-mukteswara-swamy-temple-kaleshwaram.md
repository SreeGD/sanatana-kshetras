Kaleshwaram, at the confluence of the Godavari river and its Pranahita tributary in Jayashankar Bhupalpally district, Telangana, is home to the Kaleshwara Mukteswara Swamy Temple -- one of the three Trilinga Kshetras, alongside Srisailam's Mallikarjuna Swamy temple in Rayalaseema and the Bhimeswara Swamy temple at Draksharamam in coastal Andhra. Tradition holds that these three sites mark the mythic boundary points of the historic Telugu-speaking country, with Shiva said to have manifested as a linga at each; popular etymology, recorded in the Telugu-grammar tradition of the Andhra Kaumudi, traces the very word "Telugu" (and "Telangana") back to "Trilinga," the "land of three lingas," via the intermediate form "Telinga." This is devotional and etymological tradition rather than settled linguistic scholarship, but it is the origin story most consistently attached to the site's regional significance.

The temple's most distinctive physical feature is a pair of Shiva lingas -- identified in sources simply as Lord Shiva and Lord Yama -- installed together on a single shared pedestal and worshipped jointly as Kaleshwara Mukteeshwara Swamy; the customary order of worship visits Ganesha first, then Yama, then Shiva. That association with Yama, the lord of death, underlies the site's strong reputation as a place for post-funeral rites, popularly described as a "second Kashi" for such observances. Unlike the Pancharama temples of coastal Andhra researched alongside this entry, no founding date, patron dynasty, or inscriptional history for the Kaleshwara Mukteswara Swamy temple was found in the sources consulted here, so its documented architectural history remains thin even though its geographic and ritual significance -- the river confluence, the twin-linga sanctum, and its place in the Trilinga tradition -- is well attested. The temple remains an active site of pilgrimage today, drawing its largest crowds during the Karthika month, when ritual bathing observances are held over roughly ten days.
