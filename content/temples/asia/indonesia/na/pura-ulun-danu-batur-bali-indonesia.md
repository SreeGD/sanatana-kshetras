Pura Ulun Danu Batur is one of Bali's most important directional temples (kahyangan jagat), sited on the caldera rim overlooking Lake Batur and the active volcano Mount Batur in Bangli Regency. Originally founded in the 17th century at the mountain's southwest base, the temple was destroyed by Mount Batur's catastrophic 1926 eruption, which also wiped out the nearby village of Karang Anyar; the surviving 11-tiered shrine was salvaged and the temple entirely rebuilt at its present, higher location by the end of that year. It is dedicated to Dewi Danu, goddess of the lake that supplies irrigation water across much of central Bali, reflecting the deep agricultural and spiritual significance of Lake Batur to Balinese Hindu life.

Today the temple complex comprises nine separate temples with roughly 285 shrines and pavilions, and remains an active site of worship distinct from the similarly named Pura Ulun Danu Bratan at Lake Bratan elsewhere on the island. Its major annual festival, the odalan, falls on the tenth full moon of the Balinese pawukon calendar, typically in late March or early April, drawing large numbers of worshippers to a temple that stands as a striking reminder of Balinese religious resilience in the face of volcanic disaster.
