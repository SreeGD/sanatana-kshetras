Sri Sthala Sayana Perumal Temple, known in Tamil as Thirukadalmallai — "the sacred hill/place by the sea" — stands within the ancient port town of Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram), the maritime capital built up by the Pallava dynasty, most famously under Narasimhavarman I "Mamalla," in the 7th century CE. Here Vishnu is worshipped reclining as Sthalasayana Perumal, facing east toward the sea; unusually for a reclining Vishnu image, he is depicted without the serpent Adishesha beneath him, a distinctive iconographic feature devotees and temple tradition connect to the site's unique legend. His consort is worshipped as Nilamangai Thayar — "the earth-born bride" — identifying her with Bhoodevi, the earth goddess, and reflecting a Bhoodevi-centered strand of the site's mythology alongside the more usual Sridevi/Lakshmi associations found at other Divya Desams.

Thirukadalmallai holds special importance in Sri Vaishnava tradition as the avatara sthalam — the legendary birthplace — of Bhoothathalvar, one of the earliest of the twelve Alvars and one of the three "Mudal Alvars." Temple legend recounts that Vishnu, in the guise of a wandering sage, tested the devotion of a sage or foster-father figure at this spot before revealing his form and remaining here as Sthalasayana Perumal; Bhoothathalvar's own hymn on the temple, brief but foundational, is counted among the very first compositions of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Thirumangai Alvar also sang here, noting that this is the one shrine where he found Vishnu reclining directly upon the ground rather than upon his customary serpent couch.

Architecturally and historically, the temple's core is attributed to the Pallavas, with substantial contributions from the Medieval Cholas — including land grants and structural additions in the 11th and 12th centuries, notably under Vikrama Chola around 1120 CE — and later renovation traditionally credited to the Vijayanagara period, when the shrine is said to have been relocated somewhat inland from its original position closer to the encroaching shoreline. Further support came from the Madurai Nayak rulers in subsequent centuries. Standing today amid Mahabalipuram's UNESCO-listed complex of rock-cut monuments and the famous Shore Temple, Sthala Sayana Perumal Temple remains a functioning place of active Vaishnava worship, distinguished within the 108 Divya Desams both as Bhoothathalvar's birthplace and as one of the very few Divya Desams situated directly on India's eastern coastline.
