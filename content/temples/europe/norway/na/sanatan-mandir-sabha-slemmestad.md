Sanatan Mandir Sabha (SMS) holds the distinction, per Wikipedia's account of Hinduism in Norway, of being the country's first registered Hindu religious community — registered on 14 April 1988, years before most of the country's other Hindu institutions. It is based in Slemmestad, a small town on the Oslofjord in Asker municipality southwest of Oslo, and reports a membership of roughly 900. Norway's Hindu population is estimated to be roughly half Sri Lankan Tamil in origin, with the remainder drawn from India and elsewhere in South Asia, and SMS's own materials do not appear (in the sources available here) to restrict membership along those lines, suggesting it functions as a broader "Sanatan Dharma" community body rather than a single-tradition or single-ethnicity temple.

Beyond the registration date, location, and membership figure, little further detail about SMS's founding, its founders, or the specific deities enshrined at its temple could be confirmed from the sources consulted for this entry. Norway is also home to several other named Hindu temples — including the Hindu Sanatan Mandir Temple in Drammen, the Sivasubramanayar Alayam (Norwegian Hindu Centre) in Ammerud, Oslo, and smaller centers in Bergen, Trondheim, and Ålesund — making SMS one of at least six organized Hindu temple communities in the country as of the mid-2010s, and the earliest of them to be formally registered.
