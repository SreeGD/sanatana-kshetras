Shri Damodar's temple at Zambaulim traces its present location to one of the defining episodes of Goan religious history: the Portuguese campaign against Hindu temples across Salcete (Sasashti) taluka, which in 1567 forced the deity's Gowda Saraswat Brahmin custodians to abandon its original shrine near Margao — on the site now occupied by the Holy Spirit Church — and carry it inland to safety. With the support of local Desai landowning families, the deity was resettled at Zambaulim, on the banks of the Kushavati river in Quepem taluka, where the temple has remained a central institution of the GSB community ever since, offering pilgrim lodging alongside regular worship.

Locally, the deity is venerated as Damodar, a name explained in temple tradition through the story of Makaji Damodar, a Margao devotee said to have died alongside his newly married wife and to have subsequently been honored as a deity in his own right. What makes this worth noting for outside readers is that "Damodar" is, in most of India, an epithet reserved for Krishna or Vishnu rather than Shiva — yet the murti at Zambaulim is explicitly identified in temple and scholarly accounts as a form of Shiva, an example of the syncretic, locally specific naming conventions found across post-relocation Goan temples. The temple's ritual calendar today centers on the Magha Poornima jatrotsav and the Konkan spring festival of Shigmo, alongside Mahashivratri observances befitting its Shaiva dedication.
