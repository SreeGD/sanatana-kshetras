Saptakoteshwar, "Lord of the seven crores (of sages)," was the patron deity of the Kadamba dynasty, whose kings styled themselves protectors of the shrine and took titles invoking the god's name. Its founding is explained in the Skanda Purana through a story of seven sages interrupted mid-prayer, who are said to have fashioned a lingam from seven different metals and installed it on Divar Island in the Mandovi river — devotional tradition rather than a dated historical event.

The temple's later history, by contrast, is one of the more thoroughly documented examples of repeated destruction and rebuilding in this collection, spanning several distinct waves of conflict across roughly three centuries: a Bahmani Sultanate-era destruction in the mid-14th century was followed by restoration under Vijayanagara patronage, and the original Divar Island shrine was destroyed again in 1560 by Portuguese colonial authorities under Dom Joao Nunes de Barreto as part of their wider campaign against Goan temples. At some point in this same period the lingam was moved across the river to the Bicholim-region mainland: tradition credits a local Hindu chieftain, Narayan Shenvi, with rescuing the lingam and building a small temple for it at Hindalem (New Naroa) in 1549, more than a decade before sources date the Portuguese destruction of the original Divar Island shrine "stone by stone" to 1560 -- an order of events the sources consulted do not reconcile, since it implies the Divar temple was destroyed after its sacred image had already been removed. The temple as it stands today at Narve, with its Kadamba-influenced architecture, Mughal-style dome, octagonal drum, and tiled roofs, dates to a reconstruction carried out in 1668 under Maratha initiative, and it remains an active site of Shaiva worship despite further restrictions on Hindu practice imposed by Portuguese authorities later in the 17th century.
