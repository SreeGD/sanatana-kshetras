Tiruchendur is unusual among the six Arupadaiveedu shrines for standing directly on the seashore, on the Bay of Bengal in what is now Thoothukudi district, rather than on a hill. Its mythology is drawn from the Kanda Puranam, a 17th-century Tamil rendering of the older Sanskrit Skanda Purana, and its documented history includes a difficult chapter under Dutch occupation from 1646 to 1648 CE, after which local devotee Vadamalaiyappa Pillai is credited with recovering the temple's processional idols in 1653. The present complex, roughly 91 by 65 metres, is fronted by a nine-tier gopuram about 157 feet (48 m) high, and the temple received ISO certification in 2016 for its facility management.

The temple's central legend is the climactic battle of the Kanda Puranam: the asura Surapadman, cornered by Murugan's forces, took his final refuge in the ocean in the form of an immense mango tree. Murugan's spear (vel) split the tree in two; from its halves emerged a peacock, which became Murugan's mount, and a rooster, which became the emblem on his war banner — Surapadman himself was thereby subdued rather than annihilated, and made to serve the god in these transformed guises. This episode, Soorasamharam ("the slaying of the asura"), is re-enacted here as the temple's largest annual festival, drawing large crowds and marking Tiruchendur's traditional role among the Arupadaiveedu as the site of Murugan's decisive victory in the war.
