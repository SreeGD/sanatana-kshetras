The Krishna image worshipped as Govind Dev Ji has a well-documented, and eventful, journey behind it. Tradition holds that the original icon was carved by Bajranabh, Krishna's great-grandson, and lost to memory for centuries until it was rediscovered in Vrindavan through the efforts of the 16th-century Gaudiya Vaishnava saint Rupa Goswami, one of the Chaitanya movement's leading theologians. A grand seven-story temple was built for the deity in Vrindavan by the Kachhwaha general Man Singh I and consecrated in 1570, making it one of the major temples of the Braj revival. That temple did not survive intact: in 1670, as Aurangzeb's campaign against temples swept through the Braj region, the deity was moved out of Vrindavan for safekeeping, beginning a period of relocation that eventually brought it to Rajasthan.

Govind Dev Ji found its lasting home in the 18th century, when Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II, founder of Jaipur, installed the deity within his own City Palace complex, effectively making Krishna the presiding deity of his new capital. The temple remains a center of Gaudiya Vaishnava practice — it is where the theologian Baladeva Vidyabhushana is said to have composed the Govinda Bhashya, an important commentary defending the school's Vedanta position — and continues to draw large crowds for Krishna festivals such as Janmashtami. Its modern Satsang Hall, built to accommodate those crowds, was recognized by Guinness World Records in 2009 as the widest single-span concrete building on earth.
