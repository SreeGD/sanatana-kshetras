Seetha Amman Temple, in the hamlet of Sita Eliya a short drive from the hill town of Nuwara Eliya, is popularly described as the only temple in the world dedicated to Sita, and it anchors one of the most visited stops on Sri Lanka's Ramayana Trail — a modern pilgrimage circuit, built mainly around Indian tourism, connecting sites across the island traditionally associated with episodes from the Ramayana. Local tradition holds that this is where Sita was held captive by Ravana after her abduction, that the neighboring Hakgala Botanical Garden was the pleasure garden Ravana created to console her, and that Hanuman met her here, presenting Rama's ring as a token of reassurance.

The temple's present structure grew out of a simple stone representation of Rama, Lakshmana, and Sita and was built up into a fuller temple by Indian Tamil workers brought to Sri Lanka's central highlands as indentured plantation labor during British colonial rule, with construction completed by the early 21st century. It remains most closely tied to and maintained by Sri Lanka's Indian Tamil community today, functioning as an active center of Vaishnava devotion centered on Sita's endurance and faithfulness, and drawing a steady stream of Indian pilgrims tracing the Ramayana's geography across the island.
