Widely regarded as among the first Hindu temples established in Canberra, Sri Vishnu Shiva Mandir — operating publicly as The Hindu Temple and Cultural Centre ACT — has served the Australian Capital Territory's Hindu community since 1989 from its site at 82 Mawson Drive in the Mawson suburb. As its name indicates, the temple's central dedication spans both Vishnu and Shiva, the two great devotional currents of Vaishnavism and Shaivism, with additional shrines reported for Ganesh, Krishna, Satyanarayana Swamy, Parvati, Murugan, Maha Devi, the Rama-Sita-Lakshman triad, Hanuman, and Venkateswara — a broad roster reflecting the pan-Indian character typical of diaspora temples serving a capital-city community drawn from across India's regional traditions.

Beyond regular worship — daily puja conducted by a resident priest, with visiting pandits conducting festivals and utsavas in the Agama tradition — the centre operates as a genuine community hub: it houses a public library serving as a resource centre for South Asian art and literature, and offers yoga classes, language classes, hall bookings, and catering services. Its festival calendar includes Ganesh Chaturthi, Krishna Janmashtami, Onam Sadhya, periodic Shiva Abhishekam ceremonies, and an annual Akhand Ramayan Paath — a continuous 24-hour recitation of the Ramayana.
