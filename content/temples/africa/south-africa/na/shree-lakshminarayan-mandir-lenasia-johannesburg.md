Serving Lenasia, a Johannesburg suburb established for Indian residents under apartheid-era group areas policy, this temple traces its founding to 1970, when — per the temple's own history page — "the first flame of the Ramakrishna Vedanta Society was lit," credited to pioneers Bhikhabhai Ukabhai Master and Dayabhai Hari together with Rajnikantbhai Master. The temple building itself was officially opened on 25 December 1972 by Swami Venkatesanandji, with Shivram Maharaj performing the Murthi Sthapana (consecration) using murtis crafted in Jaipur, India; Jinabhai Parekh served as the temple's first chairman.

Dedicated primarily to Lakshmi Narayan (Vishnu with Lakshmi), with subsidiary worship of Hanuman and Ram, the temple today runs daily aarti, weekly Vedic-chanting and Bhagavad Gita study classes, a Sunday children's school, and a large online library of Hindu philosophy texts, alongside observance of major festivals such as Ram Navami and Hanuman Jayanti.
