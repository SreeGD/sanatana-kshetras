When Rao Jodha founded Mehrangarh Fort in 1459 as the new seat of Rathore power, he brought the family's protecting deity with him: an image of Chamunda Mata carried from Mandore, the dynasty's earlier capital, and installed within the new fort in 1460. Chamunda — a fierce form of Durga associated in devotional tradition with the slaying of the demons Chanda and Munda — has remained the Isht Devi, or chosen deity, of the Rathore royal family ever since, and is venerated widely by Jodhpur's citizens beyond the palace itself.

The temple remains an active and heavily visited shrine within the fort, drawing its largest crowds during Dussehra, when Navratri celebrations culminate in the burning of Ravana's effigy elsewhere in the city. That popularity turned tragic on 30 September 2008, when a stampede among Dussehra pilgrims at the temple killed 224 people and injured several hundred more, one of the deadliest such incidents at an Indian temple in recent decades — a sober reminder of how central this shrine remains to the city's devotional life.
