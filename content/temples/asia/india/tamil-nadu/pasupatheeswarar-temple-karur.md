The Pasupatheeswarar Temple at Karur is a Paadal Petra Sthalam where the Nayanar saint Sundarar composed a Tevaram hymn cycle in praise of the presiding deity, worshipped here as Pasupatheeswarar and also as Aanilaiappar. Unusually, the temple maintains two separate forms of the goddess as consort, Sundaravalli and Alankaravalli, alongside a swayambu, or self-manifested, lingam in the main sanctum. Documented royal patronage under the Chola emperor Rajendra Chola I in the first half of the 11th century included a grant of land to the temple.

Fronted by a 120-foot gopuram and centered on a hundred-pillar mandapam, the temple also houses the samadhi of Karuvurar, a Shaiva saint-poet associated with the town. A notable solar alignment is observed each year during the Panguni Brahmotsavam, when for three days sunlight is said to fall directly upon the sanctum lingam.
