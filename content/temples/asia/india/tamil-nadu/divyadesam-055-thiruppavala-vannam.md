The Pavala Vannan Temple at Thiruppavala Vannam, within the sacred precincts of Kanchipuram in Thondai Nadu, enshrines the Lord in His coral-hued form as Pavala Vannar (Pravala Varnan), the presiding Moolavar from whom the kshetra takes its very name. Counted among the 108 Divya Desams, the abodes of Vishnu hallowed by the song of the Alwars, this shrine is celebrated for the Lord's resplendent appearance, His complexion likened to gleaming coral or red coral, a vision of beauty that the devotee is invited to behold and adore. The temple stands as one of the cluster of divine sanctuaries that make Kanchipuram one of the foremost concentrations of Divya Desams, the city sometimes revered as a seat of liberation. According to tradition, the place is honoured as one where the Lord graciously manifests His auspicious form for the contemplation of His devotees. The sacred shrine received the consecration of hymn through Thirumangai Alwar, the last and most prolific of the twelve Alwars, whose pasurams in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham sing the glory of this place and secure its standing within the canonical roll of the Divya Desams. By the grace of the Alwar's verses, Pavala Vannan is remembered and worshipped in the unbroken Srivaishnava tradition, and pilgrims journeying through the Divya Desams of Thondai Nadu pause here to take darshan of the coral-coloured Lord and to seek His benevolence.

## Festivals

As one of the Divya Desams within sacred Kanchipuram, the Pavala Vannan temple traditionally follows the Srivaishnava festival cycle observed across the temple city. The annual Brahmotsavam, with its sequence of processions in which the Lord is taken around on various vahanas, is the principal celebration. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the Tamil month of Margazhi is observed with great devotion, along with the recitation of the Tiruvaymozhi and the Nalayira Divya Prabandham during the Adhyayana Utsavam. Pavithrotsavam, performed for the purification of the year's worship, and Pongal and Tamil New Year observances are also kept. Sri Jayanthi, Rama Navami, and the festival days of the Azhwars and Acharyas are honoured in keeping with the rich devotional heritage of Kanchipuram.

## Location & Access

The Pavala Vannan temple stands in Kanchipuram, the historic temple city of the Thondai Nadu region in northern Tamil Nadu, well connected by road and rail and lying within reach of Chennai, the nearest major city and transport hub. The shrine generally observes morning and evening darshan hours like most Vishnu temples, remaining closed during the midday interval. Pilgrims often visit it together with the other Divya Desams clustered in and around Kanchipuram. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
