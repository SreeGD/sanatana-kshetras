The Parthasarathy Temple at Triplicane, the sacred Thiruvallikeni in Thondai Nadu, is among the most celebrated of the 108 Divya Desams and stands at the heart of the great city of Chennai. The presiding deity is Parthasarathy, 'the charioteer of Partha,' the form in which Lord Krishna served as Arjuna's charioteer upon the field of Kurukshetra. In keeping with this legend, the Lord here is traditionally depicted bearing marks upon His countenance, understood as the scars of the arrows received in battle while He shielded His devotee, a vivid emblem of the saulabhya, the gracious accessibility, with which He stands ever ready to protect those who seek refuge. The place-name Thiruvallikeni, the 'sacred lily pond,' derives from the Tamil "Thiru-Alli-Keni," referencing the temple tank (Kairavani pushkarani) once ringed by a large tulasi forest -- the area is separately called "Brindaranyam" in the Brahmanda Purana for its abundant tulasi. Three of the Alwars sang the glory of this shrine: Pey Alwar, one of the three foremost Mudhal Alwars, together with Thirumazhisai Alwar and Thirumangai Alwar, and their hymns enshrine Thiruvallikeni firmly within the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The presence of Pey Alwar's verse links the temple to the very dawn of the Alwar tradition. Five forms of Vishnu are enshrined within the complex: Yoga Narasimha, Rama, Gajendra Varadaraja, Ranganatha, and Krishna as Parthasarathy, the divine charioteer, whose processional image, Sri Venkatakrishnan, is traditionally shown with a moustache and bearing scars from the arrows he took shielding Arjuna at Kurukshetra -- a vivid emblem of the saulabhya, the gracious accessibility, with which He stands ever ready to protect those who seek refuge.

The temple's documented history includes extensive Chola-era rebuilding and major 15th-16th century Vijayanagara expansion under kings Sadasiva Raya and Sriranga Raya. In the 18th and 19th centuries the temple was the site of sectarian disputes between the Tenkalai and Vadakalai Srivaishnava subsects over recitation rights, resolved by an 1754 petition; it was administered by the East India Company from 1700 to 1842. Tradition connects the birth of the philosopher Ramanuja to this temple, his father having performed a ritual at the temple tank seeking a son. In more recent centuries the temple has drawn devotion from figures including the Carnatic composer Muthuswami Dikshitar, the poet Bharathiar, and Swami Vivekananda, whose 1893 letter to a disciple is inscribed on a temple corridor wall. Its grand celebrations and the daily devotion of countless pilgrims make Thiruvallikeni a living jewel among the Divya Desams, where the Lord who guided Arjuna continues to guide and guard all who come before Him.

## Festivals

The Parthasarathy Temple at Triplicane keeps a rich annual festival calendar. Its celebrated Brahmotsavam is observed with the utsava deities carried in procession on successive vahanas and on the great chariot through the Mada streets, an event of much antiquity and renown. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is a major occasion, marked by the Adhyayana Utsavam with Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu, the recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, and the opening of the Paramapada Vasal. The float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank, the Pavithrotsavam, the Udayavar (Ramanuja) and Vedanta Desika utsavams, and the procession honouring the Lord as Parthasarathy bearing the marks of the Mahabharata war are all traditionally observed, along with the regular monthly and Tamil festival days.

## Location & Access

The Parthasarathy Temple lies in the historic Triplicane (Thiruvallikeni) neighbourhood of central Chennai, close to the Marina shoreline in the Thondai Nadu region. Being within the city, it is easily reached by Chennai's bus and suburban rail networks and is near the central railway and other major transport hubs. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours, closing for a period at midday. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
