Thirukkudanthai, the sacred town of Kumbakonam in the heart of Chola Nadu, is among the most celebrated of the 108 Divya Desams, and its temple of Sarangapani is one of the foremost Vishnu shrines of the Tamil land. The presiding deity (Moolavar) is worshipped as Sarangapani, the Lord who bears the great bow Sarnga, reclining in regal repose; the sanctum is famously fashioned in the form of a grand chariot drawn by horses and elephants, evoking the descent of the Lord to earth, with openings on either side. According to the sthala purana, the Lord manifested here for the sage Hemarishi, and the place is linked to the divine 'kumbha' or pot of nectar from which the town takes its name, marking it as a holy site where the waters of creation and grace are said to gather. Thirukkudanthai holds extraordinary distinction in the Prabandham, for it was sung by a remarkable assembly of Alwars: the first three Mudhal Alwars—Bhoothath Alwar and Pey Alwar—together with Thirumazhisai Alwar, the great Nammalwar (credited with 11 pasurams here), Periyalwar, his foster-daughter Andal the only woman Alwar, and Thirumangai Alwar (credited with 25 pasurams). This rare concourse of saintly voices marks the shrine as supremely beloved within the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Pilgrims revere Sarangapani as a master of compassion and the consort of Komalavalli Thayar.

Documented history confirms building contributions across several dynasties: early epigraphical evidence places the earliest confirmed work in the late Chola period (10th-12th centuries), with further additions credited to the Vijayanagara Empire and the Thanjavur Nayaks. The temple's 11-tiered rajagopuram rises about 173 feet (53 m), and the complex includes a 100-pillared hall leading to the sanctum and the Potramarai ("golden lotus") tank just outside the western entrance. Worship follows six daily ritual periods. The temple's twin processional chariots -- each weighing roughly 300 tonnes at the base, among the heaviest temple chariots in Tamil Nadu -- are drawn through the streets twice a year, during the Chittirai Brahmotsavam and the Thai Ratha Saptami. The temple is administered by the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments (HR&CE) Department.

## Festivals

The Sarangapani Temple at Kumbakonam observes a grand annual Brahmotsavam in the Tamil month of Chittirai (April/May), when the celebrated 300-tonne twin chariots are drawn through the streets; the chariots are drawn a second time each year during Ratha Saptami in Thai (January/February). Navaratri in Purattasi (September/October) and a Deepavali observance in Aippasi (October/November) are also kept, alongside the wider Srivaishnava cycle of Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi, with its Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitations of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and customary observances such as the Float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank and Pavithrotsavam.

## Location & Access

The Sarangapani Temple stands in the heart of Kumbakonam (Thirukkudanthai), a major temple town in the Chola Nadu region of the Thanjavur district, Tamil Nadu. Kumbakonam is itself a well-connected rail and road hub, with Thanjavur and Tiruchirappalli the nearest larger cities. The temple follows six daily worship periods (approximately 8:00 AM, 9:00 AM, 12:30 PM, 6:00 PM, 7:30 PM, and 9:00 PM). The shrine sits within the town and is easily reached on foot from the Kumbakonam railway and bus stations. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
