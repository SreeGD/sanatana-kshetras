Temple Changy, in the Changy locality of Capesterre-Belle-Eau on Guadeloupe's Basse-Terre coast, is described by French Wikipedia as the largest Hindu temple on the island. Built in 1974, it replaced a smaller chapel dedicated to Mariamman (known locally as "Maléimin") that had already stood for more than a century, reflecting the long continuity of Tamil-Hindu ("Malabar") worship on Guadeloupe since the first convoy of Indian indentured laborers arrived in 1854 following the abolition of slavery. Its religious custodian for decades was René Komla, who died in 2020.

Architecturally, the temple follows South Indian Tamil temple conventions transplanted to the Caribbean: a rectangular, stepped pyramidal gopura tower of an odd number of tiers crowned with kalasam finials, decorated with statues of deities and guardian animals, and entered through a columned portico. Alongside its principal Mariamman shrine — the fierce, protective village-goddess form central to Tamil folk Hinduism — the temple maintains subsidiary altars to Kali, Ganesha, Saraswati, and the folk deities Madurai Veeran and Nagur Mira, the same syncretic pantheon venerated by Tamil-descended Hindu communities elsewhere in the French and English Caribbean, including neighboring Martinique and Trinidad.
