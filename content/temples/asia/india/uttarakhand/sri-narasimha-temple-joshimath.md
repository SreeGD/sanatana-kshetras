Thiruppirithi, the Divya Desam of Joshimath, is dedicated to Narasimha, worshipped here as Paramapurushan in a reclining (bhujanga-sayana) posture rather than the fierce standing or seated forms more common elsewhere. Thirumangai Alvar's ten pasurams on this sthala are notable for weaving together three avatars of Vishnu — Rama, Krishna and Narasimha — within the same set of verses, reflecting the composite character of worship at this Himalayan site, where local tradition holds that Adi Shankaracharya established both a Narasimha shrine and a standing form of Vasudeva.

Joshimath's greatest significance within the Vada Nadu group, however, lies in its role as the winter seat of Badrinath: when the high Himalayan Badrinath temple closes for approximately six months each year under heavy snow, the ceremonial worship of Badrinarayan is transferred with much ritual to this Narasimha temple, making Joshimath and Badrinath a linked pair of shrines sharing one continuous line of worship across the seasons. Joshimath is also one of the four cardinal mathas (Jyotirmath) established by Adi Shankaracharya. In recent years the town has drawn attention for land subsidence and structural cracking linked to the fragile Himalayan terrain, a concern that pilgrimage authorities and geologists continue to monitor.
