Doleshwor Mahadeva, a pagoda-style Shiva temple in Suryabinayak, southeastern Bhaktapur District, is an active pilgrimage site within the Kathmandu Valley's dense constellation of Newar temples. Its exact founding date and construction history are not established in the sources consulted for this entry, but it functions today as a living center of Shaiva worship, drawing devotees especially for the three major observances of Maha Shivaratri, Teej, and Balachaturdashi.

The temple sits within a valley whose temple-building tradition spans well over a millennium, alongside better-known Bhaktapur District landmarks such as Changu Narayan and the Suryavinayak Ganesh shrine nearby — a reminder of how densely the Kathmandu Valley's sacred geography is layered even beyond its most famous pilgrimage sites.
