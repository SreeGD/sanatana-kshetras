The Mayuranathaswami Temple gives its town, Mayiladuthurai ("town of the peacock"), its name, and is a Paadal Petra Sthalam glorified in the Tevaram by both Appar and Thirugnanasambandar. Shiva is worshipped here as Mayuranathaswamy, alongside his consort Abhayambika. Tradition dates an original shrine to the 7th century CE, with the temple substantially renovated and expanded from the 10th-century Chola period onward into its present form, fronted by a nine-storey gopuram rising roughly 194 feet.

The temple maintains a Brahma Theertham sacred tank on its eastern side and an Avayambal Amman shrine housing bronze processional idols dated to 1229 CE. It is administered today by the Thiruvavaduthurai Adheenam, one of the principal Shaiva mathas of the Kaveri delta, and its festival calendar includes the Mayura Natyanjali dance festival, the Margazhi Thiruvathirai observance, and a Theppotsavam (float festival) on its temple tank.
