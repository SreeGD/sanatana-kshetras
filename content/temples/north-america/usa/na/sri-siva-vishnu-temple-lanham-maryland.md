One of the largest Hindu temples in the United States, Sri Siva Vishnu Temple in Lanham, Maryland took an unusually long fourteen years to complete: construction began in 1988 and continued in phases until 2002, when the final deity was consecrated. Distinctively, it houses two co-equal main shrines rather than a single primary deity — Shiva as Ramanathaswamy and Vishnu as Ananthapadmanabha — reflecting an ecumenical approach adopted by many South Indian-style American temples serving religiously diverse immigrant communities, with its architecture drawing on the Pallava, Chola, and Vijayanagara traditions of South India. Numerous subsidiary shrines to Parvathi, Lakshmi, Durga, Rama, Krishna, Hanuman, and other deities surround the two main sanctums, and the temple today serves as a major religious and cultural center for the Hindu community of the greater Washington, D.C. area, hosting regular concerts and classical dance performances alongside daily worship.
