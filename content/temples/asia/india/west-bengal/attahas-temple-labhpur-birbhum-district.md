Attahas is counted among Bengal's 51 Shakti Peethas, the site where tradition holds that the lips of Sati fell after her body was cut apart following her self-immolation at Daksha's yajna. The Goddess is worshipped here as Phullara, an aspect of Parvati, and the name "Attahas" itself is traditionally read as referring to loud or unrestrained laughter (hasya) associated with the Goddess's presence at this spot — though the sources checked do not spell out the exact etymological story in detail.

Precisely which village hosts the shrine is not entirely consistent across the sources found: the town of Labhpur in Birbhum district, on the Mayurakshi river, is identified on Wikipedia's own Labhpur page as "one of the 51 Shakta pithas," and separately, Wikipedia's Birbhum district article names "Fullara at Labpur" directly, alongside Bakreshwar and Tarapith, as one of the district's recognized pilgrimage attractions — two independent sources now pointing to a real, physically located Fullara shrine at Labhpur specifically. Weighed against that, a separate Wikipedia article on a place called "Attahas, Katwa" locates a shrine of the same name and legend in Dakshindihi village, in the adjoining Purba Bardhaman district, with the Bhairava named Vishweshwar. No source checked reconciles the two claims directly, so this entry follows the weight of evidence toward Labhpur without treating the Dakshindihi alternative as ruled out. No dated inscriptional, architectural, or founder history could be found for either candidate site in the sources checked, so this entry should be read as documenting the Attahas Shakti Peetha tradition broadly rather than a single precisely verified structure.
