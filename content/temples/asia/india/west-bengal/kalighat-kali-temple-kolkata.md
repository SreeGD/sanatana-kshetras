Kalighat Kali Temple, on the bank of a now largely silted channel of the Hooghly river in south Kolkata, is among the most visited and revered Kali temples in India and one of the principal Shakti Peethas of Bengal. Tradition holds that when Sati's dismembered body fell to earth after Vishnu's discus cut it apart, the toes of her right foot landed here, making the site a seat of the Goddess as Kali long before any masonry temple stood on it; the city name Kolkata is popularly, if not certainly, linked to "Kalikshetra" or "Kalighat." The location appears in Bengali literary works that the source consulted dates to the 15th and 17th centuries, though no documented date survives for any earlier temple building on the spot.

The temple as it now stands was built between 1798 and 1809 under the patronage of the Sabarna Roy Choudhury family, a landed Bengali family long associated with the area, and follows the ath-chala (eight-roofed, gable-hut derived) style typical of Bengal temple architecture, its terracotta ornament and metallic-finished spires reflecting that regional idiom. In 2024 the temple underwent a large-scale renovation — reportedly around Rs 200 crore, with the Reliance Foundation among the contributors — that regilded its spires and brought older terracotta detailing back into view without altering the core structure. The temple remains an intensely active center of Kali worship, particularly during Kali Puja, Durga Puja, and the Bengali New Year.
