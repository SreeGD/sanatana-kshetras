Pura Agung Jagatnatha, built in 1953 in the heart of Denpasar, is described as the most important Hindu temple in Bali's provincial capital. Unlike the island's older directional and lineage temples, it was purpose-built as a state/civic temple for the city, centered on a padmasana ("lotus throne") shrine — an architectural form used across Bali specifically to honor Sang Hyang Widhi Wasa, the singular, formless supreme godhead of Balinese Hindu (Agama Hindu Dharma) theology, rather than any single anthropomorphic deity image.

As Denpasar's principal civic Hindu temple, Pura Jagatnatha serves the city's urban Balinese Hindu population much as a cathedral serves a diocese, hosting major public ceremonies and remaining an active site of worship at the symbolic center of Bali's largest city and seat of provincial government.
