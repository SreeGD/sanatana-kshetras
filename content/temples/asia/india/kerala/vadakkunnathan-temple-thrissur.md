An ancient Shiva temple at the center of Thrissur city, traditionally associated with Parashurama, who, per Kerala's foundation legend, first asked the sea-god Varuna to raise the land of Kerala from the ocean and then persuaded Shiva to manifest as a lingam beneath a banyan tree at Sri Moola Sthana, installing the deity as the first of the 108 Shiva temples he consecrated in Kerala. The main sanctum houses a roughly 16-foot lingam entirely covered by a mound of ghee. The temple complex includes three main sanctums -- a circular shrine for Shiva, a rectangular shrine for Rama, and a circular, double-storied shrine for Shankaranarayana -- along with four gopurams facing the cardinal directions and a koothambalam (temple theatre) used for classical performance traditions. In 2012 the Archaeological Survey of India recommended the temple, together with thirteen other Kerala sites, for UNESCO World Heritage status; it remains on UNESCO's tentative list rather than formally inscribed. The Thekkinkadu Maidan surrounding the temple is the focal point of the Thrissur Pooram, often called the mother of all poorams in Kerala, a 36-hour festival featuring elaborately caparisoned elephants.
