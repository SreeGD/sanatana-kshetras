Unique among the eight Ashtavinayak shrines for being named after a human devotee rather than a demon or epithet of Ganesha himself. Tradition holds that a boy named Ballal, absorbed in devotion to Ganesha to the point of neglecting his studies, was tied to a tree and abandoned in the forest by his exasperated father; Ganesha appeared, freed him, and — at Ballal's request — agreed to remain at the site permanently as Ballaleshwar. The temple faces east and is built largely of black stone.

The present stone structure dates to 1760, when Nana Phadnavis, a powerful minister of the Peshwa administration, rebuilt an earlier wooden shrine on the site. The east-facing temple has two sanctums — an outer hall about 12 feet tall leading to an inner sanctum roughly 15 feet tall — supported by eight carved pillars said to represent the eight directions, with the stone blocks bonded using molten lead rather than mortar. The murti's eyes and navel are set with diamonds, and its trunk curves to the left. A carved mouse (Ganesha's mount, Mushika) holding a modaka sits facing the deity. Unusually for the Ashtavinayak circuit, the customary prasad offered here is besan laddoo rather than modak.
