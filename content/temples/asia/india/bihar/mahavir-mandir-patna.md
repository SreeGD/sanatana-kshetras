One of the most-visited Hanuman temples in India and, by some measures, the second-wealthiest temple trust in North India after Vaishno Devi. Its exact founding is contested: a 1948 Patna High Court ruling described it as having existed 'since time immemorial,' while historical accounts more specifically attribute its establishment to Swami Balanand, an ascetic of the Ramanandi sect, around 1730 CE. Located directly opposite Patna's main railway station, the temple sees queues reportedly exceeding a kilometer during major festivals such as Rama Navami, and its trust — known for funding a network of low-cost hospitals across Bihar — reports daily earnings above a lakh of rupees. The temple also runs a free-meals service, Ram Rasoi, serving devotees daily between 11:30 AM and 3:00 PM.
