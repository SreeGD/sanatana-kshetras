Korsnäs Gård, formally known within the community as New Radhakunda, is a rural ISKCON (Hare Krishna) community and temple in the Grödinge area of Botkyrka Municipality, part of Stockholm County, Sweden, on a farmstead dating back to the 1600s whose current main building was constructed in 1901. Unlike most other ISKCON farm communities documented elsewhere in this directory, its distinguishing role is publishing rather than agriculture: Korsnäs Gård's main function, per both its own site and Wikipedia's overview of Hinduism in Sweden, is to house the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust (BBT) operation that translates Srila Prabhupada's Hare Krishna books into languages used around the world, making it a significant institutional node for the movement's literature distribution well beyond Sweden's own borders. Both sources describe it as "Scandinavia's only Radha-Krishna temple."

The Swedish ISKCON movement's own broader history traces back to 1973, when its founder, A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, visited Sweden in person — around the same period he was also establishing ISKCON's presence in Switzerland, Italy, and elsewhere in Europe. Sources disagree on exactly when Korsnäs Gård itself became a temple: the community's own site credits Prabhupada personally with its 1976 founding, while a Swedish Wikipedia article instead dates the movement's temple there to 1980. The property is also of independent historical interest, having yielded significant Neolithic-era archaeological finds from the Pitted Ware culture (roughly 3300-2300 BCE), predating its religious use by millennia.
