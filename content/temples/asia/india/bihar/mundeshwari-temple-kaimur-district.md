Among the oldest surviving Hindu temple structures in India, though its exact age is genuinely disputed: the Bihar State Religious Trust Board dates it to 108 CE based on an inscription naming a ruler, Maharaja Udayasena, while architectural historians place the surviving structure closer to the 6th-7th century CE, with the present octagonal shrine itself possibly rebuilt as late as the 16th-17th century using salvaged older material. A Brahmi-inscribed seal of the Sri Lankan king Dutthagamani (1st century BCE), found at the site in 2003, points to very ancient pilgrimage traffic regardless of the exact construction date. The sanctum houses both a four-faced (Chaturmukh) Shiva linga and a ten-armed image of the goddess as Mahishasuramardini, reflecting a combined Shaiva-Shakta character. It has been an Archaeological Survey of India-protected monument since 1915.
