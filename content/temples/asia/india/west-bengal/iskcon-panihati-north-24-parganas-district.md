Panihati, on the Hooghly River in North 24 Parganas district, is one of Gaudiya Vaishnavism's most storied pilgrimage towns, both for Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's own recorded visit and for the events surrounding his closest associate, Nityananda Prabhu. In 1517 CE, Nityananda encountered the young Raghunatha Das — a wealthy zamindar's son who longed to renounce his household life and join Chaitanya's movement but was kept back by his family — at a gathering beneath a historic banyan tree on the riverbank at what is now called Mahotsav Tala Ghat. Nityananda playfully accused him of being "a thief who tried to hide" and imposed a mock penalty: that he arrange a feast of chida (flattened rice) and dahi (yogurt) for the assembled devotees. Raghunatha Das threw himself into the task, and Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition holds that Chaitanya Mahaprabhu himself mystically appeared to share in the feast alongside Nityananda. Raghunatha Das went on to become one of the six principal Goswamis of Vrindavan.

The resulting festival, the Panihati Chida Dahi Utsav (also called Danda Mahotsav, the "Festival of Punishment"), is still celebrated at Panihati every year on Sukla Trayodashi in the month of Jyeshtha (May/June), now drawing hundreds of thousands of pilgrims to the town and replicated at more than a hundred ISKCON and Gaudiya Math temples around the world. In 2013, the ISKCON teacher and administrator Bhakti Charu Swami oversaw the development of a new ISKCON temple in the town itself, explicitly sited there as "the home of Raghava Pandit [another close associate of Chaitanya] and the place of Lord Nityananda's Flattened Rice Festival" — giving the historic festival site a permanent, present-day temple alongside its centuries-old riverside gathering ground.
