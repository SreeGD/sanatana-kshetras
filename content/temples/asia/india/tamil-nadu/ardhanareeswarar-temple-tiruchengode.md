The Ardhanareeswarar Temple at Tiruchengode, in Namakkal district, is one of the very few Paadal Petra Sthalams dedicated specifically to Ardhanarishvara, the composite half-male, half-female form of Shiva and Parvati united in a single body — a comparatively rare iconographic dedication for a major temple, and this entry's representative of the Kongu Nadu region within this directory. The presiding deity is glorified by the Nayanar saint Sambandar in the Tevaram. The temple crowns a sacred hill roughly 650 feet high, reached by a flight of 1,156 steps, and its documented history runs through inscriptions from the Chola kings Parantaka and Gangaikonda Chola, through the Vijayanagara and Nayak periods.

The temple's 84.5-foot rajagopuram was raised under the Vijayanagara emperor Krishnadevaraya in 1512, and the hillside itself bears a 60-foot carved serpent known as Nagagiri. Its principal annual event is a fifteen-day chariot festival in the Tamil month of Vaikasi (May/June), while devotees also perform Girivalam — a circumambulation of the sacred hill — on full-moon days throughout the year.
