Built in 1905 by indentured Indian labourers, this Shiva temple in Nadi stood for over a century as one of the oldest Hindu temples in Fiji, a direct legacy of the community brought to the islands under British colonial indenture. Its age and community significance made it a recognized local landmark among Fiji's Indo-Fijian population well before its destruction.

On 16 October 2008, the temple was destroyed by an arson fire, the fourth Hindu temple in Fiji burned within a two-week wave of communal violence that year. The *Fiji Times* editorial board condemned the attack as the loss of a local landmark and publicly urged Christian church leaders to call on their congregations to stop the burnings. This entry documents that history; whether the temple has since been rebuilt is not confirmed in the sources consulted, so it is recorded here for its historical significance rather than as a current place of worship.
