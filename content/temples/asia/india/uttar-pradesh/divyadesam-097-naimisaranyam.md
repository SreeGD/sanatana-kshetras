The Devaraja Perumal Temple at Thirunaimisaranyam (Naimisaranyam), in the Vada Nadu or Northern Region, enshrines the Lord as Devaraja Perumal, one of the 108 Divya Desams sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Naimisaranyam is among the most venerable of all sacred forests in the wider Hindu tradition, celebrated across the Puranas as the great assembly-ground where sages gathered in retreat to hear and recite the eternal narratives of the Lord; tradition holds it to be a place where the merit of austerity and the recitation of holy lore is greatly multiplied. The very name evokes a primeval forest set apart for tapas and the unbroken contemplation of Narayana, and the presence of the Lord here as Devaraja sanctifies that long lineage of devotion. Among the Alwars, it was Thirumangai Alwar, the warrior-saint whose pilgrim songs reach the farthest of the northern shrines, who sang the glory of this abode and secured its place within the Divya Desam canon. As one of the comparatively few Divya Desams set in the distant north, far from the Tamil country of most of these shrines, Naimisaranyam holds a special distinction: it binds the southern devotional heritage of the Alwars to one of the most ancient pan-Indian seats of sacred recitation. Pilgrims revere it as a place where worship of Vishnu and the hearing of His glories have continued from time immemorial, and where the grace of Devaraja Perumal rewards the devotee who comes seeking refuge in the forest of the sages.

## Festivals

As one of the northern Divya Desams revered in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, the observances here follow the broad Srivaishnava festival cycle rather than the elaborate Tamil temple calendar of the southern shrines. The annual Brahmotsavam, when celebrated, honours Devaraja Perumal with processions and special abhishekams, and Vaikunta Ekadasi is traditionally kept as a day of fasting and darshan. Sri Rama Navami, Krishna Janmashtami, and the great Vaishnava Ekadasis are marked here, fitting for a place celebrated in the Puranas as Naimisaranyam, the forest where sages gathered to hear sacred recitation. Pilgrims often combine their visit with the wider sanctity of this tirtha rather than with a single named utsavam.

## Location & Access

Naimisaranyam (Naimisharanya) lies in Sitapur district of Uttar Pradesh in the northern region, near the Gomti river, roughly northwest of Lucknow, the nearest major city and transport hub with rail and air connections. The temple is set within this ancient pilgrimage forest area. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday break.
