The presiding deity, the Moolavar, is worshipped here as Neeragathan, the Lord whose very name evokes his association with sacred waters, neer meaning water in Tamil. Tradition counts this among the cluster of Divya Desams gathered in and around Kanchipuram, several of which are revered as shrines whose Lord is present in subtle and inward forms rather than always in grand independent temples. The glory of Thiruneeragam was sung by Thirumangai Alwar, the great warrior-saint whose extensive pilgrimages and copious pasurams secured for so many shrines of the Thondai country their place in the canonical corpus. By his hymning, this abode was raised into the sacred hundred and eight, and his verses preserve its memory as a place where the Lord graciously chose to dwell and bless his devotees. Faithful to the reticence of the tradition where elaborate legend is not firmly attested, the abiding significance of Thiruneeragam rests in its inclusion in the Divine Prabandham itself, for the Alwars sang only of those abodes where they beheld the living presence of the Lord.
