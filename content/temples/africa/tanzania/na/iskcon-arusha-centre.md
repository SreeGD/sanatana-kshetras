The ISKCON Arusha Centre is the second of two documented Hare Krishna (International Society for Krishna Consciousness) centers in Tanzania, alongside the better-attested ISKCON Dar es Salaam Centre on Mosque Street. Arusha itself is a northern Tanzanian city long known as a hub for the country's Asian trading community and as the gateway town for Mount Kilimanjaro and Serengeti tourism, and it hosts its own small cluster of Hindu institutions, including a separate BAPS Shri Swaminarayan Mandir alongside the ISKCON center.

As with ISKCON centers worldwide, practice at the Arusha center would be expected to center on the worship of Radha and Krishna and the congregational chanting (kirtan) that the movement's founder, A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, established as central to Gaudiya Vaishnava devotional life after founding ISKCON in New York in 1966. No detailed founding history specific to the Arusha center - comparable to the documented 1969-1975 timeline available for ISKCON's early activity in neighboring Kenya - could be located during this research pass; its presence is confirmed only through its listing alongside Dar es Salaam's center in Wikipedia's broader inventory of Tanzania's Hindu temples.
