High on the Saptashrungi ("seven peaks") hill range near Vani in Nashik district, the goddess Saptashrungi Nivasini is worshipped in the form of an eighteen-armed, roughly eight-foot rock-cut image, her surface kept covered in vermilion and each of her hands holding a weapon said to have been given to her by a different deity for the battle against evil — Shiva's trident and Vishnu's discus among them. The site is counted among the Shakti Peethas formed when Sati's body was cut into pieces; the temple's own account holds that her right arm fell here, though a separate reference listing of the Shakti Peethas instead names the chin, so the precise tradition is not settled across sources. Local legend additionally credits the sage Markandeya with composing the Devi Mahatmya, the central scripture of Durga worship, at this hill.

The temple's documented history is thinner than its devotional importance: the Devi Bhagavata Purana is said to name Saptashringa among the Shakti Pithas, but no firm construction date survives for the shrine itself. What is clearly attested is the 1710 CE construction of the 510-stone-step stairway up the hillside, funded by Umabai Dabhade of the Maratha Dabhade family — evidence of substantial Maratha-era patronage even where the temple's own founding predates any surviving record. Today the site draws some of the largest pilgrim crowds among Maharashtra's goddess temples, especially during Chaitrotsav, when as many as a million pilgrims are said to visit over nine days, aided since 2018 by a funicular railway that shortens the climb from the base.
