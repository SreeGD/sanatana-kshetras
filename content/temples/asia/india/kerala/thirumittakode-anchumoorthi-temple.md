## Origin and legend

The Thirumittakode temple, known locally as Thiruviththuvakkodu, is an "Anchumoorthi" (five-deity) shrine on the banks of the Bharathapuzha: it houses Vishnu, worshipped as Uyyavantha Perumal (also called Abhaya Pradhan, "giver of protection"), alongside Shiva (as Thiruvithamdappan), the goddess Lakshmi as Padmasini/Vithuvakootuvalli, Ganesha (Vinayaka), and Ayyappan, all within a single temple complex -- an arrangement uncommon among the Divya Desams, most of which are dedicated solely to Vishnu.

## Historical significance

The temple preserves a Chola inscription of Emperor Rajendra I (reigned 1012-1044 CE), an unusual find in Kerala given the region's predominantly Chera/Travancore political history, and one that points to the medieval reach of Chola influence or patronage into the Malabar region. The temple is administered today by the Malabar Devaswom Board (distinct from the Travancore Devaswom Board that manages most of the other Malai Nadu Divya Desams further south), reflecting its location in the historically separate Malabar region of Kerala.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thirumittakode is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. The Vishnu form here, Abhaya Pradhan, is revered in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by Kulasekara Alvar -- himself traditionally held to have been a king of the Chera dynasty native to this Malai Nadu region -- securing the temple's place among the 108 Divya Desams of the Sri Vaishnava canon.
