Manakula Vinayagar, a shrine to Ganesha near Puducherry's Promenade Beach, is old enough to predate the French arrival at Pondicherry, though no precise founding century is documented. Its best-attested brush with history dates to the mid-18th-century governorship of Joseph François Dupleix, when colonial administrators reportedly moved to demolish the temple; it was spared after strong protest from the local Hindu population and, the tradition adds, concern over provoking British or Maratha intervention — a small but telling episode in the religious politics of French Pondicherry. Local tradition also holds that a saint attained enlightenment, or samadhi, at the site roughly three centuries ago, which is part of why the temple remains a customary first stop for parents bringing newborn children for blessing before visiting other temples.

The temple's most visible modern landmark is its golden processional chariot, plated with about 7.5 kg of gold and first taken out in procession on 5 October 2003; it remains a centerpiece of the temple's annual Ther (chariot) festival. Sitting just a few minutes' walk from the Bay of Bengal in Puducherry's heritage quarter, the temple continues to draw both religious pilgrims and visitors to the old French town.
