Thanesar, the old core of modern Kurukshetra, takes its own name from this temple: 'Sthaneshwar' — the place of the god — is remembered as the epithet by which Pushyabhuti, founder of the Vardhana dynasty, invoked Shiva when he made the town his capital around 500 CE. That makes the site's sacred association with Shiva at least fourteen centuries old, even though the structure a visitor sees today is considerably younger; Wikipedia's own account of the temple is thin on specifics, crediting the present building to Raghunath Rao Peshwa of the Maratha confederacy without giving a firm construction year, which suggests an 18th-century rebuilding of an older shrine rather than an unbroken architectural lineage back to the Vardhana period.

The temple sits on the southern bank of Brahma Sarovar and is counted as the central and most important stop on the traditional 48 kos parikrama, the roughly 90-km circumambulation of tirthas said to ring the Kurukshetra battlefield region. Popular tradition holds that the Pandavas, together with Krishna, prayed to Shiva here and received his blessing before the battle described in the Mahabharata — a devotional association kept alive by pilgrimage rather than by any independent historical record, and offered here as such.
