Thiruthangal, a hill shrine now within the town of Sivakasi in Virudhunagar district, takes its name - tradition explains - from an episode in which Vishnu chose to "stay" (thangal, in Tamil) at this spot together with his consorts, after Sridevi descended to earth, fashioned a sacred tank, and won his grace through her devotion. The temple is built upon a granite hillock roughly a hundred feet high, enclosed by a granite wall along with its tank; unusually for a major Divya Desam, it has no rajagopuram, its entrance instead integrated into the rock-cut hill architecture.

The presiding deity, worshipped standing as Ninra Narayana Perumal ("Narayana who stands"), is venerated together with his consort Arunakamala Mahadevi, also known locally as Shenbagavalli. The shrine is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham by Bhoothathalvar, one of the earliest of the Alvars, and by Thirumangai Alvar, whose four hymns here are part of his extensive hymn-circuit of Pandya-country shrines. The temple's antiquity is unusually well attested for a site of its size: it preserves inscriptions from as early as the 8th century CE cut into its rock-cut caves, along with a later inscription of 1032 CE, and a subsidiary shrine to Narasimha was added during the reign of the Pandya king Maravarman Kulasekara Pandyan I in the early 14th century - giving Thiruthangal a documented architectural history stretching across more than five centuries of Pandya-era patronage.
