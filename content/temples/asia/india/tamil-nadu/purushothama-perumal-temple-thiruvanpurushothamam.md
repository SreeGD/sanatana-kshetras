Set amid the paddy fields surrounding Thirunangur, this modest three-tiered Dravidian shrine is dedicated to Purushottaman - "the Supreme Person," one of Vishnu's classical epithets - worshipped here with his consort Purushottama Nayaki. Like its ten sister shrines in the Thirunangur cluster, the temple's founding legend is bound up with the story of Shiva's grief-stricken dance after the death of his consort, and of Vishnu's eleven-fold appearance to console him; this temple marks one of those eleven manifestations.

Thirumangai Alvar's devotional hymns, preserved in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, place this shrine among the 108 Divya Desams glorified by the Alvars. Each year in the Tamil month of Thai, the temple takes part in the Thirumangai Alvar Mangalasasana Utsavam, when its festival deity is carried, together with those of the other ten Thirunangur temples, in the Garudasevai procession - a shared festival that binds the eleven shrines of Thirunangur into a single ritual and devotional unit even though each is reckoned individually among the 108. Beyond its Alvar-era veneration, detailed inscriptional or dynastic building history specific to this temple was not found in the sources consulted for this entry.
