ISKCON's first temple in Moscow was established in 1991 on Begovaya Street, near the Begovaya metro station, at the point when the collapse of the Soviet Union allowed devotees of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON) — the Gaudiya Vaishnava movement founded in the West by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada — to move freely in and out of Russia for the first time. Kirtiraja Dasa headed ISKCON Russia at the time of the temple's founding and asked a fellow devotee, Vaidyanatha (later known as Bhakti Vijnana Goswami), to take over leadership of the Moscow temple, though Vaidyanatha chose instead to return to Sweden to continue translating religious texts.

Hinduism in Russia today includes both ISKCON's Gaudiya Vaishnava community — estimated at around 10,000 Hare Krishna devotees in Moscow as of the mid-2000s — and a broader Hindu population of Indian, Sri Lankan, Nepalese, and Mauritian residents and migrants. The presence of Hindu communities in Russia is not a purely modern phenomenon: a 17th-century "Indian Court" in Astrakhan, on the Volga, is documented as having included a small Hindu temple alongside merchants' living quarters and warehouses, reflecting a much older history of Hindu trading communities on Russian soil.
