BAPS (Bochasanwasi Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha), the Vaishnava Swaminarayan organization already responsible for large hand-carved stone mandirs in London (Neasden) and elsewhere, built its first such temple in France at Bussy-Saint-Georges, in the eastern suburbs of Paris. The project followed the organization's established building method: a ground-sanctification ceremony in October 2021 and a foundation stone-laying in September 2022 preceded construction, which began in April 2024 using sandstone quarried at Gwalior in India and marble sourced from Italy, Greece, and India, all hand-carved by artisans in India before being shipped to France for assembly — the first shipments of finished stone arriving in January 2026.

The completed mandir spans roughly 5,000 square metres across a basement and two upper floors, incorporating five shikhars (spires), four chhatris (domed canopies), ten ghummats (domes), seventy-three carved gavakshas (ornamental windows), and fifteen carved stambhas (pillars), and is dedicated to Akshar-Purushottam alongside Radha-Krishna, Sita-Ram, and Shiva-Parvati. It was consecrated by Mahant Swami Maharaj on 6 September 2026, the centerpiece of a thirteen-day Festival of Culture that drew several thousand attendees representing 38 nationalities and around 3,000 volunteers. BAPS describes it as the largest traditionally hand-carved Hindu temple in mainland Europe and the first of its kind in France, marking a significant expansion of the organization's presence in continental Europe alongside its earlier mandir at Neasden, London.
