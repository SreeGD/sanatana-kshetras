Nairobi is home to more than one Swaminarayan-tradition mandir, reflecting how the sampradaya's various branches built independently among Kenya's Gujarati Hindu community during the twentieth century. Alongside the East African Swaminarayan Sanstha's (EASS) "Devasadan"-era temple already documented in the city, Wikipedia's inventory of Hindu temples in Kenya separately names the "BAPS Shri Swaminarayan Mandir Nairobi," affiliated with the Bochasanwasi Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha (BAPS) — the same globally active branch of the tradition that has also built a mandir in Kampala, Uganda, and temples across the Indian diaspora more broadly.

BAPS's presence in Nairobi dates at least to 1966, when devotees purchased the former Indian Christian Union premises at Ngara and remodelled them into a three-spired temple, formally inaugurated in 1970 by Yogiji Maharaj during his third overseas journey. The mandir now standing on Forest Road, inaugurated on 29 August 1999 by Pramukh Swami Maharaj, is described by Wikipedia as "the first traditional stone and marble Hindu temple to be constructed on the African continent" — built from roughly 350 tonnes of hand-carved Jaisalmer sandstone by about 150 craftsmen, and honoured with the Architectural Association of Kenya's Millennium Award in June 2000. Worship centres on murtis of Bhagwan Swaminarayan alongside Radha-Krishna, Ganesh, and Hanuman, and the temple serves as a major hub for the city's Gujarati Hindu community, especially around Diwali and Annakut.
