In Hanamkonda, the twin city adjoining Warangal, the Thousand Pillar Temple stands as one of the earliest and most architecturally significant monuments of the Kakatiya dynasty, which rose to dominate the Telangana region from the twelfth through fourteenth centuries. Built in 1163 CE under the Kakatiya king Rudra Deva, the temple takes the form of a trikutalayam — a triple shrine honoring Shiva, Vishnu, and Surya together within a single star-shaped platform, an arrangement that reflects the eclectic royal patronage of multiple traditions characteristic of the early Kakatiya court. Its Telugu name, Veyyi Stambhala Gudi, refers to the roughly one thousand intricately carved pillars supporting its halls, no two said to be identical, alongside a monolithic dolerite Nandi and richly worked stone screens that together mark a transition from late Chalukyan to distinctively Kakatiyan architectural style.

Now protected by the Archaeological Survey of India and renovated in 2004, the temple sits alongside Warangal Fort and the Kakatiya Kala Thoranam on India's UNESCO World Heritage tentative list, recognized as a foundational monument of Kakatiya temple architecture that would later reach fuller expression at sites such as the Ramappa Temple at Palampet. Worship continues at the temple even as it functions as a heritage site, with Maha Shivaratri drawing the largest crowds of the year to Hanamkonda.
