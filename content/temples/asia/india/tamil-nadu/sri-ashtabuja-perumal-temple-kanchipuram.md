Sri Ashtabuja Perumal Temple, in the Ashtabujakaram quarter of Kanchipuram, enshrines Vishnu as Ashtabuja ("eight-armed") Perumal, also worshipped here as Adi Kesava Perumal, standing with eight arms bearing the chakra, sword, a flower, and an arrow on his right hands and the conch, bow, shield, and mace on his left. Temple tradition holds that Brahma once performed penance at this spot seeking a vision of Vishnu; pleased with his devotion, Vishnu manifested in this uniquely eight-armed form to grant the boon, making Kanchipuram — already famed for the Varadaraja shrine nearby — the rare site of two distinct Brahma-related Vishnu legends within the same town. The temple tank, Gajendra Pushkarani, is locally associated with the Gajendra Moksha legend, in which Vishnu rushed to save the elephant-devotee Gajendra from the grip of a crocodile, and this episode is commemorated annually in the temple's Aadi-month festival.

Ashtabujakaram is counted among the 108 Divya Desams on the strength of a hymn jointly composed by Peyalvar and Thirumangai Alvar in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, linking the shrine to the earliest stratum of Tamil Vaishnava devotional poetry. The temple's architectural core is generally attributed to the Pallava period (later 8th century CE), with the Dravidian-style superstructure, four-tiered rajagopuram, and granite compound wall developed and endowed further under the Medieval Cholas — inscriptions here record grants from the reigns of Rajendra Chola I and Kulothunga Chola I — and again under the Vijayanagara kings, who were active patrons of Kanchipuram's Vishnu temples generally.

As one of the fourteen Divya Desams clustered in and around Kanchipuram, the Ashtabuja Perumal temple forms part of the dense sacred geography that makes the town a uniquely important pilgrimage center for Sri Vaishnavas, standing alongside the far larger Varadaraja Perumal temple as a living record of the Alvars' devotion to Vishnu in his many manifest forms.
