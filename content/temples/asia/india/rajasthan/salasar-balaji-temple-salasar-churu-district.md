Salasar Balaji, in the small town of Salasar in Rajasthan's Churu district, is one of North India's most visited Hanuman shrines, drawing pilgrims from across the country by road along the Jaipur-Bikaner highway. Its founding is remembered entirely through local legend rather than documented history: the story goes that a farmer in the nearby village of Asota unearthed an idol of Hanuman while ploughing his field on a Tuesday during the Navami of the month of Shravan, an auspicious combination of day and deity for Hanuman worship. He is said to have offered churma, a sweet wheat-flour preparation, to the discovered image — a ritual still performed at the temple daily. According to tradition, both a local landlord and a devotee named Mohandas separately received instructions in dreams to have the deity installed at Salasar, and the shrine grew from that joint undertaking into today's temple complex.

No independently verifiable date survives for exactly when this happened, so the temple's early history should be treated as devotional tradition rather than settled fact. What is well documented is its present-day stature as a major pilgrimage destination: two large annual fairs, held in the Hindu months of Chaitra (spring) and Ashwin (autumn), bring enormous crowds of devotees to Salasar, many of them walking in organized pilgrim groups from towns across Rajasthan and neighboring states, making it — alongside Khatu Shyam, a short distance away — one of the region's most important modern centers of popular Hindu devotion.
