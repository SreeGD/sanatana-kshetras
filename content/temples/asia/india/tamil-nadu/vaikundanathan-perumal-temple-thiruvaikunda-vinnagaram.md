Thirunangur, a cluster of villages a short distance from Sirkazhi, is the anchor of one of the most distinctive pilgrimage circuits among the 108 Divya Desams: eleven separate Vishnu shrines, each a Divya Desam in its own right, gathered within a few kilometres of one another. This temple, dedicated to Vaikunta Nathan Perumal - "the lotus-eyed Lord of Vaikuntha" - and his consort Vaikunta Valli, is traditionally regarded as the principal shrine of the group, the one from which the annual Garudasevai procession sets out.

According to the tradition shared across all eleven Thirunangur temples, the eleven forms of Vishnu enshrined here trace back to a moment of cosmic grief: when Shiva, devastated by the death of his consort Uma (in some tellings, Sati), danced in fury across the earth, each place his matted locks touched the ground gave rise to a fierce form of Rudra. To calm his sorrow and rage, Vishnu is said to have appeared before Shiva in eleven forms, one for each of these Rudras, and Shiva's anguish subsided at the sight. The eleven Thirunangur temples are held to mark these eleven appearances. Three Alvars - Periyalvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, and Thirumangai Alvar - are credited with hymns to this shrine in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and the temple's central place in the group is reaffirmed every year during the Tamil month of Thai, when the festival images of all eleven Thirunangur Perumals are brought together in a spectacular procession, the Garudasevai, mounted on an elaborately constructed Garuda vahanam, commemorating Thirumangai Alvar's own pilgrimage of praise (mangalasasanam) through these shrines.
