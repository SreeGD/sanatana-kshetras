One of the most important Shakti Peethas, Kamakhya is a center of Tantric Shakta worship, traditionally marking the site where the yoni (womb/generative organ) of Sati fell after Shiva's grief-stricken wandering with her corpse (per the Daksha-yagna/Sati legend common to all Shakti Peethas). The temple observes no idol; the goddess is worshipped in the form of a natural rock fissure, and the annual Ambubachi Mela marks her yearly menstruation cycle in tantric tradition. Worship at the site likely predates written history, with the earliest epigraphic evidence of the goddess's cult dating to the 9th-century Mlechchha dynasty; after the earlier structure fell into ruin, the temple was rebuilt in 1565 CE by the Koch king Nara Narayan, with further renovations later carried out under the Ahom kingdom.
