The Asamai Temple stands at the foothills of Koh-e Asamai, a hill in the Old City of Kabul whose name is traditionally associated with Asha, an ancient goddess of hope. The site is home to an Akhand Jyoti — a continuous ritual flame — that sources describe as having burned uninterrupted for many centuries, and both the temple and its flame are described as having survived numerous conflicts across Kabul's turbulent modern history, standing as a living reminder of Afghanistan's Hindu Shahi-era past.

Afghanistan's documented Hindu heritage extends well beyond this single site: Kabul's Old City is also home to the Bhairo Mandir and Mangalwar Mandir, and archaeological finds elsewhere in the country — including a Brahmanical temple excavated at Khair Khaneh in Kabul, with remains dated to the 7th-8th century Turk Shahi period, and the Gardez Ganesha statue from Paktia province, now housed at Kabul's Dargah Pir Rattan Nath — attest to a substantial pre-Islamic Hindu and Shahi presence in the region. Afghanistan's Hindu and Sikh communities, however, have shrunk dramatically over recent decades through repeated waves of conflict and emigration, and this entry does not assert a confirmed current operational status for the Asamai Temple; it is documented here as a historically and religiously significant site whose present condition would need independent, on-the-ground verification.
