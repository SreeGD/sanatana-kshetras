The Loganatha Perumal Temple at Thirukkannankudi, near Sikkal on the Tiruvarur-Nagapattinam highway, enshrines Vishnu as Loganatha Perumal — "lord of the world" — standing facing east in the gesture of blessing, with his consort worshipped as Loganayagi. The temple is traditionally grouped among the Pancha Krishnaranya Kshetrams, a set of five Divya Desams with a particular association with Krishna.

Its sthala purana tells of the sage Vasishtha, who worshipped an idol of the infant Krishna fashioned from butter; the idol miraculously came to life, and Krishna, moved by the devotion of the assembled sages, promised he would remain permanently at this spot — the origin of the deity's name, Loganatha, "lord of all the worlds." This legend situates the temple within a broader constellation of Krishna-themed shrines along the Cauvery delta.

The temple's construction is generally attributed to the Medieval Cholas of the late 9th century CE, with later contributions credited to the Vijayanagara empire and the Madurai Nayaks, though no specific inscription for this temple was located in the sources consulted, leaving its precise dating less firmly documented than some neighboring Divya Desams. The temple's glorification in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham is itself subject to some uncertainty: while Wikipedia attributes a single hymn to Tirumalisai Alvar, a pasuram-citation database's numbering pattern instead suggests a fuller decad more typically associated with Thirumangai Alvar's Periya Thirumozhi — a discrepancy this entry has not been able to resolve with certainty. The temple's principal annual festival, the Panguni Brahmotsavam, is held each spring, alongside the Thiruneerani Vizha, a festival associated with the ceremonial application of sacred ash.
