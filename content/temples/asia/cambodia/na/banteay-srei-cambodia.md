Consecrated on 22 April 967 CE under its original name Tribhuvanamahesvara, "Great Lord of the Threefold World" — an epithet of Shiva — Banteay Srei stands apart from most other major Angkorian temples in that it was commissioned not directly by a king but by two court officials, Yajnavaraha and Vishnukumara, serving under King Rajendravarman II. Built roughly 25 km northeast of the main Angkor complex from fine-grained pink and red sandstone, the temple is celebrated for the exceptional depth and delicacy of its relief carving, which art historians have long singled out as among the finest surviving examples of Khmer stone sculpture, earning it the popular epithet "jewel of Khmer art."

Though its miniature scale and remote setting long kept it comparatively less visited than the great temples nearer Angkor, Banteay Srei is now a well-established stop on the Angkor Archaeological Park circuit, having undergone dedicated restoration work between 2000 and 2003. Like Angkor Wat, it survives today purely as an archaeological and heritage site rather than a functioning place of Hindu worship, its significance resting on its documented Shaiva dedication and its standing as one of the most technically accomplished Hindu temples of the Angkorian era.
