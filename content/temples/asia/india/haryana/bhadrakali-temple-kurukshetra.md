Bhadrakali Temple is counted among the network of Shakti Peethas -- the roughly fifty-one (by some reckonings fifty-two) sites across the subcontinent where, according to Puranic tradition, a piece of the goddess Sati's body is said to have fallen after Vishnu cut apart the corpse Shiva carried in his grief following her self-immolation at Daksha's yajna. At Kurukshetra, tradition holds the ankle bone came to rest, and the goddess is worshipped here as Bhadrakali with the attendant Bhairava named Sthanu -- the same epithet, notably, that gives old Thanesar its name.

Unlike the region's better-documented sites, Bhadrakali Temple has no dedicated Wikipedia article of its own as of this writing; it surfaces only as an entry in broader lists of Shakti Peethas and as a numbered stop on Kurukshetra's traditional 48 kos parikrama pilgrimage circuit. That leaves its actual construction history, architecture, and present-day administration undocumented in the sources checked here, even though its identity and location in Kurukshetra are well attested across multiple reference lists.
