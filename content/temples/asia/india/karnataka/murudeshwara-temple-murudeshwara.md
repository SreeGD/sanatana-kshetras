Murudeshwara sits on Kanduka Hill, a rocky promontory on the Uttara Kannada coast surrounded on three sides by the Arabian Sea, and shares its founding legend with nearby Gokarna. In the Ramayana account, the demon king Ravana had won Shiva's Atma Linga through penance and was carrying it toward Lanka; Ganesha, disguised as a boy, offered to hold the linga while Ravana attended to an urgent errand, then set it down on the ground at Gokarna, making it immovable. The cloth (or covering) that had wrapped the linga during the journey is said to have blown away and landed here, giving the place its name — Mrideshwar, later Murudeshwar — and its own sanctified Shiva linga, the Mridesa Linga, now enshrined in the temple's sanctum.

The temple's modern profile owes as much to twentieth-century patronage as to ancient legend: a 123-foot (37-metre) statue of Shiva, among the tallest Shiva statues anywhere, rises on the hill above the shrine, financed by the businessman R. N. Shetty and executed by the sculptor Kashinath of Shivamogga, alongside a twenty-storey gopura completed in 2008 whose elevator carries visitors to an observation deck looking out over the statue, the temple complex, and the sea. That combination of an ancient Puranic sanctity with a strikingly contemporary monumental statue has made Murudeshwara one of coastal Karnataka's most visited pilgrimage and tourist destinations, particularly crowded during Maha Shivaratri.
