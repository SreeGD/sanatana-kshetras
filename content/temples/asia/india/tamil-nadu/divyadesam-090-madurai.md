The Koodal Azhagar Temple at Madurai, the place revered as Thirukkoodal, enshrines the Lord as Koodal Azhagar, the surpassingly beautiful Lord of the meeting-place, and is honored as one of the 108 Divya Desams sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Madurai, the ancient seat of the Pandya kings and of Tamil learning, was traditionally called Koodal, the place of confluence, and the Lord who presides here bears the epithet Azhagar, the Beautiful One. A distinctive marvel of this shrine is its sanctum where the Lord is venerated in three postures, seated, standing, and reclining, beheld at different levels within the vimana, so that the worshipper may contemplate the manifold forms of the Supreme in a single sacred precinct. Tradition holds the place to be associated with the Lord's grace shown to a king, and with the assembly of devotion in the heart of the Tamil land. The glory of Koodal Azhagar was sung by Thirumazhisai Alwar, the profound sage-poet of deep philosophical insight, and by Thirumangai Alwar, the prince among the Alwars whose hymns garlanded the shrines of the Pandya country. Through their verses this abode in Madurai was sealed forever among the perpetually praised dwellings of Vishnu. Drawing pilgrims to its threefold vision of the Lord and standing within the celebrated city of the south, the Koodal Azhagar Temple holds a cherished place among the Divya Desams of Pandya Nadu.

## Festivals

Koodal Azhagar, the Divya Desam within the city of Madurai, observes the major Srivaishnava festivals alongside the wider festive life of the temple city. The annual Brahmotsavam, with the Lord taken in procession through the surrounding streets on the various vahanas, is the chief celebration. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is kept devoutly, with the chanting of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham. The Tamil month of Chithirai, famous in Madurai for its great festival, sees Vaishnava observances at this shrine as well, and the Float festival (Theppotsavam), Pavithrotsavam and Tamil New Year celebrations are traditionally observed. Festival days bring processions, special abhishekams and recitation of the Prabandham hymns.

## Location & Access

The Koodal Azhagar temple stands near the centre of Madurai, the great temple city of the Pandya region, not far from the famed Meenakshi shrine and the city's central districts. Madurai is itself a major city and a well-connected transport hub by road, rail and air, making the temple readily accessible to visitors. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours, with a midday closure.
