About 70 km from Bhopal, in Sehore district's Rehti tehsil, the Bijasan Mata Temple crowns an 800-foot hill at Salkanpur, reached by a stairway of some 1,400 steps or, for pilgrims who prefer not to climb, a ropeway to the summit. The goddess here is worshipped as Vindhyavasini Beejasan Devi, an incarnation of Durga, and the site is recognized as a Siddhpeeth — a place where a deity's power is held to be especially and reliably present, a status shared with other major Shakti shrines in this directory such as Pitambara Peeth at Datia, also in Madhya Pradesh.

Unlike many of the state's other major Devi shrines, Salkanpur's temple lacks a well-documented founding narrative in the sources available for this entry: no patron, dynasty, or dated origin legend could be confirmed, and the available account is limited largely to the site's physical description and its current standing as a pilgrimage destination. What is well attested is its place in the region's devotional calendar — Salkanpur draws large crowds during both Chaitra and Sharad Navratri, when pilgrims from across central India make the climb to the hilltop shrine, making it one of the more significant, if less historically documented, Shakti pilgrimage sites in Madhya Pradesh's Malwa-Nimar region.
