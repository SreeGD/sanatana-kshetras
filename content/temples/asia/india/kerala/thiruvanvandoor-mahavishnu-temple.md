## Origin and legend

The Thiruvanvandoor Mahavishnu Temple enshrines Vishnu as Pambanaiappan -- a name derived from "Pampa Anaintha Appan," the Lord embraced by the river Pampa (Pamba), which encircles the temple site. A secondary shrine within the complex honours Vishnu as Gosala Krishna. Local legend holds that the temple is one of five ancient shrines in the Chengannur area said to have been built by the five Pandava brothers of the Mahabharata during their travels; this temple is attributed to Nakula, with sibling temples at Thrichittat (Yudhishthira), Puliyoor (Bhima), Aranmula (Arjuna), and Thrikkodithanam (Sahadeva).

## Historical significance

Built in the Kerala style of temple architecture, the temple is administered by the Travancore Devaswom Board. Its most striking modern tradition is a 51-day annual festival that concludes in a Gajamela -- a grand procession of 21 caparisoned elephants -- accompanied by classical Kerala performing arts including Kathakali, and a separate Tiruvaymoli festival specifically commemorating Nammalvar's devotion to the shrine.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thiruvanvandoor is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. The earliest literary references to the temple appear in the hymns of Nammalvar, composed around 800 CE as part of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, placing this Pampa-riverside shrine firmly within the canon of the 108 Divya Desams glorified by the Vaishnava Alvar saints.
