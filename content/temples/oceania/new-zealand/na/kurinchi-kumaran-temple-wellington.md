Kurinchi Kumaran Temple traces its origins to 1992, when Wellington's South Indian Tamil community began worship in a rented property under the auspices of the New Zealand Hindu Association. The congregation grew steadily through the devotion and volunteer priestly service of its members, moving into its own premises at 3 Batchelor Street, Newlands, in 2000. After extensive renovation to build out a traditional temple structure, the temple held its formal Kumbabhishekham consecration ceremony in December 2013 — a milestone widely cited as marking it the first South Indian-style Hindu temple established in New Zealand.

The temple takes its name from Murugan's association with "Kurinchi" — one of the five traditional Tamil landscape regions (the hill country) over which the deity presides — and centers worship on Murugan (Kartikeya) alongside Goddess Ambal (Parvati) and Lord Ayyappan. It now serves a diverse congregation of devotees drawn from India, Sri Lanka, Malaysia, Singapore, South Africa, and Fiji, reflecting Wellington's broader South Indian and Indian Ocean diaspora communities, with a regular calendar of monthly and annual religious services and cultural events.
