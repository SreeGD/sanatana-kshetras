The Adikesava Perumal Temple at Thiruvattaru, set amid the waters and greenery of the Malai Nadu region in present-day Kerala, is revered as one of the 108 Divya Desams, the sacred abodes of Lord Vishnu celebrated by the Alwars. The presiding deity, the Moolavar, is Adikesava Perumal, who is enshrined in a majestic reclining posture (sayana thirukkolam), his vast form filling the sanctum so that devotees behold him through three doorways. The name of the place is traditionally connected with the river that encircles and flows around the shrine, and the temple stands as one of the foremost Vishnu sanctuaries of the southern Malai Nadu, closely linked in tradition and proximity to the great Padmanabhaswamy shrine of the same region. According to the sthala purana, the Lord here is associated with the subduing of demonic forces, and his reclining form is venerated as the very fount of creation, 'Adikesava' signifying the primordial Lord with beautiful locks. Among the Alwar saints it is Nammalwar, the foremost of the Alwars and author of the Tiruvaymoli, who sang the glory of this divine abode, enshrining it forever within the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, the Tamil canon of four thousand sacred verses. Through his hymns the Lord of Thiruvattaru is established among the celebrated Divya Desams, drawing pilgrims who seek the grace of the reclining Perumal whose serene and immense form embodies the protective repose of the Supreme in the sanctified waters of this ancient land.

## Festivals

As a celebrated Divya Desam set amid the lush Malai Nadu country, Thiruvattaru traditionally observes the great Vaishnava festival cycle around its reclining Lord Adikesava Perumal. A principal annual Brahmotsavam is kept with grand processions of the utsava deity around the temple and town, accompanied by recitation of the Tamil Nalayira Divya Prabandham. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the Tamil month of Margazhi is among the holiest occasions, when devotees gather for special darshan and the Paramapada Vasal observance customary at major Perumal temples. Other traditional events include Pavithrotsavam, the annual purification festival, and a Float festival (Theppotsavam) where the processional Lord is taken upon a decorated raft. Krishna Janmashtami, Sri Rama Navami and the Tamil New Year are also reverently marked through the year.

## Location & Access

The temple stands in Thiruvattaru village in the Kanniyakumari district of the old Malai Nadu (Kerala border) region of Tamil Nadu, set near the river country at roughly 8.335 N, 77.281 E. It lies within reach of Nagercoil and the major hub of Kanniyakumari, with Thiruvananthapuram being the nearest large city and transport centre. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday closure.
