Tiruttani's shrine crowns Thanigai hill above the town, reached by 365 steps traditionally said to mirror the days of the year, and is named among Murugan's sacred sites in the Sangam-era Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai; the 14th-century poet-saint Arunagirinathar, whose seated image is kept in the temple, later composed verses on it in his Tiruppukal. The temple's nine-tiered gopuram leads through four precincts to the sanctum, where Murugan is depicted holding a vajra vel (a thunderbolt-tipped spear) and riding the elephant Airavata rather than his more usual peacock — an iconographic form Wikipedia notes is shared with only one other Arupadaiveedu shrine, Swamimalai.

The site's popular name is bound up with its legend: after his victory over the asura Surapadman at Tiruchendur, Murugan is held to have come to this hill to cool his battle-fury, with "Tiruttani" popularly explained as a contraction of the Tamil "Seruttani" — seru (war/anger) and thani (to quench). It was here, tradition holds, that he went on to court and marry Valli, the huntress-consort of Tamil Murugan lore, making Tiruttani's traditional role among the Arupadaiveedu the resolution of the war and the courtship that follows it — though other shrines associated with the Valli story, such as Vallimalai and Pazhamudircholai, are also woven into different local tellings of the same courtship.
