Mata Basanti Devi Mandir, in Sector 24 of Chandigarh, is dedicated to Shitala Devi, a folk form of the goddess traditionally worshipped across much of North India as a protector against disease -- historically smallpox in particular -- and as a guardian of children's health. It appears to be the one Hindu temple of genuine local standing situated administratively within Chandigarh's own union territory boundary, distinct from the better-documented Shakti shrines of the surrounding region (Chandi Mandir, Mata Mansa Devi Mandir, and others), which lie a short distance away but technically fall within Panchkula district, Haryana.

Available sourcing for this temple is thin: no dedicated historical account of its founding, builder, or architectural development was located, and the only confirmation found is a brief mention inside Wikipedia's general Chandigarh article, which describes the temple simply as "historic" without elaborating further. What that source does confirm is the temple's principal annual observance, held on the first Tuesday of the Chaitra month following Holi, when devotees turn out in particular numbers -- a pattern consistent with Shitala Devi worship elsewhere in the region. Given how little independently verifiable history could be assembled here, this entry is presented as a starting point for further research into the temple's origins rather than a settled account.
