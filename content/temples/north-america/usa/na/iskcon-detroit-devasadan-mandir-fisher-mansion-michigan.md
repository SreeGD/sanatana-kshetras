Housed in the Fisher Mansion, a historic residence on Detroit's east side near the Detroit River, ISKCON Detroit describes itself as the first Vedic temple to open in Michigan. In 1975, Alfred Brush Ford — great-grandson of Henry Ford — and Elisabeth Reuther Dickmeyer, daughter of longtime UAW president Walter Reuther, jointly purchased the Fisher Estate to establish the Bhaktivedanta Cultural Center on the site. The temple, also known as the Devasadan Mandir, worships the presiding Deities Sri Sri Radha Kunjabihari and hosts regular yoga classes, Sunday feasts, and educational programs, alongside major festivals such as Ratha Yatra. The complex today includes an art gallery, a grand ballroom with 25-foot ceilings, restored residential spaces, and 4 acres of formal gardens, and mansion tours are offered alongside the temple's regular worship schedule. Author and ISKCON devotee Stephen Knapp has headed the temple in recent years.
