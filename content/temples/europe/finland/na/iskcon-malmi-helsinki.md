Wikipedia's overview of Hinduism in Finland confirms, in a single sentence, the existence of an ISKCON (International Society for Krishna Consciousness) temple in the Malmi district of Helsinki — the Finnish capital's northeastern suburb, reachable via its own commuter rail station. This appears to be the only organized Hindu temple presence documented for Finland in the sources available for this audit, reflecting the country's very small Hindu population relative to its Nordic neighbors.

As with ISKCON centres generally, worship here can be presumed to follow the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition founded in the West by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, centered on Radha Krishna and the chanting of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra, though this has not been independently confirmed for this specific location. No founding date, address, or further institutional history could be confirmed in the source consulted; fuller documentation would need to come directly from ISKCON Finland.
