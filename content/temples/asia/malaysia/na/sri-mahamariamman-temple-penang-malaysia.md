The Sri Mahamariamman Temple on Queen Street in George Town is the oldest Hindu temple in the state of Penang, tracing to land granted in 1801 by the British colonial administration to Betty Lingam Chetty, then Kapitan (community headman) of Penang's Tamil and South Indian population; the temple itself was formally established in 1833. Dedicated to the goddess Mahamariamman, a protective form of the Divine Mother, the temple is built in Dravidian South Indian style, its facade dominated by an elaborate, richly sculpted gopuram crowned with a vimanam dome depicting numerous Hindu deities.

Also known locally as the Queen Street Indian Temple, it continues to hold daily pujas at approximately 7:30 AM and 6:30 PM and remains a central landmark of George Town's UNESCO-listed historic core, distinct from the similarly named but architecturally and historically separate Sri Mahamariamman Temple in Kuala Lumpur already documented elsewhere in this directory. As Penang's oldest Hindu temple, it anchors the island's Tamil Hindu community much as its Kuala Lumpur counterpart does for the capital.
