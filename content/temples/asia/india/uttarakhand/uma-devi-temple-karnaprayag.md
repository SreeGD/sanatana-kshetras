Karnaprayag, where the Alaknanda meets the Pindar river descending from the Pindari glacier, is named for Karna, the Mahabharata's tragic warrior-hero born to Kunti and the Sun God Surya. Local tradition holds that Karna performed penance here and worshipped Surya at this confluence, receiving in return the divine armor and earrings (kavacha and kundala) that made him nearly invincible in battle; another strand of local legend places Karna's cremation, performed by Krishna, at the same spot. These associations are devotional tradition tied to the epic rather than documented history.

An ancient temple dedicated to Uma Devi (Parvati) stands at the confluence; one source states the stone temple was rebuilt by Adi Shankaracharya, though this attribution could not be independently corroborated here and is treated as unverified tradition rather than settled fact. The site has drawn more recent visitors of note as well — Swami Vivekananda is reported to have meditated at Karnaprayag for eighteen days.

Karnaprayag is a Chamoli district town on National Highway 58, about 172 km from Rishikesh via Rudraprayag, and functions as a standard stop for pilgrims on the Char Dham Yatra route toward Badrinath. A rail line extending toward Karnaprayag from Rishikesh was under construction as of the sources checked, which would eventually improve access beyond the present road and Haridwar-railhead connections.
