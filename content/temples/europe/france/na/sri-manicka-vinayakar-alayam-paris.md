Sri Manicka Vinayakar Alayam, popularly known simply as the Ganesh temple of Paris, was established in 1985 to serve the Sri Lankan Tamil community that settled around the La Chapelle neighborhood of Paris's 18th arrondissement — an area whose concentration of Tamil-run shops, restaurants, and religious institutions has earned it the nickname "Little Jaffna." As the surrounding community grew through waves of arrivals fleeing Sri Lanka's civil war from the 1980s through the 2000s, the temple outgrew its original premises and was relocated and enlarged on rue Pajol in March 2010, where it remains the neighborhood's principal Hindu place of worship, dedicated to Ganesha (known here by his Tamil name, Vinayakar).

Since the mid-1990s the temple has organized an annual Ganesh Chaturthi procession through the streets of La Chapelle, deliberately scheduled in late August or early September — independent of the Indian festival calendar — so as not to disrupt the neighborhood's daily life. The procession, which features decorated ceremonial chariots including one carrying a gilded bronze image of Ganesha, has grown into one of the largest public Hindu festivals in France, drawing an estimated 15,000 participants in 2008, including Tamil visitors from other European countries.
