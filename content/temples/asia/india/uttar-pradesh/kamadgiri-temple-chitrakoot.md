Chitrakoot, straddling the modern Uttar Pradesh-Madhya Pradesh border on the banks of the Mandakini river, is where the Ramayana places a substantial part of Rama's forest exile alongside Sita and Lakshmana. At the edge of the town, Kamadgiri — a low, forested hill whose name is popularly read as "the wish-fulfilling hill" — is venerated by pilgrims as the very site of that stay. Unusually for this directory, the primary object of worship here is not a conventional temple image but the hill itself: pilgrims perform parikrama, a roughly five-kilometer barefoot circumambulation around its base, and small shrines ring the perimeter, one of which is widely known locally as Kamtanath (or Kamadnath). No source consulted for this entry could independently confirm a founding date, founder, or architectural history for that shrine or for the parikrama tradition itself, so these specifics are presented here as regional pilgrimage tradition rather than documented history.

The Ramayana also places at Chitrakoot the episode of Bharat Milap: after their father Dasharatha's death, Rama's half-brother Bharata journeyed here to beg him to return to Ayodhya and take the throne. When Rama would not cut short his father's vow of exile, Bharata instead carried back Rama's sandals, placing them on the throne at Nandigrama to rule as regent in his brother's name until the fourteen years were complete. Alongside Kamadgiri, the wider Chitrakoot pilgrimage circuit includes sites such as Ram Ghat on the Mandakini and a hermitage associated with the sage Atri and his wife Anasuya, and the town remains one of the most visited Rama-related pilgrimage destinations in North India, particularly around Rama Navami and the Deepotsav season.
