The Anantha Padmanabhan Temple at Thiruvananthapuram is one of the most celebrated of the 108 Divya Desams, situated in Malai Nadu, the Kerala region. The presiding deity, the Moolavar, is worshipped here as Anantha Padmanabha, the Lord who reclines in yoganidra upon Ananta, the thousand-hooded serpent Adisesha, with the lotus bearing Brahma rising from his navel. This majestic reclining form gives the city its very name, Thiruvananthapuram, the holy city of Ananta, and the shrine is renowned as one of the grandest expressions in all the divyadesams of the Lord at rest upon the serpent in the cosmic ocean. The vast image, beheld through three doorways, has drawn pilgrims and rulers in homage across the ages, and the temple stands as a foremost center of Srivaishnava worship in the southern land. The glory of this abode was sung by Nammalwar, the foremost of the Alwars, in his hymns of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and his verses secure the shrine its honored place among the sacred abodes of Vishnu praised by the saint-poets. Tradition holds the Lord here to be the supreme protector who, in his serene repose, upholds and governs the universe, granting liberation and refuge to those who surrender at his feet. Worshipped with profound devotion and surrounded by enduring sanctity, Anantha Padmanabha of Thiruvananthapuram remains among the most resplendent of the Divya Desams, where the reclining Lord upon Ananta continues to receive the reverence of countless devotees.

## Festivals

The renowned Anantha Padmanabhaswamy Temple at Thiruvananthapuram, where the Lord reclines majestically upon the thousand-hooded Ananta, observes a rich annual round of festivals. Its two great Utsavams, traditionally held in the Malayalam months around the spring and autumn (Alpasi and Painkuni seasons), culminate in the celebrated Aarat procession, when the deities are taken to the sea at Shanghumukham for the ceremonial holy bath. Vaikunta Ekadasi is kept with great devotion, alongside the Srivaishnava observances of Brahmotsavam-style processions, recitation of the Divya Prabandham, and festive vahanas. The temple is also famed for the once-in-many-years Murajapam and Lakshadeepam, when myriad lamps illuminate the shrine. Daily and seasonal worship here is conducted with the elaborate ritual discipline for which this royal temple is celebrated.

## Location & Access

The temple stands in the heart of Thiruvananthapuram (Trivandrum), the capital city of Kerala in the Malai Nadu region, near the East Fort area of the old city. Being within the city, it is well connected to Thiruvananthapuram's central railway station, bus stand, and airport. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours, with the sanctum closed through the midday interval; a strict traditional dress code is observed for entry.
