Pazhamudircholai's shrine sits on a forested hill on Madurai's outskirts, beside the Nupura Gangai stream, and its antiquity is attested less by inscriptions than by literature: it is named among Murugan's sacred sites in the Sangam-era Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai and again in the later Cilappatikaram of Ilango Adigal, while the 14th-century poet-saint Arunagirinathar sang of it in his Tiruppukal. No founding dynasty or construction date for the present structure could be confirmed from the source consulted, so this shrine's early built history remains comparatively thin next to some of the other Arupadaiveedu sites. Ganesha is worshipped here alongside Murugan, whose two consorts, Deivanai and Valli, are both represented at the shrine.

Two different legends attach to the site. One, recounted on the temple's own historical record, tells of the Tamil poetess-saint Avvaiyar meeting Murugan seated in a naaval (jamun) tree, who shook the branches to offer her its fruit — a story preserved in the very place name Pazhamudircholai ("orchard of ripened, scattered fruit"). The other, drawn from the wider Arupadaiveedu tradition, holds that this shrine's distinguishing role among the six abodes is that Murugan is here seen together with both Deivanai and Valli at once, in contrast to shrines like Thiruparankundram or Tiruttani, which are each linked to just one of the two marriages. Both traditions are current, and this entry has not attempted to adjudicate between them.
