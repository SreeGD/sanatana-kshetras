Apatsahayesvarar Temple in Alangudi, in Tiruvarur district's Valangaiman taluk, is the Navagraha shrine for Brihaspati, or Jupiter (Guru), maintained as a subsidiary sanctum within a Shiva temple whose own name reflects one of Shaivism's most enduring images: Apatsahayesvarar means, roughly, "the Lord who comes to the rescue in adversity," an epithet devotional tradition explains through a local retelling of the Samudra Manthan legend, in which Shiva swallowed the deadly poison churned up from the cosmic ocean to protect creation from its effects. Shiva is worshipped here alongside his consort Elavarkuzhali, and the temple's standing as a Paadal Petra Sthalam places its origins in devotional memory well before the 7th century, when it was hymned in the Tevaram canon by the Nayanar saints.

As with several other temples in the Navagraha circuit, the surviving stone structure is credited to Chola-era builders and was substantially rebuilt during the Nayak period in the 16th century. Spread across roughly two acres within two prakarams behind a five-tiered rajagopuram, the temple draws devotees particularly on Thursdays, the day traditionally associated with Jupiter, and during its annual Brahmotsavam, with worshippers seeking Guru's blessing for wisdom, prosperity, and favorable planetary influence in their horoscopes.
