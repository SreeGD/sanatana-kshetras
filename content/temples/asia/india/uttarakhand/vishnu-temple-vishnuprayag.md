Vishnuprayag, where the Alaknanda meets the Dhauliganga descending from the Niti valley near the Tibetan border, is counted as the first of the Panch Prayag reckoned from the north, closest to Badrinath. Tradition holds that the sage Narada meditated and worshipped Vishnu at this confluence, and that Vishnu appeared before him in response — the episode that gives the site its name. An octagonal temple stands near the confluence; one source dates it to 1889 and credits its construction to a "Maharani of Indore - Ahalyabai," though this attribution sits awkwardly against the documented dates of the historical Ahalyabai Holkar of Indore, who died in 1795, nearly a century earlier — this entry flags rather than resolves that inconsistency. A separate Hanuman Chatti temple, dedicated to Hanuman, stands nearby, though no construction history for it was found in the sources checked.

At 1,372 m, Vishnuprayag sits noticeably higher and further up the Alaknanda valley than the other four Panch Prayag, only a few kilometers below Joshimath, the principal town and transport hub for this stretch of the Char Dham Yatra route before the road climbs on toward Badrinath. It lies directly on National Highway 58; travelers typically use Joshimath as the practical base for reaching it, and, given its altitude and location, conditions here are more exposed to Himalayan winter weather than at the lower-lying prayags downstream.
