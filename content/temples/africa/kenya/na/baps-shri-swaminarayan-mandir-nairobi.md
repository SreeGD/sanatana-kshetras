Nairobi is home to more than one Swaminarayan-tradition mandir, reflecting how the sampradaya's various branches built independently among Kenya's Gujarati Hindu community during the twentieth century. Alongside the East African Swaminarayan Sanstha's (EASS) "Devasadan"-era temple already documented in the city, Wikipedia's inventory of Hindu temples in Kenya separately names a "BAPS Shri Swaminarayan Mandir Nairobi," affiliated instead with the Bochasanwasi Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha (BAPS) - the same globally active branch of the tradition that has also built a mandir in Kampala, Uganda, and temples across the Indian diaspora more broadly.

As with other BAPS mandirs, worship at this Nairobi temple would be expected to center on murtis of Bhagwan Swaminarayan and to serve as a hub for the city's Gujarati Hindu community around festivals such as Diwali and Annakut. However, no dedicated source describing this particular temple's founding, address, or day-to-day life could be found during this research pass; its existence and its distinctness from Nairobi's EASS-affiliated Swaminarayan temple rest on a single confirmed mention in Wikipedia's Kenya-wide temple inventory.
