One of the oldest Hindu temples in the American South, the Sri Ganesha Temple in Nashville's Bellevue area serves as the seat of the Hindu Cultural Center of Tennessee. Sources disagree on the exact founding date: a Wikipedia timeline of Nashville history, citing Harvard's Pluralism Project, dates the temple's establishment to 1980, while an independent Nashville heritage source describes the temple building itself as constructed around 1985 after more than two years of work using stone imported from India. It is plausible the earlier date marks the founding of the sponsoring cultural organization and the later date the completion of the physical temple, a common pattern among diaspora temples of that era, but neither source confirms this explicitly.

As is common for early Hindu temples built in the United States — where the small size of local communities made single-deity temples impractical — the Nashville temple was designed to house multiple traditions under one roof, centered on Ganesha but also honoring Shiva, Vishnu, and Durga, with sixteen forms of Ganesha depicted along its outer wall. Its architecture draws on South Indian temple traditions, with a tiered gopuram-style pinnacle reported at roughly 48 feet. The temple continues to serve as a cultural and religious hub for Hindu families across Middle Tennessee, hosting regular public tours and major festivals such as Ganesha Chaturthi.
