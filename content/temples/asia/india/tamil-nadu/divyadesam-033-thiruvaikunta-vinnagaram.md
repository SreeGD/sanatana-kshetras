Vaikuntanathan, the Lord of Sri Vaikuntam, is the presiding deity of the temple at Thiruvaikunta Vinnagaram, one of the 108 Divya Desams sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The very name of the shrine carries the word Vinnagaram, denoting a celestial temple, a "city of the heavens," and the Lord is here adored as Vaikuntanathan, the Master of the supreme abode of Vaikunta itself. By this name and form the temple is held to manifest on earth the splendour of Paramapadam, the eternal realm of the Lord, so that devotees may glimpse in their worship the bliss of the highest heaven while yet in this world. To approach Vaikuntanathan is, according to tradition, to turn the heart toward that final goal of the soul, the deathless abode from which there is no return. Thirumangai Alwar, the warrior-saint who sang the glory of more Divya Desams than any other Alwar, hallowed this kshetra in his pasurams, securing its place among the sacred shrines recited in the eternal canon of Tamil Vaishnavism. Through his hymns the faithful continue to celebrate this temple as a place where the Lord, as the very ruler of Vaikunta, makes Himself present and accessible to those who seek refuge. The shrine, bearing the title of a heavenly city, stands among the Vinnagaram temples cherished by the tradition for their evocation of the celestial, and it endures as a revered centre of Sri Vaishnava devotion where the worship of Vaikuntanathan kindles in the devotee the longing for the Supreme Abode.

## Festivals

Counted among the eleven Nangur Divya Desams, this temple shares in the great collective Garuda Sevai, when the processional deities of the Nangur temples are mounted together on Garuda vahanas for a celebrated joint darshan that attracts throngs of devotees. The annual Brahmotsavam is traditionally observed with festal processions of the utsava murti and special abhishekams. Vaikunta Ekadasi is kept with the heightened devotion common to Srivaishnava shrines, together with the recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham during the Adhyayana Utsavam. Pavithrotsavam, offered for the purification of the year's worship, and a Float festival (Theppotsavam) also feature among the temple's traditional yearly observances.

## Location & Access

The temple lies at Thiruvaikunta Vinnagaram among the Nangur Divya Desams of the Chola Nadu region, in the Nagapattinam district of Tamil Nadu, near Sirkazhi and Nangur. Pilgrims generally reach it through the road and rail links around Sirkazhi, Mayiladuthurai and Nagapattinam, with Chennai the principal city at a greater distance. In keeping with most Vishnu temples, it observes morning and evening darshan hours and remains closed during the midday hours. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
