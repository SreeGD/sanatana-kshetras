The Sri Vadaranyeswarar Temple at Thiruvalangadu, in Tiruvallur district, is a Paadal Petra Sthalam and one of the five Pancha Sabhai halls of Tamil Shaiva tradition — sites where Shiva is held to have performed his cosmic dance — here classified as the Rathna Sabha, or "gem hall," a designation distinct from but often discussed alongside the far better-known Ponnambalam (gold hall) at Chidambaram's Nataraja Temple, already covered elsewhere in this directory. Shiva is worshipped here as Vadaranyeswarar, alongside his consort Vandarkuzhali. The surviving temple structure is dated to the 12th-century Chola period.

Devotional tradition connects the temple to the woman Nayanar-adjacent saint Karaikkal Ammaiyar, who is said to have approached the sanctum by walking on her head rather than her feet, out of reverence for the sacred ground, and to have been addressed by Shiva himself as "amma" (mother). Thiruvalangadu is also frequently cited in general and art-historical writing as connected to a celebrated Chola-era bronze Nataraja and to an important Rajendra Chola-era copper-plate inscription, though those specific claims were not independently confirmed in the source consulted for this entry and are flagged here rather than asserted outright.
