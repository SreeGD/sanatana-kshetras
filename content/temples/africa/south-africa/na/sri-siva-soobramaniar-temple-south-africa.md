Standing on Sirdar Road in the Durban suburb of Clairwood, the Shree Siva Soobramoniar Temple traces its origins to around 1889, when local tradition recounts that a Sadhu (holy man) appeared near the Amanzimyama river. After his departure, residents are said to have found a boulder naturally shaped like a lingam, marked with three white stripes and a red dot, together with a vel (spear) fashioned from wire — emblems of Lord Muruga. A devotee, Arumuga Soobramoney Paradassi, began holding worship on open ground at the site, and in 1915 the growing congregation formed a formal committee. The community acquired the present Sirdar Road land in 1919, renamed the institution the Shree Siva Soobramoniar Temple, and completed its two main shrines and three subsidiary shrines by 1921 through community donations and volunteer labour.

Also known as the Clairwood Shree Siva Soobramoniar Temple (C.S.S.S.T.) and historically as the "Madras Temple," it is dedicated to Siva Soobramoniar (Murugan) alongside his consorts Valli and Devasena. The temple underwent a major refurbishment by sthapatis (temple craftsmen) from India in 2014, marked by a Maha Kumbha Abhishekam consecration ceremony for its 125th anniversary. It remains one of Durban's most active Tamil Hindu institutions, observing a full calendar of festivals through the year, most prominently the annual Thaipusam Kavady, one of the largest such observances in the region.
