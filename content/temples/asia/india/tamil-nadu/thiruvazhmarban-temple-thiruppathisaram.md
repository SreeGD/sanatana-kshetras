## Origin and legend

The Thiruvazhmarban Temple at Thiruppathisaram (also known by the older name Thiruvanparisaram) enshrines Vishnu as Thiruvazhmarban, with his consort Lakshmi worshipped as Kamalavalli Thayar. The principal image is fashioned from a compound of lime and granite rather than solid stone, a technique found at several ancient temples of the far south. As with neighbouring Divya Desams in Kanyakumari district, local tradition places the shrine's establishment in the deep antiquity of the old Chera/Travancore country, alongside legends of sage-founders and royal patrons common to the cluster of temples along the Thamirabarani-adjacent river country of the deep south.

## Historical significance

The temple is built in Dravidian style with a five-tiered gopuram over an approximately two-acre walled granite enclosure, and its festival (utsava) image is cast in panchaloha (a five-metal alloy). It is administered today by the Kanyakumari Devasthanam. The temple's Aadi Swati festival specifically commemorates Kulasekara Alvar, reflecting a regional tradition that this Alvar-king's associations extended across the old Chera territory in which Thiruppathisaram lay.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thiruppathisaram is one of the thirteen Divya Desams of the Malai Nadu group. Nammalvar glorified the presiding deity, Thiruvazhmarban, in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, placing the temple within the canon of 108 shrines "seen and sung" by the Vaishnava Alvar saints between the 6th and 9th centuries CE. As with the neighbouring Thiruvattar temple, Thiruppathisaram's classification under the traditional "Malai Nadu" (Kerala/Chera country) region group reflects its historical position within the old Travancore kingdom; its present-day administrative location, however, is Kanyakumari district of Tamil Nadu, following the 1956 linguistic reorganisation of states.
