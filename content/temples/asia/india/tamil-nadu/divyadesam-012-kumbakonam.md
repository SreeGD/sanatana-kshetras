Thirukkudanthai, the sacred town of Kumbakonam in the heart of Chola Nadu, is among the most celebrated of the 108 Divya Desams, and its temple of Sarangapani is one of the foremost Vishnu shrines of the Tamil land. The presiding deity (Moolavar) is worshipped as Sarangapani, the Lord who bears the great bow Sarnga, reclining in regal repose; the sanctum is famously fashioned in the form of a grand chariot drawn by horses and elephants, evoking the descent of the Lord to earth. According to the sthala purana, the Lord manifested here for the sage Hemarishi, and the place is linked to the divine 'kumbha' or pot of nectar from which the town takes its name, marking it as a holy site where the waters of creation and grace are said to gather. Thirukkudanthai holds extraordinary distinction in the Prabandham, for it was sung by a remarkable assembly of Alwars: the first three Mudhal Alwars—Bhoothath Alwar and Pey Alwar—together with Thirumazhisai Alwar, the great Nammalwar whose Tiruvaymozhi is the soul of the canon, Periyalwar, his foster-daughter Andal the only woman Alwar, and Thirumangai Alwar. This rare concourse of saintly voices marks the shrine as supremely beloved within the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Pilgrims revere Sarangapani as a master of compassion and the consort of Komalavalli Thayar, and the temple's grand festivals, especially in the Tamil month of Chithirai, draw devotees in great multitudes to behold the Lord who reclines in his celestial chariot at Kumbakonam.

## Festivals

The Sarangapani Temple at Kumbakonam, one of the great Divya Desams, traditionally observes a grand annual Brahmotsavam, most famously the spring festival in the Tamil month of Chithirai centred on the celebrated chariot (ther), among the largest in the region, drawn through the streets. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is kept with special solemnity, with the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitations of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham and the opening of the Paramapada Vasal for devotees to pass through. The Float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank, Pavithrotsavam, and the wider Srivaishnava observances are also customarily marked. These are presented as long-honoured traditions of the shrine.

## Location & Access

The Sarangapani Temple stands in the heart of Kumbakonam (Thirukkudanthai), a major temple town in the Chola Nadu region of the Thanjavur district, Tamil Nadu. Kumbakonam is itself a well-connected rail and road hub, with Thanjavur and Tiruchirappalli the nearest larger cities. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours and closes during the midday interval. The shrine sits within the town and is easily reached on foot from the Kumbakonam railway and bus stations.
