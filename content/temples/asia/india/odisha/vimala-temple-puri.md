Within the great temple complex at Puri, in its southwest corner beside the Rohini Kunda tank, stands the shrine of Vimala (also spelled Bimala) — a distinct goddess shrine from the main sanctum of Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra already documented elsewhere in this directory, though inseparable from it in ritual practice. Vimala is regarded as the tantric guardian and consort of the Jagannath complex, and by long custom no offering made to Jagannath is considered complete, or fit to be called prasad, until it has first been presented to Vimala; only after her acceptance does the food become sanctified prasad for the wider temple.

Accounts differ on Vimala's place among the Shakti Peethas. Some pitha lists count her among the classical fifty-one sites where a fragment of Sati's body fell (naming the foot in one such list), but other tantric sources describe the Puri shrine instead as an uchchhishta pitha — a site made sacred not by a body part but by Sati's "leftover food," tied to a legend in which Parvati, feeling excluded from Vishnu's own offerings, was promised a home at Puri as Vimala, where she would always receive his food remnants first. The temple's central icon is dated to roughly the 6th century CE, well before the present Kalinga-style structure, whose 9th-century construction is credited by different sources either to the Eastern Ganga dynasty or, per the Madala Panji chronicle, to Yayati Keshari of the Somavamshi dynasty — an unresolved attribution. A surviving 13th-century inscription, the New Delhi Konark stele, records the Eastern Ganga king Narasimhadeva I worshipping "Durga-Madhava" (identified with Vimala paired with Jagannath) during Vijayadashami, evidence that the joint Jagannath-Vimala cult was already well established by that period. Today the temple's own sixteen-day Durga Puja, distinct from the Ratha Yatra for which the wider complex is best known, remains its major annual observance, closing with the Gajapati king's traditional worship of Vimala as Durga on Vijayadashami.
