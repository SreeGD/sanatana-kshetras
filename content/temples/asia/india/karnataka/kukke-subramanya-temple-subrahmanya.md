Kukke Subramanya Temple, on the bank of the Kumaradhara River in Dakshina Kannada district, is one of Karnataka's most visited pilgrimage sites and India's best-known center for serpent-related ritual remedy. Here Kartikeya (Subrahmanya) is worshipped not primarily in his familiar form as Shiva's warrior son but as sovereign of all serpents, a role tradition traces to the legend of Vasuki, king of the nagas, taking refuge at this spot from Garuda; the same body of legend holds that Kartikeya and his brother Ganesha defeated the demons Taraka and Shurapadma at the nearby peak of Kumara Parvatha before Kartikeya's marriage to Devasena, Indra's daughter, and that the site itself was among several consecrated by the sage Parashurama. No independently datable founding record for the temple structure was found during verification, so its origins are presented here as tradition rather than settled history.

The temple follows the Tantra Sara Sangraha system of ritual associated with Madhvacharya, and for centuries its priesthood was held by the Sthanika Brahmins, reckoned among the oldest Tulu Brahmin communities in South India, before Shivalli Madhwa Brahmins took over officiating duties in the early twentieth century. Its enduring national reputation, however, rests less on antiquity than on function: devotees travel from across India specifically to perform Ashlesha Bali and Sarpa Samskara (Sarpa Dosha Parihara), rites believed to relieve afflictions attributed to wronged or unappeased serpent spirits, making Kukke Subramanya one of the subcontinent's principal destinations for naga worship alongside its standing as a major Skanda/Kartikeya shrine, especially crowded during the Champa Shasti festival.
