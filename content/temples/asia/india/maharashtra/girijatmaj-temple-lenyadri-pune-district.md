Unusual among the Ashtavinayak shrines for being a rock-cut cave temple — Cave No. 8 of the ancient Lenyadri cave complex, which also includes a number of earlier Buddhist caves — rather than a free-standing structure, and for being reached only by climbing a long flight of roughly 300 stone steps (307, by one count). The name 'Girijatmaj' ('son of Girija,' an epithet of Parvati) reflects the local tradition that Parvati performed penance at this site to be blessed with Ganesha as her son. Unlike most Ganesha shrines, the cave's Ganesha image is carved directly into the rock face rather than installed as a separate idol, and no ritual immersion is performed.

The pillarless hall, roughly 53 feet by 51 feet and 7 feet high, has no electric lighting and relies entirely on daylight entering the cave mouth. The complex forms part of the wider Lenyadri caves, 18 rock-cut chambers of Buddhist origin (also called Ganesha-leni), of which this Ganesha shrine occupies the eighth. Shivneri Fort, birthplace of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, lies about 5-6 km away.
