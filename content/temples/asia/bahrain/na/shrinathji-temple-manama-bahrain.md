The Shrinathji Temple in Manama, Bahrain's capital, was constructed in 1817 by the local Hindu merchant community, making it one of the oldest Hindu temples anywhere in the Middle East. It is dedicated to Shrinathji, a form of Krishna especially associated with the Pushtimarg tradition founded by Vallabhacharya, and its long-standing presence reflects a Hindu trading community in Bahrain with roots reaching back well before the country's modern oil-era development.

The temple's continued operation, alongside a Hindu cremation ground granted over a century ago, situates it within a broader pattern of long-tolerated Hindu religious infrastructure in Bahrain. That pattern has continued into the present: in February 2022, King Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa granted additional land for the construction of a second, larger BAPS Swaminarayan Hindu temple in the country, modeled on a similar project completed in Abu Dhabi, and Bahrain is also home to an established BAPS Shri Swaminarayan Mandir and an ISKCON-affiliated temple community. The 1817 Shrinathji Temple, however, remains the historic anchor of Hindu worship in the country.
