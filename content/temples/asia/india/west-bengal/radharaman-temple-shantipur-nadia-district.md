Shantipur, in the Ranaghat subdivision of Nadia district, was the home for most of his adult life of Advaita Acharya (1434-1559), one of the most senior associates of the 16th-century Bengali saint Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and a central figure of Gaudiya Vaishnava theology. Tradition holds that Chaitanya himself visited Advaita's home in Shantipur — by one account the first place he took alms after leaving Nabadwip — where Advaita told him "Wherever you are is Vrindavan," a saying still recounted in Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition.

The town's Radharaman Temple traces its principal deity to a Krishna image originally called "Dolgovinda," installed in Orissa by King Indradyumna. When the deity's Orissa temple came under threat from an attacking king, its priest entrusted the image to his own guru, Mathuresh Goswami — Advaita Acharya's great-grandson — who carried it to his ancestral home in Shantipur and reinstalled it in a new temple there. A Radha deity was later placed beside it, and the pair was renamed Radharaman, the form worshipped at the temple today. Shantipur's largest annual festival, Rash Yatra (Ras Utsav) in October/November, draws devotees with processions honoring Radha and Krishna. A separate deity, Madan Gopal, said to have been worshipped personally by Advaita Acharya, is maintained in the town today by the lineage of his son Krishna Mishra, underscoring Shantipur's continuing standing as a living center of the Advaita Acharya tradition within Gaudiya Vaishnavism.
