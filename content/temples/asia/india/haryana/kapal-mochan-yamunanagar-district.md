Kapal Mochan, in Haryana's Yamunanagar district near the village of Bilaspur, centers on a sacred kund (pool) held to purify visitors of even serious transgressions -- its very name meaning "liberation from the skull." Hindu tradition explains that name through Shiva himself: having cut off one of Brahma's five heads in anger, Shiva is said to have come here to be freed of the resulting sin, sanctifying the pool in the process. A second, layered legend attached to a neighboring pool, Surya Kund, holds that Rama halted here in his flying chariot after killing Ravana -- a killing that carried its own weight of sin, since Ravana was Brahmin-born on his father's side -- linking the site to two of Hinduism's central purification narratives rather than one.

The site's significance is not exclusively Hindu: Sikh tradition records that Guru Nanak visited in 1584 to argue against the ritual concept of sutak, the idea that a newborn child's birth is a source of ritual impurity, and that Guru Gobind Singh came here in 1688 after the Battle of Bhangani to honor his soldiers with ceremonial robes. That shared history is reflected in the site's built landscape today, where a historic Mahadev Temple and a smaller Gau Bacha Temple stand alongside a Gurdwara around the ancient kund. Kapal Mochan draws its largest crowds -- roughly half a million pilgrims by some estimates -- during the annual Kapal Mochan Mela each November, a scale of gathering the state government has moved in recent years to support with dedicated tourism infrastructure.
