Bajreshwari Devi Temple sits in Kangra town, seat of the old Kangra kingdom, and is dedicated to the goddess in her fierce, thunderbolt-wielding form as Vajreshwari — her name combining vajra (thunderbolt) with ishwari (goddess/sovereign). The temple complex extends beyond the main sanctum to include several subsidiary shrines, among them one dedicated to Bhairava, and the site is counted among the network of Shakti Peethas where a part of Sati's dismembered body is said to have fallen, in this case her left breast; that identification is consistent between the temple's own Wikipedia account and a separate Wikipedia listing of the Shakti Peethas, giving it somewhat firmer traditional grounding than some of the more disputed Peetha attributions.

Kangra's temples are frequently described in popular and semi-historical writing as having drawn repeated raids across the medieval period on account of their reputed wealth, a narrative often extended to Bajreshwari given its prominence in Kangra town itself. The Wikipedia source consulted first for this entry did not document any such episodes, but the official Kangra district government website separately states that the temple was looted repeatedly during the medieval period, naming Mahmud of Ghazni, and that it was destroyed in the 1905 Kangra earthquake before being rebuilt by the government -- corroborating the plundering narrative via a second, non-Wikipedia source, though still not a primary or archaeological one, and adding the specific 1905 earthquake/rebuild detail. No founding date or dynasty for the original temple is documented in either source. What is well documented is the temple's continuing active worship today, centered on the twice-yearly Navaratri observances, which remain the principal draw for pilgrims to Kangra town.
