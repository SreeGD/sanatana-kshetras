Founded in 1968 under the guidance of ISKCON founder A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, New Vrindaban was one of the first ISKCON farm communities established in the West, intended as a rural, self-sufficient model of Krishna-conscious living modeled loosely on Vrindavan, India. Its best-known structure, Prabhupada's Palace of Gold, was planned from 1972 as a residence for Prabhupada himself; construction ran from 1973 to 1985, with the building dedicated and opened to the public on 2 September 1979. After Prabhupada's death in November 1977, the project's purpose shifted to that of a memorial shrine, built of marble, gold leaf, and carved teakwood by largely untrained devotee labor at a materials cost of roughly $600,000. It became a major regional tourist attraction, drawing an estimated 50,000 visitors a year by 2008 and sometimes described in American press as "America's Taj Mahal"; a $4.27 million restoration of the palace began in 2011 to address decades of deferred maintenance.
