The Arulmigu Balathandayuthapani Temple, popularly known as the Waterfall Hilltop Temple, is Penang's principal Murugan temple and the island's own major focal point for the annual Thaipusam festival -- distinct from, and independent of, the more internationally famous Thaipusam procession at Batu Caves near Kuala Lumpur. The site's origins trace to around 1800, when a shrine was established at the foot of a waterfall in what is now the Penang Botanic Gardens, with the earliest documented evidence an 1856 painting; the temple relocated to its present hilltop position on Jalan Kebun Bunga in 1915, when Thaipusam was celebrated there for the first time.

Devotees today climb 513 steps to reach the hilltop sanctum, passing beneath a seven-story, 21.6-meter gopuram that crowns a major reconstruction completed with a grand consecration ceremony on 29 June 2012. Dedicated to Murugan in his Balathandayuthapani manifestation -- the youthful god bearing a spear -- the temple anchors Penang's own Thaipusam kavadi procession, traditionally starting from Lorong Kulit, making it one of George Town's most significant Hindu landmarks and a natural companion entry to Malaysia's other major Murugan pilgrimage sites already documented in this directory.
