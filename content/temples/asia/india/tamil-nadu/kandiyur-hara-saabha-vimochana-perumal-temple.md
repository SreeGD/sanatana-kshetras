Thirukkandiyur's Hara Saabha Vimochana Perumal Temple takes its name directly from its central legend: Hara Saabha Vimochana means "the release of Hara (Shiva) from his curse." According to the Brahmanda Purana as transmitted in temple tradition, Shiva, in a fit of anger, severed one of Brahma's five heads; the skull adhered to his hand as the sin of Brahmahatti, since Brahma is regarded as a Brahmin deity. Shiva came to this spot on the banks of the Kaveri to worship Vishnu, who absolved him of the sin — an event commemorated by the temple's sacred tank, known as Kapala Theertham (also called Kamala Moksha Theertham). The presiding deity, worshipped in his festival form as Kamalanathan or Bali Nathan, stands facing east in the gesture of blessing, with consort Kamalavalli Nachiyar; subsidiary shrines to Narasimha, Sudarshana, and Andal are also housed within the complex.

The temple's precise construction period is genuinely disputed between sources: one account credits the Medieval Cholas of the late 8th century, while another attributes the original structure to the Pallava dynasty of the 7th-9th century, later rebuilt under the Cholas, with further additions from the Vijayanagara empire, the Madurai Nayaks, and Thanjavur's Maratha rulers. Whichever the precise origin, the temple has undergone several attested modern renovations, in 1981, 1984, and 2003.

Glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by ten hymns of Thirumangai Alvar, the temple sits within the broader "Pancha Kamala Kshetram" — a traditional grouping of five lotus-associated Vaishnava shrines in the region. Its main annual celebration, the Panguni Brahmmotsavam, draws pilgrims each spring, alongside the Aipasi Pavithra Utsavam and Karthikai Deepam later in the year.
