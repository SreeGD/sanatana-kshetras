Kaundinyapur, a village on the banks of the Wardha river in Tiosa tahsil of Amravati district, is traditionally identified with Kundina (Kundinapura), the capital of the ancient Vidarbha Kingdom and, in Puranic and Mahabharata tradition, the birthplace and natal home of Rukmini — the princess who became Krishna's chief queen after he carried her away from her intended marriage to Shishupala. Local belief holds that Rukmini returned each Kartik month to visit her maternal home at Kaundinyapur, a tradition still marked by the village today. This identification of a present-day village with the Puranic city is a matter of long-standing tradition rather than an archaeologically settled fact, though the site's antiquity is independently documented: excavations by the state archaeology department uncovered the stone, foundation, and brick walls of what appears to be a palatial building of roughly the 14th or 15th century CE, along with traces of the ancient city's rampart, and further remains attributed to the Copper Age and Stone Age have been reported at the site.

An ancient Vitthal-Rukmini temple stands on a hillock overlooking the Wardha river at Kaundinyapur, honoring Vitthal (a form of Vishnu closely associated with Maharashtra's Varkari tradition) alongside Rukmini. No source consulted gives a construction date or founder for this standing temple, distinct from the separately dated palatial-building remains uncovered nearby, so its own architectural history is treated here as unverified even as its identity as Kaundinyapur's principal shrine, and the village's broader association with Rukmini's legend, are well and consistently documented.
