At Kilchalai, one of the villages making up the Thirunangur group, Vishnu is worshipped as Deivanayagan, more widely known by the name Madhava Perumal, together with his consort Kadalmagal ("daughter of the ocean," an epithet of Lakshmi recalling her emergence from the churning of the milk ocean). As with the other ten temples of the group, the shrine's founding is bound up in the shared Thirunangur legend of Shiva's grief-maddened dance after the death of his consort and Vishnu's eleven-fold appearance to bring him peace.

Three Alvars - Periyalvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, and Thirumangai Alvar - are recorded in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham as having sung here, giving the temple its standing among the 108 Divya Desams. The shrine takes part each year, together with its ten Thirunangur neighbours, in the Thirumangai Alvar Mangalasasana Utsavam during the Tamil month of Thai, when its festival deity is carried in the Garudasevai procession alongside the others - the yearly reaffirmation of Thirunangur's identity as a single sacred cluster of eleven individually glorified Vishnu shrines.
