Carved from a single mass of volcanic rock at the Ellora cave complex, the Kailasa Temple was excavated top-down — sculptors began at the summit of the cliff face and worked downward, removing an estimated 200,000 tonnes of rock — rather than built up from a foundation like a conventional structure. Built in the 8th century CE under the Rashtrakuta dynasty, primarily attributed to King Krishna I (r. 756-773 CE), it rises 32.6 meters above its courtyard and is carved with extensive relief panels depicting the Ramayana and Mahabharata. Part of the Ellora Caves UNESCO World Heritage Site (which also includes Buddhist and Jain excavations), it is widely regarded as the pinnacle of rock-cut architecture in India.
