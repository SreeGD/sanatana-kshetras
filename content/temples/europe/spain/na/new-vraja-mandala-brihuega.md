New Vraja Mandala, known in Spanish as Nueva Vrajamandala, is a rural ISKCON farm settlement built on the Santa Clara estate in a pine forest beside the Tajuña river, near the town of Brihuega in Guadalajara province, inland Castilla-La Mancha. It has functioned as the principal Spanish headquarters of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness since 1979, with a formal founding date of 1983 recorded in some references; as with New Mayapur in France and New Vrindaban in the United States, it follows ISKCON's broader pattern of establishing self-sufficient rural devotee communities combining temple worship, farming, and cow protection, modeled on the villages of Krishna's Vraja (Vrindavana) region in India, from which its name is drawn.

By 2009 the settlement had grown to around 64 residents, functioning as a combined temple, residential community, and administrative center for ISKCON's activities across Spain. Like its sister communities elsewhere in Europe, it operates under the spiritual guidance established by ISKCON founder A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada and continues to host devotees, festivals, and visitors at its hillside location roughly 90 km northeast of Madrid.
