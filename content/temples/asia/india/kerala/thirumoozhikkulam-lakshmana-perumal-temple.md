## Origin and legend

The Thirumoozhikkulam Lakshmana Perumal Temple enshrines Vishnu in the form of Lakshmana, worshipped locally as Lakshmana Swamy (also called Thiru Moozhi Kalathan or, in Sanskrit/Manipravalam usage, Sri Sukti Natha Perumal), with the temple goddess worshipped as Maduraveni Thayar. Local Ramayana-linked tradition holds that Lakshmana and Bharata worshipped jointly at this site, and that the place-name "Thirumoozhikkalam" derives from "tiru-mozhi-kalam" -- the place where sacred words were spoken. Temple tradition also credits the legendary master-architect Perumthachan with the design of the shrine.

## Historical significance

The temple, built in Kerala-style Dravidian architecture with a tower known as the Soundarya Vimanam and a temple tank called Poorna Nadi (fed by the Chalakkudy Puzha), possesses all five traditional prakaras (enclosures) of a fully developed Kerala temple complex, and forms part of the Nalambalam pilgrimage circuit of four related shrines. The temple suffered a devastating fire and desecration of its idol during Tipu Sultan's invasion of Kerala in 1790, and was later restored under the patronage of Travancore Maharajahs Sree Moolam Tirunal and Sree Chithira Tirunal. It is today administered by the Travancore Devaswom Board.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thirumoozhikkulam is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. It is glorified with eleven verses by Nammalvar in the Thiruvaymoli and three verses by Thirumangai Alvar in the Thirunetuntakam, together securing its place in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham's canon of 108 Divya Desams.
