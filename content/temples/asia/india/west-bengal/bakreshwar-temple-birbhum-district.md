Bakreshwar in Birbhum district is both a Shaiva and Shakta pilgrimage complex, centered on a cluster of shrines — the Ma Bhavatarini Mandir, the Bakreshwar (Shiva) temple, the Mahishasurmardini Temple, and the Bhairabnath Temple — set among ten natural hot springs, with names such as Agni Kunda, Surya Kunda, and Amrita Kunda, that are themselves treated as sacred bathing sites. The name "Bakreshwar" (from "vakra," bent, and "ishwar," lord) comes from a local legend of the sage Ashtavakra, said to have been cursed with eight bodily deformities after offending Indra at the wedding of Vishnu and Lakshmi, and to have received Shiva's grace at this spot after prolonged austerities. Because a part of the goddess is held in local tradition to have fallen at Bakreshwar during Sati's dismemberment, the site is also counted among the 51 Shakti Peethas, with the goddess worshipped here as Mahishmardini/Adi Shakti.

No inscriptional or archaeological dating for the temple structures themselves has surfaced in the sources consulted, so — unlike many Bengal temples with clear royal patronage on record — Bakreshwar's founding period and builder remain undocumented; its sanctity rests on Puranic and Shakti-Peetha tradition together with continuous pilgrim use rather than on a datable foundation. The site remains an active pilgrimage and bathing destination today, drawing devotees for both its Shaiva and Shakta associations, with Maha Shivaratri its most widely observed festival.
