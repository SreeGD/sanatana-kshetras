The Sivadol at Sivasagar, in Upper Assam, was built in 1734 under the patronage of Bar Raja Ambika, a queen of the Ahom king Swargadeo Siva Singha, whose reign (1714-1744) produced some of the most ambitious temple architecture of the Ahom period; the great tank beside the temple, excavated over 1731-1738, gave the modern town of Sivasagar ("ocean of Shiva") its name. At roughly 104 feet (32 meters) tall with a base circumference near 195 feet, the Sivadol is often described as one of the tallest Shiva temples in India, its four-tiered mastaka capped by a gold kalasha; the sanctum holds an unusual reversed Shiva linga rather than the more common upright form. The complex is completed by two subsidiary shrines on the same tank — the Vishnudol and the Devidol, dedicated respectively to Vishnu and Durga — reflecting the Ahom royal family's patronage across major strands of Hindu worship rather than Shaivism alone.

Sivadol remains an active site of daily worship, drawing its largest crowds during Maha Shivaratri, while the adjoining Devidol hosts its own major Durga Puja celebrations each autumn — together making the Sivasagar tank complex one of Assam's most continuously used temple sites since the 18th century.
