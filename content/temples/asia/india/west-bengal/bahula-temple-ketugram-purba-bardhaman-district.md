Bahula Temple, in Ketugram in the Katwa subdivision of Purba Bardhaman district, marks the spot traditionally held to be where the left arm of Sati fell after her body was cut apart following Daksha's yajna — one of the 51 Shakti Peethas of Bengal. The Goddess is worshipped here as Bahula, with her consort Bhairava known as Bhiruk (Bhirukeshwar); notably, the settlement itself was known as Bahula before later coming to be called Ketugram, suggesting the shrine's identity shaped the village's own name over time.

The temple's stone image of Bahula, together with images of Kartikeya and Ganesha placed alongside her, is attributed by local tradition to a ruler remembered as Raja Chandraketu, though no date or independent corroboration for this figure or the installation could be found in the sources checked. Some accounts describe the temple as originally standing on the bank of the Ajay river, though the river has since shifted and the shrine now stands at some distance from its current course — a detail consistent with the slow migration of Bengal's delta rivers over the centuries. Worship continues at the site today as part of the living Shakti Peetha pilgrimage tradition of West Bengal, though little in the way of dated inscriptional or architectural history has survived or been documented for the structure itself.
