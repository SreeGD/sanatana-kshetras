Thiruppulingudi, also known as Thirupuliyangudi, is one of the Nava Tirupathi ("nine Tirupathis") - a cluster of nine Divya Desams strung along the Thamiraparani river in the former Pandya country, each traditionally associated with one of the navagrahas (nine planets). This shrine is reckoned the Budha (Mercury) sthalam of the group. The presiding deity is worshipped here as Kaisina Vendhan Perumal, also called Bhoomipalan ("protector of the earth"), reclining in Bujanga Sayana posture facing east with a rice-measuring vessel (padi) as his pillow - an unusual iconographic detail said to recall Vishnu's role as the guardian and measurer of the earth's bounty. His consort is venerated as Malarmangai Nachiyar (also called Pullingudivalli), a local form of Lakshmi tied to the very name of the village.

The temple is glorified by Nammalvar in the Nava Tirupathi Pathigam of the Thiruvaimozhi, the decad of verses in which he moves from shrine to shrine along the Thamiraparani, petitioning Vishnu in each of the nine forms. Along with its sister shrines - Alwarthirunagari, Thirukkolur, Thenthiruperai, Srivaikuntam, Thiruvaragunamangai (Natham), Thirukkulandhai, Tholaivillimangalam and Thiruccheeravaramangai (Nanguneri) - Thiruppulingudi is customarily visited together as part of the Nava Tirupathi pilgrimage, especially in the month of Margazhi. As with several of the Nava Tirupathi temples, the precise chronology of the present structure is not well documented in the sources consulted here; the site's antiquity and its identity as one of the 108 Divya Desams rest chiefly on its standing in the Divya Prabandham and on continuous local worship rather than on securely dated inscriptions.
