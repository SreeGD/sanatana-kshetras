Kiriteswari Temple stands in Kiritkona village near the Bhagirathi river in Murshidabad district, marking the spot where tradition holds the crown (kirit, or mukut) of Sati fell after her body was cut apart — giving the presiding goddess her names Kiriteshwari and Mukuteshwari, and the village its own name. She is also worshipped here in the form of Mahishamardini, the slayer of the buffalo demon.

Local tradition holds the site's worship to be more than a thousand years old, though the temple structure itself has a more clearly documented and more recent history: it was destroyed in 1405 and later rebuilt in the 19th century under the patronage of Darpanarayan, king of Lalgola, in the char-chala style typical of Bengal temple architecture. A local legend recalls that Nawab Mir Jafar, suffering from leprosy, once sought out the temple's holy water — an anecdote traced in Wikipedia's own citations to a 1897 Bengali-language history, Nikhilnath Roy's Murshidabad Kahini, though this entry has not independently examined that source and so does not treat the story as fully confirmed. In a more recent mark of recognition, the Union Ministry of Tourism declared Kiriteswari the "Best Tourism Village of India" in September 2023, underscoring the site's continuing importance as a living pilgrimage destination.
