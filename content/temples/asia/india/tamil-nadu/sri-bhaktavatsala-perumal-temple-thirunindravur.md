Sri Bhaktavatsala Perumal Temple at Thirunindravur, a western suburb of Chennai in Thiruvallur district, enshrines a roughly ten-foot standing image of Vishnu as Bhaktavatsala Perumal — "the Lord affectionate to his devotees" — facing east, with Lakshmi as his consort Ennai Petra Thayar (also known as Sudhavalli). Temple legend recounts that Vishnu appeared here in response to the devotion of Kubera, the god of wealth, and that Varuna, the god of the waters, also worshipped him at this spot; the town's own name is said to derive from Vishnu's visit here in the company of Lakshmi.

Thirunindravur's inclusion among the 108 Divya Desams carries an unusual literary footnote. Tradition holds that when the Vaishnava saint-poet Thirumangai Alvar visited Thirunindravur, the Lord did not grant him a direct vision, and the Alvar accordingly departed without composing any pasuram on the spot. It was only later, at Thirukadalmallai (Mahabalipuram), that Thirumangai Alvar — moved by the visual resemblance between the deities of Thirukadalmallai, Thirunindravur, and Thirukkannamangai — composed hymns comparing and praising all three forms together, thereby indirectly but permanently securing Thirunindravur's place in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham and among the sacred 108.

The temple's core construction is attributed to the Pallava dynasty, with the earliest surviving inscriptions dated between 820 and 890 CE, during the reign of the Pallava king Nripatungavarman. Later Medieval Chola rulers — including Rajendra Chola II, Virarajendra Chola, and Rajaraja Chola II — and subsequently the Vijayanagara kings, added further endowments and structural elements, including the temple's five-tiered gopuram and its granite enclosure walls. In the Sri Vaishnava institutional tradition, the Pedda Jeeyar of Tirupati serves as the temple's permanent trustee. Now situated within Chennai's expanding western suburbs, the temple continues as an active place of worship and remains notable among the Nadu Nadu Divya Desams for the distinctive, indirect manner in which it entered the Alvars' devotional canon.
