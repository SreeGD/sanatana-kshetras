Finland's only currently operating ISKCON (International Society for Krishna Consciousness) temple occupies the first floor of Malminkylän kartano (Malmi Manor), a historic manor building on Vanha Helsingintie in Helsinki's Malmi district, reachable via its own commuter rail station. Finnish-language sources trace the country's Krishna movement to a center established near Tehtaankatu in central Helsinki in 1982, with formal registration as a religious community following in 1984; the temple's own materials describe over 40 years of activity in Finland, consistent with that early-1980s founding, though it is not confirmed whether the Malmi site was the original 1982 location or a later move. It draws both Finnish members and immigrants from India, Nepal, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka.

As with ISKCON centres generally, worship here follows the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition founded in the West by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, centered on Radha Krishna and the chanting of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra. The temple holds evening arati daily and a Sunday feast program combining kirtan, a lecture, and a vegetarian meal, alongside an annual festival calendar including Janmastami, Radhastami, and the Jagannath Ratha-yatra procession.
