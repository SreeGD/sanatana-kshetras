The Govindaraja Perumal Temple at Chidambaram, known in the sacred geography of the Divya Desams as Thiruchitrakoodam, enshrines the Lord in a reclining posture and is counted among the 108 sacred abodes of Vishnu celebrated by the Alwars. This shrine is remarkable for standing within the great precincts of Chidambaram, the renowned kshetra of the cosmic dance, and it represents the rare confluence of Saiva and Vaishnava sanctity in a single hallowed space. Here Govindaraja Perumal reclines majestically, the presiding Moolavar of this Divya Desam, and devotees revere the shrine as one of the holiest of the Vaishnava abodes in Chola Nadu. The name Thiruchitrakoodam evokes, in tradition, the association with Chitrakoota, linking the place to the wider devotional landscape of Vishnu's manifestations. The glory of this Divya Desam was sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham by Kulasekara Alwar, the saintly king whose hymns breathe intense surrender and longing for the Lord, and by Thirumangai Alwar, the great poet-saint whose verses traversed and immortalised the Divya Desams across the Tamil land. Through their sacred compositions -- eleven hymns from Kulasekara Alwar, thirteen from Thirumangai Alwar -- Thiruchitrakoodam was woven into the perpetual liturgy of Srivaishnava devotion, ensuring that the reclining Govindaraja would be remembered and worshipped through the centuries. This is one of only two Divya Desams housed within a Shiva temple complex.

The shrine's documented history includes a notable disruption: the Chola king Kulothunga Chola II is said to have removed the Govindaraja idol around 1135 CE on sectarian grounds, with the philosopher-saint Ramanuja traditionally credited with carrying the festival image to safety at Tirupati. Vishnu worship is said to have been formally re-established at Chidambaram around 1370 CE under Vedanta Desika, coinciding with the Vijayanagara conquest of the region, with the present shrine structure rebuilt in 1539 CE under the Vijayanagara king Achyutaraya, and king Krishnappa Nayak separately credited with reinstalling the idol in the 1560s. An 1849 court dispute is recorded as having upheld Vaishnava worship rights over the idol and the Alvar shrine. The temple remains a place of deep pilgrimage, where the reclining form of the Lord, set amid the sanctity of Chidambaram, draws devotees seeking the grace and refuge that the Alwars themselves found and proclaimed in their hymns.

## Festivals

As a Divya Desam enshrined within the great temple-town of Chidambaram (Thiruchitrakoodam), Govindaraja Perumal is honoured with the customary Srivaishnava festival cycle. An annual Brahmotsavam is traditionally observed with grand processions of the Lord through the surrounding streets on the vahanas. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the Tamil month of Margazhi is a major occasion, marked by the recitation of the Tiruvaymoli during the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu days and the opening of the Paramapada Vasal for devotees to pass through. Pavithrotsavam, observed for the ritual purification of the year's worship, and a Float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank are also traditionally kept. Margazhi mornings resound with the chanting of Andal's Tiruppavai and the Nalayira Divya Prabandham.

## Location & Access

The temple lies within the sacred precincts of Chidambaram in the Cuddalore district of Tamil Nadu, in the historic Chola Nadu region, with the Lord enshrined as part of the famed Thillai temple complex. Chidambaram is a well-connected town reachable by road and rail, the nearest larger transport hubs being Cuddalore and Chidambaram's own railway station. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday closure. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
