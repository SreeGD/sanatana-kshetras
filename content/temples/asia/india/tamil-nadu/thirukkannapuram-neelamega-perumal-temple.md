The Neelamega Perumal Temple at Thirukkannapuram, whose festival deity is popularly known as Sowriraja Perumal, is traditionally said to rank second only to Srirangam in the number of Divya Prabandham hymns dedicated to it — a claim consistent with at least one source's tally of well over a hundred pasurams, including a full ten-decad hymn of Thirumangai Alvar devoted solely to this temple. The presiding deity, Neelamega Perumal, stands facing east, with his consort worshipped as Thirukkannapura Nayagi.

The temple's most distinctive legend concerns the origin of its festival name, Sowriraja. According to local tradition, a temple priest named Rangabhatta received daily garlands from a devoted courtesan, who wore them briefly before offering them to the deity. A visiting king, discovering a strand of hair in one such garland, grew suspicious of the priest — until he inspected the deity himself and found that the image was wearing a wig (sowri), vindicating the priest and giving the deity the name Sowriraja Perumal ("the lord who wore the wig"); tradition holds that strands of hair remain visible on the festival image during full-moon processions to this day. A separate, older legend holds that the temple commemorates Vishnu revealing his particular grace and "walking beauty" to Vibhishana, king of Lanka, while a further strand connects the site to the Padma Purana story of King Vasu and his daughter Padmini's marriage to Vishnu.

Historically, the temple is attributed to Medieval Chola-era construction with later additions under the Thanjavur Nayaks, and is today administered by the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Board; no specific inscription count for the site was located in the sources consulted. The temple's principal annual festival, a twelve-day Brahmotsavam, is held in the Tamil month of Vaikasi, and a further ten-day Masi Mahotsavam includes a distinctive seaside procession to nearby Karaikal.
