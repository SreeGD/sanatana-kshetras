Nachiyar Kovil, formally the Thirunarayur Nambi Perumal Temple, is distinctive among the Divya Desams for the ritual precedence given to its goddess, Vanjulavalli Nachiyar (identified with Niladevi), over the presiding Vishnu form, Thirunarayur Nambi — a reversal reflected in the temple's popular name, which means simply "the goddess's temple." According to the sthala purana, the sage Medhavi performed penance for a daughter and discovered the infant Vanjulavalli beneath a Vanjula tree near the temple tank; when Vishnu sought her hand, she agreed to marry him only on the condition that she be given precedence in temple honors and processions — a condition honored in the temple's ritual life ever since.

The temple also houses a separate shrine to a striking kneeling stone (saligrama) image of Garuda, over ten feet tall, popularly giving the temple its informal nickname, the "Kal Garudan Temple." During the annual Garuda Sevai procession in the Tamil month of Panguni, devotional tradition holds that the number of bearers needed to carry the stone Garuda mysteriously increases through the course of the procession — a piece of local devotional lore rather than a documented physical phenomenon. Sri Vaishnava tradition further holds that Vishnu personally initiated the poet-saint Thirumangai Alvar into Vaishnavism through the Pancha Samskara rite at this very temple, a well-known episode in Alvar hagiography.

The temple is traditionally, though not firmly documentedly, attributed to the early Chola king Kochengat Cholan, with later structural contributions ascribed to the medieval Cholas and the Vijayanagara empire; no specific supporting inscription was located in the sources consulted for this entry. Its inclusion in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham — glorified by Thirumangai Alvar — provides the temple's firmest textual anchor, dating its recognition as a sacred site to the Alvar era (traditionally placed within the 6th-9th centuries CE). The town today is also known for a living craft tradition of bronze and brass lamp-making by resident artisans.
