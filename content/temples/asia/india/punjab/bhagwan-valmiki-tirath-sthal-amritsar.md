Long known as Ram Tirath, this site on the western outskirts of Amritsar is traditionally identified as the ashram of the sage Valmiki, where -- according to the Ramayana -- Sita took shelter during her exile and gave birth to her twin sons, Luv and Kush. No independent archaeological evidence confirms this identification; it is best understood as regional pilgrimage tradition rather than documented history, though the site has drawn Hindu pilgrims under the Ram Tirath name for a long time.

In 2016 the Punjab government redeveloped the site and renamed it Bhagwan Valmiki Tirath Sthal, installing an eight-foot, roughly 800-kilogram gold-plated murti of Valmiki as its central object of worship alongside a large devotee hall, a Sanskrit library, a museum, and expanded parking, all managed by the state-run Valmiki Tirath Development Board. The site today functions primarily as a pilgrimage and cultural center for the Valmiki community, which venerates Valmiki as its guru and founding figure, making it a distinctive example of a Punjab Hindu shrine whose focus of worship has visibly evolved over time even as its older Ramayana-linked legend persists alongside it.
