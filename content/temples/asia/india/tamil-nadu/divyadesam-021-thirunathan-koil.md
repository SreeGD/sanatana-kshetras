The Jaganatha Perumal Temple at Thirunathan Koil, in the Chola country, enshrines the Lord as Jaganatha Perumal, the Lord of the Universe, and is counted among the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alwars. As one of the sacred abodes of Chola Nadu, this shrine takes its place within the inner circle of Vishnu temples hallowed by the Tamil saints and recited in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Its glory was sung by Thirumangai Alwar, the warrior-saint whose far-ranging pilgrimage and abundant pasurams carried the fame of many Chola shrines into the eternal corpus of the four thousand verses. By his hymns Thirunathan Koil is established as a paadal petra sthalam, a place graced by the saint's song and thereby assured of perpetual remembrance in the daily worship of the Srivaishnava community. The Lord's name, Jaganatha, He who is the master and refuge of all the worlds, expresses the supreme sovereignty that the Alwars perceived in the deity of this place, who governs creation yet stoops in grace to dwell among His devotees. According to tradition the very name of the village, Thirunathan Koil, the temple of the holy Lord, preserves the memory of this divine indwelling. Here the faithful approach Jaganatha Perumal as the universal sovereign enshrined in their midst, and, echoing the verses of Thirumangai Alwar, cherish the shrine as one of the celebrated divine abodes of the Tamil land.

## Festivals

Jaganatha Perumal Temple at Thirunathan Koil traditionally keeps the annual Brahmotsavam, observed over several days with flag-hoisting, daily processions of the utsava deity on various mounts, and the Garuda Sevai that is held in special reverence. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is among the foremost festivals, marked by the chanting of the Tiruvaymozhi during the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu and the ceremonial passage of devotees through the Paramapada Vasal. The Float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank and the Pavithrotsavam of purification are also customarily celebrated. The Vaishnava months of Purattasi and Margazhi see heightened worship with special alankarams and devotional singing, and festivals such as Sri Jayanthi and Ramanavami are observed in keeping with longstanding Srivaishnava tradition.

## Location & Access

The temple lies at Thirunathan Koil in the Thanjavur district within the Chola Nadu region of Tamil Nadu, set amid the villages of the fertile Cauvery delta. The area is reached by road from Thanjavur and Kumbakonam, with Thanjavur and the larger city of Tiruchirappalli serving as the nearest principal rail and transport hubs. In common with most Vishnu temples it generally offers darshan during morning and evening hours, closing for a spell in the afternoon.
