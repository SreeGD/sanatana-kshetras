The Marundeeswarar Temple in Tiruvanmiyur, on Chennai's southern coast, is a Paadal Petra Sthalam glorified by both Appar and Thirugnanasambandar in the Tevaram. Shiva is worshipped here as Marundeeswarar, "lord of medicine" — a name devotional tradition links to Shiva having imparted the knowledge of medicinal herbs to the sages at this site — alongside his consort Tripura Sundari. The sanctum houses a swayambu, or self-manifested, lingam. The present masonry structure is dated to the 9th-century Chola period, with inscriptions attesting further patronage under Rajendra Chola in the 11th century and later expansion during the Vijayanagara era.

Behind twin seven-tiered gopurams, the temple's 36-pillar mandapam houses a Somaskanda image of Shiva, Parvati, and their son Skanda together. Now well within Chennai's urban southern suburbs, the temple was freshly consecrated (kumbabhishekam) in February 2020 and remains an active site of daily worship under the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department, with its Panguni Brahmotsavam as the principal annual festival.
