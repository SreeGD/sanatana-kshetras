The Sri Varadaraja Perumal Temple traces its core structure to Chola builders of the 11th century CE, with the Pandyas adding to it in the 12th century — one of the older datable Vaishnava foundations in the Puducherry region, built in Dravidian style with a five-tiered rajagopuram rising about 110 feet over its entrance. Its sanctum holds a five-foot standing granite image of Varadharaja (Vishnu) with four arms, alongside a shrine to his consort Perundevi Thayar (Lakshmi).

Like much of old Pondicherry, the temple was caught up in the colonial-era conflicts that repeatedly changed hands between European powers, and tradition records episodes of destruction and rebuilding around the 17th century, though the source consulted does not give precise dates. A clearer historical marker comes from 1902, when sacred images excavated at Vaithikupam, near Muthialpet, were reinstalled in the temple. The temple's Vasanthamandapa (spring pavilion) is notable as a structure built through joint contributions from the city's Hindu, Muslim, and Christian communities, a detail often cited as emblematic of Puducherry's multi-religious character. Today the temple's major festivals include a ten-day Brahmotsavam, a five-day Pavitrotsavam, and a roughly 25-day festival re-enacting the marriage of Rama and Sita.
