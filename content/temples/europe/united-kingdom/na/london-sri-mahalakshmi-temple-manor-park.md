The London Sri Mahalakshmi Temple was built in 1989 and consecrated the following year, 1990, by the Lakshmi Narayana Trust in East Ham, in the London Borough of Newham — a short distance from the London Sri Murugan Temple in neighboring Manor Park, both part of East London's dense cluster of South Indian Tamil temples. Its Dravidian-style sanctum, with an elevation of roughly 6.85 metres, was completed under the direction of the noted temple architect (sthapati) Padmashri Silpakalamani Muthiah Sthapathi, with further architectural work continuing through 2010.

Dedicated primarily to Mahalakshmi, the goddess of wealth and prosperity, the temple also houses shrines to Srinivasa (a form of Vishnu also known as Venkateswara), Shiva, Ganesha, Murugan, and several of their consort deities, giving it a broad devotional scope typical of diaspora South Indian temples serving a mixed regional community. It has been cited in academic scholarship on Hindu temples in the Western diaspora as an example of how South Indian temple architecture and Agamic ritual practice have been transplanted into ordinary British high-street settings — its High Street North address in East Ham placing a Dravidian gopuram-style sanctum directly amid everyday London commercial premises.
