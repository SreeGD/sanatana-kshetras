Thiru Salagramam is the only one of the 108 Divya Desams located outside India, identified with present-day Muktinath in Nepal's Mustang district, on the upper Kali Gandaki (Gandaki) river — the sole source of the black ammonite fossil stones known as Shaligrams, which are venerated across the subcontinent as a natural, aniconic form of Vishnu. The site's very name, Mukti Kshetra ("field of liberation"), reflects its standing as one of the eight self-manifest (svayambhu) Vishnu shrines and a place where tradition holds a devotee may attain moksha.

Sources differ somewhat on the precise Alvar attribution here: some accounts hold that Thirumangai Alvar, unable to physically reach the high Himalayan site, composed his pasurams in its honour from a nearer vantage point, while other compilations credit Poigai Alvar and Peyalvar with verses on Salagramam, and Periyalvar is said to have addressed the Lord here as "Salagramamudaiya Nambi" (the Lord who owns Salagramam). Because of its remoteness and the Buddhist as well as Hindu reverence attached to the site (known to Buddhists as Chumig Gyatsa, one of the sacred sites of the Dakinis), Muktinath has for centuries been a shared pilgrimage destination, with the temple itself maintained in a simple pagoda style quite distinct architecturally from the Dravidian Divya Desam temples of the south.
