The Ulagalantha Perumal Temple at Thiruooragam in Kanchipuram, Thondai Nadu, enshrines the Lord in His majestic Trivikrama form, here called Ulagalantha Perumal, the One who measured the worlds, with the consort revered as Amudhavalli Thaayar. According to the sthala purana, the temple commemorates the celebrated Vamana avatara, in which the Lord, having come as a dwarf brahmachari to the sacrifice of the asura king Mahabali and received the gift of three paces of land, grew into the cosmic Trivikrama and strode across earth and heaven to reclaim the worlds, setting His foot upon Mahabali's head and granting him grace. The towering moolavar, depicted with one leg raised aloft in the act of measuring the universe, makes this one of the great Trivikrama shrines among the Divya Desams. As one of the 108 sacred abodes sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, Thiruooragam was hymned by Thirumazhisai Alwar and Thirumangai Alwar, whose pasurams extol the Lord who spanned all creation in two strides. Counted among the cluster of Divya Desams within Kanchi, the temple stands as a vivid icon of divine sovereignty and grace, and the form of Ulagalantha Perumal remains a powerful reminder of the Lord's boundless reach and His tender accessibility to the surrendered, drawing pilgrims to behold the very gesture by which the worlds were won back.

## Festivals

This shrine of Ulagalantha Perumal, the Lord who measured the worlds as Trivikrama, observes the Srivaishnava festival cycle of Kanchipuram with great solemnity. An annual Brahmotsavam is traditionally celebrated with flag-hoisting, daily processions of the utsava deity through the temple streets, and the chanting of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is a principal occasion, drawing devotees for darshan of the towering Trivikrama form. Pavithrotsavam for ritual purification, the Float festival (Theppotsavam), and Adhyayana Utsavam with its ten-day recitation cycles are among the observances common to the city's temples. Pongal and other seasonal feasts also see special worship. Throughout, the temple maintains its traditional pattern of daily and periodic rituals in keeping with age-old Srivaishnava practice.

## Location & Access

The temple is located in the Thiruooragam quarter within Kanchipuram, the storied temple-city of the Thondai Nadu region in northern Tamil Nadu, and sits in the heart of the old town near other Divya Desams. Kanchipuram enjoys good road and rail links and is most easily reached from Chennai, the nearest major city and transport hub lying to its northeast. As with most Vishnu temples, darshan is generally available during morning and evening hours with a break at midday.
