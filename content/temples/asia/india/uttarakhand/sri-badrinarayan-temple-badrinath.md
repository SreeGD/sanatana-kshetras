Thiru Vadariashramam, the Divya Desam identified with Badrinath, is named for the badri (wild berry/jujube) grove under which the Bhagavata Purana says Vishnu undertook great austerities in the form of the twin sages Nara and Narayana for the welfare of all beings — giving the site its standing as one of the four Char Dham and among the most sacred of all Vishnu-kshetras. The temple is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham; sources vary on the precise attribution, with some crediting Poigai Alvar and Bhoothathalvar with ten pasurams each and Peyalvar with an additional verse, while others cite roughly eleven hymns by Periyalvar and thirteen by Thirumangai Alvar — in any case a multiple-Alvar sthala reflecting its exceptional standing in Vaishnava geography.

Some traditions hold that the shrine was a site of Buddhist worship before the Advaita philosopher Adi Shankaracharya, traveling through the Himalayas in the 8th century CE, revived it as a Hindu temple and installed the black-stone image of Badrinarayan said to have been recovered from the nearby Narad Kund. The temple's stewardship has since passed through Nambudiri Brahmin priests (Rawals) traditionally drawn from Kerala, a striking link between the South Indian Sri Vaishnava tradition and this Himalayan shrine. Because the site is snowbound for roughly half the year, the deity's continuous worship is maintained during the closed months at the Narasimha Temple in nearby Joshimath, itself also a Divya Desam of this same Vada Nadu group.
