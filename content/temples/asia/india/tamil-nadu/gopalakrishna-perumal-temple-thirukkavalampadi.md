Distinctive among the eleven Thirunangur shrines for its Krishna-centred iconography, this temple, also known as the Rajagopala Swamy Temple, enshrines Vishnu as Gopalakrishnan, worshipped with his consorts in two forms, Rukmini and Satyabhama - a departure from the single-Thayar pattern typical of most Divya Desams, and one that places this shrine's devotional character closer to the pastoral Krishna tradition than to the "Perumal" iconography common elsewhere in the group.

Constructed, like its neighbours, in the Dravidian style, the temple owes its inclusion among the 108 Divya Desams to hymns composed here by Thirumangai Alvar and preserved in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. It shares fully in the collective religious life of the Thirunangur cluster, joining the Thirumangai Alvar Mangalasasana Utsavam each Tamil month of Thai, when its festival deity travels together with those of the other ten Thirunangur temples in the Garudasevai procession mounted on the ornamental Garuda vahanam - the annual festival that ties this and the surrounding ten shrines into a single devotional circuit even as each retains its own separate identity as a Divya Desam.
