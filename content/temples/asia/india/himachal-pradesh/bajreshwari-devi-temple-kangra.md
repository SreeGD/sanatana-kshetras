Bajreshwari Devi Temple sits in Kangra town, seat of the old Kangra kingdom, and is dedicated to the goddess in her fierce, thunderbolt-wielding form as Vajreshwari — her name combining vajra (thunderbolt) with ishwari (goddess/sovereign). The temple complex extends beyond the main sanctum to include several subsidiary shrines, among them one dedicated to Bhairava, and the site is counted among the network of Shakti Peethas where a part of Sati's dismembered body is said to have fallen, in this case her left breast; that identification is consistent between the temple's own Wikipedia account and a separate Wikipedia listing of the Shakti Peethas, giving it somewhat firmer traditional grounding than some of the more disputed Peetha attributions.

Kangra's temples are frequently described in popular and semi-historical writing as having drawn repeated raids across the medieval period on account of their reputed wealth, a narrative often extended to Bajreshwari given its prominence in Kangra town itself; however, the specific Wikipedia source consulted for this entry does not document any such episodes, founding date, or rebuilding history, so this entry does not assert that history as confirmed. What is documented is the temple's continuing active worship today, centered on the twice-yearly Navaratri observances, which remain the principal draw for pilgrims to Kangra town.
