The temple complex at Po Nagar, on a small hill overlooking Nha Trang on Vietnam's south-central coast, was already standing by 781 CE, when a surviving stele records the Cham king Satyavarman restoring it after damage inflicted by seaborne raiders — meaning its true founding predates even that date. It was built by the Hindu-influenced kingdom of Champa in honor of Yan Po Nagar, a goddess of the land who protected the surrounding region of Kauthara; Cham religious tradition identified her with Hindu goddesses such as Bhagavati and, in some tellings, Durga in her demon-slaying form, while the Vietnamese who later inherited the site came to know her as Thien Y Thanh Mau. The tallest of the surviving brick towers, built in the Cham Tra Kieu architectural style and roughly 25 meters high, was completed over a long construction period running from the mid-10th into the 13th century, with a succession of steles across those centuries recording repeated royal donations and repairs.

Po Nagar passed out of Cham control in the 17th century as Vietnamese settlement and conquest extended down the central coast, after which the site took on its present Vietnamese name. Its towers remain physically intact today and the complex is one of the most visited historic sites in Nha Trang, but the sources consulted for this entry did not establish with confidence whether it continues to function as an active site of Hindu or Cham religious worship in the present day, so it is treated here, conservatively, as a heritage and archaeological site pending further verification.
