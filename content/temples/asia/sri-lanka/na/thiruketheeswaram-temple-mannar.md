Thiruketheeswaram, on the outskirts of Mannar town in Sri Lanka's Northern Province, is one of the island's five traditional Pancha Ishwaram Shiva shrines and is counted among the Paadal Petra Sthalams celebrated in the early Tamil Tevaram hymns, placing it among the most historically resonant Shaiva pilgrimage sites in South Asia. Tradition holds the site was already sacred long before the semi-legendary sixth-century-BCE arrival of Prince Vijaya, and devotional literature offers competing origin stories for its name — one crediting the planetary deity Ketu with worshipping Shiva here, another, drawn from the Skanda Purana, describing the wind-god Vayu depositing one of three towers from Mount Meru at this spot. None of these accounts can be independently dated, and the temple's documented history begins much later, in the colonial record.

Portuguese forces destroyed the original temple sometime after 1505, and by 1589 its stones had been repurposed for the construction of Mannar Fort and other colonial buildings, leaving the site derelict for centuries. Its location was rediscovered in 1894, when a lingam was recovered during excavation, and local Tamil Hindus rebuilt the temple in the 1910s following advocacy by the reformer Arumuka Navalar, with further major renovations in 1952 and 1976. Mannar District's long entanglement in Sri Lanka's ethnic conflict has left some disputes over the temple's traditional lands unresolved, and visitors should confirm current local access before planning a trip; the rebuilt sanctum nonetheless functions today as an active center of Shaiva worship, drawing pilgrims especially for Maha Shivaratri.
