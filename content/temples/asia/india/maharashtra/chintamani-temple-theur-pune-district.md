Associated in tradition with the sage Kapila and a wish-fulfilling jewel (the Chintamani) that Ganesha is said to have recovered and returned, giving the site its name. Theur holds particular significance in Maratha history as the place where the Peshwa ruler Madhavrao I, a devout patron of the Ashtavinayak temples, spent his final days and died in 1772; the temple complex retains strong associations with Peshwa-era patronage as a result.

The main shrine is traditionally attributed to Dharanidhar Maharaj Dev, with the outer wooden hall added roughly a century later under Peshwa patronage. The temple faces east, with a north-facing entrance; the idol's trunk curves to the left, its eyes set with carbuncle and diamonds. Behind the temple lies a lake known as Kadambteertha, recalling the site's older name, Kadambanagar, after the Kadamb tree beneath which Ganesha is said to have recovered the Chintamani jewel for the sage Kapila.
