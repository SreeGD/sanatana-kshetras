The temple at Thiruppulingudi, in the Pandya country of the Tamil land, enshrines the Lord under the name Kaaisina Vendhar (Kaaisina Vendhan), a title evoking the splendour of a valorous and majestic king, and it is counted among the 108 Divya Desams, the sacred abodes of Vishnu celebrated by the Alwars. The Moolavar here is venerated in a reclining posture, the Lord stretched in serene repose as the master of all worlds, an aspect that draws pilgrims to behold his grace in this quiet village shrine. Thiruppulingudi belongs to the cluster of intimate Divya Desams gathered in the region around Thirukkurugur, the homeland of Nammalwar, and it shares in the special devotion that the saint poured upon the temples near his birthplace. It is Nammalwar, foremost among the Alwars and the author of the Tiruvaymoli, who sang the glory of this place in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and his hymns secure for Thiruppulingudi its enduring station within that sacred corpus of four thousand verses. Tradition holds that to worship at such shrines hallowed by Nammalwar's song is to draw near to the very heart of his devotional vision, in which the Lord makes himself accessible and approachable to those who seek refuge. As one of the abodes of this Pandya region, the temple preserves the antiquity and sanctity that the Prabandham confers, and devotees revere it as a place where the Lord, reposing in tranquillity, dispenses his protecting grace upon all who come before him in surrender.

## Festivals

As one of the Nava Tirupati shrines along the Tamiraparani, this temple at Thiruppulingudi traditionally shares in the celebrated Nava Tirupati festival cycle, when the nine Vishnu temples of the region are worshipped together. The annual Brahmotsavam is observed with grandeur, featuring processions of the utsava murti, decorated mounts, and the much-loved Garuda Sevai. Vaikunta Ekadasi is kept devoutly, with special darshan and recitation of the Tiruvaymozhi of Nammalvar, whose hymns sanctify these sthalams. Pavithrotsavam for ritual purification and a Theppotsavam (float festival) on the temple tank are also traditionally held. The Tamil month observances, Pongal, and the great Tiruvadhyayana Utsavam with Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitations draw many devotees to this Pandya Nadu shrine.
