Swetharanyeswarar Temple, in the village of Thiruvenkadu near Sirkazhi in Mayiladuthurai district, houses Tamil Nadu's Navagraha shrine for Budha, or Mercury, as a subsidiary within a much older and more broadly significant Shiva temple. The principal deity, Shiva, is worshipped here as Swetharanyeswarar alongside his consort Brahmavidyambigai, and devotional legend recounts that he manifested in his fierce Agoramurthi form at this site to vanquish the demon Maruthuvasuran. The temple's standing as a Paadal Petra Sthalam — one of the 275 sites named in the 7th-century Tevaram hymns of the Nayanar saints — situates its sanctity well before the surviving stone structure, and the village is additionally known as the birthplace of the medieval Shaiva poet-saint Pattinathar.

Inscriptions found at the site record patronage from the Chola emperors Raja Raja Chola I and his son Rajendra Chola I in the late 10th and early 11th centuries, though the masonry visible today was substantially rebuilt during the Vijayanagara period in the 16th century. Spread across roughly two acres behind a seven-tiered gopuram, with three temple tanks within its precincts, Thiruvenkadu is also notable in art-historical terms: the site has yielded exceptional Chola-period bronze sculpture, including an 11th-century Ardhanarishvara image now preserved in the Government Museum, Chennai, evidence of the temple's importance as a center of metal-casting patronage under Chola rule.
