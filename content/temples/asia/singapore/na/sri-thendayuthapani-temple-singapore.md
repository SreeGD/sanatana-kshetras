Sri Thendayuthapani Temple was founded in 1859 by Singapore's Nattukottai Chettiar community, South Indian moneylenders and merchants who settled in the colonial port city and built the temple on Tank Road, where it still stands today. It is dedicated to Murugan in his form as Thendayuthapani ("the one who bears the staff") and is one of the key Chettiar-built temples that anchored the community's religious life in Singapore. The temple was substantially rebuilt in 1983 and further restored around 2022, including reconstruction of its Meenakshi Amman sanctum, and was formally recognized as Singapore's 67th National Monument in October 2014.

The temple is best known today as the focal point of Singapore's Thaipusam celebrations, distinct from but related to the observances centered on Sri Srinivasa Perumal Temple: on the eve of Thaipusam, a deity image is carried in procession from Sri Thendayuthapani Temple to another Chettiar temple and back, and devotees undertake vows of penance along the wider Thaipusam procession route, some carrying ornamented kavadi structures and undergoing body piercing with hooks and spears. This makes it, alongside the city's other historic Hindu temples, one of the central institutions of Singapore's Tamil Hindu community.
