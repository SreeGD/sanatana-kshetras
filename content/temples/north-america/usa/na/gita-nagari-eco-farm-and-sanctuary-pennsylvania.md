Gita Nagari Eco-Farm and Sanctuary is a rural Gaudiya Vaishnava community in central Pennsylvania, established as one of ISKCON's early farm communities in the tradition of self-sufficient, cow-protection-centered rural life advocated by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, alongside better-known sister communities such as New Vrindaban in West Virginia. The community gained wider notice as the place where ISKCON guru Bhakti Tirtha Swami died on 27 June 2005 following complications from melanoma. This entry documents Gita Nagari's confirmed existence and significance; a precise address and founding date could not be verified in this research pass and are flagged for future confirmation.
