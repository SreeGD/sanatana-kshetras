The temple at Sri Vaikuntam enshrines the Lord as Vaikuntanathar, affectionately known as Kallapiran, and ranks among the 108 Divya Desams of the Sri Vaishnava tradition, one of the celebrated cluster of nine Tirunelveli shrines (Nava Tirupathi) along the sacred Tamiraparani river in Pandya Nadu. The Moolavar graces devotees in a standing form, accompanied by His consort Vaikundavalli Thayar, and the very name of the place, Sri Vaikuntam, declares this earthly abode to be a likeness of the supreme heaven of Vishnu. The Lord's endearing title Kallapiran, "the Lord who is the great deceiver" or "the divine thief," recalls the sthala purana in which He is remembered for His playful and protective intervention on behalf of His devotees, stealing away their burdens even as He blesses them. This shrine is sung by Nammalwar, foremost of the Alwars, whose hymns in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham enshrined Sri Vaikuntam within the sacred corpus and exalted Kallapiran as a manifestation of the Lord of the highest realm made accessible upon earth. As one of the Nava Tirupathi worshipped along the Tamiraparani, Sri Vaikuntam holds a special place in the devotional circuit of the Pandya country, where pilgrims revere the river as the divine theertham and adore the Lord by His name that promises Vaikuntha itself. The temple stands as a luminous reminder that the Supreme Abode is not distant but graciously present, drawing the faithful to the feet of Vaikuntanathar.

## Festivals

At Sri Vaikuntam, the temple of Vaikuntanathar (Kallapiran) traditionally observes the principal Srivaishnava festivals. The annual Brahmotsavam, celebrated over several days with daily processions of the Lord on various vahanas, the Garuda Sevai, the great chariot festival and the concluding Theerthavari, is the foremost observance. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is kept with special grandeur, the Nalayira Divya Prabandham being chanted through the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu and the Paramapada Vasal (gateway of liberation) opened for devotees. Pavithrotsavam for the year's ritual purification and the Theppotsavam (float festival) on the temple tank are also traditionally celebrated. Krishna Jayanthi, recalling the Lord's name Kallapiran, along with Andal's Aadi Pooram, Navaratri and Rama Navami, are marked with festive abhishekam, alankaram and special worship.

## Location & Access

The Vaikuntanathar temple lies in the town of Sri Vaikuntam, on the Thamirabarani river in the Thoothukudi (Tuticorin) region of the former Pandya Nadu in southern Tamil Nadu. The city of Thoothukudi and nearby Tirunelveli are the closest major transport hubs, with road and rail links to the town. As with most Vishnu temples, it generally keeps morning and evening darshan hours with a break around midday, extending its hours on festival occasions.
