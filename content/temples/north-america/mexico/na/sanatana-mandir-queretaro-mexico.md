Reported in Indian and international press as Mexico's first dedicated Ram temple, this small mandir in the city of Querétaro was built by and for the local Indian diaspora community through the Indian Association of Querétaro (IAQ), with support from the Indian Embassy in Mexico and the Indian Council for Cultural Relations, which helped arrange for the murtis to be brought from India. Its consecration (pran pratishtha) was deliberately timed to coincide with the much larger consecration of the new Ram Mandir in Ayodhya, India, on 22 January 2024 — a symbolic link that drew notice from Indian outlets covering the Ayodhya event's global echoes among the diaspora.

Modest in scale compared to India's diaspora temples elsewhere in the Americas, the Sanatana Mandir functions primarily as a community and cultural space for Querétaro's Indian residents, hosting poojas, kirtans, and celebrations of Ram Navami, Krishna Janmashtami, and Dussehra, and describing its own mission partly in terms of promoting Vedic learning and the ideal of Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam ("the world is one family") among both the Indian community and the wider Mexican public.
