Consecrated in May 1992 and organized under the Seychelles Hindu Kovil Sangam, the Arulmigu Navasakti Vinayagar Temple in Victoria is described as the first and, to date, only dedicated Hindu temple in Seychelles, serving an island nation whose Hindu community is small relative to its Christian majority but has maintained an organized place of worship since the early 1990s. Ganesha (Vinayagar) has held the position of presiding deity since 1999, with the inner mandapam also housing icons of Murugan, Nataraja, Durga, Sreenivasa Perumal, Bhairawa, and Chandekeswarar, giving the temple a multi-deity character typical of South Indian Tamil kovils that serve a whole diaspora community rather than a single sampradaya.

The temple's best-known annual event is the Taippoosam Kavadi Festival, which began modestly within its inner courtyard in 1993 before growing into a larger outer-courtyard observance with kavadi-bearing processions. Its growth mirrors the temple's own rising visibility on the island: by 1998 the Seychellois government had recognized the festival's importance enough to declare it a public holiday for the country's Hindu community, a rare instance of an African island nation with a very small Hindu minority formally accommodating a Hindu festival calendar.
