Mathura's Krishna Janmasthan Temple Complex, also known as Katra Keshav Dev, marks the site Vaishnava tradition holds as the birthplace of Krishna, the eighth avatara of Vishnu. The Bhagavata Purana recounts that Krishna was born here to Devaki and Vasudeva while they were imprisoned by Devaki's tyrannical brother, King Kamsa, who had been warned that her eighth child would bring about his death.

Archaeological evidence points to religious activity at this site from at least the 6th century BCE, and a major temple was rebuilt here around 400 CE under the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II. Like many prominent temples in the region, the site suffered repeated destruction under later rulers; most significantly, the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb's forces destroyed the standing Keshavdeva temple in 1670 and built the Shahi Eidgah mosque on part of the grounds — a mosque that still stands immediately adjacent to the temple complex today.

The present temple complex took shape gradually across the 20th century, with construction running from 1953 to 1982 and funded substantially by industrialist families including the Dalmias and the Birlas; it is managed today by the Shri Krishna Janmasthan Seva Sansthan trust and houses shrines including the Keshavdeva Temple and the Bhagavata Bhavan, dedicated to Radha Krishna. The complex's shared boundary with the neighboring Shahi Eidgah mosque remains the subject of active, unresolved litigation in the Mathura courts over a 1968 agreement governing the site — a dispute that has drawn national attention in recent years even as pilgrimage to the site continues, peaking each year around Krishna Janmashtami, when devotees gather in especially large numbers through the night to mark Krishna's birth.
