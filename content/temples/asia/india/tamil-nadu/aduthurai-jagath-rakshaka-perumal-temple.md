The Jagath Rakshaka Perumal Temple, known in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham as Thirukoodalur, stands at Vadakurangaduthurai on the outskirts of Kumbakonam. The presiding deity, Jagath Rakshaka Perumal ("protector of the world," also known locally as Vaiyam Kaatha Perumal), is worshipped alongside his consort, named in different sources as Pushpavalli or Padmasini Thayar.

The temple's legendary associations are unusually layered. One tradition holds that a gap in the sanctum floor marks the point where Vishnu, as the boar-avatar Varaha, rescued the earth-goddess Bhudevi from the demon Hiranyaksha. Another credits the temple's founding to King Ambarisha, who was cursed by the short-tempered sage Durvasa but defended by Vishnu's discus, the Sudarshana chakra, which pursued Durvasa until he submitted — a story recognizable from the wider Bhagavata Purana tradition, here localized to this site. A further tradition holds that the river Kaveri herself came to this spot to pray for release from the accumulated sins of her devotees, and the place name "Thirukoodalur" (from koodal, "gathering" or "meeting") is traditionally explained as the site where the gods gathered to seek Vishnu's aid.

On firmer historical ground, the temple is documented to have been restored by Rani Mangammal, a Madurai Nayak-era queen who reigned in the late 17th century, after flood damage; she commissioned decorative pillars bearing her own likeness, and the temple's Ambarisha Ratha chariot, renovated under her patronage, remained in ceremonial use until the 1940s. Broader claims of earlier Chola-era construction are more generic and less independently verified. The temple is glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by Thirumangai Alvar, and its principal annual festival, the Brahmotsavam, is held each year in the Tamil month of Vaikasi.
