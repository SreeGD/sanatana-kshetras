ISKCON New Gaya is one of two centers of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON) — the Gaudiya Vaishnava movement founded in the West by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada — documented in Japan, the other being ISKCON's Osaka Center. It serves Tokyo's Hindu community, which per recent estimates includes a substantial population of Nepali, Indian, and Sri Lankan residents and migrants.

Japan's wider Hindu religious landscape also includes a Shirdi Saibaba Temple and a Shiva Shakti Mandir, both in Tokyo, a BAPS Shri Swaminarayan Mandir in Tokyo, and the Vedanta Society of Japan (representing the Ramakrishna Mission tradition) based in Kanagawa — together reflecting a diverse, if largely diaspora-serving, Hindu presence across the country. Specific founding dates, addresses, and institutional histories for these Japanese temples, including ISKCON New Gaya itself, are thinly documented in English-language sources and would benefit from independent, on-the-ground verification.
