The source-seat of the goddess Yamuna and, with Gangotri, one of the two Char Dham sites reached only after a Himalayan trek (the true glacial source lies still higher, at about 4,421 m, and is not accessible, so worship takes place at the lower temple instead). The present temple was built in 1839 under Raja Sudarshan Shah of the Tehri princely state (some sources instead credit a later Tehri ruler, Pratap Shah, flagged here since accounts differ), replacing an earlier, smaller shrine at the site. The temple's black-marble image of Yamuna sits beside the Surya Kund hot spring, where pilgrims traditionally cook rice and potatoes in the naturally boiling water as a blessed offering to carry home.
