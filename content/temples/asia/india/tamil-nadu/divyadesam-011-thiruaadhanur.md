Thiruaadhanur, situated in the fertile Chola Nadu amid the shrines of the Kaveri basin, is one of the 108 Divya Desams celebrated by the Alwars of Sri Vaishnavism. The presiding deity (Moolavar) is worshipped here as Aandalakkum Ayyan, a name expressing the Lord as the master who 'rules and measures'—the sovereign who governs and protects his devotees with sovereign grace. As with the great Trivikrama tradition, the epithet evokes the Lord who measured the worlds and who reigns over all beings, here gracing the small but holy settlement of Aadhanur. The temple stands among the closely-clustered Divya Desams of the Kumbakonam region, where the Lord is enshrined in serene majesty for the worship of pilgrims who traverse the sacred delta. Of the Alwar saints, it was Thirumangai Alwar, the soldier-poet renowned for the vast number of Divya Desams he visited and sang, who immortalised this shrine in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, the four-thousand-verse corpus that forms the scriptural heart of the tradition. By his hymns Thiruaadhanur is enrolled among the eternal abodes of the Lord, deserving of the same reverence as the most renowned temples. According to tradition the Mother Lakshmi too is worshipped here beside the Lord, and devotees seek at this shrine the bestowal of prosperity, protection and ultimate refuge. Pilgrims approach Aandalakkum Ayyan with the prayer that the Lord who measures and rules all things may also measure out his boundless compassion upon them.

## Festivals

Thiruaadhanur traditionally follows the familiar Srivaishnava festival cycle, with an annual Brahmotsavam as its principal celebration, observed over several days of processions and the chanting of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham before the Lord (Aandalakkum Ayyan) and his consort. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is kept with particular devotion, drawing pilgrims for darshan of the utsava murti. The temple also customarily observes Pavithrotsavam for ritual purification and honours the seasonal Tamil-month festivals shared across the Kumbakonam-area Divya Desams. Where little place-specific lore survives, the observances follow the general Vaishnava pattern of monthly Ekadasi worship and the great annual festival, presented here as long-standing tradition rather than fixed dated events.

## Location & Access

The temple lies at Thiruaadhanur, a village in the Chola Nadu region very close to Kumbakonam in the Thanjavur district of Tamil Nadu, with Kumbakonam serving as the nearest town and principal transport hub. As with most Vishnu temples it generally keeps morning and evening darshan hours and closes during the midday interval. Pilgrims commonly reach it by road from Kumbakonam, which has rail and bus links to Thanjavur and beyond. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
