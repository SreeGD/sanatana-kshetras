Built by Rani Rashmoni, a wealthy widow of the Mahishya caste who, defying the era's caste conventions, financed and dedicated one of Bengal's most important Kali temples; construction ran from 1847 to 1855, with the deity's image ceremonially installed on Snana Yatra day, 31 May 1855. It is closely associated with the 19th-century mystic Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, whose brother Ramkumar served as the temple's first head priest before Ramakrishna succeeded him in 1856; Ramakrishna's own tenure here, and his teachings transmitted through his disciple Swami Vivekananda, carried the temple's influence worldwide.
