Thiru Vadamathurai is the Divya Desam associated with Mathura, revered in the Vaishnava tradition as the birthplace of Krishna and one of the sapta puri (seven cities bestowing liberation), alongside Ayodhya, Kashi, Dwaraka and others. Seven Alvars are credited across the Naalayira Divya Prabandham with a combined 56 pasurams glorifying this sthala, celebrating Krishna's birth in the prison of the tyrant king Kamsa and his destined role in restoring dharma; the presiding deity is worshipped here as Govardhana Nesa Perumal (also styled Govardhanagiridhari), recalling Krishna's lifting of Mount Govardhan to shelter the cowherds of Vraja from Indra's storm, with Sathyabhama as the attendant Thayar.

The site's temple history is one of repeated destruction and rebuilding: temples marking Krishna's birthplace here are recorded as having been demolished during the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal periods, notably under Aurangzeb in the 17th century, with a mosque later constructed adjoining the site. The present Krishna Janmasthan Temple Complex, including the Keshavdev temple and the Garbha Griha shrine marking the traditional spot of Krishna's birth, was substantially rebuilt in the 20th century and remains one of the most visited Krishna pilgrimage sites in India, especially during Janmashtami.
