The temple of Purushothaman at Thirukkarambanur, popularly known as Uttamar Koil, in the Chola country near the confluence of the Kaveri, is reckoned among the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alwars. The presiding deity, the Moolavar, is worshipped as Purushothama, the Supreme Person, a title of Vishnu signifying the highest among beings; from this exalted name the shrine derives its familiar appellation of Uttamar Koil, the temple of the foremost one. The site is distinguished in tradition for sheltering within its precincts the shrines of the divine consorts and the trinity, and it is cherished by devotees who hold that worship here bestows the grace of the Lord together with his Mother Lakshmi. The temple takes its place in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham through the hymns of Thirumangai Alwar, who sang the glory of so many abodes of the Lord across the Chola land and by whose verses Thirukkarambanur is sanctified among the Divya Desams. As one of the cluster of sacred temples surrounding Srirangam, Uttamar Koil forms part of the holy circuit of the Kaveri delta, and pilgrims revere it as an abode where the Supreme Person grants his presence to those who seek refuge at his feet.

## Festivals

Thirukkarambanur, known as Uttamar Koil, is unusual among the Divya Desams in housing shrines for Vishnu (Purushothaman), Brahma, Shiva and their consorts, and its festivals reflect this rare confluence. The annual Brahmotsavam for the Lord, with processions on various vahanas, is traditionally observed, as is the Vaikunta Ekadasi season with recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham. The presence of the shrine to the consort Poornavalli Thayar lends importance to the goddess-centred observances. Pavithrotsavam and the seasonal and monthly festivals common to Perumal temples are kept here, drawing devotees who value the temple's distinctive gathering of the principal deities under one roof.
