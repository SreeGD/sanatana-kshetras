The Samaleswari Temple, on the bank of the Mahanadi river in Sambalpur, is western Odisha's most important Shakti shrine, built in the 16th century under King Balaram Dev in the region historically known as Hirakhanda. Its Kalinga-style sandhara design — a square sanctum ringed by a twelve-pillared covered gallery about ten feet wide — houses the goddess Samaleswari, known locally as Samalei Maa, carved from a single block of granite and adorned with gold ornaments; she is worshipped as an all-encompassing mother goddess identified with Durga, Mahalakshmi, Mahasaraswati, and Adishakti alike. Regional tradition holds that, after Jagannath of Puri, Samaleswari is the only Odishan deity whose devotional reach extends across an entire cultural region — western Odisha and into neighboring parts of Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh — rather than being tied to a single locality.

The temple's ritual calendar peaks not only at the two Navaratris (Chaitra in spring, and Ashwin in autumn, celebrated with particular splendor) but at Nuakhai, the signature agrarian festival of western Odisha, when farmers ceremonially offer the season's first harvest to the goddess before eating any of the new crop themselves — a practice that ties Samaleswari as closely to the region's agricultural life as to its devotional life.
