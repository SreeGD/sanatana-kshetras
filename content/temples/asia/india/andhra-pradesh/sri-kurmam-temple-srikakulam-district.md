Sri Kurmam, a village about 14.5 km southeast of Srikakulam town in Andhra Pradesh's Gara mandal, is home to one of the very few temples in India dedicated to the Kurma avatar — Vishnu's incarnation as a tortoise, best known from the Puranic account of the churning of the ocean (Samudra Manthan), in which he bore the mountain Mandara on his back. The site's traditional name, Kurmakshetra ("the field of the tortoise"), reflects this identity, and the temple's inscriptional record — spanning roughly the 11th to the 19th centuries CE — makes it one of the better epigraphically documented sites associated with any avatar-specific Vishnu shrine.

The clearest point in that record is a dated inscription from 1281 CE, which states that Kurmakshetra was restored under the influence of the Eastern Ganga king Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva, with the restoration itself credited to the Vaishnava philosopher-saint Ramanujacharya. A later Eastern Ganga ruler, Anangabhima Deva, is credited with building the temple's pradakshina mandapa (circumambulatory hall), and the temple subsequently passed under the jurisdiction of the Suryavanshi Gajapati kings of Odisha — placing Sri Kurmam within the same broad Eastern Ganga/Gajapati patronage network responsible for major temples such as Puri's Jagannath Temple and Konark's Sun Temple, though this entry does not assert a direct architectural connection beyond shared royal patrons. What the 1281 CE inscription documents is a restoration, not the temple's original founding; no independently confirmed date for an earlier, original shrine at this site was located for this entry, and the site's deeper association with Vishnu's Kurma avatar rests on Puranic tradition rather than dated history.

Today, Sri Kurmam functions as an active pilgrimage site, and its Dolotsavam festival is by far its best-attested modern draw, reported to bring more than 20,000 pilgrims to the temple. Alongside far better-known Vishnu avatar sites — Rama and Krishna temples above all — dedicated Kurma-avatar shrines like this one are genuinely rare, which is a central part of what makes Sri Kurmam a distinctive stop within Andhra Pradesh's wider network of Vaishnava pilgrimage centres.
