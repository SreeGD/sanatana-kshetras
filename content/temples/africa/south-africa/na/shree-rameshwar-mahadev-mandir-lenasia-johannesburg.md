Lenasia's Hindu community, together with an organisation called the Divine Life Society, formed the organisation that became the Shree Rameshwar Mahadev Mandir in 1965, per the temple's own history page; land for the temple was acquired in 1974 and an initial temporary structure built, before a grand official opening ceremony — drawing participants "from all over the world" — took place in 1988. Shree Chotubhai Govan oversaw construction of the main building; later chairmen have included Shree N.G. Patel (from 2004) and Shree Chotoobhai Daya (from 2016).

The temple is dedicated to Shiva under the Rameshwar Mahadev name — the same epithet associated with the Rameshwaram Jyotirlinga in Tamil Nadu, reflecting the South Indian Tamil heritage of much of the area's Hindu population. Lenasia was established southwest of central Johannesburg under apartheid-era group areas legislation as a designated area for Indian residents, and its temples, including this one, have since served as anchors of religious and community life for Johannesburg's Indian-origin Hindus. Standing today at the corner of Rose Avenue and Willow Street in Extension 3, the temple continues to host regular worship, festivals, and charitable activities for the local community.
