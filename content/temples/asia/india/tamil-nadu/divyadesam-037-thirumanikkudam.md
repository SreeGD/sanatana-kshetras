The Varadaraja Perumal Temple at Thirumanikkudam, in the Chola Nadu region, is revered as one of the 108 Divya Desams, the sacred abodes of Sri Maha Vishnu celebrated by the Alwar saints in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The presiding deity (Moolavar) is Varadaraja Perumal, the Lord who graciously bestows boons upon those who seek refuge at His feet, the very name Varada signifying "the giver of blessings." The place-name Thirumanikkudam, evoking a vessel or assembly of precious gems, points in tradition to the luminous sanctity attributed to this shrine. The glory of this Divya Desam was sung by Thirumangai Alwar, the great prince among the Alwars renowned for the wide compass of his pilgrimage and the depth of his devotional verse; through his hymns the temple was woven into the sacred geography of the Prabandham and assured its place among the divine abodes worshipped by the Srivaishnava tradition. As with the cluster of shrines that adorn the fertile Cauvery delta of the Chola country, this temple stands as a place where the Lord is held to remain ever-accessible to His devotees, dispensing grace to all who approach. Pilgrims who undertake the circuit of the Divya Desams come here to behold Varadaraja Perumal and to recall the verses through which Thirumangai Alwar enshrined this holy place in the memory of the faithful, honouring it as a perpetual seat of the Lord's saving compassion.

## Festivals

As one of the eleven Nangur Tirupathis, this temple traditionally shares in the celebrated Garuda Sevai, when the processional deities of the Nangur Divya Desams are borne together on their Garuda mounts in a grand collective procession that draws large gatherings of devotees. An annual Brahmotsavam is traditionally observed with festal processions of the utsava murti through the surrounding streets. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the Tamil month of Margazhi is kept with special devotion, along with the recitation of the Tiruvaymozhi and Nalayira Divya Prabandham. Pavithrotsavam, observed for the ritual purification of the year's worship, and a Float festival (Theppotsavam) are traditionally part of the temple's sacred calendar, together with the customary monthly and seasonal Srivaishnava observances.

## Location & Access

The temple stands at Thirumanikkudam in the cluster of Nangur Divya Desams within the Chola Nadu region, in the area of Nagapattinam district in Tamil Nadu, reached via the town of Sirkazhi, which is the nearest railway and road hub. Nagapattinam and Chidambaram lie within convenient reach for onward travel, and Tiruchirappalli is the broadly nearest major city with wider connections. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday interval. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
