A 19th-century neo-Gothic château in the Belgian Ardennes, properly named Château de Petite Somme, acquired by ISKCON and developed into one of continental Europe's principal Gaudiya Vaishnava communities and a base for the Bhaktivedanta College seminary, Radhadesh has served as a hub for Hare Krishna practice, education, and public festivals in Belgium and neighboring countries since its establishment in 1980. Wikipedia's biography of devotee Kadamba Kanana Swami notes that the community "was started mostly by devotees from the Netherlands," reflecting close ties between the Belgian and Dutch ISKCON communities.

The 47-hectare property draws an estimated 50,000 visitors a year and today combines the château's Krishna temple with a Museum of Sacred Art, an Indian craft boutique, a vegetarian restaurant and bakery, a retreat center, and a goshala (cow protection sanctuary). It is open to visitors daily during the high season (15 June-15 September) and on most days outside it, making it one of the more publicly accessible ISKCON rural communities in Europe.
