Neervanna Perumal at Thiruneermalai, in the Thondai Nadu region near Chennai, is venerated as one of the 108 Divya Desams, the sacred abodes of Vishnu hymned by the Alwars. The presiding deity (Moolavar), Neervanna Perumal, takes his name from his complexion likened to water (neer), and the temple is distinctive for being spread across the foot of a small hill and its summit, so that the Lord is worshipped here in the standing, sitting, and reclining postures within the same kshetra. According to the sthala purana, the place was once surrounded by water, from which it draws its name Thiruneermalai, the sacred hill of waters. Tradition associates the kshetra with Sri Rama, who is said to have graced this hill, and the Lord here is also revered in the form of Ranganatha in repose. The Divya Desam was sung by two of the early Mudhal Alwars and Mangai saint of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham: Bhoothath Alwar, one of the three foremost Mudhal Alwars, and Thirumangai Alwar, the prolific warrior-poet whose verses enrich the glory of many Thondai Nadu shrines. Their pasurams secure Thiruneermalai's place within the four-thousand-verse corpus and mark it as a hallowed site of pilgrimage. Devotees ascend the hill to behold the Lord in his several forms, regarding the climb itself as an act of devotion and the darshan at the summit as the crown of the visit.

## Festivals

At Thiruneermalai, the year is traditionally marked by the annual Brahmotsavam, observed over several days with the customary processions of the utsava murti, vahana sevai and temple car festival, drawing devotees up to the hill shrine of Neervanna Perumal. Vaikunta Ekadasi is kept with great reverence, when devotees seek darshan through the Paramapada Vasal during the sacred Margazhi season, alongside the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitation of the Tiruvaymoli. Other Srivaishnava observances traditionally followed here include Pavithrotsavam for the ritual purification of the temple, the Float festival (Theppotsavam) of the Lord and Consort, and the recitation festivals honouring the Alvars. The cycle of Sri Jayanthi, Sri Rama Navami and Navaratri is also marked in keeping with wider Vaishnava custom.

## Location & Access

The temple stands at Thiruneermalai, a hill shrine in the southern outskirts of the Chennai metropolitan area within the historic Thondai Nadu region, administratively linked to the Kanchipuram country. It lies a convenient road journey from Chennai, the nearest major city and transport hub, reachable via the Pallavaram and Chromepet localities. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday interval.
