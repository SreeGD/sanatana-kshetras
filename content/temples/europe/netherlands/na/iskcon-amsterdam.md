ISKCON has maintained a presence in the Netherlands since the movement's early expansion into Europe in the 1970s, with centers historically recorded in Amsterdam and eight other Dutch cities, and Henk Keilman serving as chairman of the Dutch ISKCON Foundation in the early 1980s. Rather than concentrating around one single flagship temple, the Dutch Hare Krishna community has long been closely intertwined with Belgium's Radhadesh community at Château de Petit-Somme, which Dutch devotees played a leading role in establishing; Amsterdam's own most visible yearly public expression is a Harinam kirtan procession through the city center on King's Day (Koningsdag).

A Hare Krishna temple operating under the ISKCON banner is recorded at Lizzy Ansinghstraat 80 in Amsterdam, though independent sourcing on this specific address's founding date and institutional history is thin. As of this writing, the community's current organizational website is running an active fundraising campaign for a new, purpose-built Amsterdam temple, which suggests the Dutch ISKCON presence today may be smaller or more provisional in its physical footprint than the well-established rural temple-and-farm communities found in neighboring countries such as Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, and Spain. This entry should be treated as provisional pending direct confirmation from the temple.
