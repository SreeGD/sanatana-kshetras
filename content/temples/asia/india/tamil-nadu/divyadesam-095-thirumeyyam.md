The Sathyamoorthy Perumal Temple at Thirumeyyam (also spelled Thirumayam), in the Pandya Nadu region near Pudukkottai, enshrines the presiding Lord as Sathyamoorthy (Sathyamurthi Perumal), with his consort worshipped as Ujeevana Thayar, and stands among the 108 Divya Desams celebrated in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The shrine is renowned for its setting amid a great rock, and the Lord is worshipped here in a majestic reclining posture upon the serpent Adisesha, an Anantasayi image reported to be the largest rock-cut sculpture of its kind in India, alongside a standing image of Sathyamurthi Perumal roughly 7 feet tall; the place name itself, associated with truth (satya/mey), reflects the tradition that this is the abode of the Lord who is Truth embodied. Scholarship places the rock-cut sanctuary's construction in the 9th century CE, with the art historian M.A. Dhaky dating it specifically to the seventh decade of that century; an inscription credits Perumbidugu Perumdevi, mother of Sattan Maran, a vassal of the Pallava king Nandivarman II (r. 731-796 CE), with contributions to the shrine. Carved in Dravidian and rock-cut style with a five-tiered rajagopuram within a granite-walled complex, the temple stands immediately beside the separate Sathyagiriswara Shiva shrine, making this a joint Vaishnava-Shaiva rock-cut site, and near a later 15th-century, 40-acre fort. Worship here follows the Tenkalai tradition according to Vaikhanasa Agama practice. The temple's glory was hymned by Thirumangai Alwar, whose verses in the Divya Prabandham include this shrine among the sacred abodes of the Pandya country, securing its place in the canon sung by the Alwars. Unusually for a still-worshipped Divya Desam, the temple is maintained and administered by the Archaeological Survey of India as a ticketed monument rather than by the state's HR&CE department.

## Festivals

At the rock-cut shrine of Sathyamoorthy Perumal at Thirumeyyam, the car festival held in the Tamil month of Vaikasi (April-May) is the principal annual celebration. Krishna Jayanti is observed in Avani (August-September), and Adi Puram is kept in Aadi (July-August). The temple follows four daily rituals, with additional weekly, monthly and fortnightly ceremonies. The cave temple, with its reclining form of the Lord and its ancient inscriptions, draws devotees during these festival days for special abhishekams and alankarams.

## Location & Access

The temple is located at Thirumeyyam (Thirumayam) in Pudukkottai district of Tamil Nadu, within the Pandya Nadu region, about 15 km from the Madurai-Pudukkottai road and beside the 15th-century Thirumayam Fort. Pudukkottai is the nearest town with rail and road connections, while Tiruchirappalli and Madurai are the larger cities and transport hubs serving the area. As a monument administered by the Archaeological Survey of India, entry requires a ticket. Verify current darshan/visiting hours and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
