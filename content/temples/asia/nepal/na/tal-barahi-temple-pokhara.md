Tal Barahi Temple occupies a small island in the middle of Phewa Lake in Pokhara, Nepal's second-largest city, and is widely described as the most important religious monument in the entire Pokhara valley. Reached only by a short boat crossing, the two-story stone temple with its thatched pagoda roof is dedicated to Barahi (Varahi), worshipped here as a form of the goddess linked to the locally significant female divine force known as Ajima, and it is classified among Nepal's Shakta pithas.

No firmly documented founding date survives for the shrine, but it remains a living center of Shakta worship at the heart of one of Nepal's most-visited cities, with devotees converging especially during the Nepali months of Baisakh and Kartik and for the autumn festival of Dashain — a striking contrast to Pokhara's modern identity as a lakeside tourism hub, and a reminder that the city's sacred geography predates its tourist one.
