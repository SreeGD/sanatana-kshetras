This Thirunangur shrine enshrines Vishnu in a recumbent, four-armed form facing east, worshipped as Pallikonda Perumal ("the Lord reclining") together with his consort, known here as Bhujangavalli or, in some traditions, Sengamalavalli. As with the neighbouring Thirumanikoodam temple, local legend attributes the digging of the temple tank to Arjuna of the Mahabharata and recalls Krishna's appearance here as Parthasarathy, the charioteer-teacher - a story shared, with local variations, across several of the Thirunangur shrines and reflecting how closely interwoven this cluster's foundation legends are.

Two Alvars are credited with hymns to this temple in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham: Poigai Alvar, one of the earliest of the twelve Alvars, and Thirumangai Alvar, whose extensive circuit of the Chola Nadu shrines gave rise to the Mangalasasana Utsavam still celebrated here every Tamil month of Thai. During that festival, the temple's festival deity joins the Garudasevai procession alongside the other ten Thirunangur Perumals, a shared celebration that marks this shrine as one of the eleven that together constitute the Thirunangur Divya Desams.
