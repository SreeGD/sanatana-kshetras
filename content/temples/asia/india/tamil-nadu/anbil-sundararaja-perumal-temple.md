The Sundararaja Perumal Temple at Anbil, on the north bank of the Kollidam river in Lalgudi taluk, enshrines Vishnu as Sundararaja Perumal, whose festival name Vadivazhagiya Nambi ("the handsome lord") is also commonly used for the temple itself. It is one of seven Divya Desams named together in a well-known verse of Thirumalisai Alvar celebrating shrines where Vishnu reclines in the Anantasayana posture — the others being Thirukkudandai (Kumbakonam), Tiruvekkaa and Thiruevvul near modern Chennai, Srirangam, Thirupper Nagar, and Tirupparkadal (the Milky Ocean itself). This places Anbil within a small, celebrated group of "reclining Vishnu" shrines glorified together in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham.

Anbil's documented history is tied less to an elaborate sthala purana — the temple's own origin legend is only lightly attested, briefly describing Brahma and the sage Valmiki discussing Vishnu's reclining beauty as the occasion for a divine manifestation here — and more to the village's standing as a genuine center of Chola-era administration and learning. Anbil was the ancestral village of Aniruddha Brahmarayar, a historically documented Vaishnava Brahmin minister who served the Chola king Parantaka II with the honorific title "Brahmadhiraja." The Anbil copper-plate inscriptions of Sundara Chola, among the most valuable surviving records of Chola royal genealogy, take their name from this village. While these records establish Anbil's importance under the Cholas, they document the village and its administrative elite rather than the temple's construction specifically, which is conventionally, though not conclusively, dated to the late 8th century CE with later Vijayanagara and Madurai Nayak-period additions.

Today the temple's principal living festival is Theerthavari in the Tamil month of Maasi, observed alongside the routine cycle of six daily rituals characteristic of Divya Desam worship.
