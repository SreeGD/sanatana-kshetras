The Maha Ganapati Temple of Arizona began in 1999 when Satguru Sivaya Subramuniyaswami of Kauai Aadheenam (Hawaii) donated a 1,400-pound, four-foot Ganesha statue to a founding group of devotees; the organization was formally established as a non-profit in November 2000. In 2002 the Gunnela family donated 15 acres of land near Maricopa along with a trailer to serve as a temporary shrine, and a grand opening drew roughly 500 attendees. Construction of the permanent temple broke ground in August 2007, and its first phase — an 8,000-square-foot structure with four towers, the tallest rising 26 feet, decorated with Indian sculptural work inside and out — was completed and consecrated in May 2008, with further murti installations in 2011 and tower sculpture work finished in 2014.

Centered on Ganesha, the temple serves Hindu families across the Phoenix metropolitan area and is reachable within about two hours from Tucson. It is catalogued in Harvard University's Pluralism Project directory of American religious organizations and offers daily pujas alongside special observances for major Hindu holidays.
