Barsana, twin pilgrimage town to Nandgaon in the Braj region of Mathura district, is venerated in Vaishnava tradition as the birthplace and childhood home of Radha, Krishna's chief consort. Its principal shrine, the Radha Rani Temple (also called the Shri Ji Temple), sits atop the Bhanugarh hill and is reached by a climb of more than 200 stone steps; its Rajput-period architecture combines red and white sandstone, popularly read as symbolizing the union of Radha and Krishna, whose images are worshipped jointly here as "Shri Laadli Lal" — the beloved daughter and son of the town.

Devotional tradition traces the site's founding to King Vajranabh, Krishna's great-grandson, some five thousand years ago — a Puranic dating rather than a historically documented one. Its more concretely dated history begins with the 16th-century Vaishnava scholar Narayan Bhatt, a follower in Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's devotional lineage, who is credited with rediscovering the temple's deity icons; the present structure was then built in 1675 CE under the patronage of Raja Bir Singh Deo, with involvement from Raja Todarmal, a governor under the Mughal emperor Akbar. Barsana is best known today for Lath Mar Holi, held in the week before Holi and running into Rang Panchami, in which women of the town ceremonially strike visiting men with sticks (laths) in a playful re-enactment tied to Radha and Krishna's courtship; Radhashtami, Radha's birthday, is the temple's other major annual festival. Together with Mathura, Vrindavan, Govardhan, Nandgaon, Kurukshetra, and Dwarka, Barsana forms one of the core stops of the Krishna/Braj pilgrimage circuit.
