About 15 km north of Tiruchirappalli, on the highway toward Chennai, the village of Samayapuram is home to one of Tamil Nadu's most visited and most richly endowed goddess temples: the shrine of Samayapuram Mariamman, worshipped as a form of Adi Parashakti and revered particularly for protection from disease, especially smallpox and other epidemic afflictions traditionally associated with Mariamman across Tamil Nadu and neighboring states. Unusual among major Tamil temple deities, the image of the goddess here is not carved stone but is fashioned from sand and clay blended with medicinal herbs, renewed periodically according to temple tradition.

Local legend connects the deity's origin to the great Ranganathaswamy temple at nearby Srirangam, from which the goddess is said to have been relocated to Samayapuram, where devotees subsequently built her a dedicated shrine. The temple in its present form is attributed to early 18th-century royal patronage, a period during which many of the smaller Shakti shrines of the Kaveri delta region received significant architectural investment and expansion.

Samayapuram draws enormous crowds, particularly on Tuesdays, Fridays, and Sundays, the days traditionally held most auspicious for Mariamman worship, and its annual Chithirai car festival in April is among the most heavily attended temple car festivals in the Tiruchirappalli region. Frequently cited as the second-wealthiest temple in Tamil Nadu by cash offerings, after only the Palani Dhandayuthapani temple, Samayapuram Mariamman occupies a place in Tamil devotional life that is distinct from — though geographically close to — the grand Vaishnava temple complex of Srirangam, representing the vigorous, votive-offering-centered folk-Shakta worship tradition that runs alongside Tamil Nadu's better-documented Agamic temple architecture.
