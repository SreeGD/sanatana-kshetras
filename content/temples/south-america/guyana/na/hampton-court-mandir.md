Hampton Court Mandir is named, without further institutional detail, among a short list of Guyanese temples in Wikipedia's "Hinduism in Guyana" article. Hampton Court itself is a real village on the Essequibo Coast in Pomeroon-Supenaam Region (Region 2) — a former sugar-plantation estate that closed in 1934 and was later redeveloped as a rice-farming community, a pattern common to many Essequibo Coast villages settled by the descendants of Indian indentured laborers.

Like most rural Guyanese mandirs, it likely serves as a Sanatan Dharma-affiliated congregational temple for its local Indo-Guyanese Hindu residents, consistent with the wider pattern described in this directory's existing Guyana entry. However, no founding date, architecture, or presiding deity could be independently confirmed for this specific temple this pass, and it should not be assumed to share any history with the separately-documented Shri Krishna Mandir in Georgetown or the Edinburgh Shree Krishna Mandir named in the same source list.
