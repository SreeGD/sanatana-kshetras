Described as the first temple in the United States dedicated specifically to the deity Murugan, the Murugan Temple of North America was conceived in the 1980s by Tamil Saivite devotees in the Washington, D.C. area and opened in 1999 in Lanham, Maryland — a separate institution from the larger Sri Siva Vishnu Temple located nearby in the same town. Its main shrine enshrines Murugan flanked by his consorts Valli and Devasena, with four subsidiary shrines to Ganesha, Shiva, Meenakshi, Durga and Palani Aandavar (a form of Murugan associated with the Palani hill temple in Tamil Nadu), reflecting its identity as a traditional Saivite temple. The temple draws devotees from Maryland, Washington D.C. and Virginia, and as far as Canada, for its full calendar of Hindu and Tamil festivals. It underwent a second consecration ceremony (Kumbabhishegam) on May 13, 2011, and consecrated a new processional temple chariot (Ratham) on August 7-8, 2009.
