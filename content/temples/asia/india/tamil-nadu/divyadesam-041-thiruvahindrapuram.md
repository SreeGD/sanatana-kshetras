The Devanatha Perumal Temple at Thiruvahindrapuram, in the central Nadu Nadu region, is revered as one of the 108 Divya Desams, the sacred abodes of Vishnu hymned by the Alwars. The presiding deity, Devanatha Perumal, the Lord of the celestials, stands as the gracious Moolavar of this kshetra, and the place is held in great esteem within the Srivaishnava tradition. According to tradition, this hallowed site rests near the slopes associated with the serpent-realm, and the very name Thiruvahindrapuram carries the sense of a place sanctified by the Lord who is sovereign over the divine beings. The glory of this Divya Desam was sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham by Thirumangai Alwar, whose far-ranging hymns gathered the sacred abodes of Vishnu into the enduring corpus of Tamil devotion, and through his verses Thiruvahindrapuram took its honoured place among the celebrated Divya Desams. Documented history traces the temple's current form to the medieval Chola period, with later expansion under the Pandyas, the Hoysala Empire, and the Vijayanagara Empire; more than fifty surviving inscriptions name patrons including Kulothunga Chola I, Vikrama Chola, Rajaraja Chola III, the Pandya king Jatavarman Sundara Pandyan, and the Vijayanagara emperor Achyuta Deva Raya. The temple's five-tiered western rajagopuram rises some 60 feet, and a hilltop shrine to Hayagriva here is said to be the only historical dedicated Hayagriva temple of its kind in South India. In later Srivaishnava history this kshetra became especially dear as the home of the great acharya Vedanta Desika, who is said to have lived to the age of 102 and spent much of his life here, authoring the Sanskrit works Devaneya Panchasat and Achyuta Shataka on this very deity, along with seven Tamil minor works; he has his own shrine and flagstaff within the temple premises. Pilgrims who come to Devanatha Perumal seek the protection of the Lord of the gods, drawn by the same devotion that moved the Alwar and, later, Vedanta Desika, to immortalise this place in verse.

## Festivals

At Thiruvahindrapuram, the shrine of Devanatha Perumal observes a twelve-day Brahmotsavam in the Tamil month of Chittirai (April/May), with a chariot procession on the ninth day, and a ten-day Masi Mahotsavam in Masi (February/March). The wider calendar includes Krishna Janmashtami, Karthigai, Tamil New Year, Margazhi, Sankaranthi, Panguni Uthiram, and Rohini Utsavam, alongside Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi and Vedanta Desika's Tirunakshatram, observed with special reverence. Six daily rituals follow the Vadakalai sampradayam.

## Location & Access

Thiruvahindrapuram lies near the town of Cuddalore in central Tamil Nadu, in the Nadu Nadu region, on the Outshadagiri foothills beside the Kedilam river (also called the Gadilam, and known locally as "Uttaravahini" for its auspicious south-to-north flow). It is reached by road from Cuddalore, the nearest major town and transport hub, which is itself connected by rail and bus to Chennai and other cities.
