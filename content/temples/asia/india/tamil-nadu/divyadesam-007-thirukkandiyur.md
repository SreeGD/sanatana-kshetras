The temple at Thirukkandiyur in the Chola Nadu enshrines the Lord as Harasapa Vimochana Perumal, counted among the 108 Divya Desams sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The very name of the presiding deity proclaims the legend most closely associated with this sacred place. According to the sthala purana, it was here that the Lord delivered Hara (Siva) from a grave curse and the burden of sin that clung to him, the divine name Harasapa Vimochana meaning the one who freed Hara from his curse. Tradition holds that Siva, afflicted after an act of transgression involving Brahma, found release and purification only through the grace of Vishnu at this kshetra, and so the place stands as an enduring testimony to the supreme cleansing power of the Lord's compassion. For this reason Thirukkandiyur is remembered as a place where even the gods sought refuge and were redeemed. The glory of this Divya Desam was sung by Thirumangai Alwar, whose verses in the Prabandham secured its place among the celebrated abodes of Vishnu and carried the renown of the Lord here to devotees across the generations. As one of the Divya Desams of the fertile Chola country, the temple draws pilgrims who come to behold the Moolavar and to seek the same liberation from sin that tradition says the Lord granted to Hara himself, making it a shrine especially venerated for the forgiveness and grace bestowed upon those who worship there.

## Festivals

Like other Chola Nadu Divya Desams, this temple at Thirukkandiyur traditionally observes the principal Srivaishnava festival cycle through the year. An annual Brahmotsavam is celebrated with great devotion, marked by the procession of the utsava murti through the temple streets on a succession of vahanas. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the Tamil month of Margazhi is observed with particular reverence, the Lord granting darshan to devotees during this most auspicious day. Other traditional observances include Pavithrotsavam, the purificatory festival, and a Float festival (Theppotsavam) where the deity is taken out upon the temple tank. The temple, dedicated to the Lord who freed Brahma from his sin, also marks the major Vaishnava days such as Sri Rama Navami, Sri Jayanthi and the Pancha Parva days according to its traditional calendar.
