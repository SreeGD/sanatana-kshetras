At Thiruvithuvakkodu in the Malai Nadu of the Kerala region rises the temple of Uyyavandha Perumal, whose very name proclaims the Lord as "He who came that we might be saved," and which is honoured among the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alwars. The presiding Moolavar is worshipped as the redeemer who descends for the upliftment of his devotees, and the kshetra carries a deep association with surrender and rescue from peril. This Divya Desam holds a singular and tender place in the Prabandham, for it was sung by Kulasekara Alwar, the royal saint and author of the Perumal Tirumoli, whose intense devotion is poured into his verses on this Lord. Tradition cherishes here the famous theme of the child Kulasekara's heart, and the saint's longing to be born even as a stone, a step, or a bird at the Lord's abode rather than be parted from Him. According to the sthala purana, the Lord of Thiruvithuvakkodu is the protector who shields the helpless and grants refuge to those in distress, and the place is celebrated for the depth of the bond between the surrendered devotee and his Saviour. As one of the Divya Desams of the Kerala land, sung uniquely by Kulasekara Alwar, Thiruvithuvakkodu stands as an enduring emblem of saranagati, the loving self-surrender at the feet of the Lord who comes for the salvation of all.
