The largest and holiest temple complex of Balinese Hinduism (Agama Hindu Dharma), Pura Besakih is a 'mother temple' comprising 23 separate but interconnected temples across six terraced levels on the slope of the sacred volcano Mount Agung. The site's origins likely predate Hindu influence (some stone bases resemble megalithic structures over 2,000 years old); it became a site of Hindu worship from 1284 and, by the 15th century, the state temple of the Gelgel dynasty. Balinese Hinduism represents a living, continuously-practiced Hindu tradition outside India with its own distinct ritual calendar and cosmology, sharing core Vedic and Puranic deities while incorporating local Balinese custom (adat).
