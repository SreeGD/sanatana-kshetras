Founded on 29 September 1929 by Pandit Mehta Jaimini, an Arya Samaj preacher who had been exiled from India, Arya Diwaker became the leading institution of the Arya Samaj — the 19th-century Vedic reform movement started by Swami Dayananda Saraswati — among Suriname's Hindustani community descended from indentured laborers. The Dutch colonial government formally recognized the association on 5 February 1930. True to Arya Samaj practice, the temple rejects image worship: there is no murti, and worship centers on the havan, a Vedic fire ceremony, with congregants seated in circles around a central hearth to emphasize equality rather than a hierarchy of officiants and audience.

The association's building has been rebuilt more than once. An initial structure went up in 1936 and was formally inaugurated in 1947; it was demolished in 1975 to make room for expansion, and the current building — a two-story octagonal structure designed by Dutch architect Arthur E. de Groot, incorporating three towers said to symbolize God, humanity, and nature, along with four-tiered roofs referencing the four Vedas — opened on 11 February 2001. In 2022 the association corrected the English spelling of its own name from "Dewaker" to "Diwaker."

Widely described as the largest Hindu temple in Suriname, Arya Diwaker remains an active center of worship and community life in Paramaribo, drawing Hindu and non-Hindu visitors alike to its distinctive architecture.
