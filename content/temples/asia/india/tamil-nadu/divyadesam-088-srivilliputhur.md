The Vatapatrasayi Temple at Srivilliputhur, in the Pandya Nadu region, enshrines the Lord as Vatapatrasayi, who reclines upon the serpent in the posture of cosmic rest, and is revered as one of the 108 Divya Desams sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The name Vatapatrasayi recalls the form of the infant Lord reposing upon a banyan leaf (vata-patra) afloat upon the waters of dissolution, the sustaining cause of all worlds. Srivilliputhur is hallowed above almost every other shrine as the birthplace of two of the Alwar saints. It is here that tradition holds Periyalwar, the garland-maker devotee, discovered and raised the divine child Andal, who grew into the only woman among the twelve Alwars. Periyalwar sang the glory of the Lord, and Andal, whose surpassing love made her long to be wedded to Vishnu himself, composed her Thiruppavai and Nachiyar Thirumozhi, the most beloved devotional verses of the Srivaishnava tradition, both associated with this sacred place. The temple is therefore cherished as the cradle of Andal's devotion and of the daily Thiruppavai recitation observed throughout the month of Margazhi. Its towering gopuram, an emblem of the town itself, and its annual festivals, especially the celebration of Andal's wedding to the Lord, draw multitudes of devotees. As the abode where the bride of Vishnu first offered her flower-garlands and her heart, Srivilliputhur holds an exalted and intimate place in the sacred geography of the Divya Desams.

## Festivals

Srivilliputhur, the birthplace of Andal, observes a festival calendar of unusual richness and is among the most celebrated of the Pandya Nadu shrines. The grandest occasion is traditionally Andal's Aadi Pooram in the Tamil month of Aadi, marking the day Andal is said to have been found in the temple's tulasi garden; it is observed with great fervour and processions. The annual Brahmotsavam, with the deities taken in procession on various vahanas, is kept with solemnity. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi, the month dear to Andal who composed the Tiruppavai, is observed devoutly. The Float festival (Theppotsavam), Pavithrotsavam and the Tamil New Year and Margazhi observances also figure in the temple's yearly round, drawing pilgrims from across the south.

## Location & Access

The Vatapatrasayi temple stands in the heart of Srivilliputhur town in the Virudhunagar region of the old Pandya country, its towering gopuram a landmark of the area and the emblem of Tamil Nadu. It lies south of Madurai, the nearest major city and transport hub, and is reachable by road and rail from there. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours, closing during the midday interval.
