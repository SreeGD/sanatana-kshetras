Commissioned in 1117 CE by the Hoysala king Vishnuvardhana, the Chennakeshava Temple at Belur is regarded as one of the "five foundations" of his reign and stands among the finest surviving examples of Hoysala temple architecture. Vishnuvardhana's turn toward Vaishnavism, associated with the influence of the philosopher-saint Ramanujacharya, is reflected in the temple's dedication to Vishnu, worshipped here as Chennakeshava ("handsome Kesava") and also known as Vijayanarayana. Construction and embellishment of the temple complex continued for roughly a century after its initial consecration, and 118 inscriptions found on site, dated from 1117 CE through the 18th century, provide an unusually well-documented history of its patronage and expansion over time.

The temple is renowned for the density and detail of its stone carving: friezes of dancers, musicians, mythological narratives, and scenes of contemporary 12th-century court life cover its walls, pillars, and ceilings. Together with the Hoysaleswara Temple at nearby Halebidu and the Keshava Temple at Somanathapura, it was inscribed by UNESCO in 2023 as part of the "Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas" World Heritage Site. Unlike many equally significant Hoysala monuments that survive today only as protected ruins, the Belur temple remains a living, actively worshipped shrine, with daily puja continuing much as it has for centuries.
