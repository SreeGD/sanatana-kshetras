Mumba Devi is the goddess from whom Mumbai (and its former English name, Bombay) is directly named — a local form of Parvati worshipped here as a black-stone image, and one of the city's oldest continuously venerated deities. The temple's own sourced history is not fully consistent: an infobox date gives 1635 as a completion year, while the accompanying narrative describes an earlier temple built by Koli fisherfolk near the site of the present Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus, demolished around 1737, after which a new temple was raised at the current Bhuleshwar location, then known as Phansi Talao. Both figures are reported here as found, since no independent source was located this pass to reconcile them.

Today the temple remains an active, heavily visited civic landmark in South Mumbai's Bhuleshwar market district, drawing hundreds of devotees daily along with dedicated Navratri crowds, its enduring role as the eponymous deity of India's largest city giving it significance well beyond its modest physical scale.
