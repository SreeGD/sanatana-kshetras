In the Basavanagudi neighborhood of South Bengaluru — a name that itself derives from this temple ("Basavanagudi" meaning "bull temple locality") — stands one of the largest monolithic Nandi statues in the world, carved from a single block of granite roughly 15 feet high and 20 feet long. The temple was built in 1537 CE under Kempe Gowda I, the chieftain traditionally credited as the founder of Bengaluru, in the Dravidian architectural idiom favored by the Vijayanagara Empire; its distinctive tower was added only in the early 20th century.

The Nandi image is continuously coated in a thick layer of butter and charcoal by devotees and temple staff, a practice believed to protect the black granite surface. Each year in the Kannada month of Karthika (November/December), the temple's vicinity hosts the Kadalekaayi Parishe, or groundnut fair, when farmers from the surrounding region bring their harvest to sell and offer to the deity — a tradition popularly said to trace back to a legend of a giant bull that once ravaged the area's groundnut crops before being pacified and turned to stone as this very Nandi, though that specific story survives only as local legend rather than documented history. The fair remains one of Bengaluru's best-known traditional urban festivals.
