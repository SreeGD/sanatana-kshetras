The Karunakara Perumal Temple at Thirukkaragam enshrines the Lord whose name proclaims him as the very ocean of grace, Karunakara, the boundless bestower of compassion (karuna). Counted among the 108 Divya Desams, the sacred abodes of Sriman Narayana celebrated by the Alwars, this temple lies in the region of Thondai Nadu, the northern Tamil country rich in ancient Vaishnava shrines. The Moolavar here is worshipped as Karunakara Perumal, the Lord who graciously turns toward his devotees and removes their sorrows. Tradition holds that the very place-name Karagam recalls the presence of the Lord in this hallowed spot, where divine mercy is said to flow freely to all who seek refuge. Among the Alwar saints, it is Thirumangai Alwar, the prince-poet of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, who sang the glory of this temple and thereby secured its place in the sacred canon. His verses, born of a heart wholly surrendered to the Lord, garland this shrine and invite generations of devotees to behold the Karunakara, the merciful one, here. As one of the comparatively lesser-known but no less sacred Divya Desams of the Kanchipuram cluster in Thondai Nadu, Thirukkaragam holds a cherished place in the pilgrimage of the faithful, who circumambulate these abodes recalling that each was hallowed by the song of an Alwar. To meditate upon the Lord here as Karunakara is, in the tradition, to place oneself within the reach of his unfailing grace.

## Festivals

As a Divya Desam set within the great temple-town of Kanchipuram, Thirukkaragam traditionally follows the classical Srivaishnava festival calendar. An annual Brahmotsavam, observed over several days with processions of the utsava murti through the temple's surroundings, stands as the principal celebration. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the cold month of Margazhi is kept with special reverence, with the recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham and the symbolic passage of the deity, as is common across the Kanchi temples. Pavithrotsavam, observed for the ritual purification and renewal of the year's worship, and the Float festival (Theppotsavam) are also traditionally marked. Throughout the year the recitation of Tamil hymns and the regular monthly observances tied to the lunar asterisms accompany daily worship.

## Location & Access

Thirukkaragam lies within the temple-rich city of Kanchipuram in the Thondai Nadu region of northern Tamil Nadu, in the cluster of Divya Desams around the Kanchi area. Kanchipuram is well connected by road and rail to Chennai, the nearest major city and transport hub, and the temple is reached through the local streets of the town. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours, closing during the midday interval.
