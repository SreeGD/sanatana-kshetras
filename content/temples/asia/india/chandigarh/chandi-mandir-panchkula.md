Chandi Mandir is, in a direct sense, the origin of Chandigarh's name: when Punjab's post-Partition capital was planned in the early 1950s on open land near this pre-existing Shakti shrine, the new city took its name from the temple — "Chandigarh," the fortress/settlement (garh) by the temple of Chandi. No founding date or builder is documented for the temple itself in the sources consulted; its historical significance here rests entirely on this naming connection rather than on a dated construction history.

Today the temple stands inside the Chandimandir Cantonment, headquarters of the Indian Army's Western Command, on the Chandigarh-Kalka highway about 15 km from Chandigarh city — administratively part of Panchkula district, Haryana, and not within the Chandigarh Union Territory's own boundaries. Alongside its central shrine to the goddess Chandi, the complex also houses images of Radha-Krishna, Hanuman, Shiva, and Ram, and is managed by the same Shri Mata Mansa Devi Shrine Board that administers the nearby Mansa Devi temple. It draws large crowds during Navratri, when thousands of devotees from the wider Chandigarh-Panchkula-Mohali region visit.
