Yogadhyan Badri, at Pandukeshwar on the road between Joshimath and Badrinath, is one of the five shrines of the Panch Badri group and enshrines Vishnu in a seated, meditative (yogadhyan) posture. According to tradition, this is the site where King Pandu, father of the Pandavas, undertook penance and meditation after unwittingly killing an ascetic who had taken the form of a deer; the Pandavas are said to have been born here, and Pandu himself is held to have died and attained salvation at this spot. Copper-plate inscriptions discovered at Pandukeshwar independently attest to the area's rule by early Katyuri-dynasty kings, though they do not by themselves date the founding of this particular temple, which otherwise rests on the Pandava-era tradition rather than documented history.

Because Pandukeshwar sits at a comparatively modest elevation directly on the motorable Rishikesh-Joshimath-Badrinath highway, the temple is far easier to reach than the high Panch Kedar shrines and is accessible for much of the year. Its importance peaks each winter: when Badrinath itself closes under snow, its festival image (utsava-murti) is brought down and installed at Yogadhyan Badri, which continues to host worship on Badrinath's behalf until the main shrine reopens with the following year's Char Dham season.
