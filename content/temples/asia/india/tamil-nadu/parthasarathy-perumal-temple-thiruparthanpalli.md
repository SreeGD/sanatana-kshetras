Thiruparthanpalli takes its name - "Arjuna's school" or "Arjuna's abode" - from a legend that ties this shrine directly to the Mahabharata: the Pandava prince Arjuna, thirsting in this region, is said to have been directed by the sage Agastya to pray to Krishna, who appeared, gave Arjuna a sword with which to dig for water, and created in this way the temple's sacred tank, known as Katka Pushkarani. Krishna is then said to have appeared here again as Parthasarathy, charioteer and teacher, to instruct Arjuna - the episode from which the temple's presiding deity draws his principal identity, worshipped locally as Thamaraiyaal Kelvan ("consort of the lotus-seated one," an epithet pairing him with Lakshmi) together with his consort Shenbagavalli (Sengamalavalli) Thayar.

Two Alvars, Poigai Alvar and Thirumangai Alvar, are credited with hymns to this shrine in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, securing its place among the 108 Divya Desams. As one of the Thirunangur group, the temple observes the shared Thirumangai Alvar Mangalasasana Utsavam every Tamil month of Thai, when its festival deity joins the Garudasevai procession together with the ten other Thirunangur Perumals.
