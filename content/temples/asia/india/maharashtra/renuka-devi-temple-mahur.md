Mahur, a hill town in Nanded district also known as Mahurgad, is revered as the seat of Renuka Devi, worshipped here as Matapur Niwasini Jagdamba Devi and counted among what Maharashtrian Shakta tradition calls the "three and a half" principal Shakti pithas of the state. The site's sacred status is unusual among Shakti Peethas because two distinct goddess legends converge on it. The pan-Indian Shakti Peetha tradition holds that a part of Sati's body fell here after her self-immolation and dismemberment, tying Mahur into the wider network of 51 or so peethas scattered across the subcontinent. Separately, and probably older locally, is the story of Renuka herself: wife of the sage Jamadagni, she was beheaded by her own son Parashurama at his father's command, and tradition places her head's resting place on this very hill, with Jamadagni afterward granting her renewed life as a boon to his son. How these two narratives came to overlay one another at Mahur is not addressed in the sources consulted, and this entry does not attempt to resolve the ambiguity.

Classical references reinforce the site's long-standing sanctity without dating its architecture: both the Devi Bhagavatam and the Devi Gita name Mahur, under the older toponym Matripura or Matapur, as a significant place of Devi worship. No accessible source gives a founding king, dynasty, or century for the temple building itself, and the Wikipedia account of the site carries several unreferenced claims, so any specific claim about its construction history should be treated with caution. What is better attested is the temple's living devotional life: the site draws its largest crowds during the Vijayadashami/Dussehra fair, and also sees substantial gatherings during Navaratri and Datta Pournima, reflecting Mahur's additional importance in the Datta Sampradaya tradition of the wider hill complex.
