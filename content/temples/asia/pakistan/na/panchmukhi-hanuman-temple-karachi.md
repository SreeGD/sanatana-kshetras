Panchmukhi Hanuman Temple, in Karachi's Soldier Bazaar neighborhood, is one of the city's oldest and most prominent functioning Hindu shrines, housing a large image of the five-faced (Panchamukhi) form of Hanuman. Local tradition holds the site is roughly 1,500 years old and connects its origin to Rama's period of exile, when Rama is said to have visited the area, with a Hanuman image later unearthed there prompting the temple's construction — a devotional account that could not be independently verified for this entry.

The temple has been legally recognized as a national heritage site under Sindh's 1994 Cultural Heritage Preservation Act, and its community undertook a significant renovation beginning in 2012 after decades of neglect and land disputes, including a 2006 government order aimed at restoring encroached temple property. In 2019, a further eight to nine Hanuman images were discovered at the site, estimated by the National Museum of Pakistan's director to be around 300 years old, adding a documented layer to the temple's long history. It remains an active place of worship today, with its largest gatherings on Hanuman Jayanti, Diwali, and Holi.
