Wikipedia's article on Hinduism in Martinique identifies, without giving it a proper name, "a historic Hindu temple in Basse-Pointe in Martinique built in the 19th century, which is still in use" — making it, alongside similar temples on neighboring Guadeloupe (such as Temple Changy) and in Trinidad and Guyana, one of the oldest continuously used sites of Hindu worship in the Caribbean basin. It stands as a legacy of the roughly 25,000 Indian indentured laborers, some from French Pondicherry, brought to Martinique beginning in 1853 after the 1848 abolition of slavery to work the island's plantations. French Wikipedia's article on the Basse-Pointe commune itself separately names two local Hindu temples among its heritage sites — the "Temple hindou Moulin l'Étang" and the "Temple hindou Gradis" — though without dates or further detail, so it remains unconfirmed whether either is specifically the 19th-century temple described here, or whether both, one, or neither matches that description.

Martinique's Tamil-descended Hindu tradition centers on the goddess Mariamman (locally "Mariemen") and the folk-deity guardian Madurai Veeran (locally "Madevilen"), the same syncretic South Indian village-Hinduism pantheon venerated at Guadeloupe's Temple Changy and among Tamil-descended communities in Trinidad. Today only an estimated 20-30% of Martinique's roughly 10%-Indian-descended population still actively practices Hindu ritual, with many families having converted to Christianity over the generations — making this temple's continued active use a notable exception.
