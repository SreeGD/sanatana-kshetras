Built on a former cornfield purchased in 1984 and officially opened in 1989 at a cost of $4 million funded entirely by donations from Canadian Hindus, the Hindu Temple of Ottawa-Carleton was the first Hindu temple in Eastern Ontario. Its construction was driven by Pandit Madhu Sahasrabudhe, a food-science researcher who had served as a priest since 1960 and remained central to the temple and its community until his death in 2004 — a figure significant enough in Ottawa's civic and interfaith life that he led prayers at a multi-faith thanksgiving event attended by Queen Elizabeth II in 2002.

The temple complex, at 4835 Bank Street in Gloucester on Ottawa's rural south-eastern edge near the airport, houses nine shrines including Ganesh, Shiva, Krishna and Radha, Lakshmi Narayan, Durga, and Hanuman, reflecting the pan-Hindu, multi-deity worship style common among diaspora temples serving a broad regional Hindu community rather than a single sectarian tradition. Its architecture adapts traditional Hindu temple design to Canada's cold climate and snow loads, and the complex includes community halls and a library, serving as the principal Hindu institutional and cultural hub for the roughly 6,000 Hindus of the Ottawa-Carleton region.
