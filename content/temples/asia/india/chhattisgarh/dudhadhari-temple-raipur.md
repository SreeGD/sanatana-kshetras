In the Mathpara quarter of central Raipur stands the Dudhadhari Temple, a Rama-Hanuman shrine and monastic institution (math) whose name preserves the memory of its 16th-century founder, the ascetic Swami Balabhadradas Ji Maharaj. Tradition holds that he sustained himself on milk alone, earning him the title "Dudh Ahari" — literally, "one who lives on milk" — later shortened to "Dudhadhari," a name that passed from the founder to the institution itself. The math and temple, dedicated jointly to Rama, to Hanuman under the name Balaji, and to Balabhadradas as founder-saint, went on to receive formal royal patronage from the Bhonsle rulers of Nagpur: Maharaja Raghuraoji Bhonsle granted the institution a charter bestowing the hereditary title "Rajeshri" on each successive head of the order, a lineage that continues today under its present head, Rajeshri Dr. Mahant Ramsundar Das Ji Maharaj.

Centuries after its founding, the Dudhadhari Math remains one of Raipur's best-known civic landmarks, its prominence visible even in the city's modern infrastructure: the major Inter-State Bus Terminal serving Raipur is formally named after it — the "Shri Balaji Swami Trust Sri Dudhadhari Math Inter State Bus Terminal" — a naming choice that reflects how thoroughly the math's identity is woven into the everyday geography of the city, well beyond its role as a place of worship. While the available sources document the founder's 16th-century biography and the institution's Maratha-era royal patronage clearly, they are less specific about the exact construction history of the temple building itself, leaving its precise architectural dating an open question even as its status as a long-established Raipur landmark is well attested.
