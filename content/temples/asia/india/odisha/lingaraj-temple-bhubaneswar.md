Bhubaneswar's largest and most important temple, Lingaraj is popularly described as enshrining Harihara, a combined form of Shiva and Vishnu reflecting Odisha's historical syncretism of the two traditions; the temple does include images of Vishnu, plausibly reflecting the rising prominence of the Jagannath sect. The present structure dates substantially to the 11th century CE, with the assembly hall, sanctum, and tower believed built under the Somavamshi dynasty (traditionally credited to king Yayati I) and the hall of offering added in the 12th century under later Eastern Ganga (Ganga) patronage. Its 55-meter (180 ft) deul (tower) is a landmark of Kalinga-style temple architecture.
