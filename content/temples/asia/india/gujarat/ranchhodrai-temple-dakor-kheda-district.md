The Ranchhodrai Temple at Dakor, in Gujarat's Kheda district, centers on an image of Krishna worshipped as Ranchhodrai — literally "the one who fled the battlefield," a reference to Krishna's strategic withdrawal from conflict with Jarasandha before establishing his kingdom at Dwarka — who is also identified here with Dwarkadhish, the Lord of Dwarka. Devotional tradition holds that the deity was brought to Dakor from the original Dwarkadhish Temple in Dwarka in 1151 by an aged, devoted follower named Bodana, who enshrined the murti in his own home; this account, cherished by pilgrims, belongs to legend rather than verified history, and the 1151 date should be read as traditional rather than confirmed.

What is better documented is the temple's institutional history from the 17th century onward: in 1625, the Vallabhite (Pushtimarg) leader Harirai formally introduced Vallabh-sampradaya worship rites for the deity and appointed Khedaval and Shrigaud-Mewad Brahmin families to its service, shaping the temple's ritual tradition into the form it largely retains today. The present temple structure was built considerably later, in 1769-70, by Gopal Tambvekar, replacing an earlier formal shrine that had been established in the early 18th century. Dakor today is one of Gujarat's most important Vaishnava pilgrimage towns, and its Kartik Purnima fair — often called Dakor no Melo — is among the largest annual religious gatherings in the state, drawing pilgrims who walk to Dakor from across the region.
