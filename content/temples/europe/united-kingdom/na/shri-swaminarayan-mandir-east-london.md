This East London Swaminarayan mandir, also known locally as the Shree Kutch Satsang Swaminarayan Temple, began as a charity registered in 1986 by Gujarati Hindus from the Kutch region settled in the London Borough of Newham. Construction of the main temple building started the following year, in 1987, though the project took a further fifteen years to complete, finishing in 2002. Like Cardiff's Shri Swaminarayan Mandir, it belongs to the NarNarayan Dev Gadi branch of the Swaminarayan Sampraday, a Vaishnava tradition distinct from the larger BAPS organization that built the Neasden temple elsewhere in London.

Beyond its main shrine hall, the mandir complex includes an upper floor used for seminars and community gatherings, a library selling religious books and other materials, and a separate kitchen and dining hall that served as a temporary place of worship during the building's construction. The temple also runs Sahajanand School for Gujarati-language instruction, a Swaminarayan Yuvak Mandal youth group, a devotional band/lazium team, and a bhajan (devotional singing) ensemble, giving it a broad cultural and educational role for Newham's Gujarati Hindu community alongside its religious functions.
