At Teetwal, a village in the Karnah tehsil of Kupwara district hugging the Line of Control, a rebuilt Sharda Devi temple marks the Indian side of a centuries-old pilgrimage tradition whose original center, the ancient Sharda Peeth, lies roughly 40 kilometers away across the Line of Control in Pakistan-administered Kashmir and remains inaccessible to pilgrims from India. Teetwal's shrine had historically served as a base camp on the approach to that older site until it was destroyed amid the tribal raids that accompanied the 1947 Partition, leaving the tradition without any actively accessible shrine on the Indian side of the border for decades.

That changed in the early 2020s: local Muslim residents donated land for the temple's reconstruction in 2021 and took part directly in both the rebuilding and the eventual inauguration, a notably documented instance of interfaith cooperation in a still-sensitive border region. Union Home Minister Amit Shah formally re-inaugurated the rebuilt temple on 22 March 2023, and a Kumbhabhisheka consecration ceremony was performed in June 2023 by a successor Jagadguru Shankaracharya, while a mosque has also been built nearby as a further gesture of shared local investment in the site. As a 2021-2023 reconstruction rather than an unbroken ancient structure, the present-day Teetwal temple draws its significance chiefly from this symbolic and historical link to the original Sharda Peeth tradition -- one of the most revered centers of Saraswati/Sharda worship and Sanskrit learning in the wider Kashmiri Hindu tradition -- and from the cross-community effort that restored it.
