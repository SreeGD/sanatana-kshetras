Lepakshi's Veerabhadra Temple is one of the great surviving monuments of Vijayanagara temple architecture, built in 1530 AD (some sources say 1540 AD) under King Achyuta Deva Raya by the courtier-brothers Virupanna and Viranna. Enclosed within two walled courtyards, its mukha mantapa, ardha mantapa, and garbhagriha carry an extraordinary sculptural and painting programme: the ardha mantapa's ceiling holds a fresco said to be Asia's largest, and a single-figure image of Veerabhadra elsewhere in the temple is considered the largest fresco of its kind in Asia, alongside painted narratives of Shiva and Parvati's wedding, Ravana lifting Mount Kailasa, Draupadi's swayamvara, and the Kiratarjuna episode. Beyond the temple's famous "hanging pillar" — displaced just enough from its base that cloth or paper can be passed underneath it — a monolithic granite Nandi some 20 feet high and 30 feet long stands about 200 meters away, among the largest of its type anywhere. Additional shrines within the complex hold images of Shiva and Parvati and of Vishnu, alongside the temple's principal deity, Veerabhadra, Shiva's fierce warrior form.

Devotional tradition ties the village's very name to the Ramayana: after Ravana wounded Jatayu, the vulture-king who had fought to stop Sita's abduction, Rama is said to have found the dying bird here and commanded him to rise — "Le Pakshi," Telugu for "Get up, bird" — a phrase tradition holds became "Lepakshi." This is one of several places across South India that claim an association with Jatayu's fall and final rites, alongside Chadayamangalam in Kerala and the Vijayaraghava Perumal and Thirupullabhoothangudi temples in Tamil Nadu; it is a legendary attribution layered onto a temple whose Vijayanagara-era construction and artistry are, by contrast, well documented. Today the temple is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India as a monument of national importance and sits on UNESCO's Tentative List of World Heritage Sites, drawing pilgrims, heritage tourists, and researchers alike.
