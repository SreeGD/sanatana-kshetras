The temple at Sri Vaikuntam enshrines the Lord as Vaikuntanathar (Srivaikuntanathan), affectionately known as Kallapiran, and ranks among the 108 Divya Desams of the Sri Vaishnava tradition, one of the celebrated cluster of nine Tirunelveli/Thoothukudi shrines (Nava Tirupathi) along the sacred Thamirabarani river in Pandya Nadu, second in importance within that group only to the Alwarthirunagari temple. The Moolavar, a Shaligrama-stone image of the standing Lord upon the serpent Adishesha, graces devotees in a standing form, accompanied by His consort Vaikunthavalli (Vaikundavalli Thayar), and the very name of the place, Sri Vaikuntam, declares this earthly abode to be a likeness of the supreme heaven of Vishnu. The Lord's endearing title Kallapiran, "the Lord who is the great deceiver" or "the divine thief," recalls the sthala purana in which He is remembered for His playful and protective intervention on behalf of His devotees, stealing away their burdens even as He blesses them. The temple's documented history includes grants from the Pandya ruler Jatavarman Kulasekaran I (1190-1216 CE) and further significant offerings under Maravarman Sundara Pandyan (1216-1238 CE); in a very different chapter of its history, the fortified temple complex was used by the British as a fort in 1801 during the campaign against Veerapandiya Kattabomman. Built in Dravidian style with a nine-tier rajagopuram some 110 feet tall over about 5 acres, enclosed by 580-by-396-foot granite walls, the temple's Thiruvengadamudayan hall preserves rare life-size 16th-century sculptures. This shrine is sung by Nammalwar, foremost of the Alwars, in verses of the Tiruvaymoli within the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, which enshrined Sri Vaikuntam within the sacred corpus and exalted Kallapiran as a manifestation of the Lord of the highest realm made accessible upon earth. As one of the Nava Tirupathi worshipped along the Thamirabarani, Sri Vaikuntam holds a special place in the devotional circuit of the Pandya country, where pilgrims revere the river as the divine theertham and adore the Lord by His name that promises Vaikuntha itself.

## Festivals

At Sri Vaikuntam, the temple of Vaikuntanathar (Kallapiran) observes a ten-day Brahmotsavam in the Tamil month of Chittirai (April-May) as its principal celebration, with daily processions of the Lord on various vahanas. The Garuda Sevai Utsavam in Vaikasi (May-June) is a particularly notable event, when the processional deities of all nine Nava Tirupathi temples are brought together. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is kept with special grandeur, the Nalayira Divya Prabandham being chanted through the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu and the Paramapada Vasal (gateway of liberation) opened for devotees. Pavithrotsavam for the year's ritual purification and the Theppotsavam (float festival) on the temple tank are also traditionally celebrated. Krishna Jayanthi, recalling the Lord's name Kallapiran, along with Andal's Aadi Pooram, Navaratri and Rama Navami, are marked with festive abhishekam, alankaram and special worship. The temple follows the Tenkalai Sri Vaishnava tradition.

## Location & Access

The Vaikuntanathar temple lies in the town of Sri Vaikuntam, on the Thamirabarani river in Thoothukudi district, about 22 km from Tirunelveli, in the former Pandya Nadu of southern Tamil Nadu. The city of Thoothukudi and nearby Tirunelveli are the closest major transport hubs, with road and rail links to the town. Four daily poojas are held at approximately 8 AM, 12 PM, 6 PM and 8 PM. The temple is maintained by the Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department, Government of Tamil Nadu.
