Sri Vaikunta Perumal Temple, known by its Divya Desam name Tiru Parameswara Vinnagaram, is among the most historically significant monuments in Kanchipuram. It was built in the 8th century CE by the Pallava emperor Nandivarman II Pallavamalla — scholarly estimates for its construction range from around 690 CE to circa 770–775 CE — and is celebrated for its unusual three-tiered sanctum, in which Vishnu is enshrined in seated, reclining, and standing forms on three separate levels, one of the few temples in India to present all three postures of Vishnu in a single structure.

The temple is renowned among art historians and epigraphists for the sculpted narrative panels lining its inner pillared veranda, which depict scenes from Pallava dynastic history — including the semi-legendary origin of the Pallava line and the succession of Pallava kings — each accompanied by an explanatory inscription. This makes the temple an unusually direct primary source for Pallava political history, in addition to its religious significance, and its architectural plan is considered by historians to have served as a prototype for larger Dravidian temple complexes built across Tamil Nadu in subsequent centuries.

As a Divya Desam, it is one of the 108 sacred Vishnu shrines glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham: Thirumangai Alvar dedicated ten hymns to this temple in his Periya Tirumoli, among the more substantial sets of verses devoted to any single Kanchipuram shrine. The temple was later modified and maintained under the Cholas and the Vijayanagara rulers, and remains an active place of worship today, standing as both a living Vaishnava shrine and a protected monument documenting over 1,300 years of continuous history.
