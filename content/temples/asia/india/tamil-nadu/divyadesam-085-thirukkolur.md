The Vaithamanidhi Perumal Temple at Thirukkolur, in the Pandya Nadu country of the Tamil land, enshrines the Lord as Vaithamanidhi Perumal, also known by the alternate name Kuberasthalam, and is counted among the 108 Divya Desams, the sacred abodes of Vishnu hymned by the Alwars. The Moolavar is enshrined in a reclining posture facing east, with his consort revered here as Kumudavalli (Kolurvalli). The very name Vaithamanidhi, traditionally understood as "the treasure that is laid up," expresses the Lord's character as the imperishable wealth safeguarded for His devotees, a refuge richer than any worldly fortune. Built in Dravidian style with a three-tiered rajagopuram across two granite-walled prakarams, the temple's original structure is traditionally believed to have been refurbished by Rani Mangammal of the Madurai Nayak line (1689-1704 CE). Within the Nava Tirupathi's Navagraha associations, Thirukkolur is reckoned an Angaraka (Mars) sthalam. Lying close to the cluster of nine Divya Desams gathered along the banks of the Thamiraparani river in the deep south, about 4 km from Alwarthirunagari, Thirukkolur is intimately linked with the life of Nammalwar, the foremost of the Alwars, who was born nearby, and is also honoured as the birthplace of Madhurakavi Alvar, the disciple who discovered Nammalwar in his long meditation. Nammalwar alone sang the glory of this shrine in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, the collected four thousand verses of the Alwars, and his benediction has shaped its devotional fame ever since. Tradition also cherishes Thirukkolur for its association with the celebrated saintly woman remembered as Thirukkolur Ammal, whose poignant utterances -- recalling the great surrendered souls of scripture and lamenting her own lack of their fervour -- have become a beloved expression of prapatti, loving self-surrender to the Lord, though this specific narrative element has not been independently corroborated beyond the 108divyadesam.org dataset used to compile this entry. Pilgrims who tread the southern circuit of Divya Desams come here to behold the Lord who is the hidden, unfailing wealth of the soul.

## Festivals

As a Srivaishnava Divya Desam celebrated in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, Thirukkolur traditionally observes the great annual Brahmotsavam, when Vaithamanidhi Perumal is taken in procession on the garuda, hanumantha and other vahanas amid Vedic chanting and recitation of the Tamil Prabandham. Vaikuntha Ekadasi in the month of Margazhi (December-January) is kept over twenty days combining the Pagal Pathu (ten daytime) and Ra Pathu (ten nighttime) observances, the Lord granting darshan to devotees throughout. An Avani festival (August-September) runs for ten days with daily processions of the deity on different vehicles, and Purattasi Garudasevai (September-October) brings Saturday processions through that month. The Nammalvar Garuda Sevai in Vaikasi (May-June) brings festival idols from across the Nava Tirupathi shrines to nearby Alwarthirunagari. The annual Pavithrotsavam of purification and a float festival (Theppotsavam) are also observed where local custom maintains them.

## Location & Access

The temple stands at Thirukkolur in the Thoothukudi district of the historic Pandya Nadu, on the Tiruchendur-Tirunelveli route on the southern bank of the Thamiraparani river, about 4 km from Alwarthirunagari, a short way from Tirunelveli and Thoothukudi, the nearest major towns and transport hubs. The temple follows six daily rituals -- Kalasanthi (8 AM), Uchikalam (12 PM), Sayarakshai (6 PM) and Ardha Jamam (8 PM), with additional weekly, monthly and fortnightly observances -- and is administered by the Hindu Religious and Endowment Board of the Government of Tamil Nadu.
