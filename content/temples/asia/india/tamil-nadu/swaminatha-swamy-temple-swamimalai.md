Swamimalai's sanctum sits atop a modest 60-foot hillock on the banks of a tributary of the Kaveri, near Kumbakonam, and is counted among the sites named in the Sangam-era Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai as sacred to Murugan; the 14th-century poet-saint Arunagirinathar later sang its praises in his Tiruppukal. The temple complex is arranged around three concentric precincts reached through three gateway towers, the most prominent a five-storey gopuram on the south side, while shrines to Shiva and Parvati stand at the base of the hill below the main Murugan sanctum. A flight of 60 steps climbs to the hilltop shrine, traditionally read as representing the 60-year cycle of the Tamil calendar. The temple underwent a Kumbhabhishekam (ritual renovation and reconsecration) in 2015.

The temple's name preserves its central legend: Swaminatha means "teacher of the Lord [Swami]," recalling the story in which the child Murugan is said to have explained the meaning of the Pranava mantra, Om, to his own father Shiva atop this hill, after Shiva found himself unable to articulate the syllable's significance himself. Among the six Arupadaiveedu, this episode of the son instructing the father is Swamimalai's distinctive traditional role, distinguishing it from the more war-centred legends attached to Tiruchendur or Thiruparankundram.
