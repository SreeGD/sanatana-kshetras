On the banks of the Kshipra (Shipra) river in Ujjain — the ancient Avantika of Puranic geography — stands Sandipani Ashram, also known as Shri Krishna Vidya Sthali ("Krishna's place of learning"), traditionally identified as the hermitage of the sage Maharishi Sandipani. The Bhagavata Purana's account has Krishna and his brother Balarama, together with their friend Sudama, sent here to complete their education — not only in the Vedas but, per tradition, in disciplines ranging from astronomy and archery to the training of horses and elephants — and it is this same episode that gives rise to one of the best-loved stories in the Krishna tradition: after finishing his studies, Krishna is said to have retrieved his guru's deceased son from the realm of death as gurudakshina, the customary gift a student offers a teacher.

Unlike many places whose only connection to Mahabharata- or Bhagavata-era events is a legendary place name with no built shrine, Sandipani Ashram is independently attested as a real, continuously identifiable pilgrimage site: it is home to Baithak no. 73 of the 84 seats associated with the Vaishnava reformer Vallabhacharya's pilgrimages, located at a tank called Gomti Kund within the ashram grounds, and at its rear stands Shri Chitragupta Prakatya Tirth, revered particularly by the Kayastha community, with a Brahma shrine unusually placed on an upper floor. The present-day complex, per travel-guide descriptions, centers on a main shrine housing images of Krishna, Balarama, Sudama, and Sandipani himself, alongside a separate Sarveshwar Mahadev temple to Shiva and the stepped Gomati Kund tank — traditionally said to hold water gathered from all of India's sacred rivers at Krishna's request, for his guru's use. No source consulted gives a construction date or founder for these standing structures, so their architectural history is treated here as unverified even as the site's identity and continuous use as a pilgrimage destination are well documented.
