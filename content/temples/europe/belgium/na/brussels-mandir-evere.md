Brussels Mandir traces its origins to informal gatherings of Indian families who had settled in Brussels, coming together in a borrowed church or school hall to worship together and, most notably, to celebrate Maa Durga Jaagran in honor of the goddess Durga; Wikipedia's account places the start of this practice around the year 2000, while the Mandir's own site suggests it began somewhat earlier. As the community grew, it moved from these informal gatherings toward establishing a dedicated place of worship, holding a soft opening ceremony for the Brussels Mandir on Rue Colonel Bourg in the municipality of Evere on 19 February 2012, organized by the local Hindu Mandir Association fundraising body together with Brussels' wider Indian community.

Since opening, the Mandir has held weekly Sunday prayers from 11:00 am to 1:00 pm and marks the major Hindu festivals with special events, including an annual Ram Katha and participation in the Indian Brussels Mela, alongside yoga and classical Indian dance classes that give it a role as a cultural as well as religious hub for Brussels' Indian Hindu community. Further detail on its individual founders and building would still need to be confirmed directly with the community.
