Vanamamalai Perumal Temple at Nanguneri, on the banks of the Thamiraparani river in Tirunelveli district, is revered as one of the nine Divya Desams of the Nava Tirupathi group - specifically its Rahu sthalam, remembered in the Divya Prabandham under the older name Thiruccheeravaramangai. The presiding deity, worshipped as Totadri Nathan or Vanamamalai Perumal, is unusual among Divya Desams for manifesting as eleven distinct swayambhu (self-manifested) murtis within a single sanctum, a feature the temple's own tradition holds up as one of its most singular distinctions. His consort is venerated as Varamangai Thayar, a form of Lakshmi whose very name - and one associated legend, that Lakshmi herself was born at this spot as Srivaramangai - is bound up with the place-name Thiruccheeravaramangai.

A separate temple legend tells of a childless king, Kaarya, who worshipped Vishnu here and was instructed in a vision to dig into the earth beneath Nanguneri; finding a flow of blood at the spot, he began a practice of daily oil ablution upon the deity, was blessed with children, and went on to raise the temple in gratitude - an origin story that, alongside a further legend of the serpent Adishesha's penance to be allowed to serve as Vishnu's couch, frames the site's sanctity in devotional rather than documentary terms. Nammalvar sang ten hymns of the Thiruvaimozhi in praise of this shrine as part of his circuit of the Nava Tirupathi. Historically, an inscription of 1236 CE under the Pandya king Sundara Pandya I is among the earliest firm epigraphic references to the temple, and it received further endowments from the Vijayanagara empire and the Madurai Nayaks. In 1447 CE, administration of the temple passed to the newly founded Vanamamalai Mutt, which grew into one of the principal seats of the Tenkalai school of Sri Vaishnavism, eventually overseeing more than two hundred subsidiary mathas across South India - giving Nanguneri a continuing importance in Sri Vaishnava institutional life well beyond its standing as a Divya Desam alone.
