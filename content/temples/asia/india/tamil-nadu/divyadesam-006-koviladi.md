Koviladi, also known as Thirukkudalur, lies in the Chola Nadu region near the confluence of sacred waters in the Kaveri belt, and is counted among the 108 Divya Desams celebrated by the Alwars. The presiding deity (Moolavar) is revered here as Appakkudathaan, the Lord associated in tradition with the offering of appam, the sweet cake presented as the distinctive prasadam of this shrine, from which the perumal takes His beloved name. According to the sthala purana, the Lord graced this place to receive the loving offering of His devotees, and the giving of appam remains the cherished mark of worship here. Thirukkudalur enjoys a notable distinction among the Divya Desams in that its glory is sung by four of the Alwars in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, an unusual abundance of praise: Thirumazhisai Alwar, the profound early saint; Nammalwar, the foremost of the Alwars whose Thiruvaymozhi is regarded as the very essence of the Vedas; Periyalwar, who sang the protective pallandu over the Lord; and Thirumangai Alwar, who garlanded so many sacred abodes with his verses. That four Alwars should have sanctified this shrine marks it as a place of singular devotional importance within the corpus. Pilgrims revere Appakkudathaan for His grace and for the sweetness of the offering that bears His name, and the temple stands as a living testament to the Prabandham heritage, where the songs of the Alwars and the puranic glory of the place continue to draw the faithful in worship and surrender.

## Festivals

The Appakkudathaan temple at Koviladi traditionally observes a grand annual Brahmotsavam as its foremost festival, with the processional deity drawn around the streets on successive vahana mounts to the accompaniment of the Divya Prabandham. Vaikunta Ekadasi is celebrated with great devotion, the Tiruvaymoli being recited through the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu days and the Paramapada Vasal opened for the faithful. Pavithrotsavam, the annual purificatory rite, is also kept, as is a Float festival (Theppotsavam) when the utsava murti is taken upon a raft on the temple tank. The temple's association with the offering of appam (sweet rice cakes) to the Lord lends a distinctive devotional flavour, alongside the regular Vaishnava observances of monthly Ekadasis and Tamil month festivals.

## Location & Access

Koviladi, also known as Thirukkudalur, lies in the Chola Nadu region on the banks of the Kaveri, between Tiruchirappalli and Thanjavur. Thanjavur is the associated city, while Tiruchirappalli (Trichy) offers the nearest major rail and air connections; the temple is usually approached by road. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a closure during midday. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
