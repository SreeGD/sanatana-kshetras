Koodal Azhagar Temple stands in the very center of Madurai, the ancient Pandya capital, and is one of the very few Divya Desams where the presiding deity is shown in three postures at once: standing as Koodal Azhagar, seated as Viyooga Sundararajan (or Aravamudan) directly above, and reclining higher still, all within a single elevated, multi-tiered sanctum. Only a small handful of Divya Desams share this "three-in-one" arrangement, and it gives the temple its distinct architectural identity, the Ashtanga Vimana, a form otherwise seen at Thirukoshtiyur and a few other Vaishnava shrines.

The city's own name is bound up with the temple's legend: "Koodal" refers to a confluence or bulwark, and tradition holds that Vishnu manifested here as Koodal Azhagar to shelter Madurai from cosmic dissolution (Mahapralaya), the city then coming to be called Naanmadakoodal, "the confluence guarded on four sides." The temple's consort shrine venerates the goddess as Mathuravalli Thayar.

Koodal Azhagar is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham by three Alvars: Periyalvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, and Thirumangai Alvar, placing it among the Pandya Nadu group of Divya Desams sung by the poet-saints in the early medieval period. Epigraphic evidence, including an 8th-century inscription recording donations of granite for the temple's construction, points to a Pandya-era origin, with the Madurai Nayak rulers of the 16th century responsible for much of the temple's present pillared halls and the five-tiered Rajagopuram that fronts it. A further renovation is recorded in the early 20th century. Today the temple, administered by the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Board, remains an active center of worship a short walk from Madurai's more famous Meenakshi Amman Temple, the two together reflecting the city's long-standing devotion to both Vaishnava and Shaiva traditions.
