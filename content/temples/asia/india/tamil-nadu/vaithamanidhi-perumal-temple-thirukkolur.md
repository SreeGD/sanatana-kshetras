Thirukkolur, a small village a few kilometres from Azhwarthirunagari on the Thamirabarani river, is counted as the eighth of the Nava Tirupathi and holds particular significance as the traditional birthplace of Madhurakavi Alvar, the saint who discovered and became the first disciple of Nammalvar. Here Vishnu is worshipped as Vaithamanidhi Perumal, with his consort Lakshmi honoured as Kolurvalli.

The place-name, commonly explained as "the place where wealth was obtained," is tied to a temple legend involving Kubera, the guardian deity of wealth, who is said to have been cursed by Shiva for disrespecting Parvati. Kubera performed penance at this spot on the Thamirabarani, after which Vaithamanidhi Perumal restored his riches and standing, and the site came to be known as Thirukkolur (Kolur, from the Tamil for having acquired wealth). A secondary tradition recorded at the temple instead credits King Ambarisha's steadfast devotion, and Vishnu's subsequent intercession, with removing a curse laid on the king by the irascible sage Durvasa; both accounts circulate locally and are preserved here as parallel legends rather than a single canonical version.

Nammalvar refers to the shrine in his Tiruvaimozhi as part of his sequence of hymns on the nine Nava Tirupathi, cementing its place within the fixed list of 108 Divya Desams. The present temple structure is traditionally said to have been refurbished under the patronage of Rani Mangammal, the Nayaka regent of Madurai who ruled at the turn of the eighteenth century, though a fuller epigraphic history was not available in the sources consulted. As the presumed birthplace of Madhurakavi Alvar — remembered above all for his single surviving composition, the eleven verses of Kanninun Siruthambu in praise of Nammalvar himself — Thirukkolur is regarded by Sri Vaishnavas as a site of guru-parampara significance nearly as much as one of Vishnu's own shrines.
