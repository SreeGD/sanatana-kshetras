The Purushothaman Temple at Devaprayag, known in the Divya Prabandham as Thirukkandam — sung as the 'kadi nagar', the fragrant and well-guarded city — lies in the Vada Nadu or Northern Region amid the Himalayan confluences of the sacred rivers, and enshrines the Lord as Purushottama, one of the 108 Divya Desams. The name Purushottama, the Supreme Person who transcends both the perishable and the imperishable, marks this as an abode where the Lord is adored in His highest aspect, presiding over one of the most holy meeting-points of the northern waters where, in tradition, the streams descending from the mountains unite on their long journey to the sea. This is among the rare Divya Desams set far in the north, distant from the Tamil country of most of the shrines, and its inclusion in the canon testifies to the vast sweep of the Alwars' devotion. It was Periyalwar — the great saint of Srivilliputhur, foster-father of Andal and singer of the Lord's tender glory — who hymned this abode, an unusual honour, for his verses most often celebrate the shrines of the south; here his song reaches the far northern heights to praise Purushottama. By his hymns Thirukkandam takes its place among the 108 sacred abodes. Tradition reveres the surrounding land as hallowed ground sanctified by the austerities of sages and by the sacred confluence of holy rivers, and pilgrims hold that to worship Purushottama at this remote northern seat is to behold the Supreme Person in the loftiest of His earthly dwellings.

## Festivals

At Devaprayag, the Thirukkandam Divya Desam praised by the Azhwars, worship of Purushothaman follows the devotional pattern of the Himalayan shrines rather than an elaborate Tamil utsavam cycle. The great Vaishnava observances are traditionally kept here: Vaikunta Ekadasi and the principal annual Ekadasis as days of fasting and darshan, Sri Rama Navami, and Krishna Janmashtami, when the Lord receives special abhishekam and offerings. The temple's setting at the confluence of the Bhagirathi and Alaknanda, where the Ganga is born, lends it a deep tirtha sanctity, and pilgrims commonly venerate it within the seasonal Char Dham pilgrimage rather than for a single named festival. Where a Brahmotsavam is observed, the deity is honoured with processions and ritual worship.

## Location & Access

Devaprayag (Thirukkandam) stands in the Tehri Garhwal area of Uttarakhand in the northern hill region, at the sacred confluence of the Bhagirathi and Alaknanda rivers that forms the Ganga. The nearest major hubs are Rishikesh and Dehradun, from which hill roads lead up to the town. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours, subject to seasonal mountain access. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
