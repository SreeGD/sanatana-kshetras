Ayodhya, on the banks of the Sarayu, is revered as the birthplace of Rama and the capital of the Ikshvaku dynasty's kingdom of Kosala; it is counted among the sapta puri, the seven cities said to grant moksha. Thiru Ayodhi is glorified as a Divya Desam in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, where Poigai Alvar, Bhoothathalvar, Peyalvar, Thirumalisai Alvar and Nammalvar together contributed verses recalling Rama's exile, the rescue of Sita from Lanka, and his righteous reign (Rama Rajya) as king of Ayodhya; the sthala is counted among the seven mukti-kshetras of Vaishnava tradition.

Historically, the site identified with Rama's birthplace (Ram Janmabhoomi) was long a subject of dispute between Hindu and Muslim communities after a mosque was built at the location in the 16th century; following a prolonged litigation, the Supreme Court of India ruled in 2019 to allot the disputed site for the construction of a Rama temple. A new Rama temple (Ram Mandir) was consecrated at the site on 22 January 2024, and continues to draw pilgrims in vast numbers alongside older Vaishnava shrines associated with the Divya Desam tradition in the town.
