Toronto's Hare Krishna Temple occupies a striking piece of adaptive reuse: a Gothic Revival church built in 1899 as the Presbyterian Church of the Covenant, designed by the Toronto architectural firm Gordon & Helliwell. The International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON), founded in New York in 1966 by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, acquired the building around 1976 and converted it into a temple, preserving much of the original church architecture while installing shrines for Krishna-conscious worship inside. It has since grown into one of Canada's largest Hare Krishna centers, hosting daily kirtan and philosophy classes as well as Toronto's public Ratha Yatra chariot festival, and serves as a hub for the city's ISKCON community alongside newer temples that have since opened elsewhere in the Greater Toronto Area, such as ISKCON Brampton.
