Bhojeshwar Temple, in the village of Bhojpur about 28 km from Bhopal, is best known for two things: an enormous, unfinished Shiva lingam and an unusually rich body of physical evidence about how an 11th-century Indian temple was actually built. The lingam itself, carved from limestone, stands roughly 7.5 feet tall and nearly 18 feet in circumference, set on a massive platform inside a sanctum whose superstructure was never completed — the tower that should have risen above it was abandoned partway through construction, for reasons no surviving record explains.

The temple's attribution to the Paramara king Bhoja, an 11th-century patron famed for his scholarship as much as his temple-building, is not based on an inscription within the temple itself but rather on indirect evidence: mason's marks at Bhojeshwar match those found on a nearby Jain temple at the same site that carries an independently dated inscription of 1035 CE, placing both structures within Bhoja's reign. That indirect method of dating is itself part of what makes the site so valuable to historians, since the surrounding rock faces still bear over 1,300 mason's marks and what appear to be architectural drawings, offering a rare window into 11th-century temple-construction technique. Today the Archaeological Survey of India protects Bhojeshwar as a Monument of National Importance; while it is not the site of regular daily worship in the manner of a fully active temple, thousands of devotees still gather here for Maha Shivaratri, when the Madhya Pradesh government also stages the Bhojpur Utsav cultural festival at the site.
