Suryanar Kovil, a village a short distance from Aduthurai in Thanjavur district, is the anchor temple of Tamil Nadu's nine-temple Navagraha circuit and, unusually among them, the only one built from the outset around worship of all nine planetary deities together rather than housing a single graha as a subsidiary shrine inside an older Shiva temple. Surya, the sun god, is enshrined here as Suryanarayana with his consorts Usha and Chhaya, while individual shrines to the remaining eight grahas — Chandra, Angaraka, Budha, Brihaspati, Shukra, Shani, Rahu, and Ketu — are arranged around the central sanctum, letting a single visit take in the entire Navagraha pantheon. Devotional legend traces the site's sanctity to the sage Kalava, who is said to have been drawn into a curse Brahma placed on the nine planetary deities themselves after they intervened on his behalf; banished to what was then a forest of white-flowered Vellurukku shrubs, the grahas found the ground sanctified by Shiva, who granted them a permanent home here from which to bless devotees still today.

The surviving stone temple is credited to the Chola emperor Kulottunga Chola I in the 11th-12th century, when it was known as Kulottungachola-Marttandalayam ("marttanda" being an epithet of Surya), with the Vijayanagara rulers adding further structures in later centuries. Built in Dravidian style behind a five-tiered rajagopuram, the temple draws devotees seeking relief from difficult planetary periods in their horoscopes, with Sundays and the Tamil month of Thai — traditionally associated with the sun's northward turn (Uttarayana) — considered especially auspicious for worship here.
