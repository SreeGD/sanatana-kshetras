The Sri Senpaga Vinayagar Temple, on Ceylon Road in Singapore's Joo Chiat/Katong district, is one of Singapore's most distinctive heritage Hindu temples, tracing its origins to the 1850s when Sri Lankan Tamil immigrants settled the area. Temple tradition holds that a statue of Lord Vinayagar (Ganesha) was discovered beside a pond near a Chempaka (Senpaga) tree, giving the temple its name; a permanent concrete structure was completed in 1929, followed by the temple's first major consecration ceremony on 3 February 1930. Managed by the Singapore Ceylon Tamils' Association since 1923, the temple has been dedicated principally to Ganesha, with additional shrines to Shiva, the Divine Mother (Ambal), Murugan (Subramaniam), Vairavar, and Nageswarar.

On 7 February 2003, Singapore's National Heritage Board declared the temple a historic site, recognizing both its age and its distinctive place among Singapore's Hindu heritage landmarks. Among its architectural features is a musical pillar said to be the first of its kind in Southeast Asia, alongside a striking 21-meter Raja Gopuram entrance tower. As a rare-deity, Ganesha-centered heritage temple with a continuous history stretching back over 170 years, it stands as a distinctive complement to Singapore's other major, already-documented Hindu landmarks.
