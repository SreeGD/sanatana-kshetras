Set in the small town of Sarahan in upper Shimla district, the Bhimakali temple served for centuries as the family shrine of the rulers of the former princely state of Bushahr, and its architecture still carries that royal patronage in its scale: twin towers, three courtyards, and gateways finished in gold and silver work, built in the timber-and-stone kath-kuni style that blends Hindu and Buddhist construction traditions common across this Himalayan belt. Tradition places the temple's origins as far back as the 13th century, though the structure visible today owes much to a substantial reconstruction carried out by Raja Padam Singh in the 1920s, after earlier damage; notably, the temple's main tower survived the severe 1905 Kangra earthquake and is said to have straightened itself after later tremors. A portion of the temple's foundation collapsed in 2022, prompting a structural assessment by IIT Mandi to guide conservation work.

Bhimakali, a fierce form of Durga worshipped here, is counted by tradition among the 51 Shakti Peethas said to mark where a fragment of the goddess Sati's body fell — an identification that belongs to devotional tradition rather than documented history, as with most sites on that list. The temple remains an active pilgrimage site and draws its largest crowds during the Navaratri and Dussehra festivals, when its connection to the former Bushahr royal house is still visibly marked in the rituals observed there.
