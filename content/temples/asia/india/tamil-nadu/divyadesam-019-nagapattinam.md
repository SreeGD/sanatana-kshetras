The Soundararaja Perumal Temple at Nagapattinam, known in the sacred tongue as Thirunagai, enshrines the Lord as Soundararaja Perumal, the Beautiful King, and counts among the 108 Divya Desams glorified by the Alwars. The very name of the place, evoking the serpent (naga), and its situation upon the eastern coast of the Chola country have long marked it as a holy seat where the Lord grants His resplendent darshan to devotees. As one of the Divya Desams of Chola Nadu, the shrine belongs to the inner circle of abodes celebrated in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, the four-thousand sacred verses of the Tamil saints. Its glory was sung by Thirumangai Alwar, the warrior-poet whose pasurams traversed the length of the Tamil land and carried the renown of many coastal and Chola shrines into the eternal corpus of the Prabandham. By his hymns the Lord of Thirunagai is reckoned a paadal petra sthalam, a place that has received the saint's song, and is thereby assured of perpetual remembrance in the daily recitation of the Srivaishnava tradition. According to received tradition, pilgrims to Nagapattinam approach the Lord in His form of surpassing beauty, beseeching His grace; and the temple, set in a region dense with Vaishnava sanctity, takes its place among the foremost of the divine abodes of the south. Here the faithful behold Soundararaja Perumal as the gracious sovereign whose comeliness gives the shrine its name and whose presence sanctifies the ancient port-city.

## Festivals

The temple at Thirunagai traditionally observes the annual Brahmotsavam, celebrated over several days with ceremonial flag-hoisting, processions of Soundararaja Perumal through the surrounding streets on a succession of vahanas, and the Garuda Sevai that draws large gatherings of devotees. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the Tamil month of Margazhi is among the most cherished occasions, when the recitation of the Tiruvaymozhi during the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu observances and the opening of the Paramapada Vasal (the gateway to Vaikunta) mark the festival's spiritual height. The annual Theppotsavam or Float festival, when the processional deity is taken upon a decorated raft on the temple tank, and the Pavithrotsavam of ritual purification are also traditionally kept. Throughout the year the months of Margazhi and Purattasi see special Vaishnava worship and devotional singing.

## Location & Access

The temple stands within the coastal town of Nagapattinam (Thirunagai) in the Chola Nadu region of Tamil Nadu, near the Bay of Bengal coast. Nagapattinam is well connected by road and rail to nearby centres such as Thiruvarur and Thanjavur, with the larger city of Tiruchirappalli serving as the principal transport hub for the area. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes darshan during morning and evening hours, remaining closed for a period in the afternoon. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
