The Simhachalam Temple sits in the Zielberg district of Jandelsbrunn, a small municipality in the Bavarian Forest near the Austrian border. Established by ISKCON around 1980, it was one of the movement's early continental European outposts and, according to German-language sources, served for a time as a base for Hare Krishna missionary activity across Europe. What sets it apart from most other ISKCON centers, including the other communities in this directory, is its presiding deity: rather than the more typical Radha-Krishna pairing, the temple is dedicated to Narasimha, Vishnu's half-lion, half-human incarnation, together with his young devotee Prahlada — a dedication German sources describe as unique among Hare Krishna temples in the West.

Beyond its role as a Vaishnava temple, the community at Jandelsbrunn maintains a farm with cow protection, offers guesthouse stays and retreats, and runs educational programs under the banner of an ISKCON Bhagavata College. It remains open to visitors daily and continues to draw devotees and Hindu community members from across Bavaria, Austria, and further afield, particularly for its annual Radhastami and Brahmotsava festival celebrations each September, alongside the wider Vaishnava calendar established by ISKCON founder A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada.
