Sri Varadaraja Perumal Temple — also called Devarajaswamy Temple, and known by its hill-name Athigiri (Hastigiri) — is the largest and most prominent Vishnu temple in Kanchipuram, occupying a complex of roughly 23 acres in the area traditionally called South Kanchi or Vishnu Kanchi. Local tradition holds that Lord Brahma performed a yagna (fire sacrifice) atop the Hastigiri hillock here, and that when the flood-waters of the river Vegavati threatened to sweep away the sacrificial ground, Vishnu laid himself down to hold back the flood; pleased with Brahma's devotion, Vishnu then rose up before him as Varadaraja — "the boon-giver" — granting Brahma's wish and making the spot eternally sacred. The temple's principal processional deity, the wooden image known as Athi Varadar (carved from the athi/fig tree), is kept submerged in the temple's Anantha Saras (Anantha Theertham) tank and ceremonially displayed to devotees only once every 40 years — most recently for 48 days from July to August 2019 — drawing millions of pilgrims from across the world.

The temple is one of the 108 Divya Desams glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham: Thirumangai Alvar sang four pasurams in its honor, with Bhoothathalvar and Peyalvar each contributing hymns, placing its sanctity firmly within the Sri Vaishnava devotional canon compiled from the hymns of the twelve Alvars. Temple tradition also recounts that Ramanuja, the towering 11th–12th century Vaishnava acharya, received answers to six probing philosophical questions at this very shrine, an episode still recalled in local oral history.

Architecturally and epigraphically the temple is exceptionally well documented: it preserves roughly 350 inscriptions spanning the Chola, Pandya, Kandavaraya, Chera, Kakatiya, Sambuvaraya, Hoysala, and Vijayanagara periods, attesting to continuous royal patronage from at least the 10th century onward, when the shrine reached its present form, with major renovation under the Cholas around 1053 CE and further expansion (including outer walls and gopurams) under later Chola and Vijayanagara rulers in the 14th century and after. During the turbulence of the late 17th century, in 1688, the temple's principal deity was moved to Udayarpalayam for safekeeping amid fears of invasion, and was returned to Kanchipuram only in 1710. In the colonial era, temple tradition remembers the British general Robert Clive as having donated a valuable necklace to the deity. The tradition also credits the 8th–9th century philosopher Adi Sankara with reorganizing worship at Kanchipuram and supporting expansion of this and other Kanchi temples, though details of this episode rest more on tradition than on independent epigraphic proof.

As one of the grandest and most continuously worshipped Divya Desams in the Nadu Nadu (Thondai Nadu) region, the temple remains a major pilgrimage center for Sri Vaishnavas, central both to the sacred geography of Kanchipuram (which uniquely hosts a cluster of Divya Desams around it) and to the living memory of the Alvars' devotional poetry.
