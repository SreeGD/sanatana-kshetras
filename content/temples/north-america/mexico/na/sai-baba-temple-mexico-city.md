Wikipedia's "Hinduism in Mexico" article records that a Sai Baba temple, alongside a separate, unnamed Vaishnav temple, was constructed in Mexico City by the Sangam Organization — evidently a Hindu community body distinct from the Indian Association of Querétaro, which this directory separately documents as the sponsor of Mexico's first Ram Temple (the Sanatana Mandir) in Querétaro. This Mexico City temple is presumably dedicated to Sai Baba of Shirdi, the syncretic Maharashtrian saint venerated across Hindu (and some Muslim) devotional traditions in India and its diaspora as a manifestation of divine grace transcending sectarian lines.

Mexico City, as the country's capital and largest metropolis, plausibly hosts a broader and more organizationally diverse Hindu presence than the smaller, single-temple diaspora community documented in Querétaro, consistent with the source's further note that "there are several centers from some other worshipers as well" in the capital. However, no address, founding date, founders, or further institutional history could be confirmed for this specific temple in this pass, and this entry should be treated as provisional pending fuller sourcing.
