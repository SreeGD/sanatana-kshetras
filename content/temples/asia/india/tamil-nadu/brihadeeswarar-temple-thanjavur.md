Built by the Chola emperor Rajaraja I between 1003 and 1010 CE, the Brihadeeswarar Temple is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 1987 as part of the Great Living Chola Temples, extended 2004) and one of the great achievements of Dravidian temple architecture. Its 16-storey vimanam (tower) rises 63.4 metres (208 ft) and is capped by a single monolithic granite block of roughly 80 tons, topped by a further 25-ton octagonal cupola. Extensive Tamil inscriptions on its walls record the temple's original endowments, administration, and the era's dance and music traditions in unusually precise historical detail.
