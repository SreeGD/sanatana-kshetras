Founded in 1970 by Alagappa Alagappan and fellow devotees who began by meeting in his living room, the Hindu Temple Society of North America built its permanent home in Flushing, Queens on a site that had previously held a Russian Orthodox church, giving the exterior a South Indian temple facade in an unlikely New York City setting. The temple was completed and consecrated on July 4, 1977 — making it, by most accounts, the second-oldest Hindu temple in the United States built by Indian immigrants, after the Pittsburgh Sri Venkateswara Temple — and it was reconsecrated in 2009 after decades of growth. Though centered on Ganesha (giving it its popular name, "the Ganesh Temple"), the sanctum houses more than 40 sacred images spanning Vaishnava, Shaiva and Shakta traditions, including Venkateswara, Lakshmi, Shiva, Parvati and Durga. Long led by president Dr. Uma Mysorekar (since 1994), the temple has become a major cultural anchor for New York's Hindu community, running a temple canteen since 1993 that serves roughly 4,000 people a week and a youth education center, the Ganesha Patasala, opened in October 1998.
