Tirta Empul, near Tampaksiring in Bali's Gianyar Regency, is one of the island's most important Hindu water temples, built around a natural spring traditionally dated to 962 CE during the Warmadewa dynasty. Dedicated to Vishnu, the temple is centered on the belief that its spring produces holy water (amritha, "water of immortality"), and it remains one of Bali's most actively visited sites for melukat, a ritual purification bath performed by moving through a row of carved spouts in the temple's pools.

The temple's continuing importance to Balinese public life is reflected in a villa built on the hill overlooking it for Indonesian President Sukarno's 1954 visit, and it remains a major pilgrimage destination today, drawing both devout Balinese Hindus performing purification rites and visitors participating in the same ritual out of curiosity or spiritual interest. In 2017 Indonesian health authorities investigated reports of water pollution at the spring after E. coli was detected, a reminder of the pressures heavy visitation places on an actively used sacred water source.
