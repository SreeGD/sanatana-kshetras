The Hare Krishna Cultural Centre is ISKCON's (International Society for Krishna Consciousness) presence in central Dublin, now based at 83 Middle Abbey Street, Dublin 1. It represents a second, distinct node of organized Hindu worship in Dublin alongside the Ananda Sidhi Vinayaka Temple in the suburb of Kingswood, Dublin 24, already documented elsewhere in this directory — together reflecting the growth of Ireland's Hindu community across both the city centre and its suburbs.

ISKCON's presence in Dublin dates to spring 1973, when devotees led by Prabhavisnu first arrived in the city. The community's first temple opened in 1978 at Belvedere Place, where it registered ISKCON as an Irish charity, before a 1982 move to Castlefield House in Templeogue, where Sri Sri Radha Madhava deities were installed in 1983 as Ireland's first Radha Krishna temple. Over the following two decades the congregation relocated repeatedly across Dublin and even briefly into County Meath, before settling into its current combined temple-and-restaurant premises on Middle Abbey Street, building on the Govinda's vegetarian restaurant chain the community launched in Dublin in 1998. Today the centre follows the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition founded in the West by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, holds daily darshan from noon to 8 pm, and reports more than 25 festivals a year and over 500 community members.
