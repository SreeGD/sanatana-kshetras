Founded in 1989 by Tamil refugees who had fled the Sri Lankan civil war and settled in the Westphalian steel town of Hamm, this temple began as a small shrine in an apartment basement before moving to a converted commercial laundry building in 1992. As the local Tamil community grew through the 1990s, it raised funds — reportedly around €1.5-1.7 million — to build a purpose-built Dravidian-style (South Indian) temple on the same site, with a foundation stone laid in March 2000 and full consecration performed on 7 July 2002. The project was led by priest Siva Sri Arumugam Paskarakurukkal, trained at the Kamakshi Amman Temple in Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu, after which Hamm's temple and its presiding goddess, Kamakshi, are modeled; a local Hamm architect, Heinz-Rainer Eichhorst, designed the building, while sculptors brought from India carved its more than 200 deity figures and its roughly 17-metre gopuram tower.

The temple has since become one of the most visible symbols of the Tamil diaspora in Germany and is often cited as one of the largest Hindu temples in continental Europe by floor area. In 2017 the state of North Rhine-Westphalia granted its operating association status as a corporation under public law, an unusual formal recognition for a Hindu institution in Germany. Following several months of renovation, the temple was re-consecrated on 29 May 2026 in a ceremony attended by roughly 400 people and priests from abroad — part of a re-consecration cycle that, per South Indian Agamic tradition, recurs roughly every twelve years.
