Chilkur Balaji Temple, a 14th-century shrine to Venkateswara (Vishnu) in Chilkur village near Osman Sagar lake on Hyderabad's southwestern edge, is popularly known across India as "Visa Balaji" or the "Visa Temple." The nickname reflects a widespread, well-documented modern practice of devotees — particularly those seeking H-1B and other overseas visas — visiting to pray for successful visa outcomes, a phenomenon that has drawn sustained media coverage.

Institutionally, the temple is distinctive for maintaining no hundi (donation box) at all, one of only a small number of Hindu temples known to follow this practice, and for a stated policy of treating all devotees equally with no separate VIP or expedited darshan arrangements. Its central ritual asks devotees to circle the inner shrine 11 times while making a wish, then to return and complete 108 circumambulations once that wish is fulfilled — a practice closely associated with its visa-related reputation. The temple's other major observance is its annual Brahmotsavam festival.
