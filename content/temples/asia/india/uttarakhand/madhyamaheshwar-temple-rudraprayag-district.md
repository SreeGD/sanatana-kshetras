Madhyamaheshwar is dedicated to Shiva in the form of his middle body/navel (madhya), one of the five body-part manifestations that anchor the Panch Kedar legend. Tradition holds that after the Pandavas' victory at Kurukshetra, seeking to atone for killing their kin, they pursued Shiva through the Garhwal Himalayas as he fled in the form of a bull; when Bhima caught hold of the bull, it is said to have plunged into the earth and resurfaced in five places as five parts of its body, with Madhyamaheshwar marking the navel. As with the other Panch Kedar sites, no independently documented founder or construction date survives outside this tradition; the sanctum enshrines a navel-shaped black-stone lingam within a temple built in the North Indian Himalayan style.

The shrine sits above Gaundar village in the upper Madhmaheshwar valley and is reached by a trek of roughly 16-18 km from the roadhead at Ransi village or from Aktoli Dhar, near Ukhimath. It opens for worship with the arrival of summer after the winter snows recede and remains accessible through October or November, after which it closes for the season and its deity is carried in procession down to Ukhimath for continued worship, mirroring the seasonal pattern followed at the other high-altitude Panch Kedar and Char Dham shrines.
