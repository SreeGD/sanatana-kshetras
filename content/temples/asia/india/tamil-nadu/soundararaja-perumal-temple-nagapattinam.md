Thirunagai, on the Nagapattinam coast where the Kaveri delta meets the Bay of Bengal, is unusual among the 108 Divya Desams for being sung of a deity said to appear in three forms within a single sanctum: reclining as Ranganathan, seated as Govindarajan, and standing as Soundararajan, the form that gives the temple its common name, Soundararaja Perumal Koil. Local tradition holds that the town's very name - Naga-pattinam, "town of the serpent" - recalls Adisesha, the celestial serpent on whom Vishnu reclines, coming here to worship the Lord, and that the deity revealed himself in this place to the sage Markandeya, to Dhruva, and to the earth goddess Bhudevi in her distress.

The temple is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham through the hymns of Thirumangai Alvar, the 8th-century Vaishnava poet-saint whose compositions cover an unusually large share of the Chola Nadu Divya Desams. As a port town, Nagapattinam was for centuries a hub of maritime trade and of Buddhist as well as Hindu institutions, and the present Vishnu temple reflects building activity credited to the Medieval Cholas, with substantial later renovation under the Thanjavur Nayaks and, still later, the Thanjavur Maratha rulers, whose patronage of Vaishnava institutions across the delta is well attested in inscriptions and temple records even where the details for this particular shrine remain thinly documented in easily accessible secondary sources. The temple's Chittirai car festival, when the towering temple chariot is drawn through the streets of the old town, remains its best-attended annual event today.
