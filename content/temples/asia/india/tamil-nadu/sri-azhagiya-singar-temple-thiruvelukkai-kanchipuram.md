Sri Azhagiya Singar Temple at Thiruvelukkai, one of the fourteen Divya Desams within Kanchipuram, enshrines Vishnu in his Narasimha (man-lion) form, worshipped here under the gentle epithet Azhagiya Singar — "the beautiful lion" — seated facing west beside his consort Amruthavalli, with a separate shrine for Garuda completing the temple's three principal sanctums. The temple's foundational legend holds that when Brahma undertook a great yagna, the goddess Saraswati, aggrieved at being excluded, sent a host of demons to disrupt the sacrifice; when even the fearsome asura Kapalika was dispatched to wreck the ritual, Vishnu intervened by assuming the Narasimha form to destroy him, thereby ensuring the yagna's completion and sanctifying the site.

Thiruvelukkai holds an unusually rich place in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, being glorified by no fewer than four Alvars — Peyalvar, Bhoothathalvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, and Thirumangai Alvar — whose combined hymns mark it as a site of sustained devotional attention from the earliest generations of Tamil Vaishnava poet-saints. Architecturally, the temple's Dravidian-style structure, three-tiered rajagopuram, and granite enclosure are traced to a Pallava-period origin in the late 8th century CE, with subsequent Medieval Chola patronage recorded in inscriptions from the reigns of Rajadhiraja Chola and Kulothunga Chola I, and further embellishment credited to the Vijayanagara kings who supported many of Kanchipuram's Vishnu shrines.

As a Narasimha shrine within the larger constellation of Kanchipuram's Divya Desams — a town otherwise dominated by forms of Vishnu as Varadaraja, Ulagalantha Perumal, and Adi Kesava — Thiruvelukkai preserves a distinct devotional thread centered on Vishnu's fierce, protective aspect, and remains an active site of worship for Sri Vaishnava pilgrims tracing the Alvars' sacred geography through the town.
