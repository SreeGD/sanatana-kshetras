Langalbandh, on the banks of the old Brahmaputra River in Narayanganj District southeast of Dhaka, is one of the largest Hindu river pilgrimage sites in Bangladesh and marks the historic confluence of the old Brahmaputra and Meghna rivers — a sangam whose sanctity long predates the rivers' modern courses. Its name, meaning "the place where the plough stopped," reflects the site's founding legend: that Parashurama, the axe-wielding avatar of Vishnu, came here to bathe away the sin of having killed his mother Renuka at his father's command, his plough furrowing the riverbank as he sought release. Unlike most entries in this directory, Langalbandh centers not on one named temple but on a complex of bathing ghats — including the named Annapurna Ghat and Gandhi Ghat — around which numerous smaller temples and ashrams have grown up over time.

The site's living significance is immense: its annual Ashtami Snan, or Punya Snan, held on the eighth day of the Bengali month of Chaitra, draws an estimated 1.5 million pilgrims from Bangladesh, India, and Nepal, making it one of the largest single-day Hindu gatherings in South Asia outside the Kumbh Mela cycle. That scale has also brought tragedy: a stampede during the March 27, 2015 observance killed ten devotees and injured roughly thirty more, a stark reminder of the crowd-management challenges facing a pilgrimage of this magnitude, and a detail worth bearing in mind for anyone planning to attend.
