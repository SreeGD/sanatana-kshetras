Candi Cetho stands in Dusun Cetho, Gumeng village, on the western slope of Mount Lawu at roughly 1,495 meters elevation, near the Central Java/East Java provincial border and a short distance uphill from the similarly dated Candi Sukuh, already documented separately in this directory. Built circa 1451-1470 during the waning years of the Majapahit empire, Cetho combines Hindu-Buddhist elements with the native Indonesian punden berundak (stepped-pyramid) architectural form, and Javanese religion and art of this late period had already diverged noticeably from Indian precepts, with only sparse historical records of the era's specific ceremonies and beliefs surviving.

Rather than centering on a single anthropomorphic deity, the site is traditionally associated with the veneration of ancestral spirits and figures such as Sabdapalon and King Brawijaya V, reflecting a syncretism between Hindu-Javanese and indigenous Indonesian religious traditions. The temple's wooden pavilions (pendapa) remain in active ceremonial use today, hosting religious observances including Tawur Agung. A major restoration campaign in 1975-1976, led by Sudjono Homardhani, has been criticized by scholars for lacking archaeological rigor and for introducing non-original elements, including wooden structures mimicking Balinese temple design — a caveat worth bearing in mind when assessing which parts of the present complex are historically original.
