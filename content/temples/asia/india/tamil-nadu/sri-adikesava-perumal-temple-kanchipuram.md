Sri Adikesava Perumal Temple, popularly known by the name of its locality, Ashtabujakaram ("the place of the eight-armed one"), enshrines Vishnu as Ashta Bhuja Perumal, depicted with eight arms, alongside his consort Lakshmi as Alamelu Mangai. Temple tradition holds that this is one of the sites where Brahma performed penance and worshipped Vishnu, and local legend attributes the temple's founding to the Thondaiman chieftain popularly called Vayiramoghan.

The shrine is one of the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alvars in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham; Peyalvar and Thirumangai Alvar both composed pasurams glorifying the deity here, placing Ashtabujakaram firmly within the sacred geography celebrated by the twelve Vaishnava saint-poets. Architecturally the temple follows Dravidian conventions and is believed to have been renovated under the Pallavas in the late 8th century CE, with subsequent contributions from the Medieval Cholas and the Vijayanagara kings. Three inscriptions survive on its walls: two dated to the reign of Kulothunga Chola I (1070–1120 CE) and one to Rajendra Chola (1018–1054 CE), documenting continuous royal patronage over several centuries.

Situated only about two kilometers from the great Varadaraja Perumal Temple, Ashtabujakaram forms part of the dense cluster of Divya Desams that makes Kanchipuram unique among Divya Desam sites, and it remains an active center of daily worship and festival observance today.
