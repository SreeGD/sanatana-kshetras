## Origin and legend

The Puliyoor Mahavishnu Temple, also called Thripuliyoor, enshrines Vishnu as Mayapiran (also known as Thripuliyoorappan), with consort Lakshmi worshipped as Porkodi Nachiyar. It is counted among five ancient shrines of the Chengannur area of Kerala that local tradition links to the Mahabharata: each of the five Pandava brothers is said to have built one temple during a pilgrimage taken after the coronation of Parikshit, with this one attributed to Bhima.

## Historical significance

Constructed in the Kerala style of temple architecture, the temple is administered by the Travancore Devaswom Board. Its festival calendar includes a ten-day annual festival in the Malayalam month of Medam, an Aratu festival in Margazhi, Kavadiattam observances in the month of Thai, and Vaikuntha Ekadashi and Thiruvonam celebrations shared with its sibling Chengannur-area shrines.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Puliyoor is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. The earliest literary references to the temple appear in the hymns of Nammalvar (c. 800 CE); Thirumangai Alvar also mentions Thirupuliyur in his Siriya Thirumadal. Together these place the temple among the 108 Divya Desams glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by the Vaishnava Alvar saints.
