Built in late 1958 in the Petit Bazar district of Saint-André, on Réunion's northeast coast, the Temple Mardévirin - also known as Temple Patley Karly, Kovil Moutoussamy Sangly, and (on its own Wikipedia page) Temple de Petit Bazar - was registered as a French historic monument on 17 September 2010, reflecting its architectural and cultural significance among the island's Tamil ("malbar") Hindu temples built by the descendants of 19th-century indentured Indian labourers.

French Wikipedia's article names Maurice Sinama, described as a wealthy entrepreneur of Petit Bazar, as the temple's builder/funder, while its infobox separately credits an architect named Michel Sinama - most plausibly two different people in two different roles (funder and architect) rather than a naming error, though this reading is not independently confirmed. The article does not identify the temple's presiding deity. It stands close to, and shares an informal name with, the separately documented Temple de Petit Bazar in the same Saint-André neighbourhood - itself a likely source of local naming confusion between the two distinct temples.
