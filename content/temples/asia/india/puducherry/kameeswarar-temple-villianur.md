About nine kilometers south of Puducherry town, on the road toward Villupuram, the Kameeswarar Temple at Villianur is one of the region's principal Shiva shrines, worshipped as Kameeswarar in lingam form alongside his consort Parvati as Kokilambigai. The original structure is believed to date to the 12th century CE, with the temple subsequently expanded and endowed under the Medieval Cholas and later the Vijayanagara Empire; a particularly significant grant of roughly 850 acres of land is recorded from the 14th-century Sambuvaraya chieftain Raja Narayana Sambuvarayar. The present complex, built in Dravidian style within concentric granite-walled enclosures covering about two acres, remains one of the two principal landmarks of Villianur, alongside the town's well-known Catholic shrine, Our Lady of Lourdes.

Six daily rituals are conducted at the temple, which today falls under the administration of the Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department of the Government of Puducherry. Its major annual festival, the Brahmotsavam, is celebrated in the Tamil month of Aadi (July/August), alongside weekly Monday and Friday observances and monthly new-moon and full-moon rites. One distinctive local practice is the absence of oil anointing during abhishekam, a departure from the customary ritual at many other Shiva temples in the region.
