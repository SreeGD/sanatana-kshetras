The smallest and least visited of the eleven Thirunangur Divya Desams, this shrine is dedicated to Perarulalan, depicted standing and facing east, whose festival image is known locally as Hemaranngar or Chemponarrangar - names built on the Tamil word chempon, "gold." Local tradition explains the temple's very name, Thiruchemponsey, by way of a story in which a devout Brahmin, having come into a quantity of gold, used it to build this shrine in gratitude, though the story survives chiefly in local telling rather than in an independently documented historical record.

Despite its modest scale, Thirusemponseikovil holds full standing among the 108 Divya Desams thanks to the mangalasasanam verses composed here by Thirumangai Alvar and preserved in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. As one of the eleven shrines of the Thirunangur group, it shares in the annual Thirumangai Alvar Mangalasasana Utsavam each Tamil month of Thai, when its festival deity is carried together with the other ten in the Garudasevai procession - the single occasion each year when this otherwise quiet shrine takes centre stage alongside its larger neighbours.
