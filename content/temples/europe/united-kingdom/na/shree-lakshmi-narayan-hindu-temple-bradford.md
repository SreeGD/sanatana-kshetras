Bradford's Shree Lakshmi Narayan Hindu Temple was formally opened on 24 May 2007 in a ceremony attended by Queen Elizabeth II and Prince Philip, an unusual mark of state recognition for a British Hindu temple. Built at a cost of roughly £3 million and faced in local Yorkshire stone by Yorkshire-based architects and builders, the temple was designed, in its own community's words, to reflect "the position of Hindus as part of contemporary Yorkshire society" through a deliberately modern architectural idiom rather than a direct import of a South Asian temple style.

Named for its presiding deities, Lakshmi and Narayan (Vishnu), the temple is nonetheless unusual among diaspora Hindu temples for housing images of most of the major deities revered across Hindu traditions, reflecting a common pattern in Britain where a single community temple must serve worshippers from many different regional and sectarian backgrounds rather than followers of one specific deity alone. It is recognized as the largest Hindu temple (mandir) in Northern England, serving as a major hub for Bradford's Hindu community and West Yorkshire's wider Hindu population.
