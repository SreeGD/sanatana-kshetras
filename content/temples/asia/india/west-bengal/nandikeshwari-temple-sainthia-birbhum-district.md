Nandikeshwari Temple, in the town of Sainthia in Birbhum district, is counted among Bengal's Shakti Peethas as the spot where tradition holds the collarbone of Sati fell after her body was cut apart. The goddess's name, Nandikeshwari, is explained locally as "one worshipped by Nandi" — Shiva's bull mount — linking the site to the broader Shaiva-Shakta mythology surrounding Sati's dismemberment. The temple's importance to the town runs deep enough to have shaped Sainthia's own older name: the settlement was previously known as Nandipur, combining "Nandi" from the temple's name with the Sanskrit "pur" for town, a naming pattern echoed elsewhere in this directory by Ketugram's earlier identity as "Bahula."

The present temple structure dates to 1913 (1320 in the Bengali calendar), though no source checked names the individual or family who funded or built it, nor is anything documented about what may have stood at the site earlier. The central image is a black stone murti, now tinged reddish from generations of sindur (vermilion) offerings, crowned in silver with three golden eyes. The temple grounds have grown into a small pilgrimage complex in their own right, hosting subsidiary shrines to Ram-Sita, Shiva, Saraswati, Lakshmi-Ganesha, Narayana, Radha-Govinda, and Hanuman, a statue of horses within the complex, and a sacred banyan tree where visitors tie threads in hope of having their wishes fulfilled.
