The Jeshoreshwari Kali Temple, in Ishwaripur village of Satkhira district (within the older historical region after which it is named, greater Jessore, though the modern city of Jessore/Jashore lies in a separate present-day district), is one of the network of Shakti Peethas, venerated as the place where Sati's palms and soles fell — an identity preserved in its name, "goddess of Jessore." The temple's own account traces an original establishment to the 13th century, attributed to a figure recorded only as "Analee," a name this entry cannot further verify or contextualize from the sources available, so it is offered here as recorded tradition rather than a settled historical fact. Popular and tourism literature about the temple often links it instead to the 16th-century Bengali zamindar Raja Pratapaditya, a connection frequently repeated in secondary accounts of the site; that link could not be confirmed from the source consulted for this entry and is therefore not asserted as fact here.

The temple's more recent history is better documented: the structure suffered a partial demolition in 1971, during Bangladesh's Liberation War period, and underwent a major restoration completed in 2021, preserving its Ratna-style architecture. That restoration drew international attention when Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi visited and prayed at the temple in March 2021, donating a ceremonial crown that was later reported stolen in October 2024. The temple remains under the administration of the Government of Bangladesh and continues as an active site of daily worship, with Kali Puja as its principal annual festival alongside Durga Puja.
