Uthamar Koil, anciently known as Thirukkarambanoor, is one of only two Chola Nadu Divya Desams (the other being the Hara Saabha Vimochana Perumal Temple at Thirukkandiyur) where Vishnu, Shiva, and Brahma are each enshrined in their own consecrated sanctum within a single temple complex. The presiding Vishnu form here, Purushothaman — popularly "Uthamar," meaning "the noble/supreme one" — gives the temple its common name, and is worshipped alongside his consort Poornavalli Thayar.

The temple's cluster of legends centers on Shiva's atonement. According to the sthala purana, after Shiva severed one of Brahma's five heads in a fit of anger, the skull adhered to his hand as the sin of Brahmahatti (killing a Brahmin, since Brahma is considered one). Shiva wandered as the mendicant Bhikshadanar seeking alms to expiate the sin, and it was Lakshmi — worshipped here as Poornavalli — who gave him alms and ended his torment, a deed said to have earned her that name ("the completing one"). A separate strand of the temple's legend holds that Vishnu himself once manifested as a Kadamba tree at this spot, from which the older place name Karambanur (or Thirukarambur) is said to derive; Brahma is said to have performed a sacred bathing ritual here, filling the temple's Kathamba Theertham tank.

Historically, local temple-history sources cite an 11th-century inscription naming the site "Thirukarambur" and attributing patronage to the Chola emperor Rajendra Chola I, alongside a further land-grant inscription from Rajathiraja Chola I — though these citations have not been independently checked against a formal epigraphical publication. Later renovation is attributed, with varying corroboration across sources, to the Vijayanagara empire and the Madurai Nayaks; the temple underwent a Maha Kumbhabhishekam (consecration) as recently as September 2019. The temple is glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by Thirumangai Alvar, whose hymn is understood to be the origin of the deity's popular epithet "Uthamar."
