Somarama, the Someswara Swamy Temple in the Gunupudi locality of Bhimavaram (West Godavari district), is one of the five Pancharama Kshetras, the group of Shiva temples that share a common origin legend centered on the demon Tarakasura. Tradition holds that Tarakasura's invincible Atma-linga, a gift from Shiva, was broken apart by Kartikeya using the agneyastra on Vishnu's counsel, and that its five fragments were consecrated at five separate sites. At Bhimavaram, local legend credits Chandra, the moon god (Soma), with installing and worshipping the fragment that fell here in order to absolve himself of a sin -- the origin of the deity's name, Someswara, "Lord of Soma." Devotees also describe the lingam as visibly changing shade across the lunar cycle, darker near the new moon and lighter near the full moon, a piece of temple lore rather than a documented phenomenon. The temple's consort goddess is worshipped as Sri Rajarajeshwari Ammavaru, and a sacred tank, Chandra Kundam, stands before the shrine.

The temple's documented history is thinner in the sources available than for some of its sister Pancharama shrines: the earliest confirmed inscription dates to the reign of the Eastern Chalukya king Saktivarma I, roughly 1001-1011 CE, which anchors a general construction estimate in the 10th to early 11th century, with architectural evidence suggesting later renovation phases. Beyond that reign-linked inscription, no further dated epigraphy or detailed construction chronicle for the Someswara temple specifically was found in the sources consulted -- a contrast with Amararama or Draksharamam, where a longer run of dated inscriptions survives. The town of Bhimavaram itself carries a separate popular tradition connecting its founding to the Eastern Chalukya king Bhima (reigned roughly 890-918 CE), but this is a claim about the town rather than a documented claim about the Someswara temple's own construction. Today the temple functions as an active place of worship, with Maha Shivaratri drawing its largest crowds.
