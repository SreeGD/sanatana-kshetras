Ground was broken for this mandir in Stafford, Texas — within Fort Bend County, part of the Greater Houston metropolitan area — with a foundation-stone ceremony in September 2000, and construction of the traditional stone shikharbaddha structure ran from March 2002 to July 2004. It was consecrated on 25 July 2004, one of the earlier large-scale BAPS mandirs built in the American South, assembled from roughly 33,000 pieces of marble and limestone hand-carved by artisans in India and erected, per BAPS practice, without any structural iron or steel. The central shrine enshrines Bhagwan Swaminarayan with Gunatitanand Swami, alongside Radha-Krishna, Sita-Rama, Shiva-Parvati, Hanuman, and Ganapati, and the complex has since become a hub for Houston's large Gujarati and broader Hindu community. A 2021 lawsuit alleged labor-law violations in connection with the temple's original construction; that dispute is noted here but not assessed further.
