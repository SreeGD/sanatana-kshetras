Distinct from the Hindu Temple of Greater Chicago in nearby Lemont, this BAPS Shri Swaminarayan Mandir traces its Chicago-area roots to 1972, when small groups of devotees began gathering in members' homes under the guidance of Pramukh Swami Maharaj, who visited the community in 1974, 1977 and 1980. An initial mandir opened in Glen Ellyn in 1984 before the growing congregation acquired its current 27-acre site in Bartlett in 1994. A haveli (cultural and community hall) was inaugurated on October 29, 2000, and the mandir proper was consecrated and opened on August 7, 2004. Built in traditional Shilpa Shastra style from hand-carved Italian marble and Turkish limestone — carved by roughly 2,000 craftsmen in Rajasthan and shipped to Illinois for assembly — the 22,000-square-foot, 78-foot-high structure features 16 domes, 151 pillars, 117 arches, five pinnacles and four balconies. Its central shrine enshrines Bhagwan Swaminarayan alongside Gunatitanand Swami, with additional shrines to Radha-Krishna, Rama-Sita and Shiva-Parvati. The mandir has drawn architectural recognition beyond the Hindu community, winning a Chicago Building Congress Merit Award in 2004 and being named one of 150 Great Places in Illinois by the American Institute of Architects.
