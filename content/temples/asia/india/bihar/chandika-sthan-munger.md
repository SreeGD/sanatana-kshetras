Chandika Sthan, in the Basudevpur area of Munger, is counted among the 51 Shakti Peethas of the subcontinent: local tradition holds that when Sati's dismembered body fell to earth, her left eye landed at this spot, consecrating it as a seat of the goddess worshipped here as Chandika (Chandi Mata) — a form regarded locally as equal in stature to the Kamakhya temple near Guwahati. The temple carries a particular regional reputation as a place of healing for eye ailments, a belief evidently linked to the eye-based origin legend itself. A further local legend associates the site with Karna, the Mahabharata's tragic hero and king of Anga (the ancient name for the Munger region), who is said to have worshipped the goddess here daily and given away some 50 kilograms of gold in charity — a story that reflects Munger's identification with ancient Anga rather than an independently documented historical event.

No dated inscription, founding king, or construction record for the present temple structure was found in the sources consulted, which is typical of many Shakti Peethas whose sanctity rests on Puranic tradition rather than epigraphy. Chandika Sthan remains an active and significant pilgrimage site in the Anga region of Bihar, with Sharad Navaratri drawing its largest crowds of worshippers.
