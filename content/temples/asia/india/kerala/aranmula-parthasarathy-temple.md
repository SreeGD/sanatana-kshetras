## Origin and legend

The Aranmula Parthasarathy Temple enshrines Krishna as Parthasarathy, Arjuna's charioteer in the Mahabharata, in a six-foot Vishvarupa-pose image traditionally said to depict the cosmic form Krishna revealed to Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukshetra. Temple legend holds that the deity's image arrived at the site on a raft made of six pieces of bamboo, giving the place its name Aranmula ("aaru" + "mula," six bamboos), and that the temple itself was built by the Pandava prince Arjuna following the Mahabharata war -- one of five Pandava-founded shrines in this stretch of the Pamba valley. An older tradition also places the original shrine near Sabarimala before it was relocated to the present site.

## Historical significance

Built in classic Kerala temple style with sloping roofs, ornately carved wooden panels, and pillars depicting scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata, the temple's walls also carry murals dated to the 18th century. It plays an important ceremonial role in the Sabarimala pilgrimage: the sacred ornaments of Lord Ayyappa (Thiruvabharanam), carried in procession from Pandalam to Sabarimala each pilgrimage season, halt at Aranmula along the route. The temple is equally famous for the Aranmula Boat Race (Uthrattathi Vallamkali), held on the Pamba river during the Onam season, in which ornately decorated snake boats over a hundred feet long compete before the deity.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Aranmula is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. Nammalvar, one of the twelve Vaishnava Alvar saints, revered this temple in his hymns, and Thirumangai Alvar composed eleven verses in praise of the deity, together placing Aranmula within the canon of 108 Divya Desams recorded in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham.
