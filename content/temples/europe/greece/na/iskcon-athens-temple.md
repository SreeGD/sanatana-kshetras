ISKCON's presence in Athens dates to 1992, when Italian ISKCON devotee Dayanidhi Prabhu brought a murti (sacred image) of Srila A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada to the Greek capital and held an installation ceremony, establishing one of the earliest Hindu ISKCON temples in Greece. That initial foundation grew over the following decades, and in 2019 Dayanidhi Prabhu opened the current ISKCON temple in the center of Athens, where it now holds classes each morning and evening and a weekly Sunday feast open to the public.

A significant milestone came in 2021, when ISKCON Greece received official state recognition, granted legal status as a "house of prayer" — the Greek Ministry of Religion's formal category roughly comparable to church status — by the Greek state. This recognition marks the temple as one of the more institutionally established Hindu religious organizations in Greece, a country with a historically small Hindu population, and situates it as Athens's confirmed civic-level ISKCON presence within this directory's European coverage.
