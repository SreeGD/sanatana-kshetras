ISKCON Juhu's Sri Sri Radha Rasabihari Mandir occupies Hare Krishna Land, a plot Srila Prabhupada resolved to develop into a temple in Juhu, Bombay (now Mumbai), as part of his broader push in the early 1970s to establish major ISKCON temple complexes in India. According to Prabhupada's own biography, the man who had originally sold the land to ISKCON attempted to swindle it back from the devotees; Prabhupada persisted through the ensuing dispute and ultimately prevailed. In 1972 Prabhupada appointed his disciple Giriraja Swami as president of ISKCON Bombay and a trustee of the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust, and Giriraja Swami went on to oversee the development of Hare Krishna Land into the temple complex that stands today. Bombay was one of only a handful of major Indian temple projects — alongside Vrindavan, Hyderabad, and the Mayapur world headquarters — that Prabhupada personally focused his attention on establishing during his final years leading the movement.

The temple's presiding deities are worshipped as Sri Sri Radha Rasabihari. Today the Juhu complex functions as one of ISKCON's active centers in western India, following the standard round of daily darshan, kirtan, and prasadam distribution observed across ISKCON temples worldwide, with Janmashtami as its major annual festival.
