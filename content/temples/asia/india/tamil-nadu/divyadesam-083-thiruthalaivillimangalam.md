Thiruthalaivillimangalam, in the Pandya country, is one of the celebrated 108 Divya Desams and is known as one of the two shrines that together form the Irattai Thirupathi, the 'twin sacred abodes,' alongside the neighbouring Devapiran temple, a distinction by which two temples are honoured as a single divine pair. Here the presiding Lord bears the name Aravinda Lochanan, the 'lotus-eyed one,' worshipped with consorts Sridevi and Bhudevi; tradition holds that the sage Suprabha, following Vishnu along a lotus pond, requested the Lord to remain there, whence the name 'the one who appeared from lotuses.' The roughly one-acre Dravidian-style complex, enclosed by granite walls, is distinguished by an unusually flat rajagopuram, and its sanctum houses a shaligrama-stone image; subsidiary shrines within the complex honour Senai Mudaliyar, Garuda, Venugopala, the Srivaishnava teacher Manavalamamunigal, and the Alvars. Within the Nava Tirupathi's Navagraha associations, the temple is reckoned a Ketu Sthalam. As an Irattai Thirupathi, this shrine is worshipped in close companionship with its paired Divya Desam, the two together drawing pilgrims who seek the darshan of both in one sacred sojourn. The glory of this place was sung by Nammalwar, the greatest of the Alwars, whose Tiruvaymoli enshrines Thalaivillimangalam within the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and it is through his hymns that the temple holds its place among the abodes that the Alwars sanctified. Lying within the constellation of Divya Desams clustered near Nammalwar's native Thirukkurugur, the temple partakes of the intense, intimate devotion that the saint directed toward the Lord of these Pandya shrines, and worship follows the Tenkalai tradition to this day.

## Festivals

Thiruthalaivillimangalam, one of the twin shrines known as Irattai Tirupati and counted among the Nava Tirupati, traditionally observes the shared Nava Tirupati festival cycle in which the nine Tamiraparani Vishnu temples are venerated together. The Garuda Sevai in Vaikasi (May-June), part of the wider Nammalvar festival that brings together processional deities from all nine Nava Tirupati temples, is a major highlight. The annual Brahmotsavam is celebrated with great devotion, with ceremonial processions among its features. Vaikunta Ekadasi is kept with special reverence, accompanied by recitation of Nammalvar's Tiruvaymozhi, the alvar closely associated with this sacred land. Pavithrotsavam and a Theppotsavam on the temple tank are also part of the traditional calendar. The Tiruvadhyayana Utsavam, with its Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitations, along with Tamil New Year and Pongal observances, mark the devotional year at this Pandya Nadu temple.

## Location & Access

The temple is located at Thiruthalaivillimangalam in the Pandya Nadu region, near the Thamiraparani river in the Thoothukudi (Tuticorin) district of Tamil Nadu, and forms one half of the Irattai Tirupati within the Nava Tirupati group. Tirunelveli and Thoothukudi are the nearest major towns and transport hubs offering road and rail connections. The temple follows four daily pujas: Kalasanthi (8 AM), Uchikalam (12 PM), Sayarakshai (6 PM) and Ardha Jamam (8 PM). Many pilgrims worship here as part of the Nava Tirupati circuit.
