Tholaivillimangalam is the Ketu sthalam of the Nava Tirupathi, the group of nine Divya Desams devoted to the navagrahas that lie along the Thamiraparani river in the ancient Pandya country. The presiding deity, Aravindalochanar Perumal ("he whose eyes are like lotuses"), stands immediately beside the shrine of Srinivasa Perumal at Thirukkulandhai - the Saturn sthalam - and the two temples together are popularly known as the Irattai Tirupathi, the "twin Tirupathis," sharing a common outer precinct on the river's tranquil bank.

The temple's sthala purana tells of the sage Athreya Suprabha, who while clearing ground for a yagashala (sacrificial hall) came upon a gleaming weighing scale (tarasu) and a bow. The moment he touched them, the two objects transformed into a man and a woman, who explained that they had been cursed by Kubera, the lord of wealth, to remain as inanimate objects, and that only the touch of the sage could release them from the curse - which duly came to pass. The site's sanctity is thus doubly framed, by this curse-and-release legend and by its place in Nammalvar's Nava Tirupathi Pathigam within the Thiruvaimozhi, where the Alvar moves in sequence through each of the nine river-side shrines invoking Vishnu in his different planetary aspects. As with its neighboring Nava Tirupathi shrines, the temple's building history before the medieval Vaishnava revival is not well attested in the sources consulted, and its standing rests principally on scriptural glorification and unbroken local worship.
