A center of Gaudiya Vaishnava practice serving Sydney's Hare Krishna community, ISKCON Sydney's mission traces to May 1970, when its Potts Point premises became, per the temple's own account, "the first Hindu Temple and Cultural Centre to be established in Australia." The community relocated to Paddington by 1971 — the year A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada personally visited Australia and founded the country's first Hare Krishna centre in Sydney — where its presiding deities, Sri Sri Radha Gopinath, were installed on 10 May 1971. After occupying a total of eleven different Sydney premises over the following two decades, the community settled at its present home, 180 Falcon Street in North Sydney, on 18 March 1992.

Officially named Sri Sri Radha Gopinath Mandir (not "Bhaktivedanta Swami Mandir," a name belonging to a different ISKCON temple in Kolkata that this entry previously — and incorrectly — carried), the temple is open daily from 4:30am to 9:15pm and hosts regular public programs and an annual Ratha Yatra chariot festival in the city, part of the same public-kirtan tradition ISKCON introduced in New York and London in the movement's earliest years.
