Krishna Mandir, on Lahore's Ravi Road opposite the city's Timber Market, is one of only two Hindu temples still functioning for public worship in a city that, before the 1947 Partition, had a large Hindu and Sikh population. Managed by Pakistan's Evacuee Trust Property Board, the temple suffered damage amid communal violence that followed the 1992 demolition of the Babri Masjid in India, and the Board later allocated funds for its renovation, with more than half the restoration work completed by mid-2005.

The temple briefly became the subject of a false alarm in 2006, when Pakistani media reported it had been demolished for commercial redevelopment; Pakistan's Foreign Office publicly clarified within weeks that the report was incorrect, and that the property actually demolished lay several kilometers away, with Krishna Mandir itself intact and safe. No founding date for the temple was available in the sources consulted, but it continues today as an active place of Vaishnava worship for Lahore's small remaining Hindu community.
