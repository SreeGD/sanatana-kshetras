Pahari Mandir crowns a hill in the middle of Ranchi, Jharkhand's capital, rising about 350 feet above the surrounding city and visible from much of it -- making it as much a civic landmark and viewpoint as an active Shiva shrine. The hill was known locally as Tiriburu before the colonial period, when the British renamed it "Hanging Gallows" after using the site to execute freedom fighters; that grim history is now balanced by a more celebratory one, since K. C. Das is credited with hoisting independent India's tricolour here on 15 August 1947, an event marked today by a commemorative stone and by the annual re-hoisting of the flag alongside the temple's religious banners each Independence Day and Republic Day.

No documented founding date survives for the Shiva lingam shrine itself in the sources reviewed for this entry; unlike many of the ancient dynastic foundations recorded elsewhere in this directory, Pahari Mandir's best-attested history is comparatively recent and civic rather than royal or scriptural. It remains, nonetheless, one of Ranchi's most visited and recognizable places of worship, drawing large crowds for Maha Shivaratri and through the Shravan month, and is maintained today by a dedicated body, the Pahari Mandir Vikas Samiti.
