Thirunallar's Dharbaranyeswarar Temple, about 5 km from Karaikal town in the Karaikal district of Puducherry Union Territory, is one of Tamil Shaivism's most distinctive pilgrimage sites: a Paadal Petra Sthalam — one of the 275 temples honored in the canonical 7th-century hymns of the Nayanar saints Sambandar and Appar — that is also, uniquely, one of the nine Navagraha temples built around worship of the nine planetary deities. Shiva is enshrined here as the lingam Dharbaranyeswarar, with his consort worshipped as Praneswari Amman, but it is the subsidiary shrine to Shani (Saturn) that draws the temple's largest crowds, particularly on Saturdays and especially when an Amavasya (new moon) falls on one. The surviving stone temple, spread across roughly two acres behind a five-tiered gopuram, is generally dated to the Chola period around the 9th century CE, with further additions credited to the later Vijayanagar rulers.

The temple's Saturn-relief tradition is anchored in the legend of King Nala, a figure whose fortunes, according to the story, collapsed under the astrological affliction of Shani; coming to this site and bathing in its sacred tank is said to have freed him from Saturn's hold, and the temple has functioned ever since as a destination for devotees seeking similar relief from difficult planetary periods (Shani dosha) in their own horoscopes. As at other Paadal Petra Sthalams, the temple's veneration in devotional hymnody by 7th-century poet-saints likely predates the surviving Chola-era stonework by a considerable margin, a common pattern across Tamil Nadu's older Shiva temples.
