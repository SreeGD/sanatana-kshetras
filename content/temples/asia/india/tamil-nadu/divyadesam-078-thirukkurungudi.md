The Azhagiya Nambi Temple at Thirukkurungudi, formally the Vaishnava Nambi and Thirukurungudivalli Nachiar temple, in the Pandya Nadu country at the foot of the Western Ghats, is one of the most celebrated of the 108 Divya Desams. The presiding deity, the Moolavar, is Azhagiya Nambi (Vaishnava Nambi), 'the beautiful and perfect Lord,' with his consort worshipped as Thirukurungudivalli Nachiar; the temple is worshipped here in a unique tradition that venerates Vishnu in three forms across the locality, his beauty and accessibility drawing the devotion of saints and seekers alike. Inscriptions at the temple date to the 10th century, during the Medieval Chola period, recording grants to the shrine; the Nayak period (15th century) saw significant additions including pillared halls, and copper-plate inscriptions from the Vijayanagara period (1456, 1537 and 1592 CE) document further endowments. Built in Dravidian style, the temple's rajagopuram rises some 110 feet (34 metres) across five tiers, and the complex is laid out across five prakarams (enclosures) spanning roughly 7.5 hectares, with a granite perimeter wall enclosing its shrines and water bodies and sculpted pillars dating to the Nayak period. According to the sthala purana, this is a place hallowed by the Lord's intimate presence, and tradition holds it in special esteem as a shrine where the Supreme reveals himself with peculiar grace and approachability. The greatness of Thirukkurungudi is sung by no fewer than four of the Alwar saints in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham: Thirumazhisai Alwar, Nammalwar the foremost of the Alwars, Periyalwar the father-in-law of Andal, and Thirumangai Alwar the prolific singer of the Divya Desams. Their combined hymns mark this abode as one richly extolled within the four thousand verses, a distinction shared by only the most beloved of the sacred shrines. The Lord here is affectionately known as the one who is ever 'beautiful,' and the temple's traditions speak of his delight in the company of his devotees, including the legend in which he himself is said to have received instruction in the path of surrender. Set amid the verdant hills of the Pandya land, Thirukkurungudi remains a great centre of Srivaishnava pilgrimage, where the grace of Azhagiya Nambi is sought as the embodiment of the Lord's perfect and approachable beauty.

## Festivals

Thirukkurungudi, the abode of Azhagiya Nambi, is renowned for a rich and well-attested festival tradition in the Pandya country. Its ten-day Chittirai Utsavam (Brahmotsavam, April-May) features splendid processions of the Lord on successive vahanas around the temple and village, with Prabandham recitation and large crowds of pilgrims. Vaikasi Jestabishekam (June) and Aavani Pavithrotsavam (August-September) follow the customary calendar, while Kaisika Ekadasi is observed annually with particular devotion, and a Float Festival is held in January-February. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is solemnly observed with the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitations and the opening of the Paramapada Vasal, drawing devotees for the auspicious darshan of the Lord. The temple is especially celebrated for its dance and ritual heritage, including traditional Araiyar Sevai. Krishna Janmashtami and Sri Rama Navami are also traditionally kept, making the temple's year a continuous round of devotion.

## Location & Access

Thirukkurungudi is located in the Tirunelveli district of the Pandya Nadu region of Tamil Nadu, at the foot of the Western Ghats, about 45 km from Tirunelveli city and 11 km from Nanguneri. The town lies south of Tirunelveli, the nearest major city and rail junction, from which road connections reach the temple. The temple follows four daily ritual timings (Kalasanthi, Uchikalam, Sayarakshai and Ardha Jamam) and is maintained by the Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department of the Government of Tamil Nadu.
