Kottiyoor, in the forested hills of Kannur district along the Bavali river, is one of Kerala's most distinctive pilgrimage sites precisely because of what it withholds for most of the year: its principal shrine, on the river's eastern bank, is ritually closed and left to revert to forest for eleven months, opening only during an annual festival of roughly four weeks in May and June. Tradition identifies this eastern site, Kizhakkeshwaram, as the location of the Daksha Yaga described in the Shiva Purana — the sacrifice at which Daksha insulted his son-in-law Shiva, prompting Daksha's daughter Sati to immolate herself in grief and rage, an event that in the wider Puranic tradition scatters Sati's remains across the subcontinent to found the network of Shakti Peethas. Across the river, the permanent Vadakkeshwaram temple, dedicated to Shiva, remains open to worshippers throughout the rest of the year.

The annual Kottiyoor Vysakha Mahotsavam, drawing pilgrims from across northern Kerala and beyond into an area otherwise protected as a wildlife sanctuary, has earned the site comparison to Kashmir's Amarnath as one of Hindu India's few major pilgrimages defined by a strictly time-limited access to its holiest point. Outside the festival window, Kottiyoor's eastern shrine is left undisturbed, its temporary structures dismantled, so that the forest along the Bavali briefly reclaims the sanctuary each year before the cycle of worship resumes.
