Seattle's Hare Krishna community traces back to the 1970s, when it grew to around 120 devotees before running into what a 2008 Seattle Times feature (republished by ISKCON's own news service) described as "its own controversies." By 1991, when Harry Terhanian arrived as its new temple president and head priest, the community was, in his own words, on the verge of closing, with "more deities than devotees." Over the following years the temple moved from Capitol Hill to Madrona within Seattle proper, and its congregation regrouped in a "run-down three-bedroom house" in what was then Issaquah, rebuilding gradually as Indian immigrant families joined from the mid-1990s onward.

That revival culminated in August 2008 with the opening of a purpose-built, $4.5 million, 12,200-square-foot temple in Sammamish, in Seattle's eastside suburbs, formally named the Vedic Cultural Center. Today it serves roughly 300 regular worshippers and houses four deity groups — Sri Sri Radha Nila Madhava; Sita Ram Laxman Hanuman; Sri Jagannath Baladev Subhadra; and Sri Sri Gaura Nitai — continuing to operate as ISKCON's Seattle-area center under its official name, the Vedic Cultural Center.
