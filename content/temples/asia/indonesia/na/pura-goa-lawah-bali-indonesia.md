Pura Goa Lawah, on Bali's southeastern coast in Klungkung Regency, is one of the island's Sad Kahyangan Jagad — the six holiest directional sanctuaries of Balinese Hinduism — and is traditionally said to have been founded in the 11th century by the priest Mpu Kuturan. The temple takes its popular name, the "Bat Cave Temple," from a natural cave at the site that houses a large colony of nectar bats, and the shrine complex is built directly around the cave's mouth, integrating the site's striking natural feature into its sacred architecture.

Within its three-part compound of outer, middle, and inner courtyards, the temple houses multiple shrines, including a meru tower dedicated to Shiva. Beyond its religious significance, the site also carries a layer of historical memory: it served as a strategic location during the 1849 War of Kusamba, a conflict between Dutch colonial forces and the Klungkung kingdom. Pura Goa Lawah remains an active place of worship today, commonly visited by pilgrims travelling the eastern Bali temple circuit alongside Pura Besakih.
