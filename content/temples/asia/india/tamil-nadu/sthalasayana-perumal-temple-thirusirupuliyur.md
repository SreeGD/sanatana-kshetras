Thirusirupuliyur, a small village on the Kaveri near Kollumangudi in Thiruvarur district, holds one of only two Divya Desams (the other being Srirangam itself) where Vishnu is enshrined in the reclining Sayana posture facing south rather than east. Local tradition explains the temple's alternate name, Arulmaakadal Perumal, by way of a legend in which the serpent-king Adisesha sought Vishnu's darshan, and the Lord obliged by resting atop him here as a young child, in Bala Sayana form, so that an aged sage with failing eyesight could still perceive his full effulgence in a form gentle enough to behold. Uniquely among the Divya Desams, the temple maintains a separate shrine dedicated to Adisesha himself.

The presiding deity, worshipped here as Sthalasayana Perumal with his consort Thirumagal Naachiyar, is celebrated in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham through the verses of Thirumangai Alvar, part of that saint's extensive circuit of hymns across the Kaveri delta shrines. Constructed in the Dravidian style typical of the region's Divya Desams, the temple's architectural and inscriptional history beyond its Alvar-era veneration was not well documented in the sources available for this entry, and would benefit from a closer look at regional epigraphic surveys before this account is treated as fully verified.
