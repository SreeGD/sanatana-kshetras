ISKCON's presence in Los Angeles traces to 1968, when followers of A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada first began operating out of storefronts in the LA/Hollywood area, moving briefly in 1969 to a former church on La Cienega Boulevard. The current temple at 3764 Watseka Avenue — known as New Dvaraka — was formally established on 25 February 1970, and Prabhupada personally installed the presiding Deities, Sri Sri Rukmini-Dvarakadisha, in the summer of 1971. Prabhupada is said to have spent more time at this temple than at any other Western ISKCON center outside India, and by the early 1970s Los Angeles had become the movement's American headquarters. Today the temple remains an active center of daily worship, home to an on-site Bhagavad-Gita museum and the long-running Govinda's vegetarian restaurant, and it organizes an annual Festival of the Chariots (Ratha Yatra) as a major public event in the city.
