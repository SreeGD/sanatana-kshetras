Trimbakeshwar is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, the sites where Shiva is held to have manifested as a self-formed column of light, and it is theologically distinctive among them: the linga here is carved with three faces representing Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva together, rather than depicting Shiva alone. The temple also sits at the traditional source of the Godavari river, one of India's most important waterways and often called the "Ganga of the South." Tradition credits this to the penance of the sage Gautama on nearby Brahmagiri Hill, after which Shiva is said to have released the Ganga to flow from the hill as the Godavari — a devotional account of the river's origin rather than a documented geological or historical claim.

The temple seen today is an 18th-century rebuilding in black stone, undertaken by the Peshwa ruler Balaji Baji Rao after an earlier structure at the site was destroyed under Mughal rule during Aurangzeb's reign — part of a broader pattern of temple destruction and Maratha-era reconstruction across the Deccan in this period. Its Hemadpanthi-style architecture and setting among the Brahmagiri, Nilagiri, and Kalagiri hills have made it both a major Shaiva pilgrimage site and, along with Nashik city nearby, one of the four rotating host sites of the Kumbh Mela. A notable historical footnote: the Nassak Diamond, once part of the linga's ornamentation, was seized by British forces during the Third Anglo-Maratha War in the early 19th century and eventually left India entirely, passing through several private Western collections.
