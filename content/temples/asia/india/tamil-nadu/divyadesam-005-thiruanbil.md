Thiruanbil, situated in the fertile Chola Nadu country along the Kaveri, is one of the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alwars in praise of Sriman Narayana. The presiding deity (Moolavar) is venerated here as Vadivazhagiya Nambi, the Lord of perfect and beautiful form, also honoured as Sundararaja, the handsome king. The very name of the place, evoking anbu or loving devotion, is cherished by the tradition as expressive of the bond of love between the Lord and His devotees. According to the sthala purana, this is a hallowed spot graced by the Lord's presence to bless sages and seekers who undertook worship and penance here, and the perumal stands in His enchanting form to draw the hearts of those who behold Him. The glory of Thiruanbil is enshrined in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham through the hymns of Thirumazhisai Alwar, one of the early Alwars renowned for the depth of his philosophical devotion and his uncompromising affirmation of the supremacy of Vishnu. His verses upon this shrine secured its place among the Divya Desams and have carried its sanctity down the centuries. As one of the Divya Desams of the Kaveri region, Thiruanbil shares in the rich devotional landscape of the Chola country, where temple after temple was sanctified by the songs of the Alwars. Devotees revere Vadivazhagiya Nambi for the beauty of His form and for the grace He bestows, and the temple endures as a cherished abode within the sacred geography of Srivaishnava pilgrimage.

## Festivals

At this temple of Vadivazhagiya Nambi (Sundararaja) in Thiruanbil, the principal annual celebration is traditionally the Brahmotsavam, when the Lord is carried in procession upon the various vahanas amid Veda and Prabandham recitation. Vaikunta Ekadasi is observed with devotion, including the chanting of the Tiruvaymoli across the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu days. The temple also keeps Pavithrotsavam as a rite of purification and, where the tank permits, a Float festival (Theppotsavam) upon the temple waters. Beyond these, the customary Vaishnava cycle of monthly Ekadasi observances, Tamil month festivals sacred to Vishnu, the goddess's Tirukalyanam, and special abhishekas on auspicious asterisms are honoured here in keeping with long-standing tradition.
