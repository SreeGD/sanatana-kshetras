Tarakeswar, a temple town in Hooghly district about 60 km from Kolkata, is built around one of Bengal's most visited Shiva shrines, centered on a swayambhu (self-manifested) lingam worshipped as Taraknath. Devotional tradition credits a Rajput zamindar, Raja Bharamalla Rao, with founding the original shrine, but the sources consulted for this entry disagree sharply on both the date and the specific legend involved: one account places the original structure around 1634, tied to a milkman named Mukunda Ghosh whose cow was discovered pouring milk over a buried stone that turned out to be the lingam; a separate Wikipedia article instead gives 1729 and credits a Shiva devotee named Vishnudas from Uttar Pradesh with the founding. Given this unresolved conflict between sources consulted, this entry treats the exact founding date and legend as unsettled rather than picking one account as authoritative.

What is comparatively well documented is the temple's later building history: the standing atchala-style (multi-gabled Bengal roof) structure is dated to 1739 under the patronage of Govardhan Rakshit, zamindar of Karjana, with recorded renovations in 1744 and 1800 under successive Maharajas of Bardhaman, a refurbishment of the temple's sacred Dudhpukur pond in 1854, and a congregational hall added in 1874 — a layered, multi-generational pattern of zamindari patronage more typical of well-documented Bengal temples than the disputed origin story above. The temple's administrative history in this period was not always smooth, either: the sources consulted note an 1874 episode of serious misconduct involving a temple mahant and a 1924 satyagraha movement directed at a later mahant's alleged mismanagement, both treated here as documented rather than legendary history even though this entry does not detail them.

Today Tarakeswar is best known for its Shravan-month pilgrimage, when devotees dressed in saffron carry Ganges water on foot from Baidyabati and nearby Tribeni to pour over the lingam, drawing crowds reported in the millions and forming one of the largest annual melas in West Bengal, comparable in scale and character to the Kanwar pilgrimage to the Baidyanath Jyotirlinga at Deoghar. The temple remains an intensely active pilgrimage site, especially popular on Mondays and during Gajan and Maha Shivaratri.
