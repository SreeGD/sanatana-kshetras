Kantajew Temple, also known as Kantanagar Temple, stands in the village of Kantanagar north of Dinajpur town and is widely regarded as one of the finest surviving examples of terracotta temple architecture in Bengal. Its construction was begun in 1704 under Maharaja Pran Nath, the ruler of the Dinajpur zamindari, and completed in 1752 by his adopted successor, Raja Ramnath — a nearly half-century building project reflecting the scale of royal patronage the Dinajpur estate could command. The temple is dedicated to Krishna, worshipped here under the name Kantaji, alongside his consort Rukmini, and its walls are covered almost entirely in dense terracotta plaques depicting scenes from the Krishna legend, the Ramayana and Mahabharata, courtly life, and everyday Bengali scenes — a visual density that has made it a touchstone reference for the region's brick-temple tradition.

Originally built in the Navaratna style with nine ornamental spires, the temple lost all of them in the powerful 1897 Assam earthquake, and the towers have never been fully rebuilt, so the structure seen today stands without its original silhouette even as its terracotta walls remain largely intact. It continues to function as an active pilgrimage site and a protected heritage monument at once, drawing its largest crowds each November for the Rash Purnima festival associated with Krishna devotion in Bengal.
