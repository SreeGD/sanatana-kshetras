The Thirukkuralappan Temple at Aranmula, known in the Tamil hymns as Thiruvaaranvilai, is one of the 108 Divya Desams and one of the cluster of sacred shrines along the Pampa river in Malai Nadu, the Kerala region. The presiding deity, the Moolavar, is worshipped here as Thirukkuralappan, a form that tradition connects with Vamana, the Lord who appeared as Kural, the dwarf brahmachari, to subdue the asura king Mahabali by asking for three paces of earth. In keeping with this association, the place is revered as one where the Lord measured out the worlds and restored the order of dharma. Aranmula stands among the divyadesams of Kerala that share the legend and sanctity of the Pampa, and it is held in high esteem by devotees of the region. The glory of this abode was sung by Nammalwar, the foremost of the Alwars, in his hymns of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and through his verses the shrine is counted among the celebrated abodes of Vishnu praised by the saint-poets. Tradition holds that the Lord here grants the boons of those who approach Him in surrender, even as He once approached Mahabali in humble form to accomplish the welfare of the devas. Worshipped with deep devotion across generations, Thirukkuralappan of Aranmula remains a living center of Srivaishnava piety, his sanctity preserved in the Tamil hymns of the Alwar and in the continuing reverence of pilgrims who seek the grace of the Lord who measures the three worlds.

As independently documented on Wikipedia, the temple actually venerated at this Aranmula site today is dedicated to Krishna in his form as Parthasarathy, Arjuna's charioteer during the Mahabharata war, rather than to the Vamana-linked form described above; local legend credits Arjuna himself with building the shrine as penance for killing the unarmed warrior Karna. This entry retains the Vamana/Thirukkuralappan framing drawn from the Divya Desam devotional tradition while noting this discrepancy, which a contributor with deeper Srivaishnava-tradition sourcing could help resolve. The documented temple's central image is a six-foot Krishna in Vishvarupa (cosmic) form, said to be the tallest Krishna image among Kerala's temples, housed in a Kerala-style shrine with a two-storey gopuram, an elevated platform reached by five steps, and wall paintings dating to the early 18th century. The temple is administered by the Travancore Devaswom Board and also serves as a repository for the sacred jewels of the Sabarimala temple.

## Festivals

Aranmula is most famous for the Aranmula Uthrattathi Vallamkali, an annual snake boat race held during the Onam season in which ceremonial boats up to nearly 100 feet long, crewed by rowers and singers, race along the Pamba in the deity's honour. The temple's own ten-day utsavam culminates on Thiruvonam with a Garuda Vahanam procession, and the Arattu festival carries the deities in procession to the Pamba riverbank in the Malayalam month of Meenam (March/April). The town is also known for the Aranmula Kannadi, a traditional hand-polished metal mirror craft historically associated with families connected to the temple.

## Location & Access

The temple stands at Aranmula, a riverside village on the southern bank of the Pamba in Pathanamthitta district, Kerala. Chengannur is the nearest railway station, with Tiruvalla and Kottayam as broader regional transport hubs, and Thiruvananthapuram and Cochin as the nearest international airports. Darshan is generally offered from about 4 AM to 11 AM and 5 PM to 8 PM. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
