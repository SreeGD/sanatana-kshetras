The Swaminarayan Mandir in Ahmedabad's Kalupur neighborhood is the founding temple of the Swaminarayan Sampraday, built at the direct instruction of the sect's founder, Swaminarayan, and personally inaugurated by him on 24 February 1822 before a crowd of roughly 50,000 people, three years after construction began in 1819. As the first temple the movement ever built, it holds a status within the Swaminarayan tradition comparable to a mother church, and it now serves as the headquarters of the NarNarayan Dev Gadi — one of the two administrative dioceses Swaminarayan established by will to govern the sect after his death, the other being centered at Vadtal.

Architecturally, the temple is notable for blending local Gujarati craftsmanship with the wider currents of early 19th-century Ahmedabad: elaborately hand-carved Burma-teak pillars and doorways sit alongside Corinthian columns and other British colonial-era detailing, with Mughal-influenced elements woven through the decorative program as well — a synthesis that made the temple as much a civic landmark of the old walled city as a religious one. The sanctum enshrines Nar Narayan Dev as its principal deities, alongside images of Radha Krishna, Dharmadev, and Bhakti Mata (understood in the tradition as Swaminarayan's own parents), with a marble image of Ghanshyam Maharaj — Swaminarayan as a child — housed separately in the temple's Akshar Bhavan hall. More than two centuries after its founding, the complex functions as an active, multi-purpose religious campus, combining daily worship with pilgrim guesthouses, a medical clinic, and a Sanskrit school, and remains one of Ahmedabad's most recognizable heritage buildings in the heart of the old city.
