The Kutralanathar Temple at Courtallam (Thirukutralam), about 5 km from Tenkasi at the edge of the Western Ghats, is one of the Pancha Sabhai — the five halls of Shiva's cosmic dance as Nataraja — corresponding here to the Chitra Sabha, the hall of art or pictures, distinct from the golden Kanaka Sabha at Chidambaram and silver Rajata Sabha at Madurai (both already documented elsewhere in this directory) and from the jeweled Ratna Sabha at Thiruvalangadu and copper Tamra Sabha at Tirunelveli, both added alongside this entry. Shiva is worshipped here as Kutralanathaswamy, with his consort Parvati honored as Kuzhalvaaimozhi Ambal.

Two distinct legends are recorded for the site's origin. In one, the sage Agastya, unable to witness Shiva and Parvati's wedding at Mount Kailash directly, prayed at this spot to be granted a vision of it; Shiva is said to have appeared here with Parvati in wedding attire in response, giving the site its name and standing as Kutralanathar's abode. In a separate account, a sage remembered as Urosamar is said to have set flowers afloat on the Thamirabarani river, with the first to reach shore doing so here, prompting the temple's founding — a legend that echoes, without being explicitly connected in the source consulted, the story told of the sage Agastya's disciple Uromacha Munivar in the separate Nava Kailasam group of nine Shiva temples documented elsewhere in this directory.

The temple's conch-shaped outer enclosure, known as Sangakkovil, reflects Dravidian architecture credited to the Pandya, Chola, and Travancore dynasties over its history. Its distinguishing feature is the Chitra Sabha itself, whose walls carry murals depicting scenes from the Indian epics; the temple's sacred tree is the Kurum Palaa and its temple tank is called Chitranadhi. As a Paadal Petra Sthalam, it is venerated in the Tevaram hymns of Sambandar, and the site is also counted among the 64 Shakti Peethams. During festivals, an image of Nataraja is brought to the Chitra Sabha from the nearby Kurumpalaveesar temple, and the site's major observances include Arudra Darisanam, Vasanta Utsavam, Pavitrotsavam, Navaratri, and Skanda Sashti. Courtallam is also widely known as a monsoon waterfall destination, drawing large numbers of visitors independent of the temple itself.
