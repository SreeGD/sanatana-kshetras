On an island formed where the Kaveri divides around Srirangapatna in Karnataka's Mandya district stands the Ranganathaswamy Temple, the shrine that gives the town its name and, by an inscription the Archaeological Survey of India dates to 894 CE, one of the oldest continuously significant temples on the river. Vishnu is enshrined here as Ranganatha, reclining on the coiled serpent Adishesha with Lakshmi at his feet, alongside subsidiary shrines to Narasimha, Gopalakrishna, Srinivasa, Hanuman, and Garuda. The temple's documented history spans dynasties: granted in the early 12th century by the Hoysala king Vishnuvardhana to the Vaishnava philosopher-saint Ramanujacharya as a center of learning, renovated under Hoysala king Veera Ballala II per a 1210 CE inscription, extended with a Vijayanagara-style entrance tower, and further embellished under the later Wodeyar rulers of Mysore.

Srirangapatna's temple holds a specific place within South Indian Vaishnava geography: local tradition holds every island the Kaveri forms to be sacred to Ranganatha, and of the river's three principal Ranganatha shrines, this one -- the northernmost and, by tradition, the first -- is known as Adi Ranga. The pilgrimage continues downstream to Madhya Ranga at Shivanasamudra and culminates at Antya Ranga, the great temple-city of Srirangam in Tamil Nadu, already documented elsewhere in this directory as a Divya Desam; together the three make up what devotees call the Tri Ranga darshana. Protected today by the Archaeological Survey of India as a monument of national importance, the temple remains in active worship by both Smartha and Sri Vaishnava communities, easily reached from Mysuru, about 15 km away, by road or rail.
