Chotila, a taluka headquarters town roughly midway between Ahmedabad and Rajkot on Gujarat's main east–west highway, is dominated by the hill after which it is named — a 1,173-foot rise crowned by the temple of Chamunda Mata, one of the fiercer forms of Durga associated with the destruction of the demons Chanda and Munda. The temple is reached by a long stepped pathway climbing directly up the hillside from Bhaktivan Garden at its base, a route walked daily by streams of pilgrims and a familiar sight to travelers on the highway below, who can see the shrine's white outline from a considerable distance. The town itself, once known as Chotgadh, passed through several regional dynasties before being taken by the Kathis in 1566 CE, though the sources available for this entry do not connect any of that dynastic history directly to the founding of the temple itself.

Unlike several of Gujarat's other major Shakti shrines, Chotila's temple lacks a well-documented origin narrative in the sources consulted here — no inscriptional date, royal patron, or canonical legend is confirmed, and even practical details like the exact step count to the summit could not be verified. What is clear is its standing as a major, continuously visited pilgrimage site: Chotila functions as a temple town in its own right, drawing its largest crowds during Navratri, when devotees climb the hill in especially large numbers to seek the goddess's blessing. A smaller but regular rhythm of devotion also plays out monthly, on Poonam (the full moon), when pilgrims undertake walking processions (padyatra) to the hilltop shrine along routes lined with temporary food and drink camps set up to support them.
