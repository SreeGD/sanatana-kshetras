The Chaturdasha Temple in Agartala, Tripura's capital, was built between 1760 and 1761 under King Krishna Manikya of the Manikya dynasty, at the same time Agartala itself was established as the new seat of Tripura's kings; the former capital, Udaipur — also home to the Tripura Sundari Shakti Peetha — had housed the shrine's predecessor. Its most recognizable feature is a distinctive Tripuri-style dome, modeled on the thatched roofline of a traditional village hut, topped by a smaller stupa-like finial that points to a trace of Buddhist architectural influence in the region's temple-building.

The temple's name comes from the Chaturdasha Devata, the "fourteen deities" worshipped here collectively as the tutelary gods of the Manikya royal line. Tradition names among them Hara (Shiva), Uma (Durga), Hari (Vishnu) and Lakshmi, alongside deified natural forces such as Ganga (the river), Abdhi (the ocean) and Himadri (the Himalaya) — a pantheon that reflects how the indigenous Tripuri people absorbed major Hindu gods into an existing native religious framework rather than adopting Hinduism wholesale, still tended today by a hereditary priestly class called the Chantai. A Mahabharata-era legend, set in the reign of King Yudhishthira, explains their origin: Shiva is said to have promised the widow of the legendary king Tripur that she would bear an heir, on condition that these fourteen deities be worshipped in the kingdom ever after.

What survives as the temple's living tradition today is the annual Kharchi Puja, a week-long festival each July and one of Tripura's most important religious observances, with its opening day marked as a state holiday. The temple complex itself underwent government-led refurbishment beginning in May 2023, part of an effort to further develop the old capital precinct as a heritage and tourism destination.
