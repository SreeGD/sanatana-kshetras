Consecrated in 2013 on Blaschkoallee in the Britz district of Berlin's Neukölln borough, the Sri Mayurapathy Murugan Temple was Berlin's first Hindu temple, predating the much larger Sri Ganesha Hindu Temple — already documented elsewhere in this directory — by over a decade. It serves what Wikipedia's overview of the Neukölln locality describes as "mainly Sri Lankan" Hindu community, part of the wider wave of Sri Lankan Tamil refugee settlement that also founded temples such as the Sri Kamadchi Ampal Temple in Hamm and the Sri Muthumariamman Temple in Hanover, both already documented elsewhere in this directory.

The temple is dedicated to Murugan (also known as Kartikeya or Skanda), the youthful warrior son of Shiva particularly venerated in Tamil Shaiva tradition, filling a rare-deity niche within Berlin's otherwise small Hindu religious landscape. As the city's Hindu population grew — estimated at roughly 45,000 by the time the larger Sri Ganesha Hindu Temple was consecrated in 2026 — the Britz temple's role as Berlin's original, smaller Hindu place of worship helped lay the community groundwork for that subsequent, much larger construction project nearby in Neukölln. Beyond its consecration year, street, and Murugan dedication, further detail on the temple's founders, building, and congregation size could not be confirmed in the sources available for this entry.
