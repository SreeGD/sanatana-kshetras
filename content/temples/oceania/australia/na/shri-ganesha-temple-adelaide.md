Conceived in 1985 by the Hindu Society of South Australia and formally opened in 2001, Shri Ganesha Temple occupies the site of a former Lutheran church in Oaklands Park, a southern Adelaide suburb — repurposing existing consecrated ground for a new tradition of worship. The temple takes its name from its presiding deity, Ganesha, and additionally houses shrines to Lakshmi Narayan, the triad of Durga, Lakshmi and Saraswati, Muruga (accompanied by his consorts Valli and Deivanai), Hanuman, Bhairava, the Navagraha (nine celestial bodies), and a Shiva Linga — a broad, multi-sectarian roster characteristic of diaspora temples serving communities drawn from many regional traditions.

That diversity is reflected in the temple's congregation, which draws an estimated 20,000 Hindus from India, Mauritius, South Africa, Fiji, Trinidad, Sri Lanka, Guyana, Suriname, and Malaysia, alongside international students. Beyond daily worship, the temple runs more than 80 festivals and observances annually, operates a children's library, and organizes community-service programs including blood donation drives, while pursuing an expansion of its event, dining, and wedding facilities to accommodate its growing congregation.
