The temple at Thirukkavithalam, also called Kabisthalam, in the Chola Nadu enshrines the Lord as Gajendra Varadan and stands among the 108 Divya Desams sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The name of the presiding deity directly recalls the celebrated episode of Gajendra Moksha, and according to the sthala purana this kshetra is intimately associated with that great act of divine deliverance. Tradition holds that when Gajendra, the lord of elephants, was seized by a crocodile and cried out in helpless devotion, the Lord rushed to his aid and granted him liberation, and it is in remembrance of this grace that he is worshipped here as Gajendra Varadan, the bestower of boons upon Gajendra. The temple thus embodies the timeless promise that the Lord answers the surrendered cry of his devotees, hastening to rescue those who turn to him in distress. Set within the sacred Kaveri delta of the Chola country, Thirukkavithalam takes its place among the Divya Desams that grace this hallowed region. The glory of this abode was sung by Thirumazhisai Alwar, whose verses in the Prabandham enshrined the temple among the divine abodes praised by the Alwars and carried its renown to generations of devotees. Worshippers who come before the Moolavar here meditate upon the Lord's swift and boundless compassion as revealed in the deliverance of Gajendra, finding in this shrine an abiding emblem of refuge for all who seek his protection.
