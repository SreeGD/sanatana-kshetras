At Thiruvilandur, within the town now known as Mayiladuthurai, the reclining Vishnu of this shrine is worshipped as Parimala Ranganathar - "the fragrant Ranganatha" - one of the five Pancharanga Kshetrams strung along the banks of the Kaveri. The name Parimala, meaning fragrance, is explained by a legend in which Vishnu, having retrieved the Vedas from the asuras who had stolen them, imparted to the sacred texts a lasting sweetness before restoring them to the world; a related tradition holds that the deity here relieved the moon-god Chandra of a curse, adding a further layer to the temple's reputation as a place of release from affliction.

The shrine is counted among the Divya Desams through ten pasurams composed here by Thirumangai Alvar, whose Periya Thirumozhi verses are, according to local telling, credited with having caused the temple's doors to swing open of their own accord when the saint arrived to find them shut. Successive rulers across the Medieval Chola, Vijayanagara, and Madurai Nayak periods are credited with expanding and endowing the temple, in keeping with the layered building history typical of Kaveri-delta shrines of this antiquity, and the temple today maintains an active daily ritual calendar of six services along with a full complement of annual festivals including Navaratri, the Thula Mahotsavam, Vaikunta Ekadasi, and Makara Sankranthi.
