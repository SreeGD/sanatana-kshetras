Paramapadam — also called Sri Vaikuntam, or simply Vaikuntha — is the 108th and final entry in the Divya Desam collection, and the second of its two celestial (non-physical) sites. Where Thiruparkadal (the Ocean of Milk) is described as the threshold, Paramapadam is presented in Sri Vaishnava theology as the ultimate goal itself: the eternal spiritual world beyond material creation, where the Lord — here as Paramapadanathan — resides permanently with the Goddess, attended by ever-liberated beings (nityasuris), and where liberated souls (muktas) who attain moksha are held to reside eternally in His service.

Unlike every other Divya Desam, Paramapadam is not situated in any describable direction or distance from the physical world; the Divya Prabandham verses describe it in terms of its own sacred geography — the Virajaa river, said to mark the boundary between the material and spiritual worlds, and the Ayiramadha Pushkarini, a sacred tank — rather than any earthly coordinate.

Eight Alvars composed a combined 36 verses on Paramapadam, with Nammalvar alone contributing 21 of them — the single largest individual contribution to any Divya Desam by one Alvar. Perialvar, Thirumazhisai Alvar, Poigai Alvar, Aandaal, Thiruppaan Alvar, Thirumangai Alvar, and Peyalvar each add further verses, together closing the Divya Prabandham's glorification of the 108 Divya Desams with a vision of their final destination.
