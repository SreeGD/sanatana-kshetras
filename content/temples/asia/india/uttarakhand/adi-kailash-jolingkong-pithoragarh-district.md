Adi Kailash — also called Chhota Kailash ("Little Kailash"), Shiva Kailash, or Baba Kailash — is a 5,945 m Himalayan peak in the remote Kuthi Valley of Pithoragarh district, close to the India-Nepal-Tibet border tri-junction and the Lipulekh Pass route used on the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra. It is venerated as the second most sacred peak of the Pancha Kailash (Five Kailash) group, after Mount Kailash in Tibet itself, and — like its Himalayan counterparts Manimahesh Kailash, Kinnaur Kailash, and Shrikhand Mahadev — is regarded by tradition as an abode of Shiva.

Unlike a conventional temple, Adi Kailash has no built shrine at its summit, and pilgrims do not climb the peak itself. A small Hindu shrine stands instead at the base camp on the shore of Jolingkong Lake (also known locally as Gauri Kund), which echoes Lake Manasarovar in the wider Kailash pilgrimage tradition, with the peak rising above it. When a joint British-Scottish-American team made the first confirmed ascent of the mountain in October 2004, the climbers deliberately stopped short of the true summit out of respect for its sacred status — a detail that itself reflects how the site is understood by pilgrims, as a peak to be venerated from below rather than climbed.

Because the route runs through a restricted Indo-Tibetan border zone near the Lipulekh Pass, visiting requires an Inner Line Permit and is only feasible outside the harsh Himalayan winter. A motorable road extended toward Gunji and the Kuthi Valley around 2020 has considerably shortened what was previously a multi-day trek, though reaching Jolingkong still requires travelling well beyond the road system for the final stretch.
