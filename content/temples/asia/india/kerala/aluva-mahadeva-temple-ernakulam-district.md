At Aluva, a river town in suburban Kochi, the Periyar — one of Kerala's longest and most important rivers — widens across broad sandy banks known as the Aluva Manappuram, and it is here, directly on the open sand rather than within an enclosed sanctum, that the Aluva Mahadeva Temple's Shiva lingam stands. This unenclosed setting is the temple's most distinctive feature: unlike the vast majority of Kerala's Shiva shrines, where the lingam is housed within a roofed sreekovil, the deity here rises straight out of the riverbank sand, exposed to sun, rain, and the rhythms of the Periyar itself. Temple tradition, consistent with the founding narrative attached to many of Kerala's oldest Shaiva sites, credits the sage Parashurama with the linga's original installation, though no independently dated historical record of the temple's founding survives.

Administered by the Travancore Devaswom Board and counted among the traditional "108 Shiva Temples of Kerala," the temple's significance is inseparable from its riverside setting and from the enormous annual gathering it hosts: the Aluva Sivarathri, or Manappuram festival, held each year on Maha Shivaratri, when tens of thousands of pilgrims converge on the sandy riverbank for ritual bathing in the Periyar and worship at the exposed lingam, making it one of the largest and most distinctive Shivaratri observances in Kerala. The Aluva Manappuram grounds, beyond their religious use, have also long served as a public gathering space for the wider Kochi region, giving the temple and its riverbank setting a civic as well as devotional significance within Ernakulam district.
