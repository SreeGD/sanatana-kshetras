ISKCON of Houston traces its roots to 1972, when devotees first began preaching in the area, holding early services around pictures of the Deities in a home in the Montrose district. The community moved to Rosalie Street in downtown Houston in 1974, where its first Deities, Sri Sri Nitai Chandra and Sri Sri Chaitanya Chandra, were installed, and it was officially incorporated as a non-profit in 1977. In 1984 the community relocated to its present site on West 34th Street, a former church building, and after renovation installed its presiding Deities, Sri Sri Radha Nila-Madhava, in 1986. Two more deity forms followed: Sri Sri Giriraj Govardhan in 1989 and Sri Sri Radha Giridhari in 1992. Gauranga Hall, a dedicated community events space, was built in 1996, and in 2014 the Deities moved into a newly built marble temple with a custom teakwood altar. Today the temple offers daily deity worship, kirtan and meditation programs, and teaching from the Bhagavad Gita, alongside cultural festivals with vegetarian cuisine. Alongside temple worship, the community runs affiliated projects including a vegetarian restaurant (Govinda's), an apartment complex (Govardhan Palace Apartments), an academy, and an urban farm. It observes the standard Gaudiya Vaishnava festival calendar, including Janmashtami and Dussehra.
