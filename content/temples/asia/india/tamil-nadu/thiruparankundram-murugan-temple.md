Thiruparankundram's sanctum is carved into a hillock on Madurai's southwestern edge, a rock-cut temple that Wikipedia's account attributes in its present Hindu form to the Pandyas in the 6th century CE. The same hill carries older Tamil-Brahmi rock inscriptions dated to roughly the 1st century BCE, which the source consulted reads as evidence of earlier Jain ascetic use of the site before it was reclaimed for Hindu worship — a claim plausible for many rock-cut sites of that era in the Madurai region, though not independently confirmed here beyond that one source. A seven-tier gopuram about 150 feet high fronts the shrine, and a separate rock-cut cave temple to Shiva, dated to the 7th-8th century CE, stands at the same hill.

The temple's central legend continues directly from the war narrative at Tiruchendur: having defeated the asura Surapadman, Murugan is held to have married Deivanai (also called Devasena), daughter of Indra, at this site, and to have worshipped his own father Shiva here in a form enshrined as Parangirinathar. Among the Arupadaiveedu, Thiruparankundram is thus traditionally distinguished from Tiruttani and Pazhamudircholai — both linked to Murugan's other consort, Valli — as the seat of his marriage to Deivanai and, by extension, of the war's formal resolution through alliance with Indra's court.
