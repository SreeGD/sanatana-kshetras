Thiruvaipadi is the Divya Desam identified with Gokul (Aayarpadi in the Divya Prabandham), the cowherd settlement across the Yamuna from Mathura where Vasudeva is said to have secretly carried the infant Krishna to be raised in safety by the cowherd chief Nanda and his wife Yashoda, away from the murderous intent of King Kamsa. It is here that the Puranas locate Krishna's celebrated childhood pastimes — the butter theft, the subduing of the serpent Kaliya, and his play among the gopis — themes taken up by the Alvars in their own devotional idiom.

Five Alvars — including Poigai Alvar, Thiruppan Alvar, Nammalvar and Periyalvar — are credited with a combined 22 pasurams on this sthala, worshipping the presiding deity here as Navamohana Krishnan, flanked by Rukmani and Sathyabhama. The site is popularly associated with Nanda's residence (sometimes called Nand Bhavan or Nand Mahal locally), and the temple, though modest in scale compared to the major Braj shrines, remains an active stop on the Mathura-Vrindavan-Gokul pilgrimage circuit associated with Krishna's early life.
