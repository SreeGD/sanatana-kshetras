Shri Swaminarayan Mandir, on Karachi's M.A. Jinnah Road, is described as the only surviving Swaminarayan Sampradaya temple in Pakistan, built by Gujarati merchant devotees as the movement spread into Sindh in the colonial era. Dedicated to Swaminarayan and to Nara Narayana, the temple anchors a large complex spanning over 32,306 square yards that includes a sacred cowshed, a Gurdwara serving Karachi's Sikh community, and function space used for weddings — evidence of a once-substantial and still-active Gujarati Hindu presence in the city.

Sources consulted for this entry differ on the exact founding date, with one account pointing to the 1890s and another implying construction around 1788 based on a reported 216th-anniversary celebration in 2004; this discrepancy is noted rather than resolved here. What is well documented is the temple's continued vitality: it remains a functioning center of Vaishnava worship celebrating a full calendar of festivals — Swaminarayan Jayanti, Ram Navami, Janmashtami, Dussehra, Diwali, and what is described as the city's biggest Holi celebration.
