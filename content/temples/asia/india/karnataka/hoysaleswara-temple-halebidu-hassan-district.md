At Halebidu — known in its own inscriptions as Dorasamudra, the former Hoysala capital — the Hoysaleswara Temple stands as a twin shrine to Shiva, its two adjoining sanctums dedicated to Hoysaleswara and to Shantaleswara, the latter named in honor of Queen Shantala Devi, consort of the Hoysala king Vishnuvardhana. A smaller shrine to the sun god Surya also stands within the complex. Construction began around 1121 CE under Vishnuvardhana's sponsorship, overseen by his officer Ketamalla, and continued for roughly four decades; despite this long labor, the temple, like several other major Hoysala projects, was never fully completed according to its original architectural plan.

The temple is celebrated for the extraordinary density and quality of its soapstone relief carving — friezes wrapping the entire plinth depict thousands of elephants, lions, horsemen, scrollwork, and scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata, widely regarded among the finest achievements of Hoysala sculptors. The city of Dorasamudra, and the temple with it, suffered severe damage when sacked twice by Delhi Sultanate armies in the early 14th century, after which the Hoysala capital never fully recovered. Today the temple is managed principally as a nationally protected monument by the Archaeological Survey of India, with an on-site museum, though some worship continues; in 2023 it was jointly inscribed with the Belur and Somanathapura temples as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas."
