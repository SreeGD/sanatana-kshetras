The New Navadvip Temple in Wrocław, in Poland's Lower Silesian Voivodeship, is named for Navadvip (Nabadwip), the birthplace of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu in West Bengal — a naming convention shared by New Vraja-dhama-style ISKCON communities worldwide that evoke the sacred geography of Gaudiya Vaishnavism's founding saint. It is one of Poland's three main ISKCON temples, alongside the pioneering New Shantipur Temple in Czarnów (founded 1980) and the New Ramana Reti Temple in Mysiadło, near Warsaw (founded 1989), both already documented elsewhere in this directory.

The Wrocław community's roots trace to an informal student devotee group in the early 1980s; it registered officially as a religious association in 1988, installed its deities in 1990, and after an intervening move to larger premises settled at its current address on ul. Brodzka in 1998. As at its sister temples, worship centers on the Pancha Tattva in the paired form of Gaura-Nitai — Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and Nityananda — reflecting the missionary lineage of A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, who brought Krishna consciousness to the West. Since 2009 the temple has organized Poland's first Ratha Jatra (chariot festival) processions, and it has run an annual multi-day "Vedic Culture Days" festival every May bank-holiday weekend since 1994, alongside a regular Sunday program of kirtan, lecture, and a communal feast.
