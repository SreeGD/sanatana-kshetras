The Koodal Azhagar Temple at Madurai, the place revered as Thirukkoodal, enshrines the Lord as Koodal Azhagar (recorded on Wikipedia by the fuller name Viyooga Sundarrajan), the surpassingly beautiful Lord of the meeting-place, with his consort worshipped as Mathuravalli Thayar; it is honored as one of the 108 Divya Desams sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Madurai, the ancient seat of the Pandya kings and of Tamil learning, was traditionally called Koodal, the place of confluence, and the Lord who presides here bears the epithet Azhagar, the Beautiful One. A distinctive marvel of this shrine, built within an Ashtanga Vimanam, is its sanctum where the Lord is venerated in three postures, seated, standing, and reclining, beheld at different levels, so that the worshipper may contemplate the manifold forms of the Supreme in a single sacred precinct; the seated granite image stands 6 feet tall, and the panchaloha festival image 4 feet. The temple's origins are attributed to the Pandyas, with 8th-century inscriptions recording donations of granite for the Ardha Mandapa, and further expansion under the Vijayanagara empire and the Madurai Nayaks in the 16th century, followed by a renovation in 1923. Its five-tiered rajagopuram rises to 125 feet, and the complex, covering about two acres, includes the Hema Pushkarani temple tank and an unusual Navagraha shrine, a feature otherwise typically found only in Shiva temples. The glory of Koodal Azhagar was sung by Periyalvar, whose Tirupallantu verses are recited across Vishnu temples, by Thirumazhisai Alwar, the profound sage-poet of deep philosophical insight, and by Thirumangai Alwar, the prince among the Alwars whose hymns garlanded the shrines of the Pandya country. Through their verses this abode in Madurai was sealed forever among the perpetually praised dwellings of Vishnu.

## Festivals

Koodal Azhagar, the Divya Desam within the city of Madurai, observes a fourteen-day Brahmotsavam in the Tamil month of Vaikasi (May-June) as its chief annual celebration, with the Lord taken in procession through the surrounding streets on various vahanas. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is kept devoutly, with the chanting of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham. The Tamil month of Chithirai, famous in Madurai for its great festival, sees Vaishnava observances at this shrine as well, and the Float festival (Theppotsavam), Pavithrotsavam and Tamil New Year celebrations are traditionally observed. Festival days bring processions, special abhishekams and recitation of the Prabandham hymns.

## Location & Access

The Koodal Azhagar temple stands near the centre of Madurai, the great temple city of the Pandya region, not far from the famed Meenakshi shrine and the city's central districts. Madurai is itself a major city and a well-connected transport hub by road, rail and air, making the temple readily accessible to visitors. The temple follows six daily rituals including Kalasanthi (8 AM), Uchikalam (12 PM), Sayarakshai (5 PM) and Aravanai Pooja (6 PM), each with alangaram, neivethanam and deepa aradanai, in the Tenkalai tradition. It is administered by the Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department, Government of Tamil Nadu. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
