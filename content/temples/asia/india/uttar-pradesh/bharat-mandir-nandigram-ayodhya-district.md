According to the Ramayana, when Bharata learned of his father Dasharatha's death and of Rama's exile, he traveled to Chitrakoot to beg Rama to return and take the throne of Ayodhya. Rama, bound by his father's vow, refused to cut the exile short; Bharata then asked instead for Rama's sandals, which he carried back on his own head. Rather than rule from Ayodhya's palace, Bharata had the throne and royal paraphernalia brought to a village on the city's outskirts, placed the sandals upon it to stand in for Rama, and governed the kingdom of Kosala from there as an ascetic-regent for the full fourteen years of the exile. That village is named in the epic as Nandigrama.

The place identified today with that tradition is Nandigram, also known by its older name Bharatkund, a village in Sohawal tehsil of Ayodhya district, roughly 15 km south of Ayodhya city. The site includes a temple known as Bharat Mandir, a tank called Bharat Sarovar with an adjoining Aarti Kaksh pavilion, and a spot referred to as Ram Charan. It remains a stop on the wider Ayodhya-area pilgrimage circuit, reachable via Bharatkund's own railway station and the Ayodhya-Sultanpur highway. No source consulted for this entry could confirm when the present Bharat Mandir structure was built or by whom; its documentation covers the site's existence, location, and long-standing identification with the Ramayana account, not an independent architectural history. This entry is distinct from Ayodhya city itself, which is already covered elsewhere in this directory.
