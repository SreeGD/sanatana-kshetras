Basistha Temple sits in the wooded foothills southeast of Guwahati, where three small streams -- known locally as Sandhya, Lalita, and Kanta -- meet before going on to form the Basistha and Bharalu rivers. Local tradition identifies the spot as the hermitage of the sage Vashishtha, one of the seven great rishis of Vedic tradition, and the temple's sunken sanctum is said to preserve a footprint left by the sage; that identification belongs to devotional memory rather than documented history, and cannot be independently dated. What can be dated with more confidence is the standing structure: the present octagonal brick temple, built in the Nilachal style common to the Guwahati hill shrines, was raised in 1764 under the patronage of the Ahom king Rajeswar Singha, though earlier stone-temple remains at the site point to worship here as far back as the eleventh century, with no clear record of what stood on the ground in between.

The temple functions today as the centerpiece of a larger ashram complex rather than a single isolated shrine, drawing both worshippers and hikers to a site that also includes a waterfall and a meditation cave set back inside the grounds, close to the Garbhanga forest. It is a quieter counterpart to Guwahati's better-known Shakta and Vaishnava pilgrimage sites -- Kamakhya foremost among them -- offering a Shaiva, forest-hermitage character that reflects its association with a sage rather than a Puranic deity's central myth.
