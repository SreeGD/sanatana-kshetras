The Saaranathan Temple at Thirucherai in the Chola Nadu enshrines the Lord as Saaranathan, also revered as Saranathaperumal, and is honoured among the 108 Divya Desams celebrated by the Alwars. The Moolavar is worshipped here with his consort, and the kshetra is held in tradition to be a place of great sanctity associated with the deluge that once covered the world. According to the sthala purana, when the cosmic flood threatened all creation, the essence of the sacred waters and the seeds of life were preserved at this place, and from this association the kshetra draws its standing as a refuge where the Lord protects and sustains the world. Tradition also connects the shrine with the worship of the sage Markandeya and with the gathering of the sacred theerthams, so that the place is esteemed for the holiness of its waters. This Divya Desam finds its place in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham through the hymns of Thirumangai Alwar, whose verses sing the glory of Saaranathan and his sacred abode at Thirucherai. The Lord here is revered as one who grants refuge and shelter to those who surrender to him, his very name evoking the protection (sarana) that the devout seek at his feet. Pilgrims hold Thirucherai in deep reverence as a kshetra where the Lord, having preserved the world through the great flood, continues to bestow his grace, and the temple endures as a cherished abode within the sacred geography of the Divya Desams.

## Festivals

Thirucherai observes the traditional Srivaishnava festival cycle centred on the annual Brahmotsavam of Saaranathan, when the Lord is taken in procession upon the various vahanas amid Vedic and Prabandham recitation. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is kept with the opening of the Paramapada Vasal and the Adhyayana Utsavam, during which the Nalayira Divya Prabandham is sung before the deity. Pavithrotsavam, the annual purificatory rite, and a Float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank are also traditionally held. The place, linked in legend to the gathering of the rivers and the great deluge, draws devotees for its monthly asterism days and the seasonal observances common to the temples of the Chola country.

## Location & Access

Thirucherai is situated in the fertile Chola Nadu region of the Kaveri delta, a little south of Kumbakonam in the Thanjavur district of Tamil Nadu, along the route toward Nannilam. Kumbakonam is the nearest sizeable town and railway station, while Tiruchirappalli provides the closest major city and airport for travellers. Like most Vishnu temples, the shrine generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday closure.
