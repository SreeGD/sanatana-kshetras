The hill town of Dongargarh, on the Mumbai–Howrah railway line in Rajnandgaon district, is dominated by a 1,600-foot rise crowned with the temple of Bamleshwari Devi — known locally as Badi (Great) Bamleshwari to distinguish it from a smaller, companion Chhoti Bamleshwari shrine nearby — making it one of Chhattisgarh's most visited Shakti pilgrimage sites and, together with Ratanpur's Mahamaya temple and Danteshwara at Dantewada, part of the state's wider network of major goddess shrines already documented in this directory. Reaching the summit no longer requires the full climb on foot: a passenger ropeway, described as the only one of its kind anywhere in Chhattisgarh, carries pilgrims up the hillside, a modern convenience that has helped the temple draw large crowds even as it introduced its own risks — a fatal accident on the ropeway in 2016 did not affect the temple's continued operation or its pilgrim traffic.

The available sources are notably thin on the temple's deeper history: beyond noting that "several legends are associated with this shrine," no founding date, patron, or dynasty could be confirmed, leaving the site's antiquity undocumented even as its contemporary religious importance is well established. That importance is clearest during Navratri, both in its Chaitra and Sharad observances, when devotees from across the region climb — or ride — the hill in especially large numbers, making Dongargarh one of central India's significant hilltop goddess pilgrimages despite the gaps in its recorded history.
