A sacred ghat on the Ganges rather than a single enclosed temple, Har Ki Pauri ('the steps of Hari/Vishnu') is traditionally held to mark the spot where Vishnu once stood, and where drops of the mythical nectar of immortality (amrita) fell during the gods' primordial churning of the ocean — the same event that anchors Haridwar's status as one of the four Kumbh Mela sites (with Prayagraj, Ujjain, and Nasik). Tradition credits the 1st-century-BCE king Vikramaditya with first building the ghat in honor of his brother Bharthari, who meditated on the riverbank here; the site's modern form dates largely to the 19th and 20th centuries, expanded after a stampede killed 430 pilgrims during the 1819 Kumbh Mela, with further extensions in 1938 (including a clock tower) and 1986. Each evening, priests lead the Ganga Aarti at the Brahmakund pool, and crowds on both banks set adrift small leaf-and-flower lamps as an offering.
