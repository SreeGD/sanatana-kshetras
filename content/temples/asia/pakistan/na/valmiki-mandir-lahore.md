Valmiki Mandir, tucked into Bhim Street in the Neela Gumbad quarter of Lahore's historic Anarkali Bazaar, is dedicated to the sage Valmiki, traditionally credited as the author of the Ramayana, and serves in particular Pakistan's Balmiki community. Local heritage accounts trace the site's origins back roughly 1,200 years, though this estimate could not be independently verified for this entry; its modern history is considerably better documented, and considerably harder.

After the 1947 Partition, a local family claiming ownership occupied the temple, and in 1992, in the wave of communal violence that followed the Babri Masjid's demolition in India, the temple was attacked, its idols destroyed and its property looted. The site remained under disputed occupation for decades until Pakistan's Evacuee Trust Property Board pursued legal reclamation beginning in 2022, successfully evicting the occupants and restoring the temple to public religious use by August 2022. Now jointly managed by the Pakistan Hindu Council and the Evacuee Trust Property Board, Valmiki Mandir stands today as one of only two functioning Hindu temples in Lahore, celebrating Diwali and Holi as its major festivals.
