Sree Poornathrayeesa Temple at Tripunithura, now part of greater Kochi, occupied a singular place in Kerala's royal religious life as the state deity shrine of the Kingdom of Cochin, foremost among the eight temples patronized by the royal family during the centuries when Tripunithura served as their capital. The presiding image, worshipped as Poornathrayeesa, represents Vishnu in his Santhanagopala Murthy form — associated in legend with restoring life to the deceased sons of a Brahmin at Arjuna's request during the events of the Mahabharata — and the temple has accordingly become a particular destination for childless couples seeking blessings for progeny.

The temple's architecture reflects both tradition and adaptation: after a fire destroyed its original wooden sanctum in 1920, it was rebuilt in concrete, reportedly the first such reconstruction of its kind among Kerala's temples, which had otherwise adhered almost universally to wooden sanctum construction. Poornathrayeesa's ritual calendar centers on the eight-day Vrischikotsavam each November-December, one of Kerala's grandest temple festivals, in which dozens of caparisoned elephants take part in daily processions — a scale of elephant pageantry that, together with the temple's former royal status, makes it one of the defining religious landmarks of the Kochi region, distinct from the city's better-known Devi and Krishna shrines.
