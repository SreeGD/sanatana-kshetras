Chandranath Temple sits atop Chandranath Hill above Sitakunda in Chittagong District, dedicated to Shiva in his local form as Chandranath, worshipped alongside his consort Bhavani. Available sources describe its origin only as "believed" to reach back to around the 11th century, without a securely dated inscription, so the founding period should be read as tradition rather than an established fact. The site is counted among the 51 Shakti Pithas of Shakta tradition — places said to mark where parts of the goddess Sati's body fell after Shiva carried her remains in grief following her self-immolation at her father Daksha's sacrifice; local tradition holds that her right arm fell here.

Historically, the temple drew patronage from regional rulers: Dhanya Manikya, a ruler of neighboring Tripura, is recorded as having endowed the temple, though an attempt attributed to him to remove Shiva's idol to his own kingdom reportedly failed. The temple remains an active pilgrimage destination today, reached by a climb up the hill, with the largest crowds gathering for Maha Shivaratri.
