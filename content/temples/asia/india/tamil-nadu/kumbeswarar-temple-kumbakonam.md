The Adi Kumbeswarar Temple is the principal Shiva shrine of Kumbakonam, one of the Kaveri delta's most temple-dense towns, and a Paadal Petra Sthalam specifically glorified by the Nayanar saint Appar, who referred to the town by its older name Kudamuku and to the deity as "Kumbesar." Shiva is worshipped here as Adi Kumbeswarar, alongside his consort Mangalambigai Amman. The temple's origins are traced to the 9th-century Chola period, with later Vijayanagara and Thanjavur Nayak-era rulers expanding the complex through the 16th century into its present form of roughly 30,181 square feet behind four gopurams, the tallest an eleven-storey eastern tower.

Among the temple's notable features is its Navaratri Mandapam, whose pillars are carved from single stones to depict the 27 nakshatras (lunar stars) and 12 zodiac constellations. The temple, and the town more broadly, is at the center of the Mahamaham festival, held only once every twelve years, when Kumbakonam's sacred tank is believed to draw the waters of nine sacred rivers and pilgrims arrive from across India in numbers reported in the hundreds of thousands — an event often likened locally to a mini-Kumbh Mela.
