Therazhundur, a village of the Kaveri delta in present-day Mayiladuthurai district, takes its name from a story about a submerged chariot: King Uparichara Vasu is said to have driven his ther (chariot) into a flooded tract here, giving the place its name, thanks to the strange episode. In the temple's own telling, the shrine's origins reach back to the semi-legendary Chola king Karikala, said to have raised the first structure here in antiquity, with substantial rebuilding credited to later Chola-period rulers - a claim repeated widely in pilgrimage literature though not verified here against primary epigraphic sources.

The presiding deity, worshipped as Devaadi Raja Perumal and locally as Amaruviappan, is venerated together with his consort Sengamalavalli Thayar, whom Thirumangai Alvar singles out for praise in his Naalayira Divya Prabandham verses dedicated to this shrine - one of the many stops on that Alvar's extensive hymn-circuit of the Chola Nadu Divya Desams. A distinctive local iconographic detail associates the deity here with cattle gathered around him, recalling the Krishna legend in which Brahma, testing Krishna's divinity, hid away his cows and calves only to find them restored and unharmed. Therazhundur is also remembered in Tamil literary history as the traditional birthplace of the poet Kambar, author of the Kamba Ramayanam, giving the village a double significance for both devotional and literary tradition.
