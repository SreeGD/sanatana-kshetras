Carved into a monolithic outcrop in Bengaluru's Gavipuram locality, this rock-cut cave temple to Shiva as Gangadhareshwara is credited to Kempe Gowda I, the 16th-century chieftain regarded as the founder of Bengaluru. Its sanctum is fronted by four monolithic stone pillars fashioned to represent a damaru (drum), a trishul (trident), and two large discs, alongside subsidiary shrines to Parvati, Ganesha, and Murugan. The site is a protected monument under Karnataka's state heritage legislation.

The temple's best-known feature is a solar alignment built into its architecture: around Makara Sankranti in mid-January — in the present-day calendar, roughly the afternoons of 13-16 January, with a related alignment recurring from late November into early December — the setting sun's rays pass through an arc formed between the horns of the temple's Nandi (bull) image and fall directly onto the linga inside the sanctum, drawing large crowds specifically to witness the event alongside the temple's regular Maha Shivaratri observances.
