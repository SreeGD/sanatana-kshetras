## Origin and legend

The Thirunavaya Navamukunda Temple enshrines Vishnu as Navamukundan (Narayana-Vishnu), on the northern bank of the Bharathapuzha river, with subsidiary shrines to Ganesha (as Adi Ganesa/Gajendra), Lakshmi (as Malarmangai Nachiyar, in a separate shrine) and Ayyappa Swami. The temple has no well or pond of its own; all ritual water is drawn from the river, and the riverbank here is held in local tradition to be as sacred for ancestral offerings (bali tharpanam/sradha puja) as the Ganges at Varanasi.

## Historical significance

From ancient times, Thirunavaya was renowned across India as a centre of Vedic learning, with Veda Paadha Saalas (traditional Vedic schools, remembered locally as the Thirunavaya Otthanmar Maddom) teaching the Vedas, Sastras, and Tantric rituals under learned scholars. The temple's grounds also hosted the Mamankam, a grand twelve-yearly assembly of the rulers of Kerala dating from at least the 8th century CE and last held in 1755. The temple was damaged during Tipu Sultan's late 18th-century campaigns in Malabar and again during the 1921 Mappila Rebellion, and stands today rebuilt in Kerala temple architectural style.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thirunavaya is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. Mangalasasanam (verse-glorification) of the temple was performed by both Nammalvar and Thirumangai Alvar, placing Thirunavaya among the 108 Divya Desams celebrated in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, the canon of hymns composed by the Vaishnava Alvar saints between roughly the 6th and 9th centuries CE.
