Built between 1113 and 1150 CE by the Khmer king Suryavarman II as a state temple originally dedicated to Vishnu (and originally named Vrah Vishnuloka, "the sacred dwelling of Vishnu"), Angkor Wat is the largest religious monument in the world and the best-preserved temple of the Angkor archaeological complex — notably, it was never completely abandoned, unlike most other Angkorian sites. It gradually transitioned to Buddhist use from the late 13th century onward and remains an active Buddhist site today, but its foundational architecture, iconography, and dedication are Hindu (Vaishnava) in origin, making it one of the most historically significant Hindu monuments ever built. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1992 (initially also listed as "in danger," a designation removed in 2004) and is preserved primarily as a heritage and archaeological site rather than a site of ongoing Hindu worship.
