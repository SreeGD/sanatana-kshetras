Founded in 1968 under the guidance of ISKCON founder A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, New Vrindaban was one of the first ISKCON farm communities established in the West, intended as a rural, self-sufficient model of Krishna-conscious living modeled loosely on Vrindavan, India. Its best-known structure, Prabhupada's Palace of Gold, was built by community members between 1973 and 1979 as a memorial residence for Prabhupada (who visited it once, shortly before his passing) and became a significant regional tourist attraction, sometimes described in American press as 'America's Taj Mahal.'
