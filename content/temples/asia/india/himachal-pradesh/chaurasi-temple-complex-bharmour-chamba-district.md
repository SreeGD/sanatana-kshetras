At the heart of Bharmour, the former capital of the Chamba princely state (then known as Brahmapura) in Himachal Pradesh's Chamba district, stands the Chaurasi Temple Complex — a cluster of roughly a dozen stone shrines whose name, meaning "eighty-four," is popularly explained by a legend of eighty-four siddhas (accomplished yogis) who are said to have meditated at the site, though the temples themselves number far fewer. Built approximately in the 7th century, the complex is anchored by the Manimahesh Temple, which enshrines a large Shiva linga, alongside the Lakshana Devi Temple — dedicated to Durga and considered the oldest surviving structure in the group, completed around 700 CE during the reign of Meruvarman — a Narasimha Temple honoring Vishnu's man-lion avatar, a Ganesha Temple, a Dharamraj Temple, and a shrine housing a life-size metal Nandi bull. Four of these shrines — the Ganesh, Lakshana Devi, Manimahesh, and Nar Singh (Narasimha) temples — are individually listed as Archaeological Survey of India Monuments of National Importance, and the ASI has carried out conservation work at the Lakshana Devi Temple specifically, including facade cleaning and a protective roof canopy added after the 1950s to shield its Gupta-era-style wood carvings.

Bharmour served as the capital of the Chamba kingdom until the seat of power shifted to present-day Chamba town in 920 CE, and the town remains best known today as the traditional starting base for the high-altitude Manimahesh Kailash pilgrimage above it — a separate site documented elsewhere in this directory. The Chaurasi complex in the town itself, however, stands on its own as one of Himachal Pradesh's oldest surviving temple clusters, combining Shaiva, Shakta, and Vaishnava shrines within a single historic precinct.
