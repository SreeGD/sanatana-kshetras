Naguleswaram, at Keerimalai on the northern tip of the Jaffna Peninsula, is the northernmost of Sri Lanka's five traditional Pancha Ishwaram Shiva shrines and, like its sister temples, has endured a history of destruction and revival that mirrors the island's broader religious and political upheavals. Tradition places its founding in the era of the legendary Prince Vijaya, though this ancient date cannot be independently confirmed; its documented history begins in earnest with the temple's destruction by Portuguese forces in 1621.

Rebuilt in 1894 through the efforts of the Hindu reformer Arumuka Navalar, the temple suffered a further fire in 1918 requiring reconstruction, and endured the Sri Lankan civil war directly: the Sri Lankan Army occupied the site from 1983 to 1997, and Air Force bombings between 1990 and 1993 killed civilians in the surrounding area. A major reconstruction culminated in a Mahakumbhabhishekam consecration ceremony in 2012, restoring Naguleswaram to active worship. The temple's setting beside the Keerimalai springs — mineral waters credited with curative properties — adds a distinct ritual-bathing dimension to worship here, drawing its largest crowds during the Aadi Amaavaasai observance for ancestral rites in the Tamil month of Aadi.
