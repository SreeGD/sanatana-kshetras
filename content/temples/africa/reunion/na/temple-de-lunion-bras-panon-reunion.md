Built within the grounds of the former Union sugar estate in the late 19th century by the estate's owner, per the French Ministry of Culture's Mérimée heritage-database notice, the Temple de l'Union stands in the commune of Bras-Panon on Réunion's northeast coast - a Tamil ("malbar") Hindu temple within the same wave of 19th- and 20th-century temple-building by the descendants of indentured Indian labourers documented across the island. It was registered as a French historic monument on 17 September 2010 (reference PA97400107; cadastral reference AE 358). It is the only currently documented Hindu temple entry for Bras-Panon in this directory.

The Mérimée notice describes decorative guardian-figure statuettes ("monestarlons") set into concrete plinths in front of and behind the main building, a two-slope corrugated-metal roof, and masonry construction. Neither French Wikipedia's stub article nor the Mérimée notice names a presiding deity; that detail remains unconfirmed.
