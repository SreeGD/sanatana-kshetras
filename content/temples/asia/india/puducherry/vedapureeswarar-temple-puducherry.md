Vedapureeswarar Temple, in the heart of Puducherry town, is one of the union territory's principal Shiva temples, enshrining twin presiding deities: Vedapurishvarar, Shiva in lingam form, and Tripurasundari, Parvati worshipped in yoni form. Unlike Puducherry's other well-known temples -- the Ganesha shrine at Manakula Vinayagar and the Vishnu temple at Varadaraja Perumal among them -- Vedapureeswarar anchors the town's Shaiva tradition, and its history is bound up closely with Puducherry's turbulent colonial period rather than with any deep antiquity. After the French East India Company established its settlement at Puducherry from 1674, tensions between the growing Christian and existing Hindu communities erupted into religious rioting in 1748, during which the temple then standing was destroyed.

The temple was rebuilt forty years later, in 1788, through the efforts of Divan Kandappa Mudaliar and public contributions from the local community -- a reconstruction that gives the present temple its documented origin, even though an earlier shrine to the same deities is known to have existed before the 1748 destruction, on what was then Samba Ishvaran Ishvari Street, with no surviving record of when that original temple was founded. The deities were moved to the current site only later, in the closing decades of the nineteenth century. Today the temple presents a substantial South Indian Shaiva complex: a seven-tiered rajagopuram roughly 75 feet tall marks its entrance, a granite wall encloses the compound, and subsidiary shrines to Ganesha, Kartikeya, Shani, and Durga surround the twin main sanctums, with a stepped temple tank completing the grounds. Its Brahmotsavam, held annually in the Tamil month of Vaikasi, remains its largest festival.
