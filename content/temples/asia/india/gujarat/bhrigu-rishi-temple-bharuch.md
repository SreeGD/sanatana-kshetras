Bharuch, at the mouth of the Narmada on Gujarat's coast, carries a name that is itself a piece of religious history: Bhrigukachchha, or Bharukachchha, after the sage Bhrigu, one of the Saptarishi of Hindu tradition. In the old city's Dandia Bazar quarter on the Narmada's eastern bank stands the Bhrigu Rishi Temple, held by local tradition as the spot where the sage composed the Bhrigu Samhita, an early and influential text of Hindu predictive astrology said in devotional accounts to have recorded millions of individual horoscopes. The same quarter of the old city preserves nine Swayambhu, or self-manifested, Shivalingas scattered across different points in Bharuch -- among them Kamnath, Jwalnath, Somnath, Gangnath, and Kashi Vishwanath -- which local tradition credits as the very reason Bhrigu chose this stretch of riverbank for his ashram in the first place.

The Dandia Bazar riverfront has continued to draw temple-building activity well beyond the ancient layer: a Narmada Mata Temple, dedicated to the river as goddess, and a Swaminarayan Temple both stand nearby, each roughly a century and a half to three-quarters of a century old. Together the cluster makes Bharuch -- sitting where the Narmada meets the Gulf of Khambhat -- one of the river's significant pilgrimage points at its mouth, complementing the well-documented source-region shrine at Amarkantak already covered elsewhere in this directory.
