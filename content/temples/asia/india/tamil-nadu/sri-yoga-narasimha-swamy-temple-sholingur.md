Sri Yoga Narasimha Swamy Temple crowns Periya Malai, the larger of twin hills at Sholinghur (Sholingur) in what is now Ranipet district, and is known in Vaishnava tradition by its Tamil and Sanskrit sthala names, Thirukkadigai and Gatikachalam. Narasimha is enshrined here in a serene, seated Yoga posture — hence "Yoga Narasimha" — holding a conch and discus while his other two hands rest in a gesture of meditation, a markedly different mood from the fierce Ugra Narasimha forms found elsewhere. His consort is worshipped as Amirthavalli Thayar, and the neighboring hill, Chinna Malai, carries a related shrine to Bhaktavatsala Perumal and to Yoga Anjaneyar (Hanuman).

The temple's name is explained by a well-known local legend: the sage Vishwamitra is said to have attained the exalted title of Brahmarishi after a period of intense worship here lasting just one kadigai — a traditional unit of time equal to about 24 minutes — giving the site its name Thirukkadigai and its hill the Sanskrit name Gatikachalam ("mountain of the kadigai"). The town itself was earlier known as Cholasimhapuram and, before its rise as a major Vaishnava center, is remembered in local tradition as having been under Shaivite influence.

Thirukkadigai is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham through hymns of Peyalvar and Thirumangai Alvar, placing it among the 108 Divya Desams. Epigraphically the temple is well attested: its earliest inscriptions date to the 10th-century Chola king Parantaka I, with later grants recorded under Kulottunga Chola III and other regional chieftains including Mayuravarman and a ruler named Seeyagangan; the present structure, including its five-tiered rajagopuram, is generally attributed to the Vijayanagara period, with the Sri Vaishnava Acharya tradition also crediting later teachers such as Doddacharya (commemorated in the Doddacharya Utsavam) with strengthening worship here. The 15th–16th century Acharya Manavala Mamunigal is remembered in local tradition for reciting the Thiruppavai at this hill. Today the climb up Periya Malai's roughly 1,300 steps remains a defining feature of a pilgrimage to Sholinghur, one of the more physically demanding but rewarding Divya Desams of the Nadu Nadu group.
