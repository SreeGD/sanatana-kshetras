Dubai's oldest Hindu temple traces to 1958, when Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum, then ruler of Dubai, permitted a Hindu place of worship above a row of shops in what became known as the Bur Dubai Old Souk, west of Dubai Creek. Over the following decades the modest upper-floor complex grew to house separate altars to Shiva and Krishna, alongside a Sikh Gurudwara altar, serving generations of Dubai's Indian expatriate community from a site that never outwardly resembled a temple from the street.

As Dubai's Hindu and Sikh population grew far beyond what the original site could accommodate, a new, purpose-built temple and gurudwara complex opened in the Jebel Ali area on 5 October 2022, featuring nine brass-clad domes and costing a reported AED 60 million. By 3 January 2024, the Shiva Mandir and the Gurudwara had fully relocated to this new Jebel Ali complex, while the Krishna Mandir continues to operate at the original, historic Bur Dubai site — meaning what was once a single temple today functions as two related places of worship in different parts of the city. Together, the complex honors sixteen Hindu and Sikh figures, reflecting the shared devotional space long maintained by Dubai's South Asian communities.
