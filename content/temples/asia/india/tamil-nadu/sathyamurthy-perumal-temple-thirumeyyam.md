Thirumeyyam, a small town in Pudukkottai district built around a rocky hill later crowned by a fort, houses one of the more architecturally distinctive of the 108 Divya Desams. Its presiding deity, worshipped as Sathyamurthy Perumal or Sathyagirinathan, is depicted in a colossal reclining (Anantasayi) form said to be among the largest such Vishnu images in India, larger even than the celebrated reclining form at Srirangam. The consort is venerated as Ujeevana Thayar.

Regional legend explains the site's names, Sathyagiri ("hill of truth"), Sathya Kshetram, and Sathya Theertham, through the story of a sage named Satya who performed long penance here; Vishnu is said to have transformed the surrounding river into the temple's sacred tank (pushkarani) and the rock itself into Meyyam hill, granting the sage liberation and directing a king, Pururava in some retellings, to raise a temple on the spot.

The temple is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham by Thirumangai Alvar, the 8th-9th century Vaishnava poet-saint whose hymns cover an especially wide span of Divya Desams across the Tamil country. Epigraphic evidence places its construction in the 9th century CE under Pallava patronage: an inscription records a donation from Perumbidugu Perumdevi, mother of a local vassal, Sattan Maran, who served under the Pallava king Nandivarman II, with the art historian M. A. Dhaky dating the temple's fabric to the seventh decade of that century. This Pallava-era origin sits somewhat apart from the Pandya dynastic heartland more typical of neighboring Pandya Nadu Divya Desams, though Thirumeyyam is consistently placed in that regional group by dedicated Sri Vaishnava sources. Standing beside a Shiva temple and a later Nayak-period fort on the same outcrop, the Sathyamurthy Perumal temple remains an active site of worship and a marker of the layered religious history of this part of Tamil Nadu.
