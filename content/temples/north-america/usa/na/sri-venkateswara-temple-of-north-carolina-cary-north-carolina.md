Rising demand for a South Indian-style temple in North Carolina's Research Triangle, tracing back to 1988, led a local community to purchase 2.8 acres in Cary in July 1998 and hold a Bhoomi Puja land-purification ceremony the following January. After the Town of Cary approved zoning in 2002 and construction began in 2007, the Sri Venkateswara Temple of North Carolina was consecrated in a Prana Pratishtha ceremony on 29 May 2009 and opened to the public the next day, drawing more than 10,000 guests to the celebration. Modeled on the Tirumala Venkateswara Temple in Dravidian architectural style, its main sanctum houses a nine-foot, 4,000-pound murti of Venkateswara, crafted alongside black granite floors and decorative stonework hand-carved in India by fourteen visiting artisans.

The temple, which now serves an estimated 21,000 Hindus across the Triangle, has grown its campus from 2.8 to 9 acres and in October 2022 inaugurated an 87-foot Rajagopuram entrance tower said to be the tallest of its kind in North America. It has also become a landmark of civic recognition for the state's Hindu community: Governor Roy Cooper signed North Carolina's first-ever Diwali proclamation at the temple on 18 October 2017. The property additionally preserves the Nancy Jones House, Cary's oldest standing structure (built 1803), which the town purchased in 2019 for historic preservation.
