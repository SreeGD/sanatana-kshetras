Thiruppullam Boothangudi, in the Chola country near the sacred Kaveri delta, is one of the 108 Divya Desams, the holy abodes of Lord Vishnu hymned by the Alwars. The presiding deity (Moolavar) is worshipped here as Valvil Raman, that is, Rama bearing the mighty bow (val vil), recalling the Lord in his incarnation as Sri Rama, the prince of Ayodhya and slayer of evil. The very name of the place is associated in tradition with Jatayu, the great eagle-king who gave his life striving to rescue Sita from Ravana; the sthala purana holds that this region is linked to the bird-devotee whose supreme sacrifice Rama honoured, and the suffix 'pullam' (bird) is taken to commemorate this association. Devotees revere the Lord here as the gracious king who upholds righteousness and gives liberation even to a faithful bird. Among the Alwar saints it was Thirumangai Alwar, the warrior-poet and last of the twelve Alwars, who sang the glory of this shrine in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, securing its place forever in the sacred Tamil canon of Sri Vaishnavism. Through his verses the temple is counted among the cherished delta shrines where the Lord reclines and reigns close to the life-giving waters of the Kaveri. Pilgrims who visit Thiruppullam Boothangudi pray to Valvil Raman for protection, for the strength to walk the path of dharma, and for the loving grace that Sri Rama is said to bestow upon all who take refuge in him.

## Festivals

As at most Divya Desam shrines, the devotional year at Thiruppullam Boothangudi traditionally centres on an annual Brahmotsavam, observed over several days with processional honours for the Lord (Valvil Raman) accompanied by recitation of the Tamil Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the Tamil month of Margazhi is kept with special reverence, when devotees gather for darshan of the utsava deity. The temple also customarily marks Sri Rama Navami in keeping with its Ramayana associations, the procession days of Pavithrotsavam for ritual purification, and the broad Srivaishnava cycle of Tamil-calendar observances such as Pongal and the festivals of the lunar months. These are presented as long-observed traditions rather than fixed dated events.

## Location & Access

The temple stands at Thiruppullam Boothangudi, a village in the Chola Nadu region near Swamimalai, within the Thanjavur district of Tamil Nadu and within reach of Kumbakonam, the nearest larger town and transport hub. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours, remaining closed through the midday interval. Pilgrims usually approach by road from Kumbakonam or Thanjavur, with rail connections available at those towns.
