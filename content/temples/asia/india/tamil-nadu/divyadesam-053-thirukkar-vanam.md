The Karvanar Perumal Temple at Thirukkar Vanam stands among the 108 Divya Desams, the supreme sanctuaries of Lord Vishnu glorified in the song of the Alwars. Situated in Thondai Nadu, the venerable northern Tamil region that cradles many of the most ancient Vaishnava shrines around Kanchipuram, this temple enshrines the Moolavar as Karvanar Perumal. The name evokes the dark rain-laden cloud, kar, a beloved image of the Lord in Tamil devotional poetry, for Narayana is ever likened to the cool, dark monsoon cloud that pours its bounty selflessly upon the earth; the place-name Kar Vanam itself carries this fragrance of the dark-hued divine. The Lord here is thus contemplated as the rain-cloud-coloured one, whose very form bespeaks his readiness to shower grace. This Divya Desam was hallowed by the verses of Thirumangai Alwar, that ardent saint among the twelve Alwars whose copious hymns in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham celebrate temple after temple across the sacred geography of the Tamil land. Through his song, Thirukkar Vanam was lifted into the eternal corpus of Sri Vaishnava devotion and joined the company of the 108 abodes venerated by pilgrims. As one of the closely clustered Divya Desams of the Kanchipuram region, the temple shares in the deep antiquity and sanctity of that storied land. Devotees who worship here recall that to behold the Lord as the dark, beneficent cloud is to trust in the ceaseless rain of his mercy.

## Festivals

The annual Brahmotsavam is the foremost celebration, marked by daily processions of the festival deity and special alankarams over its course. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is held in high regard, with continuous chanting of the Divya Prabandham and the devotees' gathering for darshan, in keeping with the practice of the Kanchi temples. Pavithrotsavam, for the annual purification of worship, and the Float festival (Theppotsavam) are also traditionally kept where the temple tank permits. The monthly observances of the deity's birth star and the recitation of Tamil and Sanskrit hymns sustain devotion through the year, alongside the common Vaishnava observances shared across Kanchipuram.
