Tarapith, on the Dwarka river near Rampurhat in West Bengal's Birbhum district, is one of Bengal's most important Shakti Peethas and a major center of tantric (vamachara) worship dedicated to the goddess Tara, second of the ten Mahavidyas. Tradition holds that when Sati's body was cut apart by Vishnu's discus, her third eye fell at this spot, consecrating it as a seat of the Goddess; the site is also revered as a Siddhapith where the sage Vashishtha is said to have performed intense tantric penance — invoking Tara's mantra hundreds of thousands of times — after receiving guidance in left-hand tantric practice. No inscription or dated record for the temple's own construction survives in the sources checked, so its structural age remains undocumented even though its sacred status is old and widely attested.

The temple's modern renown owes much to Bamakhepa (1837-1911), a celebrated tantric saint who lived and meditated in the adjoining cremation ground and is remembered as one of Tara's most devoted votaries; a shrine to him now stands within the complex. The sanctum houses two images of Tara — an older stone form and a later metal icon — and the site draws especially large crowds during Kaushiki Amavasya, its principal annual festival, alongside Kali Puja and other Devi observances. Distinctively among Shakti Peethas, worship at Tarapith continues in the left-hand tantric idiom rather than purely orthodox ritual, reflecting its identity as a living tantric center rather than only a historical monument.
