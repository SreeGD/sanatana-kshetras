Kandariya Mahadeva is the largest and most architecturally ambitious of the surviving Khajuraho temples, built under the Chandela dynasty during the reign of King Vidyadhara in the first half of the 11th century (construction is generally dated to roughly 1025-1050 CE). Rising about 31 meters on a high plinth, its shikhara is composed of 84 subsidiary spires clustering upward toward the main tower, and its walls and interior carry something on the order of 870 individual sculpted figures — the single most elaborate sculptural program among the Chandela temples. The Chandelas built roughly 85 temples across Khajuraho between the 9th and 11th centuries, of which about 25 survive; Kandariya Mahadeva and its neighbors were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.

Khajuraho's sculpture is popularly associated with its erotic carvings, but by most accounts these make up a small fraction — commonly cited as under 10% — of the total imagery, the majority of which depicts deities, mythological narrative, and scenes of ordinary daily life. Kandariya Mahadeva itself is maintained today primarily as a protected archaeological monument rather than a site of regular worship; the nearby Matangeshwar temple, by contrast, remains in active daily use, a distinction worth noting for visitors expecting an operating shrine.
