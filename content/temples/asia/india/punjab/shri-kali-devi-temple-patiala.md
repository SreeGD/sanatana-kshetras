Patiala's Kali Devi Temple has an unusually precise, well-documented founding: it was built between 1936 and 1938 at the initiative of Maharaja Bhupinder Singh, the princely state's ruler from 1900 to 1938, who is recorded as having personally brought a roughly six-foot image of the goddess Kali, along with a sacred flame, from Bengal to consecrate the new temple. That Bengal connection places the shrine within a broader pattern of early 20th-century Shakta devotion spreading beyond its Bengali heartland through royal and elite patronage.

Sited opposite the city's Baradari Gardens, the temple has since become a significant local institution regarded by many Patiala families — both Hindu and Sikh — as their kul devi, or hereditary family deity, a role that reflects Punjab's characteristic religious overlap between Sikh and Hindu devotional practice. The two annual Navratri observances remain its busiest period, drawing large numbers of visitors over the course of each nine-day festival.
