Built in 1979 by Swami B.A. Narayan and his followers, the Nandafalva Hindu Temple in Csongrád County, southeastern Hungary, predates by over a decade the country's much larger and better-known Krishna Valley (New Vraja-dhama) community in Somogyvámos, already documented elsewhere in this directory, making it one of the earliest Hare Krishna-tradition temples established in communist-era Hungary.

Situated roughly 19 kilometers from the Ópusztaszer National Heritage Park, a major Hungarian historical site, the temple's architecture — designed by Gyula Szigeti — is described in Wikipedia's overview of Hinduism in Hungary as a deliberate blend of Hungarian and Hindu styles, distinguishing it from more purely South Asian-style temple architecture elsewhere in this directory. Beyond its 1979 founding date, founder, location, and architectural description, further detail on the temple's current congregation and activities could not be confirmed in the source available for this entry.
