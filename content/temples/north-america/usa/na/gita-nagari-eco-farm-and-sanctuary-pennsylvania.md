Gita Nagari Eco-Farm and Sanctuary is a rural Gaudiya Vaishnava community located across Spruce Hill and Beale Townships in Juniata County, Pennsylvania, near Port Royal and Academia, established as one of ISKCON's early farm communities in the tradition of self-sufficient, cow-protection-centered rural life advocated by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, alongside better-known sister communities such as New Vrindaban in West Virginia. The property includes a dining hall and temple annex, a welcome center, and a creamery. The community gained wider notice as the place where ISKCON guru Bhakti Tirtha Swami died on 27 June 2005 following complications from melanoma. This entry documents Gita Nagari's confirmed location and significance; a precise founding date could not be verified in this research pass and is flagged for future confirmation.
