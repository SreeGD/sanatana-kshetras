The Shri Venkateswara (Balaji) Temple traces its origins to Telugu Vaishnavite families who settled in and around Birmingham from the 1970s and initially gathered to worship at the Shree Geeta Bhawan Mandir in Handsworth. As the community grew, it sought a site to build a temple modeled directly on the Tirumala Venkateswara Temple in Andhra Pradesh, India — one of Hinduism's most-visited pilgrimage sites. In 1987 the Black Country Development Corporation donated a 30-acre plot in Tividale, in the Sandwell borough just outside Birmingham, and after a decade of fundraising that raised roughly £6.5 million, including a Millennium Commission grant, construction began in 1997. The temple opened to the public in 2000 and was formally consecrated in 2006.

Built as a replica of the Tirumala complex, the Tividale temple centers on Venkateswara, a form of Vishnu, and houses subsidiary shrines to Ganesha, Kartikeya, the Navagraha, Shiva, Hanuman, and Lakshmi beneath its gopuram tower. At the time of its opening it was reported to cover the largest land area of any Hindu temple in Europe, a claim that reflects both the scale of the fundraising effort and the ambitions of a Telugu community that had, a generation earlier, been worshipping in a converted hall in Handsworth. The grounds also include the Gandhi Peace Centre, which runs yoga, meditation, library, and exhibition programs on the life of Mahatma Gandhi alongside the temple's core religious functions.
