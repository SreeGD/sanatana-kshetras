Tezpur, headquarters of Sonitpur district on the north bank of the Brahmaputra in Assam, is traditionally identified with the Puranic city of Shonitpur ("city of blood," from the Sanskrit *sonita*), capital of the powerful demon-king Banasura. This identification rests on a long local and textual tradition — recorded in the locally composed Kalika Purana as well as wider Hindu memory — rather than on archaeological proof, but it is a deeply rooted one: the Assamese name Tezpur itself is popularly read as an equivalent of "Sonitpur." The Bhagavata Purana's account of Banasura's daughter Usha and her secret love for Aniruddha, grandson of Krishna — Aniruddha's abduction to Shonitpur, his imprisonment, and the resulting war in which Krishna and Banasura's patron deity Shiva both take the field — is traditionally set at and around Tezpur, most famously at the Agnigarh hillock overlooking the river, popularly said to be where Banasura confined Usha.

On a hillock in the northern part of Tezpur town stands the Mahabhairav Temple, a functioning Shiva temple that local tradition credits to King Bana (Banasura) himself, said to have built an original stone shrine here that was later rebuilt in concrete. Whatever the truth of that founding legend, the temple's later history is better documented: during Ahom rule, kings of the Tungkhungia dynasty granted land endowments to the temple and appointed pujaris and paiks (attendants) for its upkeep, and today it is managed by the Government of Assam through a committee headed by the Deputy Commissioner of Sonitpur. The temple's Shiva linga is popularly described as a "living stone" believed to grow slowly over time. Maha Shivaratri is the temple's major annual festival, drawing devotees from across the region, with distinctive local ritual offerings of bhang-laced laddus mixed with milk and spices, and a customary release of pigeons said to symbolize the liberation of ancestral spirits.

The Mahabhairav Temple is thus a genuinely active, currently worshipped, and administratively documented temple, whose founding legend directly ties it to Banasura — the same figure at the center of the Usha-Aniruddha narrative that gives Tezpur/Sonitpur its Puranic identity — even though that founding legend itself, like the broader Tezpur-Shonitpur identification, remains tradition rather than settled history.
