Ratanpur, once the capital of the Kalachuri dynasty that ruled this part of Chhattisgarh, is home to the Mahamaya temple, traditionally founded by the Kalachuri king Ratnadeva in the 12th-13th century after he is said to have experienced a vision of the goddess Kali at the site — a legend explaining the temple's origin rather than a dated inscription. The temple was substantially expanded later, in 1492 CE (Vikram Samvat 1552) under a ruler named Bahar Sai, giving the present structure, built in Nagara style and facing north beside a temple tank, a layered construction history spanning several centuries.

The goddess is worshipped here as Mahamaya, also known locally as Kosaleswari, and is associated with both Durga and Mahalakshmi; local tradition holds that the shrine originally honoured three goddesses together — Maha Kali, Maha Lakshmi, and Maha Saraswati — before Maha Kali was said to have left the site, a detail preserved in oral tradition rather than any documented record. The temple is counted among the 52 Shakta pithas of the subcontinent and stands alongside other old shrines at Ratanpur, including the Kantideval temple (the oldest structure in the complex, built in 1039 CE and later expanded under the Kalachuri king Prithvideva II) and the ruined 11th-century Kadeideol Shiva temple. It has been renovated under the Archaeological Survey of India and has been managed since 1982 by the Sidh Shakti Peeth trust, drawing its largest crowds during Chaitra and Sharad Navaratri.
