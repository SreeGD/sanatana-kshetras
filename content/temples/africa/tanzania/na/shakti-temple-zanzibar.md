The Shakti Temple in Zanzibar's Stone Town serves what is today a small but historically significant Hindu community on an island whose Indian trading connections go back generations. Zanzibar's Sultan Said bin Sultan actively encouraged merchants from the Indian subcontinent to settle and do business on the island during the nineteenth century, and Gujarati Hindu trading families, particularly from the Bhatia merchant community, became a fixture of Stone Town's commercial life; the Bombay Bhatia merchant Jetha Liladhar, for instance, is documented as having founded the Jetha Lila banking house in Zanzibar in 1880, one of the island's few locally owned banks.

Dedicated to Shakti, the divine feminine principle worshipped across many forms and names in the Shakta tradition, the temple represents a less common dedication among East Africa's Hindu temples, most of which follow Vaishnava traditions such as Swaminarayan or ISKCON. Precise details of when this particular temple was built and by which community association remain thinly documented in sources accessible for this entry, but its continued presence on lists of Tanzania's active Hindu temples indicates it remains a living place of worship for Zanzibar's Hindu residents today.
