Thiruparkadal, the Ocean of Milk, is one of two celestial (non-physical) entries among the 108 Divya Desams — sacred not as a place a pilgrim can travel to, but as a plane of existence described in the Divya Prabandham. Here Vishnu, in His form as Ksheerabdinathan/Sheeraapthi Naathan, reclines eternally upon the coils of Adishesha, the cosmic serpent, in the milky ocean that predates and underlies the material creation. His two consorts, Sridevi and Bhudevi, are present at His side, and the site is described with its own sacred waters, Amrutha Theertham (the nectar of immortality).

Traditionally, Thiruparkadal is understood as an intermediate station on the devotee's spiritual journey: having completed pilgrimage to the 106 earthly Divya Desams, a devotee is said to be granted a vision of the Lord in this celestial ocean before the final stage — Paramapadam, the eternal spiritual world itself.

Nine Alvars composed a combined 51 verses glorifying this site, making it one of the more extensively sung-of Divya Desams despite having no physical location: Thirumazhisai Alvar (14 verses) and Thirumangai Alvar (10 verses) contribute the largest shares, with Nammalvar, Perialvar, Aandaal, Peyalvar, Boodathalvar, Kulasekaralvar, and Thondaradipodi Alvar each adding further verses.
