Sirkazhi, one of Tamil Nadu's great twin centres of Shaiva and Vaishnava devotion, is home not only to the Shiva temple associated with the child-saint Sambandar but also to this Divya Desam, where Vishnu is worshipped as Thrivikrama Perumal, more popularly known here as Thadalan - "the one who measured the earth" - recalling the Vamana avatara in which Vishnu, as a dwarf, grew to cosmic size and spanned the earth, the sky, and the heavens in three strides to reclaim the universe from the asura king Mahabali.

Local tradition holds that Thirumangai Alvar composed his hymns in praise of Thadalan Perumal here as part of a spirited exchange with the Shaiva saint Sambandar, each glorifying his own chosen deity in verse - a story cherished in this region where Vaishnava and Shaiva pilgrimage sites sit within a few kilometres of each other, though it is preserved mainly in oral and pilgrimage-site tradition rather than in an independently datable historical record. The temple, built in the Dravidian style common to the delta's Divya Desams, forms a natural stop for pilgrims visiting the nearby cluster of eleven Vishnu shrines at Thirunangur, and its inclusion in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham secures its place among the 108 Divya Desams glorified by the Alvars.
