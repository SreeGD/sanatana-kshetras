Built in the early 12th century by the Khmer king Suryavarman II as a state temple originally dedicated to Vishnu, Angkor Wat is the largest religious monument in the world and the best-preserved temple of the Angkor archaeological complex. It later transitioned to Buddhist use from the late 13th century onward and remains an active Buddhist site today, but its foundational architecture, iconography, and dedication are Hindu (Vaishnava) in origin, making it one of the most historically significant Hindu monuments ever built, now preserved primarily as a UNESCO World Heritage cultural and archaeological site rather than a site of ongoing Hindu worship.
