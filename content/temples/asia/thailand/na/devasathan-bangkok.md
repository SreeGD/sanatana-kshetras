Devasathan was founded in 1784 by King Rama I, the first ruler of Thailand's Chakri dynasty, as the seat of the kingdom's Court Brahmins — a hereditary priesthood who, though functioning within an overwhelmingly Buddhist state, perform Hindu-derived royal and state ceremonies for the Thai monarchy. The complex consists of three main shrine buildings dedicated to Shiva, Ganesha, and Vishnu (worshipped locally as Narayana), together with an outdoor shrine to Brahma, and is officially regarded as the center of Hindu religious practice in Thailand.

Unlike a typical Hindu temple, Devasathan is not primarily a site of daily public worship; it functions as a working ritual institution for court ceremonies rather than a pilgrimage destination, and public access is limited. It stands beside Bangkok's Giant Swing, once the venue for the Triyampavai-Tripavai ceremony honoring Shiva and Vishnu, which was discontinued in 1935 after the swing was deemed unsafe; the swing structure itself has since been restored as a historic landmark even though the ceremony it once hosted is no longer performed there.
