Sri Veeraraghava Perumal Temple in Thiruvallur — anciently known as Thiru Evvul, and in Puranic sources as Kingirukesha Puri or Veeksharanyam — enshrines Vishnu as Veeraraghava Perumal, with Lakshmi as his consort Kanakavalli Thayar (also called Vasumathi). The presiding deity carries the additional epithet "Vaidhya Veeraraghavan," reflecting a long-held belief among devotees that worship here can help cure illness, and the site's Puranic name Veeksharanyam is said to be extolled across eleven chapters of the Markandeya Purana.

Two intertwined legends explain the town's name and the temple's founding. In one account drawn from the Markandeya Purana, an aged Brahmin — Vishnu in disguise — approached a devout householder named Salihotra and asked, "Evvul?" ("Where may I lie down?"), and the place where he was received and given rest came to be called Thiru Evvul. A second, related tradition holds that Vishnu appeared at this spot to marry Lakshmi, here identified as the daughter of a king (variously named as Dilipa or Dharmasena), further cementing the site's sanctity as a place of divine union and hospitality.

Thiruvallur is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham by the hymns of Thirumangai Alvar and Thirumalisai Alvar, securing its place among the 108 Divya Desams. The temple's construction is traditionally attributed to the Pallava dynasty in the 8th century CE, with inscriptions from the Chola period recording donations and further architectural additions and patronage from the Vijayanagara kings in later centuries. As the principal Divya Desam of Thiruvallur district and the namesake town's most prominent temple, Sri Veeraraghava Perumal Temple remains an active center of pilgrimage, drawing devotees both for its Divya Desam status and for its particular reputation, as "Vaidhya Veeraraghavan," as a place to pray for healing.
