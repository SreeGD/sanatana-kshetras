The Temple du Colosse takes its name from the Colosse sugar factory in Saint-André, on Réunion's eastern coast, one of many sites across the island where a Hindu temple grew up beside a sugar estate to serve the Tamil-origin indentured laborers recruited to work its fields from the second half of the nineteenth century onward, after slavery's abolition in 1848 created an acute labor shortage that planters filled through the "engagisme" indenture system. A first temple was built on the site in 1900; that original structure was eventually lost, and the temple standing today was rebuilt in 1991 on the same ground.

The present building follows the oblong sanctum plan typical of South Indian Hindu temple architecture, with polychrome decorative work carried out by artists brought from Madras (Chennai), and is dedicated principally to the goddess Pandialé, alongside Mariamman and Karli, whose festivals structure much of the temple's annual calendar. Ownership of the temple was formally and legally settled on 13 August 2012, when the property deed was signed over to the Association Pandialée du Colosse, the body that manages it today; a decade later, on 14 April 2023 - Tamil New Year - the temple unveiled a commemorative marble stele in its courtyard recording that transfer. The temple remains an active and visited site of Réunion's Tamil Hindu community, one of several such temples on the island now recognized for their historic and cultural significance.
