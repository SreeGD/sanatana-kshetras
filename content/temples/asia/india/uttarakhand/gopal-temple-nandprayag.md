Nandprayag (also spelled Nandaprayag) is where the Nandakini river joins the Alaknanda, the second of the Panch Prayag on the route north from Rishikesh toward Badrinath. Its name is explained by more than one local tradition: one account holds that a King Nanda performed a yagna (fire-sacrifice) here and received a divine blessing; another identifies the site with Nanda, the Yadava chief who, with his wife Yashoda, fostered the infant Krishna after Vishnu granted the boon of a son to both Yashoda-Nanda and to the imprisoned Devaki-Vasudeva, so that Krishna was born to Devaki but raised at Nanda's home. The confluence carries still further legendary associations — the sage Kanva is said to have performed penance here, and this is also given as the site of the wedding of King Dushyanta and Shakuntala, the celebrated pair of the Mahabharata and of Kalidasa's play. A Gopal Temple, dedicated to Krishna in his cowherd (Gopal) form, stands at the confluence, fitting for a site so thickly associated with Krishna's foster-family story; no construction date or founder is documented for it in the sources checked.

Nandprayag lies on National Highway 58 between Karnaprayag and Chamoli/Joshimath, and like the other Panch Prayag it is a stop for pilgrims travelling the Char Dham route toward Badrinath. Reported figures for the town's own elevation are inconsistent between sources (roughly 914 m in one figure, 1,358 m as an "average elevation" in another), and this entry has not attempted to resolve that discrepancy.
