Mathura's Krishna Janmasthan Temple Complex, also known as Katra Keshav Dev, marks the site Vaishnava tradition holds as the birthplace of Krishna, the eighth avatara of Vishnu. The Bhagavata Purana recounts that Krishna was born here to Devaki and Vasudeva while they were imprisoned by Devaki's tyrannical brother, King Kamsa, who had been warned that her eighth child would bring about his death.

Archaeological evidence points to religious activity at this site from at least the 6th century BCE, and a major temple was rebuilt here around 400 CE under the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II. Like many prominent temples in the region, the site suffered repeated destruction under later rulers; most significantly, the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb's forces destroyed the standing Keshavdeva temple in 1670 and built the Shahi Eidgah mosque on part of the grounds — a mosque that still stands immediately adjacent to the temple complex today.

The present temple complex took shape gradually across the 20th century, with construction running from 1953 to 1982 and funded substantially by industrialist families including the Dalmias and the Birlas; the Keshavdeva Temple itself was built by industrialist Ramkrishna Dalmia in memory of his mother, Jadiadevi Dalmia, and inaugurated on 6 September 1958. It is managed today by the Shri Krishna Janmasthan Seva Sansthan trust and comprises several distinct structures: the Garbha Griha shrine, set against the rear wall of the adjoining Shahi Eidgah, houses a marble pavilion over an underground prison cell said to mark Krishna's birthplace, alongside a shrine to the goddess Yogmaya; the Bhagavata Bhavan, built between 1965 and 1982, holds five shrines including a main sanctuary with 180-centimetre-tall Radha-Krishna images; and the grounds also include the stepped Potra Kund water tank, an Ayurveda Bhavan, a guest house, and a library. The complex's shared boundary with the neighboring Shahi Eidgah mosque remains the subject of active, unresolved litigation in the Mathura courts over a 1968 agreement governing the site — a dispute that has drawn national attention in recent years even as pilgrimage to the site continues, peaking each year around Krishna Janmashtami, when devotees gather in especially large numbers through the night to mark Krishna's birth; Radhashtami, Holi, and Diwali are also observed as major festivals here.
