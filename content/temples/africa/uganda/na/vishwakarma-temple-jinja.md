Jinja, on the shore of Lake Victoria at the source of the Nile, was historically one of Uganda's principal industrial and commercial towns and home to a substantial Asian (mostly Gujarati and Punjabi) trading and industrial community, a legacy still visible today in enterprises such as the Madhvani Group's nearby Kakira Sugar Works. A Hindu temple dedicated to Vishwakarma - the Vedic deity traditionally revered as the divine architect and patron of craftsmen, engineers, and industrial workers - stands in the town, documented through photographs in Wikipedia's coverage of both Jinja specifically and of Hinduism in Uganda more broadly.

Beyond its name and location, little could be verified about this temple's founding, congregation, or day-to-day life during this research pass. Its dedication to Vishwakarma would fit naturally with Jinja's history as an industrial and artisanal center of Uganda's Asian community, a community that, like Hindu communities elsewhere in Uganda, was uprooted by Idi Amin's 1972 expulsion of Asians and only partially rebuilt in the decades since; Uganda's 2014 national census recorded 13,905 Hindus in the country as a whole. No source found during this pass, however, directly documents this temple's own history or confirms this connection to Jinja's industrial community as fact rather than plausible inference.
