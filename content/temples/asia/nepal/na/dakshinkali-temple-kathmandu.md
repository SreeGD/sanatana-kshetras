Dakshinkali Temple, at the southern rim of the Kathmandu Valley near Pharping village, is one of Nepal's most important Shakti pilgrimage sites and among the country's most visited temples generally. The presiding image shows the goddess Kali in her fierce Dakshinakali aspect, depicted with a foot resting on the chest of Shiva. No firmly documented founding date or founder appears in the sources consulted for this entry, and the temple's own name carries more than one traditional explanation — one tying it simply to its position south of the valley, another to a legend in which Yama, the lord of death, is said to tremble at the mere sound of Kali's name here, a story devotees read as a promise that worship at this site helps overcome death itself.

The temple is best known for its distinctive form of worship: animal sacrifice, chiefly of roosters and uncastrated male goats, offered by pilgrims especially on Tuesdays and Saturdays, the site's principal ritual days. Crowds swell further during the Dashain festival, when the practice of sacrificial offering to the goddess is at its most intense across Nepal. Despite the lack of a settled foundation narrative, Dakshinkali functions today as a living, heavily visited shrine that Nepali tradition treats as being on a par with Pashupatinath and Manakamana among the country's major centers of Hindu devotion.
