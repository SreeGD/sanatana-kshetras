Erected in 1899 in the Inanda area near Mount Edgecombe, north of Durban, the Mount Edgecombe Ganesha Temple holds particular architectural significance as the first building designed by Kristappa Reddy, who went on to become a well-known architect and builder within Natal's Indian community. The temple was formally recognised as a provincial heritage site in a 1977 Government Gazette notice, which describes it as forming "a unique part of South Africa's cultural and architectural heritage."

Dedicated to Ganesha, the temple's architecture includes a heavily modulated perimeter wall, an entrance portal topped with a sikhara tower, and a square cella set on axis behind it — features noted in its own heritage listing. Beyond its founding date, builder, and heritage status, no further detail on its current worship schedule or festival life was located during this research pass.
