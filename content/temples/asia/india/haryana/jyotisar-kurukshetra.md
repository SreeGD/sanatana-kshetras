Jyotisar, a short distance from Kurukshetra town on the banks of the small Jyotisar Sarovar wetland, is revered as the exact spot where Krishna delivered the Bhagavad Gita to Arjuna on the eve of the Mahabharata war -- the discourse in which Krishna sets out the doctrines of karma and dharma and reveals his cosmic, universal form (vishvarupa) to a stunned Arjuna. That precise identification is itself a matter of tradition rather than something the Mahabharata's own text pins to a fixed location; later devotional memory attributes the identification of this particular site to the eighth-century philosopher Adi Shankara, centuries after the events the epic describes. A large banyan tree standing on a raised plinth at the site is popularly venerated as a direct descendant of the original tree under which Krishna is said to have spoken, though naturally no botanical or documentary record can confirm an unbroken lineage back to the epic period.

Beyond the tree and its associated legend, Jyotisar also holds an old Shiva temple, where local tradition places both the Kaurava and Pandava princes at worship before the great battle -- a detail that, like the site's central Gita association, belongs to devotional memory rather than dated history. The Kurukshetra Development Board now administers the site, which sits within the broader network of holy tanks and shrines that make up Kurukshetra's traditional 48 kos parikrama, and has in recent years become the focus of substantial state investment, including a large museum project on the Gita and the Mahabharata and inclusion on the government's Krishna Circuit pilgrimage route. The site's largest annual gathering comes with the International Gita Mahotsav, which culminates each December in the Gita Deepotsav, when the sacred tanks of Kurukshetra are lit with hundreds of thousands of earthen lamps.
