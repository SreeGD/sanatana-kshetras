Saendha Poomangalam, a village in Thoothukudi (Tuticorin) district along the Thamirabarani river corridor, is the ninth and final temple of the Nava Kailasam group and is associated with Venus (Sukran) among the Navagrahas in that group's tradition — a distinct tradition from the separate Navagraha temple circuit near Kumbakonam further north in Tamil Nadu.

The presiding deity is worshipped here as Kailasanathar, in keeping with the naming shared by most temples in the group. Tradition holds that the nine Nava Kailasam sites trace to the sage Agastya's disciple Uromacha Munivar, instructed to set nine flowers afloat on the Thamirabarani river and to install a Shiva lingam wherever each one came to rest; the disciple is said to have attained liberation only after venerating all nine of these shrines and bathing where the river meets the sea, making this ninth and final temple the traditional completion of the pilgrimage circuit.

The village's name appears in varied transliterations across the informal sources that mention it at all (Saendha Poomangalam, Sendamaram, Sendhan Poomangalam among them); only the Wikipedia "Nava Kailasam" group article could be located and loaded for this entry, so its spelling is used as primary with the likely variant recorded as an unconfirmed alt_name.
