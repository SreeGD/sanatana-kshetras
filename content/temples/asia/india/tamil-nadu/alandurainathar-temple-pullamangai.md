The Alandurainathar Temple at Pullamangai, a village in Thanjavur district's Papanasam taluk, is a comparatively small but art-historically important Paadal Petra Sthalam, glorified by the Nayanar saint Thirugnanasambandar in the Tevaram. Shiva is worshipped here in two forms, Brahmapureeswarar and Anandeeswarar; no original shrine to a consort goddess has survived. The temple is cited by the art historian James Harle as one of only four early Chola-period temples to survive largely unaltered, built in a style still closely continuing Pallava architectural conventions — making it a valuable, if lesser-known, reference point for the transition between the two dynasties' temple architecture.

The surviving garbhagriha (sanctum) and arthamandapa (front hall) preserve their original early Chola fabric, including miniature sculptural panels narrating scenes from the Ramayana and niche images of Dakshinamurthy, Lingodhbhavar, Brahma, Durga, Vishnu, Narasimha, and Tripurantaka. The temple remains an active, if modest, site of worship, with Maha Shivaratri as its principal annual observance.
