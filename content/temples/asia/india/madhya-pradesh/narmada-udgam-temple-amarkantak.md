At Amarkantak, a plateau town in Madhya Pradesh regarded as one of Hinduism's great tirtharaj ("king of pilgrimage places") sites, the Narmada Temple marks the traditional udgam, or source, of the Narmada river — one of India's most sacred waterways, revered in its own right as a goddess, Narmada Devi. Local legend holds that Adi Shankaracharya, seeking to identify the river's true point of origin among several candidate streams in the area, confirmed the stream flowing past this temple as the authentic source, cementing its status as the pilgrimage focus of the town. The present temple structure is credited to 12th-century construction under the Kalachuris of Tripuri, a dynasty that controlled the wider region at the time, with later renovation attributed to the prolific 18th-century Maratha patron Ahilyabai Holkar, who is documented elsewhere in this directory for similarly renovating the Vishnupad Temple at Gaya and other major sites.

Some general reference listings of the Shakti Peethas include Amarkantak among the fifty-one, naming the right buttock as the fragment of Sati's body associated with the site — but this claim does not appear in the main account of the Narmada Temple and Amarkantak consulted for this entry, which frames the site's sanctity entirely around the river's origin rather than the Sati legend. This entry therefore treats the Shakti Peetha attribution as one strand of a wider, not fully reconciled, pitha tradition rather than as settled fact, while the temple's role as the Narmada's sacred source and its documented 12th-century and 18th-century building history are more firmly attested.
