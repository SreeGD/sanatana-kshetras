Thirumaliruncholai, popularly known as Azhagar Koil, enshrines the Lord as Kallazhagar (Sundararaja Perumal), and stands among the foremost of the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alwars. Set amid a verdant hill cluster of forest groves north of Madurai in the Pandya country, the temple is celebrated for the surpassing beauty of its presiding deity, whose very name Azhagar declares him 'the Beautiful One.' According to the sthala purana, this is a most ancient abode, hallowed by the Lord's perpetual residence upon the sacred hill, and tradition holds it to be linked with the great Vaishnava centre of Madurai through living festival custom. The glory of this kshetra is sung richly in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham: the early Mudhal Alwars Bhoothath Alwar and Pey Alwar invoke it, and Nammalwar, the foremost of the Alwars, pours forth his devotion to the Lord of Thirumaliruncholai. Periyalwar and his daughter Andal, the garland-giving saint, also sing its praise, and Thirumangai Alwar adds his verses, making this a temple cherished across the breadth of the Prabandham. The most renowned distinction of Kallazhagar is the great Chithirai festival, in which the Lord ceremonially journeys to the river Vaigai near Madurai, a procession that draws vast assemblies of devotees and binds together Saiva and Vaishnava traditions of the region. As a hill abode of luxuriant beauty, abundant groves, and ancient sanctity, Thirumaliruncholai remains one of the most beloved and frequently extolled of the sacred shrines of Vishnu in the Tamil land.

## Festivals

Set amid the Azhagar hills near Madurai, this temple is renowned for its grand annual festival cycle, most famously the Chithirai festival, when Kallazhagar (Azhagar) traditionally journeys toward Madurai and to the river Vaigai in a celebrated procession that draws vast crowds. A ten-day Brahmotsavam is observed with the Lord taken in procession on the customary vahanas, and Vaikunta Ekadasi is kept during the Tamil month of Margazhi with special early-morning darshan. Devotees also gather for the Float festival (Theppotsavam) and Pavithrotsavam, alongside the regular festivals of the Vaishnava calendar such as Pongal, Tamil and Telugu New Year, Navaratri and Deepavali. These observances are traditionally marked with abhishekam, alankaram and Divya Prabandham recitation.

## Location & Access

The temple stands at Thirumaliruncholai (Azhagar Koil), set in the wooded Azhagar hills in the Pandya Nadu region, a short distance to the north-east of Madurai city, which is the nearest major hub for rail, road and air connections. Local buses and taxis from Madurai serve the hill shrine. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday closure.
