Thiruneeragam, located within the sacred temple-city of Kanchipuram in Thondai Nadu, is honoured as one of the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alwars in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The presiding deity, the Moolavar, is worshipped here as Neeragathan, the Lord whose very name evokes his association with sacred waters, neer meaning water in Tamil. Tradition counts this among the cluster of Divya Desams gathered in and around Kanchipuram, several of which are revered as shrines whose Lord is present in subtle and inward forms rather than always in grand independent temples. The glory of Thiruneeragam was sung by Thirumangai Alwar, the great warrior-saint whose extensive pilgrimages and copious pasurams secured for so many shrines of the Thondai country their place in the canonical corpus. By his hymning, this abode was raised into the sacred hundred and eight, and his verses preserve its memory as a place where the Lord graciously chose to dwell and bless his devotees. Faithful to the reticence of the tradition where elaborate legend is not firmly attested, the abiding significance of Thiruneeragam rests in its inclusion in the Divine Prabandham itself, for the Alwars sang only of those abodes where they beheld the living presence of the Lord. Pilgrims journeying through the Divya Desams of Kanchipuram worship Neeragathan as part of this constellation of holy shrines, receiving the grace of the Lord celebrated in the Tamil Veda of the Alwars and cherished in the devotional life of Srivaishnavas.

## Festivals

This temple at Thiruneeragam, counted among the Divya Desams of Kanchipuram, traditionally follows the established Srivaishnava festival calendar. An annual Brahmotsavam with processions on the customary vahanas forms the principal celebration. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is observed with the chanting of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, and Pavithrotsavam is kept for the ritual purification of the year's worship. The Kanchipuram region's shared observances, including Garuda Sevai and the recitation of the Tiruvaimozhi, are marked here, along with Pongal-day and other seasonal offerings. Where specific details are modest, the temple maintains the common round of Vaishnava festivals that honour Perumal through the year, all traditionally observed.

## Location & Access

Thiruneeragam lies within the temple city of Kanchipuram in the historic Thondai Nadu region of Tamil Nadu. Kanchipuram is well served by road and rail and is within travelling distance of Chennai, the nearest major transport hub. As with most Vishnu temples, darshan is generally available during morning and evening hours, with a break around midday.
