Punta Arenas, the remote Patagonian capital of Chile's Magallanes Region near the Strait of Magellan, is home to a Hindu temple serving the city's small but long-established Sindhi community — merchant families who began settling in the area from the early 1900s, drawn by regional trade. Over time the community grew large enough to establish and sustain a dedicated place of worship, one repeatedly noted in reference sources as the southernmost Hindu temple anywhere in the world, offering services in both Sindhi and Spanish.

Both English- and Spanish-language sources confirm the temple's existence and its role for the local Hindu community, but none records its formal name, exact founding date, presiding deity, or building history, so those specifics should be treated as unconfirmed pending further documentation.
