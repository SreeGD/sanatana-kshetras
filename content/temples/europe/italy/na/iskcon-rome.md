ISKCON's (International Society for Krishna Consciousness) first foothold in Italy was Rome, where the movement arrived in 1973 and opened its first Italian center — the country's earliest organized Hare Krishna presence, predating both the Matha Gitananda Ashram in Altare (founded 1984) and Villa Vrindavana near Florence, both already documented elsewhere in this directory. The following year, in 1974, the movement's founder, A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, personally visited Rome and delivered a public lecture there, an early milestone in the movement's spread across Italy. Rome's ISKCON community gained further institutional footing decades later: in 2019, Italy's national ISKCON body, the Congregazione Italiana per la Coscienza di Krishna, received official recognition by Italian presidential decree.

A centre currently operates under the name "Hare Krishna Cave 2.0" at Via Sardegna 55 in Rome's Ludovisi quarter, near Via Veneto, alongside an affiliated centre in nearby Velletri; it is not confirmed whether this is the same physical site that opened in 1973 or a later relocation. Rome is one of nine ISKCON/Hare Krishna centres across Italy today, alongside Milan, Bologna, Turin, Genoa, Terni, and the country's three flagship rural properties. Fuller documentation of the Rome centre's continuous institutional history would need to be confirmed directly with ISKCON Italy.
