Skanda Vale was founded in 1973 by Guru Sri Subramaniam, a Hindu teacher of Tamil-Malaysian background, on a 22-acre smallholding near the village of Llanpumsaint in rural Carmarthenshire, Wales. The original farmhouse became the community's first shrine, the Subramanium Temple, dedicated to Murugan (Skanda), the deity from whom the community takes its name. Over the following decades the site grew into a small cluster of temples reflecting the founder's broader devotional outlook: the Maha Shakti Temple, dedicated to the goddess Kali, was consecrated in 1991, and the Sri Ranganatha Temple, dedicated to Vishnu, followed in 1999. Guru Sri Subramaniam died in 2007, the same year the community was drawn into national controversy when Skanda, a temple bullock that had tested positive for bovine tuberculosis, was ordered slaughtered by Welsh agricultural authorities despite the community's objections rooted in Hindu principles of protecting sacred cattle — a case that drew international Hindu community involvement and wider public debate over religious exemptions to animal health law.

What distinguishes Skanda Vale from most other entries in this directory is its explicitly multi-faith character: alongside its Hindu shrines, the community also maintains shrines connected to Jesus Christ, the Buddha, and Sai Baba of Shirdi, framing itself as a non-denominational spiritual community rather than a temple of a single tradition. It nonetheless operates a working monastic order following largely Hindu ritual forms, runs an animal sanctuary and a hospice for palliative care (founded 1993), and today draws over 90,000 visitors a year to its Carmarthenshire hillside, making it one of the more visited Hindu-founded religious sites in Wales.
