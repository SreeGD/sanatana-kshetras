Vindhyavasini is worshipped on a hill above the Ganga at Vindhyachal as one of India's most important Shakti Peethas, and devotional literature ties her closely to the story of Krishna's birth. According to the Devi Bhagavata Purana, when Vasudeva secretly swapped the newborn Krishna for the daughter just born to Yashoda in order to protect him from Kansa, it was this girl whom Kansa seized in Mathura's prison and dashed against a stone — only for her to slip free and rise into the sky in her full form as the goddess, warning the tyrant that his true nemesis had already been carried to safety elsewhere. Tradition holds that she then settled on the Vindhya hills overlooking the Ganga, giving the site both its name and its standing as a place where the Devi is approached directly rather than through an avatar.

The temple sits within a wider local circuit: pilgrims traditionally also visit the Kali Khoh shrine a couple of kilometers away and the Ashtabhuja temple on a neighboring hill, treating the three as complementary aspects of one goddess. Vindhyachal draws especially large crowds during the Chaitra and Sharad Navratri festivals, and in recent years the precinct has been the focus of a large government-backed redevelopment effort, the "Vindhya Corridor," aimed at improving the ghats and approach roads around the temple.
