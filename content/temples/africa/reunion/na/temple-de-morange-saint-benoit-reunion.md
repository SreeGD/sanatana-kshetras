Construction of the Temple de Morange began in 1947, with its bell tower added in 1949, on the grounds of the former Morange sugar estate in Saint-Benoît, on Réunion's east coast. It was built by Léon Valliamé, who had acquired the Morange lands in the late 19th century, expressly "so that Indian labourers could practice their religion in a more appropriate setting" - a direct link to the temple-building tradition of Réunion's 19th- and 20th-century indentured Tamil Hindu community. It was registered as a French historic monument on 17 September 2010.

The temple honours two principal deities: Kali, the fierce form of the Devi, and Surya, the Hindu solar deity - a relatively unusual joint dedication. It also preserves several relics of the former Morange sugar processing facility on its grounds, including an antique sugar-cane weighing scale, physically linking the temple to the plantation labour history from which Réunion's Tamil Hindu community emerged.
