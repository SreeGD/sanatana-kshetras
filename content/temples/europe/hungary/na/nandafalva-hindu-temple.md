The Nandafalva Hindu Temple traces its origins to 1979, when a group of young Hungarians under the spiritual guidance of Swami B.A. Narayan founded a community in the Balástya countryside of Csongrád County, southeastern Hungary — predating by over a decade the country's much larger and better-known Krishna Valley (New Vraja-dhama) community in Somogyvámos, already documented elsewhere in this directory, and making it one of the earliest Hare Krishna-tradition communities established in communist-era Hungary. Swami B.A. Narayan led the community until his death in 1993, after which it accepted B.A. Paramadvaiti Maharaj as its spiritual guide.

The physical temple building itself did not open until 2000, following two decades of community life before a dedicated structure was completed. Its architecture — designed by Gyula Szigeti — is described in Hungarian-language sources as a deliberate fusion of Hungarian countryside style with Odishan (Orissan) temple-tower forms and ceramic roof tiles, distinguishing it from more purely South Asian-style temple architecture elsewhere in this directory. The temple sits on route 5422 between the villages of Balástya and Forráskút, roughly 18-19 kilometers from the Ópusztaszer National Heritage Park, a major Hungarian historical site. Beyond this founding and building history, further detail on the temple's current congregation and activities could not be confirmed in the sources available for this entry.
