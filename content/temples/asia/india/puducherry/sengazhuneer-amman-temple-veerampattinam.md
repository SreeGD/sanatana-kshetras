In the coastal village of Veerampattinam, a short distance from Puducherry town, the Sengazhuneer Amman Temple honors the goddess in a form said to have first appeared to a local fisherman, Veeraraghavan, after he drew a bleeding piece of wood from his net. Guided by a dream in which the goddess herself instructed him, he is said to have installed her image at a spot identified by the presence of a cobra — a founding account preserved as devotional legend rather than documented history. From that small beginning, tradition holds, the shrine grew over the centuries into its present, substantially larger temple, now estimated to be roughly a thousand years old.

The temple holds particular historical distinction as the first in the Puducherry region to possess its own temple car (ratham), a marker of its long-standing local importance. Its principal festival, held over six weeks beginning in the Tamil month of Aadi (July/August), culminates on its fifth Friday — a day so significant that the Puducherry government has declared it a public holiday — drawing an estimated 100,000 to 200,000 pilgrims from Puducherry and neighboring parts of Tamil Nadu. Additional annual observances include Masi Magam, Mattu Pongal, and Vijayadashami.
