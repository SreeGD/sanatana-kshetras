Naimisharanya ("the forest of Naimisha") is one of the most storied tirthas of Puranic literature: tradition holds that when the sage-king Naimisha's discus (or, in another telling, the wheel of Brahma) was rolled out to find the most sacred spot on earth, its rim (nemi) fell here, marking the place where 88,000 sages once gathered for a 1,000-year yagna. It is at this forest hermitage that the Puranas describe the suta Ugrashrava reciting the Bhagavata Purana and other scriptures to the assembled rishis, making Naimisharanya one of the foundational settings of Hindu sacred narrative, and it is counted among the eight self-manifest (svayambhu) Vishnu-kshetras.

Within this larger sacred forest-complex, the Sri Devaraja Perumal Temple is the Divya Desam counted among the 108, glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham by Thirumangai Alvar, whose ten pasurams (Periya Thirumozhi) praise the Lord here as Devaraja amid the tirthas of the Chakra Tirtha and the Gomti river. The temple, maintained today according to South Indian Vaishnava (Agama) ritual conventions rather than the North Indian norm typical of the region, includes separate shrines for the Thayar and for Thirumangai Alvar himself, reflecting its continued care by the Sri Vaishnava tradition far from its Tamil heartland.
