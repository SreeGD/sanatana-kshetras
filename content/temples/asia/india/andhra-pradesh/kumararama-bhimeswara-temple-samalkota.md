Kumararama, the Sri Chalukya Kumararama Bhimeswara Swamy Temple at Samalkota in Kakinada district, is one of the five Pancharama Kshetras and shares their common origin legend: the demon Tarakasura's invincible Atma-linga, a gift from Shiva, was shattered by Kartikeya using the agneyastra on Vishnu's counsel, its fragments consecrated at five separate sites. Local tradition credits Kartikeya (Kumara Swami) himself with installing the fragment that fell at Samalkota, giving the temple its name, Kumararama, "the abode of Kumara." The presiding deity, worshipped as Kumara Bhimeswara, takes the form of a striking 14-foot limestone linga, with Bala Tripura Sundari Ammavaru as the temple's consort goddess and Kala Bhairava as its traditionally paired guardian form.

The temple's documented history spans several centuries of continuous patronage even where its earliest founding date remains debated. Its construction is popularly attributed to the Eastern Chalukya king Chalukya Bhima I, though sources differ on the precision of that claim: one account gives a specific window of 892 to 922 CE for construction, while a broader cross-check places Bhima I's reign at roughly 888-918 CE and bases the attribution not on an inscription at Samalkota itself but on one found at nearby Pithapuram -- a pattern similar to the uncertainty already noted for the Bhimeswara temple at Draksharamam, whose Bhima I attribution likewise lacks a confirmed on-site foundation inscription. What is more firmly documented is later work at the site: a hundred-pillared mandapam, a single-stone Nandi, a large mandapam credited to Ganga Mahadevi of the Eastern Ganga dynasty, pillar refinishing carried out under the Kakatiyas (active roughly 1340-1466 CE), and a run of inscriptions recording gifts to the temple dated across 1147 to 1494 CE -- a period that postdates Bhima I's reign by well over two centuries. Recent excavations in the temple compound have also turned up carved figures estimated at about 1,000 years old. The temple is recognized today as a centrally protected monument of national importance and remains an active pilgrimage site, with Maha Shivaratri as its largest annual festival.
