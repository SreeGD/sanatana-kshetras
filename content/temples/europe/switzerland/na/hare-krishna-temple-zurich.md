According to Wikipedia's account of Hinduism in Switzerland, devotees of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON) first arrived in the country in 1972, and the movement's founder, A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, visited Switzerland multiple times over the following decade. That early missionary activity culminated in 1980 with the opening of a Hare Krishna temple in Zurich, which the same source describes as the first Hindu temple established in Switzerland — predating by more than a decade the wave of Sri Lankan Tamil Saiva temples that would later open in towns around Zurich, such as Adliswil and Glattbrugg, from the mid-1990s onward.

Beyond this founding date and its ISKCON (Gaudiya Vaishnava) affiliation, little further detail about the temple's exact location, founders, or subsequent history could be confirmed in the sources available for this entry. It is presented here as a historically significant but currently thinly documented entry, pending fuller sourcing.
