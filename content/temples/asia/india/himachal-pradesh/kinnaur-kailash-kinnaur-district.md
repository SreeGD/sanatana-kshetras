Kinnaur Kailash (also Kinner Kailash) is a Himalayan massif in Kinnaur district, Himachal Pradesh, whose summit reaches 6,055 m. Unlike most entries in this directory, there is no constructed temple here: the object of pilgrimage and worship is a natural rock pillar, described in sources as a Shivling, standing at roughly 4,800 m on the range — some distance below the true summit. Hindu tradition holds that Shiva and Parvati reside on the massif, and it is reckoned fourth among the five peaks of the Pancha Kailash group. The site carries dual significance in this borderland region: it is revered by both Hindu and Buddhist Kinnauris, reflecting Kinnaur's broader religious character as a meeting point of the two traditions.

The rock pillar is visible from villages in the Sutlej valley below, including Kalpa, where it is described as changing colour with the light through the day. Pilgrims can view and worship it from these vantage points, or undertake the Kinnaur Kailash Parikrama, a circumambulation trek around the massif, which the Kinnaur district administration organizes and registers each year; for the 2026 season, the district government's own public notice set the yatra for 17-23 August, starting from Purwani village, with same-day registration (capped at 50 pilgrims daily) followed by a vehicle transfer to Panosantang and a guided trek of some three to four hours to Danglidean, near the rock pillar. Since route, dates, and capacity are set administratively each year rather than fixed permanently, prospective pilgrims should confirm current-year details with the district administration rather than relying on any single year's notice.
