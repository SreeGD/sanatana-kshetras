Sydney's Durga Temple traces back to October 2002, when the Shri Durgadevi Devasthanam organization formed to build a dedicated home for Durga worship in the city. A foundation ceremony followed in August 2006 on the Regents Park site, and construction proceeded in stages over the following decade as the community raised funds, culminating in the temple's first Maha Kumbabhishekam consecration on 7 May 2017 — roughly fifteen years after the founding effort began.

Built in Dravidian architectural style, the temple centers its worship on Durga, joined by Lakshmi and Saraswati, with subsidiary shrines to Ganesha, Shiva, Krishna, Murugan, and other deities, reflecting the broad, multi-sectarian character typical of diaspora Hindu temples. Beyond daily worship, the complex houses an 800-seat event and wedding hall completed in 2010 — noted as the first such Hindu community hall built inside a temple complex in Australia — underscoring the temple's role as both a spiritual and cultural hub for Sydney's Hindu community.
