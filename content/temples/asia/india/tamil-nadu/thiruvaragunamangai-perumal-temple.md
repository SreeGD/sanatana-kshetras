Natham, known in the Divya Prabandham by its older name Thiruvaragunamangai, sits on the Thamirabarani river within the Nava Tirupathi cluster and is traditionally reckoned the second most important of the nine, associated with the Moon (Chandra) among the navagrahas honoured across the group. Here Vishnu is worshipped as Vijayasana Perumal, with his consort Lakshmi as Varagunavalli.

The temple's origin legend centres on the sage Vedavit, who, grieving after the death of his parents, undertook penance along the Thamirabarani. Vishnu is said to have appeared before him in the guise of a Brahmin and instructed him to continue his austerities specifically at Varagunamangai. After years of unbroken devotion, Vishnu manifested before the sage, who asked that the Lord remain permanently at that spot in the form of Vijayasana Perumal — a request the deity granted, giving the shrine its present form and name.

Nammalvar included Thiruvaragunamangai among the nine Nava Tirupathi shrines he glorified in his Tiruvaimozhi, and the temple's sthala purana is likewise recorded in the Navatirupati Mahatmiyam section of the Brahmanda Purana, alongside the other eight river-bank shrines. Its continuing importance in the Sri Vaishnava devotional tradition is reflected in later Tamil literature as well, including the eighteenth- to nineteenth-century devotional anthology 108 Tirupati Antati by Divya Kavi Pillai Perumal Aiyangar, which kept the Nava Tirupathi circuit alive as a distinct object of pilgrimage and poetic praise well after the Alvar era.
