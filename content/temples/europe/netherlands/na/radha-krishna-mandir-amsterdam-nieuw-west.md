The Radha Krishna Mandir in Amsterdam's Nieuw-West borough is one of the temples serving the Netherlands' large Hindustani (Indo-Surinamese) Hindu community, distinct from the already-documented ISKCON Amsterdam (Hare Krishna Temple) elsewhere in the city. Wikipedia's overview of Hinduism in the Netherlands notes that a 2003 study estimated the country's Indo-Surinamese Hindu population at around 130,000 people, served nationwide by an estimated fifty temples and 250 priests — a far larger and more dispersed network of community worship than the country's handful of internationally affiliated organizations such as ISKCON or BAPS might suggest on their own.

This mandir, dedicated to Radha Krishna, represents that wider Hindustani community temple tradition, which took root in the Netherlands following large-scale Surinamese migration after Suriname's 1975 independence from Dutch colonial rule. Beyond its name, district, and community context, further detail on the temple's founding date, address, and architecture could not be confirmed in the source available for this entry; fuller documentation would need to be confirmed directly with the temple or the wider Hindustani community organizations in Amsterdam.
