Thirumanimadam, marked by a Dravidian five-tiered rajagopuram unusual in scale for so small a village, enshrines Vishnu as Narayana Perumal with his consort Pundarikavalli. Like its ten companion shrines at Thirunangur, the temple's founding is traced to the moment when Shiva, driven to a furious dance of grief after the death of his consort Uma, scattered eleven fierce Rudra forms wherever his hair struck the earth, and Vishnu appeared in eleven answering forms to calm him - the shared legend that accounts for the unusual concentration of Divya Desams in this one small locality.

Three Alvars - Periyalvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, and Thirumangai Alvar - are credited in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham with hymns to this temple, cementing its place among the 108 Divya Desams. The temple takes part every year, alongside its ten Thirunangur neighbours, in the Thirumangai Alvar Mangalasasana Utsavam during the Tamil month of Thai, when the Garudasevai procession carries the festival images of all eleven temples together - one of the more elaborate collective observances anywhere among the Divya Desams, and the clearest annual expression of Thirunangur's identity as a single sacred landscape composed of eleven individually glorified shrines.
