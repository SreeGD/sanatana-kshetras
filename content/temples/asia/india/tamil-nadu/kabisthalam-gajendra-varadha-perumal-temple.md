The Gajendra Varadha Perumal Temple at Kabisthalam, between the Kaveri and Kollidam rivers near Papanasam, is dedicated to Vishnu in his reclining (Bhujanga Sayanam) form, worshipped here as Gajendra Varadha Perumal alongside his consort Ramamanivalli Thayar. The temple's Tamil name, Thirukkavithalam, appears in a solitary but affectionate hymn of Thirumalisai Alvar in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, in which the Alvar addresses the deity as "Kannan" (Krishna).

The temple is named for its localized retelling of the Gajendra Moksha episode familiar from the Bhagavata Purana. According to the sthala purana, a king named Indradyumna, cursed by a sage, was reborn as the elephant Gajendra, while a gandharva named Huhu, similarly cursed, became a crocodile. The crocodile seized the elephant's leg in the temple's sacred tank, and after a fierce struggle, Vishnu appeared to rescue both, restoring each to his original form. Local tradition adds that Hanuman also worshipped at this temple during his travels. The site is counted among a small group of "Pancha-Kannan" temples in which Krishna is given special devotional prominence alongside the main presiding deity, and tradition holds it to be the only one of the 108 Divya Desams where Vishnu is said to have granted darshan to two non-human devotees together.

Historical documentation specific to this temple is comparatively thin: while general Chola-era construction with later Vijayanagara and Nayak-period contributions is claimed, no site-specific inscription has been located in available sources to substantiate a precise date, in contrast to some neighboring Divya Desams with cited epigraphy. The temple's main living festival, the Gajendra Moksha Leela, is celebrated each year in the Tamil month of Aadi, along with a chariot festival in Vaikasi.
