On a wooded hill sixteen miles from Madurai, in a landscape old enough to be named in the Sangam-era Paripadal and the epic Silappadikaram, stands the shrine the Alvars called Thirumalirunsolai, "the sacred grove where Vishnu dwells." Here Vishnu is worshipped standing as Kallazhagar (also called Sundarabahu Perumal or Sundararajan), with his consort venerated as Thirumamagal, and the site is counted among the Divya Desams most densely sung by the Alvars: Andal, Periyalvar, Nammalvar, Thirumangai Alvar, and Bhoothathalvar together contributed roughly 124 verses to it, including Andal's own loving description of its hills and streams in the Nachiyar Thirumozhi.

Local tradition holds Kallazhagar to be the brother of the goddess Meenakshi, whose own temple stands at the heart of Madurai. Each year during the Chithirai festival, the deity is taken in procession from the hill down toward the city, traditionally said to attend his sister's wedding to Sundareswarar (Shiva); arriving after the ceremony has concluded, he is said to have offered the wedding gifts he carried to the divine couple at a mandapam in the middle of the Vaigai river before returning to the hill without entering Madurai itself. The episode, re-enacted every year as one of Tamil Nadu's largest public festivals, draws together the city's Vaishnava and Shaiva devotional streams in a single shared celebration.

The temple's present structure, with its five-tiered gopuram and images of Narasimha and a sixteen-armed Chakrattalvar, includes sculptural work attributed to the Vijayanagara period, layered atop a site of much older sanctity implied by its Sangam-literature associations. Together with the Aadi Brahmotsavam later in the year, the Chithirai procession remains the temple's principal living link between the Alvars' medieval hymns and present-day worship.
