Krishna Valley, known within its own community as New Vraja-dhama, is a Hare Krishna (ISKCON) ecovillage founded in 1993 in the village of Somogyvámos, in Somogy County in southwestern Hungary, after devotees purchased roughly 120 hectares of land at auction, breaking ground in February 1994. Its central temple was consecrated on 5 September 1996, and the settlement has grown substantially since, reaching nearly 300 hectares by the 2020s and encompassing not just the temple itself but residential and agricultural land, pasture, orchards, horticultural gardens, an apiary, a dairy farm, parks, forests, a botanical garden, and school and library buildings.

What distinguishes Krishna Valley within this directory is its status as a fully self-sufficient, largely off-grid intentional community: it generates power from solar and wind installations, draws water from its own wells, heats with wood, and treats wastewater through a root-zone system, combining traditional Gaudiya Vaishnava temple worship — centered on the Pancha Tattva (Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and his associates) and Radha Krishna, in the lineage of A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada — with a working model of sustainable agriculture. A 2021 academic study described it as "one of the most significant ecovillages in Hungary," home to roughly 130 residents and drawing an estimated 25,000 to 30,000 visitors annually, making it both a significant pilgrimage site and one of Hungary's most visited rural attractions.
