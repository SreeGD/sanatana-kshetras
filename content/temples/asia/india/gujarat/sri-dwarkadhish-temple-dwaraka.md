Thiru Dwarakai is the Divya Desam of Dwaraka, the city Krishna is said to have built on land reclaimed from the sea after leaving Mathura, and from where he ruled as king following his victory over Kamsa's ally Jarasandha. Dwaraka is counted among both the Char Dham and the sapta puri, and marine archaeological surveys off the Gujarat coast have long fed popular interest in the tradition that the original city was submerged by the ocean after Krishna's departure from the world — a theme distinct from, but often associated with, the Divya Desam's own legendary status as one of Krishna's principal earthly abodes alongside Mathura and Gokul.

Five Alvars are credited with a combined 13 pasurams glorifying this sthala in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, addressing the Lord here as Kalyana Narayanan, attended by Lakshmi/Rukmini and, in the temple's own tradition, by more than one consort reflecting Krishna's eight principal queens (Ashtabharya). The present Dwarkadhish Temple (also called Jagat Mandir), with its towering five-storeyed spire, is traditionally held to be around 2,200 years old at its core, though the visible structure has been rebuilt and expanded over centuries; like Badrinath, it is associated with a matha established by Adi Shankaracharya (the Sharada Peetham), linking this Vaishnava shrine into the broader network of Adi Shankaracharya's four cardinal monastic seats.
