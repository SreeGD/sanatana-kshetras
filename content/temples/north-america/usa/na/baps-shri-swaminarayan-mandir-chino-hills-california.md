Chino Hills' BAPS mandir traces back to a 1977 visit by Pramukh Swami Maharaj, whose encouragement grew into a small center inaugurated in Whittier in 1984. By 1996 the growing Southern California BAPS community proposed a larger, traditional stone mandir, and first approached the City of Chino Hills about a Peyton Drive site in 1998 — beginning a 14-year approval saga marked by local opposition, including a September 2004 city council meeting that drew 1,200-1,500 attendees over seven and a half hours and ended in a 4-1 vote denying a zoning code change. A groundbreaking ceremony followed in September 2005, and the city council unanimously approved spire heights of up to 78 feet in August 2011; the finished mandir opened to the public in December 2012.

Built from roughly 35,000 hand-carved pieces of Italian Carrara marble and Indian pink sandstone, assembled in traditional Shilpa Shastra style without structural steel, the 164,000-square-foot complex sits on a 20-acre campus with a 91-foot lotus-shaped pond. It is notable for incorporating 40 base-isolator units for seismic protection, described by sources as the first earthquake-proof stone mandir built anywhere in the world — engineering meant, along with the temple's traditional construction methods, to help the structure last many generations. The central shrine enshrines Bhagwan Swaminarayan alongside Radha-Krishna, Sita-Ram, and Shiva-Parvati, and the mandir now serves as a major hub for the Hindu community across the Los Angeles/Inland Empire region.
