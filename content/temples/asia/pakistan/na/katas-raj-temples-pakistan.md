A complex of ancient Hindu temples built around a sacred pond that tradition holds formed from Shiva's tears of grief after the death of his consort Sati, Katas Raj is associated in some traditions with episodes of the Mahabharata, including the exiled Pandavas' riddle contest with a Yaksha at the pond. Largely abandoned by its Hindu community after the 1947 Partition of India, when most Hindus left the region, Indian pilgrims' access was further restricted in 1956, 1960, and from 1965 to 1984. The site has undergone restoration since 2006 with support from the Pakistani government and Hindu community groups (including a further restoration project in 2017), and in 2018 Pakistan's Supreme Court ruled against nearby cement factories to protect the sacred pond from drying out. It now sees occasional organized Hindu pilgrimage (around 2,000 Pakistani Hindus attended Maha Shivaratri observances there as of 2010-2011) alongside its status as a heritage site.
