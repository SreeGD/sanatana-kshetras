A center of Gaudiya Vaishnava (Hare Krishna) worship serving South Africa's large Indian-origin community, itself descended largely from 19th-century indentured laborers (see the separate Sri Siva Soobramaniar Temple entry in this directory for that broader community history), the Sri Sri Radha Radhanath Temple is named as a landmark of Chatsworth in Wikipedia's own article on the suburb, which cites the temple's website (iskcondurban.net) as its source. ISKCON established a presence in South Africa in the 1970s-80s, and Durban's annual Ratha Yatra festival, organized by the local ISKCON community, has grown into one of the city's notable public events - ISKCON's own international news network (dandavats.com) has reported the festival distributing over 10,000 plates of prasadam (sanctified food) a day across a multi-day celebration in recent years, and continues to cover the temple's activities as an active, ongoing institution.

Beyond its name, general Chatsworth location, and confirmed ongoing activity, this temple's precise founding date, founders, and exact address were not located during this research pass; the temple's own website, which would likely hold that detail, was unreachable (returning server errors) at the time of verification.
