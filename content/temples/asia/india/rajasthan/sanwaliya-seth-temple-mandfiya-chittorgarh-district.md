In Mandfiya village, Bhadesor tehsil, Chittorgarh district, the Sanwaliya Seth Temple enshrines an image of Krishna venerated as "Sanwaliya Seth" -- literally "the dark-complexioned merchant" -- reflecting a distinctive local devotional idiom in which worshippers treat the deity as a business partner, dedicating a share of their earnings and success to him. Temple tradition holds that in 1840 a cowherd named Bholaram dreamed of idols buried near Bagund village; when three identical Krishna images were subsequently unearthed there, one was installed at Mandfiya, a second at nearby Bhadsoda, and the third returned to Bagund itself, all three sites lying within about five kilometers of one another.

The Mandfiya temple, regarded as the principal of the three and known as Sanwaliya Ji Dham, has grown into one of the region's most visited Vaishnava pilgrimage sites, drawing large numbers of devotees from across Rajasthan as well as neighboring Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat -- a standing local tradition ranks it second in regional importance only to the Shrinathji temple at Nathdwara. The site is reached by road, roughly 41 km from Chittorgarh's railway station and about 65 km from Udaipur's airport.
