Kapaleeshwarar Temple, in the heart of Chennai's Mylapore neighborhood, is one of the Paadal Petra Sthalams — Shiva temples specifically praised in the Tevaram hymns of the Nayanar saints, here including Sambandar and later Arunagirinathar. Those hymns describe an original temple believed to have stood along the seashore, attributed to the ruling Pallavas in the 7th century CE. That coastal temple no longer stands: the present structure, housing Shiva as Kapaleeshwarar alongside his consort Parvati as Karpagambal (goddess of the wish-yielding tree), was relocated in the 17th century, incorporating inscriptions inside the temple dated to the 12th century. Popular telling connects this relocation to Portuguese destruction of the original seaside shrine — plausible given documented Portuguese colonial presence in Mylapore (as "São Tomé de Meliapor") from 1523, but this specific causal claim was not independently confirmed in the source checked for this entry, so it is noted as tradition rather than settled history.

The temple's 120-foot gopuram, adorned with stucco figures in the characteristic Dravidian style overpowering the street below, was built in 1906. It is administered today by the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Endowment Board, and its principal annual event is the Panguni Peruvizha (Panguni Uthiram) spring festival, centered on the Arupathimoovar procession honoring the 63 Nayanar saints.
