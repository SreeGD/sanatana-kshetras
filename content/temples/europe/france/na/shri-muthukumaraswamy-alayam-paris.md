The Shri Muthukumaraswamy Alayam, on Rue des Solitaires in Paris's 19th arrondissement, is dedicated to Murugan under his name Muthukumaraswamy ("pearl-like youthful lord"), alongside Ganesh and Amman, one of several forms of the deity venerated across the network of Tamil temples that has grown up in Paris and its northern suburbs since Sri Lankan Tamil refugees began settling in the region from the 1980s onward. It joins the already-documented Sri Manicka Vinayakar Alayam and Sri Ayyappan Temple, both also in Paris proper, as part of a cluster of Tamil Shaiva shrines serving the city's South Indian and Sri Lankan Tamil Hindu community.

A detailed, though unofficial, local-history account credits the temple's founding to 1994, by Balakrishnasarma, a fourth-generation Tamil Brahmin priest from a temple in Jaffna, Sri Lanka, who fled to France in 1991 and established the alayam on a site that had previously housed a travel agency. After his death, his son Sathveegan succeeded him as a fifth-generation priest, continuing the temple's Tamil-language, music, and dance classes and its role hosting community life-cycle ceremonies. Beyond this account, no official registry record or architectural description has been confirmed for this entry; fuller documentation would need to come directly from the temple or its community.
