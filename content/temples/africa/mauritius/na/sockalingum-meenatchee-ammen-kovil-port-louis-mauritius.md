Standing on Nicolay Road in Port Louis and locally nicknamed the "Kaylasson temple" (evoking Mount Kailash, Shiva's mythic abode), the Sockalingum Meenatchee Ammen Kovil is known chiefly through its connection to the same circle of Tamil artisans, working under the supervision of Mr Goinsamy Maestry, who built the well-documented Maheswarnath Mandir at Triolet in 1888. That shared craftsmanship places the Port Louis temple within the same late-19th-century wave of temple construction by Mauritius's indentured and free Tamil community, though no source located during this pass records this specific temple's own founding year independently of that connection.

The temple's name pairs Sockalingum, a Tamil name for Shiva, with Meenatchee (Meenakshi) Amman, a form of Parvati, echoing the divine-couple dedication found at long-established South Indian temples such as the Meenakshi Amman Temple of Madurai. As with many of Mauritius's Tamil kovils built in this period, it serves the island's South Indian Hindu community in the capital, Port Louis, though this entry's history should be read as provisional pending a source that documents the temple in its own right rather than only as an aside in another temple's history.
