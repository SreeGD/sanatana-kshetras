At the start of Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana's forest exile, the Ramayana places them for a single night at the capital of Guha, king of the Nishads (a fisherfolk/boatman community), before he ferried them across the Ganga to continue their journey. The site identified with that episode is Shringverpur (also spelled Singraur), a village on the Ganga roughly 45 km from Prayagraj, in what is today Prayagraj district.

Archaeological excavation at Shringverpur, carried out by archaeologist B.B. Lal between 1977 and 1986, has uncovered ruined walls, structural remains, and a large ancient hydraulic system, along with a temple attributed to Shringi Rishi — a sage from whom the village's name may derive. A small shrine at the site marks the spot where Nishadraj (Guha) is traditionally said to have worshipped a Shiva lingam, and the modern pilgrimage complex includes a statue of Nishadraj and a memorial garden (Smriti Vatika) dedicated to Shringi Rishi, alongside an active cremation ground on the riverbank. In 2019 the Uttar Pradesh government formally elevated the site's administrative status by creating a new block named Shringverpur Dham. No source consulted for this entry could confirm when the present shrine structures themselves were built, so this entry treats their age as unverified even as the site's identification with the Ramayana crossing and its archaeological remains are well attested.
