Thiruvali and Thirunagari are twin villages near Nangur, close to Sirkazhi, that together form a single Divya Desam centred on the man-lion form of Vishnu. At Thiruvali, the presiding deity is Lakshmi Narasimha, worshipped with his consort Amirthagatavalli; at neighbouring Thirunagari, the principal deity is Vedarajan, with the goddess Amirthavalli. Local tradition explains the name Thiruvali itself through the story of an aalinganam - an embrace - given by Narasimha to a devotee at this spot, from which the place is said to take its name.

The temples together form part of a wider Pancha Narasimha Kshetram, a set of five Narasimha shrines clustered in and around Thiruvali and Thirunagari, reflecting a regional concentration of man-lion worship distinct from the largely reclining- or standing-Vishnu iconography found at most of the neighbouring Chola Nadu Divya Desams. Kulasekara Alvar and Thirumangai Alvar are both credited with hymns to this shrine in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. As with many temples of this stretch of the delta, the surviving structure is attributed to Chola-period building activity with later additions credited to the Vijayanagara and Thanjavur Nayak periods, though a fully independent inscriptional history was not available in the sources consulted for this entry.
