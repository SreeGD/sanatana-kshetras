Sanatan Mandir Sabha (SMS) holds the distinction of being Norway's first registered Hindu religious community, first organizing in 1986 and formally registering with Norway's Brønnøysund Register Centre on 31 March 1988, years before most of the country's other Hindu institutions. It is based at Nyveien 6 in Slemmestad, a small town on the Oslofjord in Asker municipality southwest of Oslo, and reports a membership of roughly 900. Norway's Hindu population is estimated to be roughly half Sri Lankan Tamil in origin, with the remainder drawn from India and elsewhere in South Asia, and SMS's own materials do not appear to restrict membership along those lines, suggesting it functions as a broader "Sanatan Dharma" community body rather than a single-tradition or single-ethnicity temple.

Beyond regular darshan, held mornings and evenings, the temple offers a range of life-cycle and community services: birthday puja (Janamdin Sanskar), weddings, housewarmings, last rites, and Satya Narayan Katha-Pujan, along with a paid matrimonial/matchmaking registry and Scandinavia-wide mundan (first-haircut) ceremonies. Norway is also home to several other named Hindu temples — including the Sivasubramanayar Alayam (Norwegian Hindu Centre) in Ammerud, Oslo, and the Sri Tiller Ganesha Temple in Trondheim, both already documented elsewhere in this directory, plus smaller centers in Bergen and Ålesund — making SMS one of at least four organized Hindu temple communities in the country as of the mid-2010s, and the earliest of them to be formally registered.
