Cardiff's Shri Swaminarayan Mandir traces back to 1979, when the local Gujarati Hindu community purchased a former synagogue in the city, opening it as a temple in 1982. As the congregation grew, it relocated in 1993 to its current site in Grangetown, just south of Cardiff city centre. A major £700,000 refurbishment between 2005 and 2007 — part-funded by a £110,000 grant from the Welsh Assembly Government — transformed the building's exterior into the style of a traditional Hindu temple, adding three white stone spires that were, at the time, the first on any Hindu temple in Wales, along with new marble deity images, classrooms, and living quarters. The temple, dedicated to Swaminarayan in his form as Ghanshyam, belongs to the NarNarayan Dev Gadi branch of the Swaminarayan Sampraday, a Vaishnava tradition.

Described as the first and largest Hindu temple in Wales, the mandir has repeatedly played a civic as well as religious role: in 2001 its devotees raised £30,000 in just three days for earthquake relief in Gujarat, and its 2007 silver jubilee was marked by a 1.6-mile procession of roughly 3,000 participants through Cardiff's civic centre. Welsh First Minister Rhodri Morgan attended the temple's Diwali celebrations in 2008, underscoring its standing as a recognized civic landmark of Welsh Hindu life alongside Skanda Vale and the Tividale Balaji Temple already documented elsewhere in this directory.
