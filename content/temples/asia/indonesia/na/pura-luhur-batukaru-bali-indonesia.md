Pura Luhur Batukaru sits on the forested southern slope of Mount Batukaru, Bali's second-highest volcanic peak, in Tabanan Regency. Originally built in the 11th century to honor the ancestors of Tabanan's ruling dynasty, the temple was destroyed in 1604 and lay in ruins for centuries before being restored in 1959. Its principal shrine is a seven-tiered meru tower dedicated to Mahadewa, worshipped here as the guardian deity of the mountain, a form of Shiva, and the temple is counted among the Sad Kahyangan Jagad, Bali's six holiest directional sanctuaries, representing the western direction in the island's sacred geography.

Much of the temple's grounds remain restricted to visitors during active ceremonies, reflecting its standing as one of the most sacred and closely guarded sites for Bali's Hindu population, distinct from the more tourist-oriented temples elsewhere on the island. It also serves as the traditional starting point for pilgrimages climbing Mount Batukaru itself, a practice that can draw large numbers of worshippers during major ceremonial periods.
