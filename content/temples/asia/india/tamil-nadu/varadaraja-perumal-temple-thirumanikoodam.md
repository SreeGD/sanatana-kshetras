One of the eleven Vishnu shrines gathered at Thirunangur, this temple enshrines Varadaraja Perumal - known here in a form called Senkanmal - together with his consorts Sridevi, worshipped as Thirumaamangai Nachiyar, and Bhudevi. Local legend attributes the digging of the temple's sacred tank to Arjuna of the Mahabharata, and holds that Krishna appeared here in his role as Parthasarathy, charioteer and teacher, to instruct the Pandava prince - a legend echoed at more than one shrine in this immediate cluster, reflecting the tightly interwoven mythology of the Thirunangur villages.

As with its neighbouring Thirunangur temples, this shrine's place among the Divya Desams rests on hymns composed here by three Alvars - Periyalvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, and Thirumangai Alvar - preserved in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The temple takes its turn each year in the Thirumangai Alvar Mangalasasana Utsavam, held in the Tamil month of Thai, when the festival deity joins those of the other ten Thirunangur shrines in the Garudasevai, a procession of the eleven Perumals mounted together on a specially constructed Garuda vahanam - one of the more visually striking collective festivals among the 108 Divya Desams, and the clearest expression of Thirunangur's identity as a single devotional unit made up of eleven distinct temples.
