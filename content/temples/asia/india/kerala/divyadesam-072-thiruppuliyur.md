Lying within the cluster of sacred Vaishnava abodes along the Pamba river in Kerala, Thiruppuliyur is, by tradition, counted among those shrines associated with the worship of the Pandava brothers during their forest exile, who are held to have installed and adored the Lord at these places. The name Mayappiran evokes the divine mystery and beguiling grace of Vishnu, the supreme "Maya" through whom the worlds arise and are upheld, here made present in worshippable form for the sake of His devotees. The sanctity of this Divya Desam was sung by Nammalwar, foremost of the Alwars, in his Tiruvaaymozhi, and by Thirumangai Alwar, the prince among the poet-saints, whose praises together secure the temple's place within the canonical corpus of the Tamil Prabandham. The benediction of these two Alwars confers upon Thiruppuliyur an exalted standing in the Sri Vaishnava tradition, drawing pilgrims to the darshan of Mayappiran. As with the other Malai Nadu Divya Desams, the temple preserves the architectural and ritual character particular to Kerala's Vaishnava heritage, while its place among the 108 abodes unites it with the wider devotional world cherished by the Alwars and the Sri Vaishnava sampradaya.
