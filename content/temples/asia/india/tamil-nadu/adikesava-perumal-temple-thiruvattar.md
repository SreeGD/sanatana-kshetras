## Origin and legend

The Adikesava Perumal Temple at Thiruvattar enshrines Vishnu as Adikesava Perumal, reclining on Adisesha, with consort Maragathavalli Thayar (Lakshmi). Temple tradition holds that the shrine was consecrated by Parashurama, the sixth avatar of Vishnu credited in Kerala/Travancore legend with reclaiming the land of the Malabar coast from the sea and establishing many of its earliest temples. The temple is also said to be referenced in the Padma Purana, and stone inscriptions at the site, dated to Kollam Era 779 (corresponding to the 17th century CE in the modern calendar used at the temple, though tradition places the deity's establishment far earlier, in the Treta Yuga), attest to its antiquity and continuous worship.

## Historical significance

Architecturally, the temple is a striking fusion of Dravidian and Kerala temple-building traditions: an outer corridor lined with 224 carved stone pillars, a roughly 30-foot fortified wall enclosing the complex, and Virabhadra-themed composite pillars added by Vijayanagara-era patrons in the early 16th century. The temple holds particular significance for the Travancore royal house: on 10 June 1741, Maharajah Marthanda Varma is recorded to have made offerings here -- 908 panams, a yataghan (sword), and silk cloth -- seeking the deity's blessing before the decisive Battle of Colachel against the Dutch, a battle Travancore won.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thiruvattar is one of the thirteen Divya Desams of the Malai Nadu (Chera country/hill country) group glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham. Nammalvar sang eleven verses in praise of Adikesava Perumal in the sixth decad of his Thiruvaimozhi, securing the temple's place in the Sri Vaishnava devotional canon. Though grouped by tradition with the Kerala Divya Desams because it lay within the historical Travancore/Chera cultural sphere, Thiruvattar's present-day location is in Kanyakumari district of Tamil Nadu, following the 1956 reorganisation of states along linguistic lines that transferred the erstwhile Travancore taluks of Thovala, Agastheeswaram, Kalkulam and Vilavancode (including Thiruvattar) from Travancore-Cochin (Kerala) to Madras State (Tamil Nadu).
