The Khlong Khuean Ganesh International Park, in Chachoengsao Province east of Bangkok, is home to one of the tallest Hindu deity statues anywhere in the world: a bronze Ganesha figure standing approximately 39 meters (128 feet) tall, completed in 2012 and described in available sourcing as the largest bronze Ganesh statue on Earth, ranking among the ten tallest Hindu deity statues globally by height.

As a large-scale, purpose-built devotional park centered on a single monumental Ganesha image, the site reflects the continuing popularity of Ganesha worship in Thailand, where the god is venerated broadly across Thai Buddhist as well as Hindu communities as a remover of obstacles and patron of the arts and crafts. Detailed institutional history — including the statue's builders and any formal consecration — could not be confirmed from the sources checked in this research pass, but its scale and documented height make it a significant, distinctive rare-deity landmark in Thailand's wider Hindu religious landscape, notably outside Bangkok.
