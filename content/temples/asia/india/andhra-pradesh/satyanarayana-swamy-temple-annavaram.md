Annavaram's temple is unusual among Andhra Pradesh's major pilgrimage sites for being comparatively young: rather than tracing to a medieval dynasty, the shrine on Ratnagiri hill was first built in 1891 and rebuilt in more durable stone between 1933 and 1934, making it largely a product of the last century and a half of devotional patronage rather than royal endowment. What it lacks in antiquity it makes up for in popularity — it is often described as second only to Tirumala among Andhra's pilgrimage destinations, particularly because of the Satyanarayana Vratam, a votive ritual widely performed across Hindu households, which pilgrims travel here to perform at its point of origin.

The temple's sanctity is explained through a puranic legend rather than a historical founding story: Mount Meru and his consort Menaka are said to have undertaken penance and won Vishnu's favor, after which their sons took the form of two hills — one becoming Bhadrachalam, associated with Rama, and the other becoming Ratnagiri, where Vishnu is said to have manifested as Satyanarayana Swamy. The temple sits a few kilometers from its own dedicated railway halt on the Howrah-Chennai coastal line, and draws its heaviest crowds during the Karthika month, when large numbers of devotees perform the Satyanarayana Vratam collectively at the hilltop shrine.
