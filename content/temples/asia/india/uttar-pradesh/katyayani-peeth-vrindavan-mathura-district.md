---

Vrindavan's association with the goddess Katyayani runs deeper than the Shakti Peetha tradition alone: the Bhagavata Purana's tenth canto describes the unmarried gopis of Vraj undertaking the Katyayani Vrata, a month-long vow in which they bathed daily in the Yamuna and worshipped a clay image of Katyayani on its bank, praying for Krishna as a husband — the episode that precedes the well-known story of Krishna hiding the gopis' garments. This scriptural episode gives the region a genuine, old textual connection to the goddess quite apart from her later identification as one of the 51 Shakti Peethas, where Vrindavan is named as the spot where the ringlets of Sati's hair fell, with the local Bhairava known as Bhuteshwar.

Today a temple called Katyayani Peeth operates in Vrindavan under a religious trust that also maintains a second ashram near Haridwar; the trust traces its own founding to Swami Keshavanand, who settled near Haridwar around 1903, but the sources checked do not give a clear, separate founding history for the Vrindavan site, whose principal on-site shrine appears to be dedicated to Siddh Ganesh rather than explicitly to Katyayani. Regional tradition also links the Shakti Peetha to the Bhuteshwar Mahadev Temple in nearby Mathura, a real and long-established shrine on the Mathura parikrama route close to the Krishna Janmabhoomi complex — but no source checked directly confirms that this is the same site the Shakti Peetha tradition intends, or gives it a documented founding date. Given these gaps, this entry should be read as recording the Katyayani Peeth tradition of the Vrindavan/Mathura area broadly, rather than certifying a single, precisely identified and dated temple structure.
