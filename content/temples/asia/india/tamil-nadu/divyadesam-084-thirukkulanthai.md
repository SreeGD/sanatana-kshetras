The temple at Thirukkulanthai, also known as Perungulam, in the Pandya region of the Tamil land, is one of the 108 Divya Desams hallowed by the song of the Alwars. The presiding deity is worshipped as Soranathan, also revered under the gracious name Maayakoothan, 'the divine dancer of wondrous play,' a title that celebrates the Lord as the master of mysterious and marvellous deeds, whose sport sustains and enchants the worlds. The Moolavar here is venerated in this temple set among the shrines of the Pandya country, where the Lord stands to grant his grace to those who seek him. Like the neighbouring abodes of this region, Thirukkulanthai belongs to the group of Divya Desams cherished near Thirukkurugur, the birthplace of Nammalwar, and it shares in the saint's deep and abiding devotion. It is Nammalwar, the foremost of the Alwars and the composer of the Tiruvaymoli, who sang the praise of this place in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and through his verses Thirukkulanthai is secured among the sacred abodes of that four-thousand-verse corpus. Tradition holds the name Maayakoothan to express the wonder of the Lord's hidden play, by which he conceals his infinite majesty within an accessible and beautiful form for the sake of his devotees. Worshippers revere Soranathan as the Lord who, through his marvellous grace, draws souls toward refuge at his feet. The temple thus endures as a sanctified abode within the devotional landscape that the Alwars consecrated, its glory affirmed by Nammalwar's hymns and its sanctity preserved across the generations of pilgrims who come before the Lord.

## Festivals

At Thirukkulanthai (Perungulam), a shrine of the Nava Tirupati cluster, the temple traditionally joins in the collective Nava Tirupati observances that honour the nine Vishnu temples along the Tamiraparani. The annual Brahmotsavam is celebrated with festal processions, decorated vahanas, and the beloved Garuda Sevai. Vaikunta Ekadasi is observed with special darshan and the chanting of Nammalvar's Tiruvaymozhi, the alvar whose verses consecrate these temples. Pavithrotsavam for ritual sanctification and a Theppotsavam (float festival) upon the temple tank are also traditionally held. The grand Tiruvadhyayana Utsavam, with its Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitations during the Margazhi season, together with Pongal and Tamil New Year, completes the devotional calendar of this Pandya Nadu shrine.
