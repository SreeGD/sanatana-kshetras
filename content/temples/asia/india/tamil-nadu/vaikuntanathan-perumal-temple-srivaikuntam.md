Srivaikuntam, on the northern bank of the Thamirabarani river in Thoothukudi district, is regarded as the principal shrine among the nine Nava Tirupathi, the Sun (Surya) sthalam in the set of nine temples each linked to one of the navagrahas. Vishnu is worshipped here as Vaikuntanathan — also known as Kallapiran and Pal Pandian — with his consort Lakshmi as Vaikunthavalli.

The temple's sthala purana recounts that the asura Somuka defeated Brahma and stole the four Vedas, plunging creation into disorder. Brahma undertook penance on the banks of the Thamirabarani at this spot, and Vishnu responded by descending in his Matsya (fish) avatar to slay Somuka and recover the Vedas for Brahma, subsequently choosing to remain here as Vaikuntanathan. The story situates the temple within the wider Dashavatara framework even though it is not one of the temples dedicated to Matsya as a primary form.

Nammalvar glorified the shrine in his Tiruvaimozhi as part of his celebrated sequence on the nine Nava Tirupathi temples, and Srivaikuntam's standing as the foremost of the nine is reflected in its comparatively richer epigraphic record: at least six inscriptions document royal patronage by the Pandya dynasty, including a land grant by Jatavarman Kulasekaran I in the early thirteenth century and endowments by Maravarman Sundara Pandyan for perpetual temple lamps and for the installation of the Vaikunthavalli shrine. Local tradition also holds that the temple's fortified precincts were pressed into service during Veerapandiya Kattabomman's uprising against the British East India Company in 1801, underscoring the site's continued significance well beyond its Alvar-era origins.
