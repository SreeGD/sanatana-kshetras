Santiago, Chile's capital, is home to the country's largest Hindu community — an estimated 2,372 of Chile's roughly 4,530 Hindus as of a 2024 count, the great majority of Sindhi descent. Migration of Indian-origin families to Chile began in small numbers in the 1920s and grew substantially from the 1970s onward, drawing arrivals not only from India but from other parts of the Sindhi diaspora as far afield as Trinidad and Tobago, Guyana, Suriname, Hong Kong, and the Philippines.

A Hindu Temple of Santiago serves this community, led by a Brahmin priest named Mahraj Ravi Kewlani. Its existence and active status come to light through an unrelated source: the Wikipedia biography of Paula Narváez, a prominent Chilean politician and diplomat (Permanent Representative of Chile to the United Nations, former Minister Secretary General of Government), which notes she regularly worships there. No dedicated source describing the temple's founding, architecture, or presiding deity could be located this pass; those details should be treated as unconfirmed pending further documentation.
