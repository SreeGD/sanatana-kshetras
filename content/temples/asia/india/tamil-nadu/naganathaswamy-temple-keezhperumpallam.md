Naganathaswamy Temple (Nagannathaswamy Temple, also called Kethu Sthalam), in the small coastal-belt village of Keelaperumpallam (Keezhperumpallam) in Mayiladuthurai district, is the Navagraha shrine associated with Ketu, completing Tamil Nadu's nine-temple planetary circuit alongside the shrines for the Sun, Moon, and the other five visible grahas plus Rahu. As at several of the other Navagraha sites, Shiva is worshipped here as the presiding deity, Naganatha Swamy, with his consort worshipped as Sowndara Nayagi (Parvathi), and Ketu honoured in a subsidiary shrine that draws devotees seeking relief from the astrological effects attributed to this shadow planet, traditionally linked in Hindu astrology to detachment, upheaval, and spiritual transformation. Tradition credits the original temple complex to the Cholas, with the present masonry structure rebuilt under the Nayak rulers in the 16th century; it is built in Dravidian style with a flat rajagopuram and a single prakaram (enclosure). The temple follows six daily rituals from about 5:30 AM to 10:00 PM and is administered by the Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department, Government of Tamil Nadu.

## Festivals

The temple's major festivals include Mahasivarathri, Margazhi Thiruvadirai, Panguni Uthiram, and the Vasuki Utsavam.

## Location & Access

Keelaperumpallam village lies about 2 km from Poompuhar and 93 km from Thanjavur, in Mayiladuthurai district. The village is reachable by road, with Mayiladuthurai and Sirkazhi the nearest railheads. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
