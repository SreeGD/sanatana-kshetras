The Oppiliappan Temple at Thirunageswaram — not to be confused with the separate Shiva Navagraha temple of the same village — enshrines Vishnu as Oppiliappan, "the lord who has no equal" (also rendered Thannoppaarillappan), with his consort worshipped as Bhoomidevi, the earth-goddess, under the name Bhoodevi Nachiar. The temple's most distinctive living tradition is its cuisine: in keeping with its founding legend, food offered to the deity here is prepared entirely without salt, a practice still followed today and reflected in the deity's very name (uppu illa appan, "the lord without salt").

The temple's central legend, drawn from the Brahmanda Purana as preserved in local tradition, tells of the sage Markandeya's thousand-year penance to be blessed with a daughter; Lakshmi herself appeared as an infant beneath a tulasi plant and was raised by the sage. When she came of age, Vishnu approached in the disguise of an old man seeking her hand, testing the sage's household before revealing his true form and marrying her as Bhoodevi. The temple's sacred tank, the Ahoratra Pushkarani, carries its own subsidiary legend of a cursed king transformed into a bird and restored to human form by its waters.

On firmer historical ground, two Chola-period inscriptions on the central shrine's walls are attributed to the emperors Rajendra Chola I and Rajaraja I, recording donations and land grants respectively — though Wikipedia itself cautions that the temple's precise founding history cannot be established from these inscriptions alone. Later structural additions are credited to Govinda Dikshitar, a minister under the Thanjavur Nayak rulers Achuthappa Nayak and Raghunatha Nayak in the late 16th and early 17th centuries. The temple is glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by Periyalvar, Thirumangai Alvar, and Nammalvar, and its Kalyana Utsavam each Aippasi ceremonially commemorates the divine wedding of Vishnu and Bhoodevi at the heart of its founding story.
