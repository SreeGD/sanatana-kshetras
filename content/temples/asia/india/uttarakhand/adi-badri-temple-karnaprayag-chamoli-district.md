Adi Badri is a cluster of ancient stone shrines set in the hills beyond Karnaprayag, geographically and historically distinct from the high-altitude Panch Badri and Panch Kedar shrines further up the Alaknanda valley. The complex is described as a group of small temples — seven of which are said to have been built during the late Gupta period, roughly the 5th to 8th centuries CE — packed into a compact site only about 14 by 30 meters, with individual shrines ranging from 2 to 6 meters in height. The principal shrine is dedicated to Vishnu and houses a black-stone image, roughly a meter tall, depicting the deity holding a mace, lotus, and discus.

Local tradition holds that Adi Badri served pilgrims as an accessible stand-in when the much more remote Badrinath temple, high in the mountains, could not be reached, and popularly credits Adi Shankara with having built or sanctioned the shrines here as part of a wider effort to plant Vaishnava worship sites across the Himalayan region — an attribution that, as with the other Panch Badri shrines, rests on tradition rather than independent documentation. Unlike its high-altitude counterparts, Adi Badri's low elevation and proximity to the Karnaprayag-Ranikhet road (near the Chandpur Garhi fort) make it accessible for most of the year rather than only during the short Himalayan pilgrimage season.
