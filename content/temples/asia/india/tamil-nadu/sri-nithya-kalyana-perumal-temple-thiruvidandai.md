Thiruvidandai, one of the Nadu Nadu (Thondai Nadu) Divya Desams, sits on the East Coast Road between Chennai and Mamallapuram. The presiding deity is enshrined as Nithya Kalyana Perumal, a form of Vishnu also identified with Lakshmi Varaha Perumal, with his consort Lakshmi worshipped as Komalavalli Thayar.

The temple's core structure is traditionally dated to the Pallava period (with the existing structure often associated with the 7th century), and the site received later attention under the Cholas, in keeping with the general pattern of temple patronage across Thondai Nadu. It is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham through hymns of Thirumangai Alvar, one of the twelve Alvar saints of the Sri Vaishnava tradition, securing its place among the 108 Divya Desams.

The temple's name and its principal legend are closely linked: according to local tradition, the sage Kalava had 360 daughters, and Vishnu appeared before them in the form of a young ascetic, marrying a different daughter on each of 360 successive days before revealing his true form as Varaha and uniting all of them as a single embodiment of his consort. Because this cycle of marriage is imagined as continuous and ever-renewing, the deity is known as "Nithya Kalyana Perumal" — the Lord who is perpetually in a state of marriage — and the temple is a favored destination for couples and families seeking blessings for marriage and marital harmony.
