Where the Vasishta Godavari, one of the Godavari's principal delta distributaries, meets the Bay of Bengal on the Andhra coast stands the village of Antarvedi -- a name that describes its own geography, the land held between river and sea. The village takes its religious identity, and per Wikipedia's account its alternate name "Narasimhakshetra," from the Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy Temple at its heart, dedicated to Vishnu's man-lion incarnation alongside Lakshmi. Stone inscriptions record that the present temple structure was completed in 1823 CE (Shalivahana Saka year 1745), its construction credited to Koppanathi Krishnamma, remembered in the temple's own tradition as a ruler of Pallava lineage and a devotee who endowed the shrine with substantial land, gold, and gems.

Reaching the temple today still involves crossing water: a launch carries pilgrims from the mainland over the Vasishta Godavari to a small island near the village, from which the point where the river gives way to the open sea can be viewed directly -- a vantage that makes Antarvedi one of the Godavari system's notable mouth-of-the-river pilgrimage sites, distinct from the river's more inland shrines such as Bhadrachalam and Draksharamam already documented elsewhere in this directory, and from its Maharashtra source at Trimbakeshwar.
