Phnom Bakheng, completed between 889 and 910 CE under King Yasovarman I, was the central temple-mountain of Yasodharapura, the new Khmer capital Yasovarman established atop and around this roughly 79-meter hill — predating the far more famous Angkor Wat, already documented separately in this directory, by more than two centuries. Dedicated to Shiva, its stepped pyramid of seven levels was designed to represent Mount Meru, the mythical home of the Hindu gods, with each level symbolizing one of the seven heavens of Hindu-Buddhist cosmology.

Today Phnom Bakheng functions primarily as a heritage and archaeological site rather than an active place of worship, and has become one of the Angkor complex's most visited spots, particularly at sunset, when its hilltop position offers sweeping views across the wider Angkor area. This popularity has placed significant strain on the ancient structure, and the World Monuments Fund has worked in partnership with Cambodia's APSARA conservation authority since 2004 to protect it from the effects of heavy foot traffic.
