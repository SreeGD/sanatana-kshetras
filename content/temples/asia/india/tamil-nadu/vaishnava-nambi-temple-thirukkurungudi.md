Thirukkurungudi, tucked at the foothills of the Western Ghats in Nanguneri taluk of Tirunelveli district, is one of the most distinctive of the Pandya Nadu Divya Desams both for the richness of its Alvar associations and for the singular way it presents its presiding deity. Four Alvars - Thirumalisai Alvar, Nammalvar, Periyalvar, and Thirumangai Alvar - each composed hymns in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham glorifying the Lord here, a concentration of Alvar attention matched by few other Divya Desams outside the very largest shrines.

The temple's central icon is worshipped as Vaishnava Nambi, a name tradition explains through a striking legend: the Lord of Thirukkurungudi is said to have taken on the role of a disciple and received Vaishnava initiation from Ramanuja himself, and has been addressed as "Vaishnava Nambi" - the Vaishnava lord - ever since. Uniquely among Divya Desams, the deity here is presented in three separate forms within a single three-tiered (Ashtanga Vimana) sanctum: standing as the principal ten-foot moolavar with consorts Sridevi and Bhudevi in the main sanctum; seated as Iruntha Nambi in the surrounding prakaram; and reclining upon the serpent Adishesha as Kidantha Nambi, all three images facing east. His consort in the main shrine is venerated as Thirukurungudivalli Nachiar. Local tradition holds the site to be some 2,300 years old, and surviving temple inscriptions from the Chola period (10th century CE) attest to substantial patronage well before further additions were made under the Vijayanagara empire. Though its proximity to the Kerala border places it near the boundary with the separate Malai Nadu group of thirteen Divya Desams, Thirukkurungudi is consistently reckoned - by dedicated Sri Vaishnava pilgrimage sources and by its administrative location within Tirunelveli district - among the eighteen Divya Desams of Pandya Nadu.
