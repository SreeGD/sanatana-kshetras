Devaprayag stands at the sacred confluence (prayag) of the Bhagirathi and Alaknanda rivers, the point traditionally regarded as the true beginning of the river Ganga; the town is named for the sage Deva Sharma, said to have performed penance here. As the Divya Desam Thiru Kandamennum Kadinagar, it is celebrated in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham for the purificatory power of the Ganges at this spot, with pasurams describing pilgrims standing with joined hands on its banks to have their sins carried away — themes consistent with the wider Vada Nadu emphasis on riverine tirthas.

The temple here, popularly known today as the Raghunath Mandir, is one of the oldest stone temples in the Garhwal Himalaya and is built in a fortress-like grey stone style unusual among Vaishnava shrines, thought to help it withstand the region's earthquakes; local tradition attributes its founding, like several other Garhwal shrines, to the era of Adi Shankaracharya. It remains an active pilgrimage stop for travelers on the Char Dham route to Badrinath, with the confluence itself (the Sangam) a major bathing (snan) site.
