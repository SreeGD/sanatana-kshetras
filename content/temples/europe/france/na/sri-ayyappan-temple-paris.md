The Sri Ayyappan Temple sits on Rue Philippe de Girard in the La Chapelle district of Paris's 18th arrondissement — the same dense cluster of Tamil-run shops, restaurants, and temples near Gare du Nord that is also home to the already-documented Sri Manicka Vinayakar Alayam. It is dedicated to Ayyappan (also called Ayyappa or Sastha), a deity particularly venerated in Kerala and parts of Tamil Nadu and traditionally held to be the son of Shiva and Mohini (Vishnu's female form), whose major pilgrimage center is Sabarimala in Kerala.

Ayyappan worship is otherwise unrepresented among this directory's European entries, making this Paris temple a distinctive addition to its coverage of South Indian devotional traditions transplanted to the diaspora. Its governing association was formally declared to French authorities on 12 October 1999, originally based in the suburb of Vincennes before relocating to its current La Chapelle address in 2018. Unlike its more visible neighbor, the Ganesh temple on Rue Pajol, the Ayyappan temple keeps a low public profile, described by one academic study as "barely visible from the street," and it does not organize a public procession through the neighborhood. Founders' names and an architectural description remain unconfirmed; fuller documentation would need to come directly from the temple or its community.
