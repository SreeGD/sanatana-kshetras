Swamibagh, in central Dhaka, has been the site of a Hindu temple complex since 1902, encompassing separate shrines historically associated with Shiva, Kali, and Vaishnava (Krishna/Jagannath) worship. The Vaishnava section of the complex is today managed by ISKCON and is popularly known among devotees as a Radha-Madana Mohan temple, though its site remains cataloged on Wikipedia simply as "Swami Bagh Temple." It functions as one of ISKCON's principal centers in Bangladesh and is best known as the starting point of Dhaka's annual Jagannath Rath Yatra, a chariot procession that travels to the Dhakeshwari National Temple, echoing the more famous Puri Rath Yatra tradition.

This temple should not be confused with a separate, smaller ISKCON temple in Chaumuhani, Begumganj, in Noakhali district roughly 150 km from Dhaka, which was the scene of communal violence in October 2021 in which a Hindu man was killed. The Swamibagh complex in Dhaka was not involved in that incident; conflating the two would misrepresent both the history of this established Dhaka institution and the specifics of the 2021 violence.
