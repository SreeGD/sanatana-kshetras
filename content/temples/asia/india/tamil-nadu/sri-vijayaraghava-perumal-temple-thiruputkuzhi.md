Sri Vijayaraghava Perumal Temple at Thiruputkuzhi is one of the 108 Divya Desams and is closely woven into the Ramayana tradition of the region. Local legend holds that after Jatayu, the mythical vulture-king, was mortally wounded while trying to save Sita from Ravana, Rama performed the bird's last rites at this very spot; the temple tank here is accordingly known as Jatayu Theertham, and the village's name — combining the Tamil words for "bird" (pul) and "pit" (kuzhi) — is said to preserve the memory of Jatayu's burial. Vishnu is worshipped here as Vijayaraghava Perumal, a form of Rama associated with this act of grace, with Lakshmi as his consort Margathavalli Thayar.

The temple is also remembered as a place of learning connected to Sri Ramanuja, the towering 11th–12th century Vaishnava theologian: tradition holds that his early teacher, the Advaita scholar Yadava Prakasha, was born in Thiruputkuzhi, and that Ramanuja himself studied here before their celebrated philosophical parting of ways. A shrine to Ramanuja stands within the temple complex in recognition of this association, and the later Vaishnava teacher-poet Vedanta Desika is also said to have composed verses in praise of the presiding deity.

Epigraphically, the temple's surviving stone inscriptions point to construction and endowment activity under the Pandya kings, with additional inscriptions from the Chola period, indicating that the site was patronized across several dynasties active in the Kanchipuram region between roughly the 10th and 13th centuries CE. Thirumangai Alvar, the prolific 8th–9th century Vaishnava saint-poet, sang a hymn glorifying Vijayaraghava Perumal here, securing the temple's place in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham and, with it, its permanent status among the sacred 108. As one of the Nadu Nadu Divya Desams lying just outside Kanchipuram proper, Thiruputkuzhi remains a modest but living pilgrimage site, its Jatayu legend and Ramanuja associations giving it a distinct place among the region's Vishnu shrines.
