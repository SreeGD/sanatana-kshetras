New Shantipur is, by the account of several Wikipedia articles on Hinduism and the Indian community in Poland, the country's oldest Hindu temple. It was founded in 1980 in the village of Czarnów, in what is now Lower Silesian Voivodeship in southwestern Poland, by devotees of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON), the Gaudiya Vaishnava movement founded in the West by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada. ISKCON missionaries had begun spreading Krishna consciousness in Poland from the mid-1970s, at the time still under communist rule, and New Shantipur represents the point at which that missionary activity put down roots as a settled temple community rather than informal preaching. As with other ISKCON farm communities of the period, its focal altar centers on the Pancha Tattva — the five combined forms of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and his closest associates (Nityananda, Advaita Acharya, Gadadhara, and Srivasa) central to Gaudiya Vaishnava worship.

New Shantipur has since been followed by other ISKCON centers elsewhere in Poland, including New Ramana Reti near Warsaw (founded 1989) and New Navadvip in Wrocław (founded 1998), making Czarnów the founding node of ISKCON's now multi-city presence in the country. Public information about the temple's precise founding circumstances, the devotees who established it, or its current facilities is thin in the sources available for this entry; what is consistently confirmed across independent Wikipedia articles is the 1980 date, the Czarnów location, and its standing as Poland's first Hindu temple.
