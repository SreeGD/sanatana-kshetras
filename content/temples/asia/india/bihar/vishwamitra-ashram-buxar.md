Buxar, a town on the Ganga in western Bihar bordering Uttar Pradesh, is identified by regional tradition as the site of sage Vishwamitra's ashram from the Ramayana. According to the epic, Vishwamitra brought the young princes Rama and Lakshmana here to guard his yajna (fire sacrifice) from demonic disruption; in the course of that episode he taught them the use of celestial weapons, and Rama killed the demons Tadaka and Subahu. Buxar district's own tourism tradition places several specific locations within this story: Chaitra Van, a forest tract said to be where the demoness Tadaka lived and was killed by Rama; Ahirauli, a site about 6 km from Buxar town where Ahalya, wife of the sage Gautama, is locally said to have been restored from stone to human form by the touch of Rama's feet; and the Kanwaldah Pokhara, also known as Vyaghra Sar, a body of water with its own legendary Ramayana associations. The district's Panchkoshi Mela, an annual multi-day fair, commemorates Rama's onward journey through the area, with its early days each devoted to retracing a different stretch of that route.

No source consulted for this entry documents a site specifically named "Ramrekha Ghat" at Buxar, despite that name's currency in general Ramayana-circuit literature; this entry has therefore described the specific sites (Ahirauli, Chaitra Van, Vyaghra Sar, and the general tradition of Vishwamitra's hermitage) that independent sources do confirm for the district, rather than asserting an unverified ghat name. Buxar's own documented history is otherwise substantial and separate from this tradition: the town was founded in 1480 and incorporated as a municipality in 1991, and its vicinity was the site of the historic Battle of Chausa and the Battle of Buxar.
