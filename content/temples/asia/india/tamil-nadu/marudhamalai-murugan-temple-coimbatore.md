Marudhamalai's hilltop shrine to Murugan, roughly 1,200 years old and set at an elevation of about 150-180 metres on a spur of the Western Ghats some 15 km northwest of central Coimbatore, is one of Tamil Nadu's most visited Murugan temples and a major civic landmark for the city. It appears in the Thiruppugazh, the devotional hymns of the 15th-century Tamil saint-poet Arunagirinathar, and an adjoining cave shrine on the same hill is dedicated to Pambatti Siddhar. Pilgrims reach the sanctum by a roughly 3 km ghat road or a flight of about 837 steps, and the temple observes a full Murugan festival calendar, from Thaipusam and Panguni Uthiram to Karthigai Deepam and Navaratri.

Marudhamalai is sometimes called, in local and devotional usage, a "seventh abode" of Murugan. This is a traditional or popular characterization rather than an official standing: the canonical Arupadaiveedu ("six sacred abodes") is a closed, fixed list of six Murugan temples — Palani, Tiruchendur, Swamimalai, Tiruttani, Pazhamudircholai, and Thiruparankundram — and Marudhamalai's informal "seventh" reputation exists alongside, not inside, that traditional list. This entry treats the two as distinct: Marudhamalai's own deep antiquity and civic importance to Coimbatore stand on their own merits, independent of any claim on the closed six-temple set.
