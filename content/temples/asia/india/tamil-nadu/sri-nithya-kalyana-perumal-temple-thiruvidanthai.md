Sri Nithya Kalyana Perumal Temple at Thiruvidanthai enshrines Vishnu in his Varaha (boar) incarnation, worshipped here under the name Nithya Kalyana Perumal — "the Lord of eternal marriage" — with his consort Lakshmi as Komalavalli Thayar. According to temple legend, the sage Kalava wished for Vishnu to marry each of his 360 daughters; to fulfil this wish, Lakshmi is said to have assumed 360 forms and been wedded to Varaha once on every day of the year, an act commemorated in the deity's name and in the temple's enduring reputation as a place where devotees, especially those seeking a good marriage or hoping to overcome obstacles to one, come to offer prayers.

The temple's origins are traditionally traced to the Pallava dynasty around the 7th century CE, with the Cholas adding to the structure in the 11th century. Its epigraphical record is unusually cosmopolitan for the region: inscriptions include a grant from the Rashtrakuta king Krishna III dated to 959 CE — notable because the Rashtrakutas, based in the Deccan, only occasionally extended patronage this far south — as well as a later inscription of the Chola king Rajadhiraja Chola dated 1052 CE, together with further records of Pandya and other rulers continuing through the 16th century. This layered patronage attests to the temple's continuous importance as a Vaishnava shrine across several dynastic transitions.

Thiruvidanthai is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham through the hymns of Thirumangai Alvar, who sang in praise of Komalavalli Thayar and the Lord here, placing the temple firmly among the 108 Divya Desams. Positioned on the East Coast Road roughly midway between Chennai and Mahabalipuram, the temple continues to draw devotees both for its Divya Desam status within the Nadu Nadu group and for its living tradition as a favored site for marriage-related worship.
