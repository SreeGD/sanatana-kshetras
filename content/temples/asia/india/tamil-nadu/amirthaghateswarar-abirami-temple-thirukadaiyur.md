The Amirthaghateswarar-Abirami Temple in Thirukadaiyur, Mayiladuthurai district, is one of the more heavily glorified Paadal Petra Sthalams, praised across more than fifty combined verses by all three principal Nayanar poet-saints — Sambandar, Appar, and Sundarar. Shiva is worshipped here as Amirthaghateswarar, and specifically in the form Kalantaka, "destroyer of Time" — a name rooted in the temple's central legend: that the young devotee Markandeya, fated to die at sixteen, was saved when Shiva intervened against Yama, the god of death, on this spot. His consort is worshipped as Abhirami. The surviving structure is dated to the 9th-century Chola period, with inscriptional evidence of continued royal patronage under Rajaraja Chola I in the following century, and later expansion under the Thanjavur Nayaks.

That Markandeya legend gives the temple its most distinctive living practice: it is a favored site for Shashti Abda Poorthi, the ritual marking a person's 60th birthday (traditionally read as symbolic triumph over death, echoing Markandeya's own reprieve), as well as Sadhabishegam at 81. Spread across roughly eleven acres and five courtyards behind an eleven-storey gopuram, the temple also maintains a secondary shrine to Vishnu as Amrithanarayana, and is administered today by the Dharmapuram Adheenam, one of the prominent Shaiva monastic institutions of the Kaveri delta.
