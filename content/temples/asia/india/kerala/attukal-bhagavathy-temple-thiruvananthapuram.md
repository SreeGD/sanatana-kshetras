Attukal Bhagavathy Temple, a short distance from Thiruvananthapuram's Sree Padmanabhaswamy Temple, is a modest-sized shrine for most of the year and, for one week each spring, the site of what is recognized as the largest annual gathering of women for a religious ritual anywhere in the world. The presiding goddess, worshipped locally as Attukal Amma, is identified as a form of Bhadrakali mounted on a vetala and is devotionally linked with Kannagi, the wronged heroine of the ancient Tamil epic Cilappatikaram, whose righteous fury is said to have been pacified and transformed into the temple's protective, motherly goddess. Temple legend traces the shrine's founding to a local family head who encountered a mysterious young girl and was later instructed, in a dream, to consecrate her presence at a nearby sacred grove.

The temple's defining event, the Attukal Pongala, takes place each year in the Malayalam month of Kumbham (February-March), when several million women converge on the temple and the surrounding streets of Thiruvananthapuram to prepare pongala — a sweet rice offering — in small earthen pots lit across the city, an outdoor ritual cooking event so vast that it has been recognized by Guinness World Records as the largest single gathering of women for a religious activity. Outside the festival period, Attukal functions as a comparatively quieter neighborhood shrine, but its Pongala has made it one of the most widely known temples in Kerala and a defining feature of Thiruvananthapuram's civic and religious calendar.
