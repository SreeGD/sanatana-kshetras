The Durga temple at Nartiang, in Meghalaya's West Jaintia Hills district, is a rare instance of an old, well-documented Shakta shrine in a state where organized Hindu temple architecture is otherwise scarce — Meghalaya's population is predominantly Christian and its indigenous Khasi and Jaintia religious traditions historically centered on animist and ancestral worship rather than temple Hinduism. The temple's establishment is credited to Jaso Manik, a king of the Jaintia kingdom who reigned in the early 17th century (c. 1606-1641), tradition holding that the goddess appeared to him in a dream directing him to found the shrine.

Nartiang is counted among the 51 Shakta Pithas, sites associated with the scattering of the goddess Sati's body in the Daksha-Sati legend; here the local tradition holds that her left thigh fell, giving the goddess her name at this site, Jainteshwari, with the associated Bhairava worshipped as Kamadishwar. What makes the temple distinctive is its syncretism with local Jaintia custom: rather than a conventional stone or metal image, the goddess is represented during Durga Puja by a plantain-trunk figure decorated with marigold flowers, which is ceremonially immersed in the nearby Myntdu River, an observance still carried out with a traditional gun salute. Ancient cannons found near the site further point to the temple's historical role within a fortified Jaintia stronghold.
