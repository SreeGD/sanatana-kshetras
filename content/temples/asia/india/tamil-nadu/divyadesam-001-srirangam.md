The temple of Sri Ranganathaswamy at Srirangam, the sacred island town set amid the Kaveri in the Chola country, is revered as the foremost of the 108 Divya Desams and is honoured in Srivaishnava tradition as Bhuloka Vaikuntam, the Vaikuntam upon earth. Here the Lord reposes as Sri Ranganatha, the Moolavar, in the recumbent posture upon the serpent Adisesha, facing south toward Lanka, an attitude tradition associates with his grace shown to the devotee-king of the south. According to the sthala purana, the self-manifest image was worshipped by Brahma and later by the solar dynasty, and was borne to the south by Vibhishana, brother of Ravana, who wished to install it in Lanka; the Lord chose instead to abide forever on the island of Srirangam, granting Vibhishana the boon that he would gaze ever upon him. No other Divya Desam is so abundantly sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham: it is glorified by nearly the whole company of the Alwars, the first three Mudhal Alwars Poigai, Bhoothath and Pey, together with Thirumazhisai Alwar, Nammalwar, Kulasekara Alwar, Periyalwar, Andal, Thondaradippodi Alwar, Thiruppaan Alwar and Thirumangai Alwar. Thiruppaan Alwar, tradition holds, sang his Amalanaadhipiraan here, beholding the Lord from foot to crown and desiring to see nothing more thereafter, while Thondaradippodi Alwar served the Lord through his flower garden and his Thirumaalai and Thiruppalliyezhuchchi. Srirangam stands as the spiritual capital of the Sri Vaishnava sampradaya, sanctified by the later acharyas, and its grandeur, vast concentric enclosures and the great festivals of the recumbent Ranganatha continue to draw pilgrims as the very heart of the Divya Desam tradition.

## Festivals

As the foremost of the Divya Desams, Srirangam observes one of the richest festival calendars in the Srivaishnava tradition. The grandest is the annual Brahmotsavam, when Lord Ranganatha is taken in procession on a succession of vahanas. Most celebrated of all is the Vaikunta Ekadasi season, traditionally marked by the twenty-day Adhyayana Utsavam with its Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitations of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, when the Lord passes through the Paramapada Vasal, the gateway of heaven, before vast crowds. The Float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank, Pavithrotsavam, the Chithirai festivals, and Jyeshtabhishekam are also traditionally observed, alongside daily worship rich in temple honours.

## Location & Access

The temple stands on Srirangam island between the Kaveri and Kollidam rivers, just north of Tiruchirappalli (Trichy) in the Chola Nadu region of Tamil Nadu, near coordinates 10.86 N, 78.69 E. Trichy, with its well-connected railway junction and airport, is the nearest major transport hub, and the island is reached easily from the city. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday break.
