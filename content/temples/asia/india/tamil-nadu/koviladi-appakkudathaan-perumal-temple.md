The Appakkudathaan Perumal Temple at Koviladi — whose Divya Prabandham name, Thirupper Nagar, and whose village name, Koviladi ("at the foot of the temple"), both reflect its position just downstream from Srirangam on the Kaveri — is one of the five Pancharanga Kshetrams, a set of Ranganatha shrines strung along the river. The presiding deity, Appakkudathaan (also called Appalaa Ranganathan), reclines facing west, with his consort Kamalavalli Thayar enshrined alongside.

The temple's name is explained by a well-loved legend: King Upamanyu, cursed by the irascible sage Durvasa, found relief when Vishnu appeared before him disguised as an old man asking for neyyappam, a sweet rice pancake — "Appakkudathaan" meaning, roughly, "the one who holds the pot of appam." A related tradition holds that the sage Markandeya was also freed from a curse of Yama at this site. These stories, transmitted through temple oral tradition and pilgrimage literature, situate the temple within the broader Pancharanga cluster of riverside Ranganatha shrines.

Documented history at the site includes inscriptions dated to the 18th regnal year of the early Chola king Aditya I, placing verifiable royal patronage in the 9th-to-10th century CE — among the more concretely dated Divya Desams in this part of the Chola Nadu group. The temple is glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by four Alvars — Periyalvar, Thirumangai Alvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, and Nammalvar — across a combined 33 verses. Its principal living festival is the 13-day Panguni Brahmotsavam chariot festival each spring.
