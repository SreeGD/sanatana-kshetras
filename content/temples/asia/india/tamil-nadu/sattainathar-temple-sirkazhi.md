The Sattainathar Temple at Sirkazhi holds a singular place among the Paadal Petra Sthalams as the traditional birthplace of Thirugnana Sambandar, the child-prodigy Nayanar saint whose hymns open the Tevaram canon itself. Tradition holds that the infant Sambandar was nursed with divine wisdom by the goddess Parvati at this very temple, prompting him to spontaneously compose the canon's opening verse, "Todudaya Seviyan." The temple is glorified in turn by all three principal Nayanar saints — Sambandar, Appar, and Sundarar.

Unusually, the temple enshrines three distinct forms of Shiva across different levels of its structure: Sattainathar (also Vatukanathar) above, Thoniappar (also Uma Maheswarar) at the middle level, and Brahmapureeswarar, represented as a Shivalingam, below; the goddess correspondingly appears in the complex as Sthira Sundari, Thirunilainayaki, and Periyanayaki. The temple's porch is dated to the 10th/11th century, with the wider complex expanded significantly under successive Chola rulers — Kulothunga I, II, III, and Vikrama Chola — between the 11th and 13th centuries.
