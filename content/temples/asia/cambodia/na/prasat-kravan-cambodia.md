Prasat Kravan, completed in 921 CE at Angkor near Siem Reap, is unusual among major Khmer monuments in having been commissioned not by a reigning king but by high court officials under Harshavarman I or Ishanavarman II. Its five reddish brick towers, set on a common terrace south of the Srah Srang baray, are dedicated to Vishnu and are best known for large interior brick bas-reliefs — a technique rare among surviving Khmer monuments — depicting the god in several forms, including a four-armed Vishnu taking a great step, illustrating his Vamana (dwarf) avatar and the Trivikrama episode in which he strides across the universe in three steps, alongside reliefs of the goddess Lakshmi.

The temple underwent restoration in stages: vegetation clearance in the 1930s, followed by more substantial tower reconstruction between 1962 and 1966 under archaeologist Bernard Philippe Groslier, with replacement bricks distinctly marked "CA" for Conservation Angkor to distinguish them from the original 10th-century fabric. Today it functions as an archaeological and heritage site within the wider Angkor complex rather than an active place of worship.
