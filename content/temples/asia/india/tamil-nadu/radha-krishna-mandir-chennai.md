One of ISKCON's centers in South India, the Radha Krishna Mandir in Chennai (formally the Sri Sri Radha Krishna Mandir, known as ISKCON Temple Chennai) serves the city's Gaudiya Vaishnava congregation alongside regular public programs of kirtan, scripture classes, and prasadam (sanctified food) distribution characteristic of ISKCON centers worldwide. Alongside Krishna and Radha (attended by Lalitha and Vishaka), the temple also enshrines Chaitanya and Nityananda, and Jagannath, Baladeva and Subhadra.

ISKCON founder A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada directed efforts to establish a centre in Madras (Chennai) as early as 1971, visiting the city himself in 1972 and 1976. The present site at Injambakkam was acquired in 2000, construction began on 17 March 2002, and the temple was formally inaugurated on 26 April 2012. Built across five levels on about 1.5 acres (45,000 sq ft) of land at a cost of roughly Rs 100 million, the complex blends Pallava and Kalinga architectural influences, and includes three teak-wood altars, a 7,000-square-foot temple hall, and a chandelier set with 500 Himalayan quartz crystals. The project was designed under the guidance of Sri Bhanu Swami and built with the support of roughly 8,000 life patrons.

## Festivals

Krishna Janmashtami (August) is the temple's major festival. Ratha Yatra is observed in February, a separate Jagannath Rath Yatra in July, and Gaura Purnima in March.

## Location & Access

The temple stands at Hare Krishna Land, Bhaktivedanta Swami Road, Akkarai, Sholinganallur, Injambakkam, on Chennai's southern IT corridor (OMR), roughly 20 km south of central Chennai, reachable by road. Darshan is offered from 7:30 AM to 1:00 PM and 4:00 PM to 8:00 PM, with a final sayana arati at 9:00 PM. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple before visiting.
