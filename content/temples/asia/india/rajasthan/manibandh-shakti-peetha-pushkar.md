Manibandh Shakti Peetha, tucked into the hills roughly 11 km northwest of Ajmer and a short distance from the far more famous Brahma Temple at Pushkar, takes its name directly from its founding legend: Manibandh literally means "wrist" or "wristband," and tradition holds that Sati's wrists fell at this spot after her body was cut apart by the Sudarshan chakra, consecrating it as one of the network of 51 Shakti Peethas. The presiding goddess is worshipped here as Gayatri, also known locally as Chamunda Mata, with Shiva present in his Bhairava form as Sarvanand.

Unlike the famous Brahma Temple down the road in Pushkar, Manibandh has no well-documented ancient history: the source consulted for this entry describes it plainly as a relatively obscure, little-visited shrine for much of its existence, with meaningful infrastructure development and wider pilgrim awareness only emerging in the early 21st century. No founding date, patron, or dynasty could be confirmed, so this entry does not claim continuous worship across centuries at this specific site, even though its identity as a named Shakti Peetha is long-standing in regional tradition. Today it draws pilgrims particularly during Navaratri, and benefits from proximity to the much larger crowds that gather nearby each year for the Pushkar Fair.
