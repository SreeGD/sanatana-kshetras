The Chamunda Devi temple on the banks of the Baner river, near Palampur in Kangra district, exists as a valley-floor alternative to a much less accessible older shrine, Aadi Himani Chamunda, set higher on the surrounding hills. Tradition holds that a priest was shown the location of an ancient image of the goddess in a dream, after which a king's men retrieved it and installed it at the present, easier-to-reach site — a fairly common pattern in the region's temple foundation legends, where a hilltop original gives rise to a valley shrine for everyday pilgrimage. Sources place this present temple's construction at roughly four centuries ago, putting its origin somewhere in the 16th or 17th century, though the exact date and founder are not established beyond that approximation.

Dedicated to Chamunda Devi, a fierce form of Durga, the temple is widely counted among Himachal Pradesh's five major Shakti temples in popular reckoning, alongside sites such as Jwalamukhi and Chintpurni, even though it does not appear on the canonical list of 51 or 52 Shakti Peethas recognized in scripture. It remains an active and well-visited pilgrimage site, particularly during the two annual Navaratri periods, and its riverside setting along the Dharamshala-Palampur road has made it a common stop for pilgrims touring Kangra's cluster of goddess temples.
