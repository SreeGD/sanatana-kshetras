Thirukadalmallai, in the ancient port town of Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram), is one of the Nadu Nadu (Thondai Nadu) Divya Desams and stands close to the famed Pallava shore monuments. Vishnu is worshipped here in a reclining posture as Sthalasayana Perumal, also called Jala Sayana Perumal ("one who reclines by the water"), with his consort Lakshmi enshrined as Nilamangai Thayar.

The temple's foundation is attributed to the Pallava dynasty, whose 7th-8th century monuments dominate Mamallapuram, with subsequent renovations and endowments recorded under the Medieval Cholas, the Vijayanagara kings, and the Madurai Nayaks — a long continuity of royal patronage reflecting the site's importance as both a temple town and a major port. It is celebrated in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, the compiled hymns of the Alvar saints (6th-9th centuries CE), which secures its place among the 108 Divya Desams of Vishnu.

The temple holds particular significance in Sri Vaishnava tradition as the traditional birthplace of Bhoothathalvar, one of the three Mudhal (first) Alvars said to have been born of divine origin rather than in the ordinary human way. This connection is commemorated annually through the Bhoothathalvar Avatara Utsavam in the Tamil month of Aipasi (October-November). The temple's proximity to the Shore Temple and other rock-cut Pallava monuments has led some scholars to note shared stylistic and historical threads between the two sites, though the Divya Desam shrine and the Shore Temple (a Shaiva monument) are distinct structures.
