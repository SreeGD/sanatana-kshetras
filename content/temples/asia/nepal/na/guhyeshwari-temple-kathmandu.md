On the southern bank of the Bagmati river, about a kilometre east of Pashupatinath Temple already documented elsewhere in this directory, Guhyeshwari Temple honors the goddess under her most esoteric name — Guhyeshwari or Guhyakali, "the hidden/secret goddess." Tantric texts, notably the Meru Tantra, identify this as the spot where Sati's anus and rectum fell when her body was cut apart, one of the more unusual body-part attributions among the Shakti Peethas; correspondingly, Kathmandu Valley tantric tradition treats Pashupati (Shiva, at the nearby temple) as Guhyeshwari's consort, binding the two sites together in ritual and cosmology even though they are physically and administratively separate temples.

The present temple structure is documented as having been renovated in the 17th century under the Malla-dynasty king Pratap Malla, though no confirmed record establishes how much older the underlying shrine or worship tradition is. Daily ritual at the temple follows the Sarvamnaya Tantra tradition and is conducted by Newar priestly communities, including Karmacharya priests specializing in Kaula tantric practice — a living continuity of esoteric Shakta worship distinctive to the Kathmandu Valley. The temple's major annual observances are Navaratri/Dashain, shared with the wider Nepali Hindu calendar, and its own Guhyeshwari Jatra procession.
