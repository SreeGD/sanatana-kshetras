The Shree Veeraboga Emperumal Temple in Tongaat's Tongaat Section traces its own origin to 1914, when a gathering of the local Tamil Hindu community read the Shree Veeraboga Vasantharaayar Sarithuram, a text described as containing "Prophecies of the Kali Yuga," under a banyan tree at the site. That reading led directly to the founding of an associated religious organization, the Shree Veeraboga Vasantharaayar Dharamah Pareepaluna Mudam, and eventually to a permanent temple dedicated to Vishnu under the name Veeraboga Emperumal ("Emperumal" being a common Tamil honorific for Vishnu). The original temple building was a modest wood-and-iron structure, later rebuilt on the same site in what came to be known as the "Gandhi & Company" building.

Like many of the Tamil Vaishnava and Shaiva institutions that grew up among indentured and free Indian communities on South Africa's Natal coast from the late nineteenth century onward, the temple has broadened its role over the decades beyond worship alone: it today also operates a Cultural Centre and a Peace & Skills Training Centre, reflecting the way such temples have long served as community anchors for Tongaat's Hindu population beyond strictly religious functions.
