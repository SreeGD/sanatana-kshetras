The Soundararajaperumal Temple at Nagapattinam is unusual among the Divya Desams for housing Vishnu in three distinct postures within a single complex: standing as Soundararaja Perumal (also called Neelamega Perumal), seated as Govindaraja Perumal, and reclining as Ranganatha Perumal. The principal consort is worshipped as Soundaravalli Nachiyar.

Local tradition situates the temple's sanctity across all four Yugas, with legendary associations involving the serpent Adishesha, the earth-goddess Bhoodevi, and the devotee Dhruvan's path to enlightenment; a Chola king remembered in some sources by the uncertain name "Salisugan" is also said to have worshipped here. A further tradition, drawn from a single lower-confidence source, holds that the temple's seven-tiered gopuram once carried a light used as a navigational aid for ships entering the historic port of Nagapattinam — a striking claim reflecting the town's long history as a major maritime and multi-religious trading center, though one that could not be independently corroborated for this entry.

Historically, two Chola-period inscriptions are documented at the site, and the temple's construction is generally attributed to the Medieval Cholas, with later contributions from the Thanjavur Nayaks in the 17th century and subsequently the Thanjavur Marathas. One source credits a patron remembered as "Jagul Nayakar" — a name suggesting the multi-religious character of Nayak-era Nagapattinam — with building the temple's tower, prayer halls, and compound wall, though this attribution has not been independently verified. The temple is glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by Thirumangai Alvar, and its principal living festival, a chariot procession, is held each year in the Tamil month of Chittirai.
