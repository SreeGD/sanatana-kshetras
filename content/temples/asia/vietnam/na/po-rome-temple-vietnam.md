Po Rome Temple, in Ninh Phuoc District of Ninh Thuan Province not far from the better-known Po Klong Garai Temple, honors King Po Rome, who ruled the Champa kingdom from 1627 to 1651. Tradition holds that the Cham people, grieving his death in 1651, built the temple as a site of veneration for him — one of a distinctive group of Cham monuments dedicated not to conventional pan-Indian deities but to deified Cham kings understood within a Shaiva devotional framework, a pattern also seen at Po Klong Garai and Po Nagar elsewhere in Vietnam.

Sources note some uncertainty about the temple's precise construction date, since a surviving carved sandstone pediment associated with the site has separately been dated as early as the 14th-16th century, suggesting the possibility of an earlier structure predating the traditional post-1651 founding account; that relief fragment is today held in the Museum of Vietnamese History. What is clear is that Po Rome remains, per available sourcing, "one of the principal deified figures in traditional Cham rites," meaning the temple continues to carry active ritual significance for Vietnam's Cham community rather than functioning purely as an archaeological ruin.
