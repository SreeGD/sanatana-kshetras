The Comunidade Hindu de Portugal (Hindu Community of Portugal) was formally established in 1982 by a small founding group of just five or six families, almost all descended from Hindus of Daman and Diu — the former Portuguese enclaves on India's west coast — who had settled in Mozambique under colonial rule and then emigrated to Portugal after Mozambique's 1975 independence. Sharing both Portuguese and Gujarati as common languages helped the community organize quickly; by the mid-1990s it had grown to roughly 8,000 members, concentrated mostly around Lisbon.

The community built its Radha Krishna Mandir at Paço do Lumiar, a former royal residence about 6 km north of central Lisbon, planning it as one of the largest Hindu temple complexes outside India. Construction was around 70% complete in early 1997, with organizers hoping to finish by the end of that year to coincide with Expo '98's commemoration of Vasco da Gama's sea route to India; the temple is recorded as having opened in 1998. Beyond worship, the complex has also hosted community services such as job-training programs and blood-donation drives run jointly with Portuguese public health bodies.
