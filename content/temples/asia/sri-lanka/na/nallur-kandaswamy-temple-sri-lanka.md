The most important Hindu temple in Sri Lanka's Tamil-majority north, Nallur Kandaswamy Temple traces its founding to 948 CE, with the site developed further in the 13th century under Puvenaya Vaku, a minister to the Jaffna kingdom's King Kalinga Magha. A third temple built in the 15th/16th century under Sapumal Kumaraya was destroyed by Portuguese colonial forces led by Filipe de Oliveira in 1624 CE. The present (fourth) temple was constructed in 1734 CE during Dutch colonial rule by 'Don Juan' Ragunatha Maapaana Mudaliyar, and was substantially expanded from the 1890s onward (bell towers, sanctum renovation, enclosing walls, and further gopuram additions into the 20th and 21st centuries). It remains the spiritual center of Sri Lankan Tamil Shaivism and a major pilgrimage site for Tamils across the island and diaspora.
