Phanom Rung, crowning an extinct volcano roughly 402 meters high in Buriram Province in northeastern Thailand's Isan region, is one of the most significant Khmer Hindu temple-mountains built outside present-day Cambodia. Originally dedicated to Shiva, with a linga enshrined in its inner sanctum, the complex was built in phases from the 10th to 13th centuries, with its main sanctuary constructed under King Suryavarman II in the 12th century — the same king who built Angkor Wat — and further additions made under Jayavarman VII in the 13th century.

Approached via a dramatic roughly 160-meter processional walkway lined with naga (serpent) bridges leading up to its sandstone-and-laterite principal tower, Phanom Rung was later repurposed for Buddhist use as Hinduism's influence in the region receded. Restored by Thailand's Fine Arts Department between 1971 and 1988, the historical park was officially opened by Princess Maha Chakri Sirindhorn on 21 May 1988 and was placed on Thailand's UNESCO World Heritage tentative list in 2005. Today it stands as one of Thailand's most important Hindu heritage monuments, illustrating the deep reach of Khmer Shaiva temple architecture well beyond the borders of modern Cambodia.
