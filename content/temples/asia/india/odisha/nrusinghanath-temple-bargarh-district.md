Nrusinghanath Temple sits at the foothills of the Gandhamardhan hills near Paikamal in Odisha's Bargarh district, enshrining Vishnu's man-lion avatar Narasimha -- one of the rarer avatara dedications documented in this directory outside the well-known Dashavatara sequence covered elsewhere. Its founding is credited to Baijal Singh Dev, an early-15th-century ruler of the historical Patna State (centred on Patnagarh, in present-day Bargarh district, and entirely distinct from the city of Patna in Bihar also covered in this directory), giving the standing Kalinga-style Deula structure a documented age of roughly six centuries. Long before that Vaishnava founding, however, the same hills are recorded by the Chinese traveller Xuanzang as having hosted a Buddhist centre of scriptural learning -- a layered religious history that the sources consulted do not fully explain, but which situates Nrusinghanath among several sites in this directory (Malinithan in Arunachal Pradesh among them) where an earlier non-Hindu or pre-Hindu religious landscape appears to underlie a later, better-documented Hindu shrine.

Local tradition also links the temple to Narasimha's defeat of a "Musika Daitya," a mouse-demon, in a feline form called Marjara Keshari -- devotional lore distinct from, though related to, the more familiar Narasimha-Hiranyakashipu legend. The temple remains an active pilgrimage destination today, with its largest gathering falling on Nrusimha Chaturdashi in the month of Vaishakha (typically May), when thousands of devotees visit both the shrine and the seasonal waterfall that flows nearby in the Gandhamardhan hills.
