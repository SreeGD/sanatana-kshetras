Azhwarthirunagari, on the southern bank of the Thamirabarani river in present-day Thoothukudi district, is revered as the foremost of the nine Nava Tirupathi shrines and, more than that, as the birthplace of Nammalvar, the most prolific of the twelve Alvars and traditionally credited with over a thousand of the four thousand verses of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Here Vishnu is worshipped in standing posture as Adhinathar (his feet said to be sunk into the earth), with his consort Lakshmi as Adhinathanayaki, also called Thirukkurukoornayaki.

According to Sri Vaishnava tradition, the infant who would become Nammalvar showed no response to food, sound, or touch after his birth in a Vellalar family in this village, then known as Thirukkurukur. His anxious parents brought him to the sanctum of Adhinathar and left him at the deity's feet, whereupon the child rose, climbed into a hollow in a nearby tamarind tree, and sat in meditation for some sixteen years, neither eating nor speaking. He remained silent until the wandering saint Madhurakavi Alvar arrived at Thirukkurukur, lit a lamp before him, and posed a riddle about the nature of the soul housed in a perishable body; Nammalvar's reply became the opening of his hymn Tiruviruttam, marking the start of his surviving corpus (Tiruviruttam, Tiruvaasiriyam, Periya Thiruvandhadi, and the 1,102 verses of Tiruvaimozhi). Madhurakavi went on to become his first disciple, and the temple and its tamarind tree are preserved today as the site of this awakening.

Because of this association, Azhwarthirunagari occupies an especially exalted place among the Nava Tirupathi, the cluster of nine Vishnu shrines strung along the Thamirabarani between Tirunelveli and Tiruchendur, each of which Nammalvar glorified as one of the nine classical grahas (planets) in his Tiruvaimozhi. The temple's existence is also attested in the Brahmanda Purana's Navatirupati Mahatmiyam, and it is built in the Dravidian style typical of the region's Pandya-era Vaishnava shrines, though a detailed epigraphic chronology was not established from the sources consulted for this entry.

Alongside its architectural and ritual life, the temple functions as the anchor of the wider Nava Tirupathi pilgrimage circuit; devotees who tour the nine shrines typically begin or end at Azhwarthirunagari, and the annual Vaikasi Visakam festival celebrating Nammalvar's appearance day draws pilgrims from across Tamil Nadu.
