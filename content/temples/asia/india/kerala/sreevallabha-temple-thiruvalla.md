## Origin and legend

The Sreevallabha Temple at Thiruvalla enshrines Vishnu as Sreevallabha ("consort of Sree/Lakshmi"), with subsidiary forms worshipped in the same complex as Sudarshanamoorthy, Kolapiran, and Thiruvaazhmarban, and the goddess as Selva Thirukozhunthu Nachiyar (also called Vaathsalya Devi). Local tradition holds that the deity was installed in 59 BCE, making it, by tradition, one of the very oldest continuously worshipped Vaishnava shrines in Kerala; the temple is also said to be mentioned in the Garuda and Matsya Puranas.

## Historical significance

Sreevallabha is among the oldest and largest temples in Kerala. By around 1100 CE it had become a significant centre of Vedic scholarship, with an extensive tradition of Vedic teaching and religious education associated with the site. Its festival calendar today centres on the Uthra Sreebali in the month of Meenam, its largest annual celebration, alongside a ten-day Kumbham festival and Vishu observances.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thiruvalla is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. Both Nammalvar and Thirumangai Alvar praised this temple -- Thirumangai Alvar alone composing ten pasurams on Lord Vallabha Perumal -- and mangalasasanam of the shrine by both saints secures its place among the 108 Divya Desams of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham.
