Tulja Bhavani Temple crowns Yamunachala hill above the Bori river at Tuljapur in Maharashtra's Dharashiv district. According to Wikipedia's account, the temple was built in the 12th century, around 1169 CE, under the patronage of a Kadamb-dynasty ruler, Mahamandaleshwara Maradadeva, though the black-stone, Hemadpanthi-style structure seen today reflects centuries of later renovation. The presiding deity, Bhavani, is worshipped as a form of Durga, and the temple is counted among the four principal Shakti Peethas of Maharashtra, alongside the Mahalakshmi temple at Kolhapur, Renuka Devi at Mahur, and Saptashrungi near Nashik — all covered elsewhere in this directory.

Puranic tradition, drawing on the Skanda Purana, holds that the goddess manifested here after slaying the buffalo-demon Mahishasura, and that she later rescued a devoted widow named Anubhuti from further demonic attack — an episode credited with giving the goddess her name, "Tulja" Bhavani. This origin story is devotional legend rather than independently documented history, and is treated as such here.

The temple carries a well-documented association with the Bhosale clan, the family of the Maratha king Chhatrapati Shivaji, who regarded Bhavani as their kuladevata (family deity); the temple's three gateways are named for Shivaji's parents, Shahaji and Jijabai, and for the Nimbalkar family. Popular legend goes further, holding that the goddess personally appeared to Shivaji and gifted him a sword that came to be known as the Bhavani sword — a story that circulates widely in regional retellings but is distinct from, and less firmly documented than, the family's broader and well-attested devotion to the temple.
