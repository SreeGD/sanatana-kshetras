Thirukoshtiyur is one of the Divya Desams glorified by the Alvars for its reclining form of Vishnu, worshipped as Uraga Mellanayan, "he who reclines gently on the serpent," with the processional utsavar image known as Soumya Narayana Perumal and the consort venerated separately as Thirumamagal Thayar. The temple's Ashtanga Vimana, rising some 82 feet, is unusually tall for a Dravidian shrine, taller even than its own gopuram, a feature it shares with only a few other South Indian temples such as Koodal Azhagar in Madurai.

Four Alvars, Periyalvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, Bhoothathalvar, and Peyalvar, are credited with hymns to this shrine in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, placing its sanctity firmly within the Pandya Nadu group of the 108 Divya Desams and the 6th-9th century devotional period of the Tamil Vaishnava canon.

Beyond the Alvar era, Thirukoshtiyur holds a central place in later Sri Vaishnava sampradaya tradition as the site where the philosopher-theologian Ramanuja received the sacred Ashtakshara mantra, "Om Namo Narayanaya," from his teacher Thirukkottiyur Nambi. Tradition recounts that Ramanuja journeyed to Thirukoshtiyur eighteen times before Nambi consented to teach him the mantra, on the condition that it be kept secret; Ramanuja is said to have instead climbed to the temple tower and proclaimed it publicly to the assembled crowd, reasoning that though he himself might be condemned for disobedience, all who heard the mantra from him would attain liberation. Nambi, rather than rebuking him, is said to have honored this act by renaming him Emperumanar, "one greater than myself." Whether read as devotional legend or as an early episode in the documented history of Sri Vaishnava philosophy and its teaching lineages (parampara), the story remains foundational to the tradition's self-understanding and is retold at the temple to this day.
