Naganathaswamy Temple, in the small coastal-belt village of Keezhperumpallam in Mayiladuthurai district, is the Navagraha shrine associated with Ketu, completing Tamil Nadu's nine-temple planetary circuit alongside the shrines for the Sun, Moon, and the other five visible grahas plus Rahu. As at several of the other Navagraha sites, Shiva is worshipped here as the presiding deity, with the Ketu association carried by a subsidiary shrine that draws devotees seeking relief from the astrological effects attributed to this shadow planet, traditionally linked in Hindu astrology to detachment, upheaval, and spiritual transformation.

Independent sourcing on this particular temple proved considerably thinner than for the other eight Navagraha shrines: available material confirms only its location and its designated role in the Navagraha tradition, without offering a founding century, ruling dynasty, or site-specific legend that could be verified during this pass. This entry is accordingly marked pending, and its coordinates have been left out rather than estimated; it is flagged here as the temple in this circuit most in need of further, better-sourced documentation.
