Sikkim is a Buddhist-majority state with a comparatively small and recent footprint of Hindu temple architecture, and the Kirateshwar Mahadev Temple at Legship is one of its more distinctive Shaiva shrines. It sits on the banks of the Rangeet River in West Sikkim (Gyalshing district), where a naturally occurring stone formation is worshipped as a self-manifested Shiva lingam. Adjoining shrines to Rama and Durga make the complex a broader pilgrimage stop rather than a single-deity temple.

The temple's sanctity rests on its identification with the Kirata-Arjuna episode of the Mahabharata, in which Shiva, testing and then blessing Arjuna's penance, appears to him in the form of Kirateshwara, a hunter-deity associated with the Kirati peoples of the eastern Himalayan foothills — hence the temple's name and its continued importance to Kirati devotees as well as Hindus more broadly in Sikkim. No historical record of when a built shrine first arose at this spot could be verified from available sources; what is documented is the site's continuing active use, its naturally formed lingam, and its role as a focus of Maha Shivaratri and Bala Chaturdashi observance in the region today.
