Established in 2009 under the Bhakti Marga Foundation, the Shree Vara Lakshmi Narshingadev temple in Warsaw is dedicated to Narasimha (Narshingadev) — the man-lion avatar of Vishnu who, per the Puranas, tore apart the demon-king Hiranyakashipu to protect his devotee Prahlada — worshipped here alongside Lakshmi. It is a rare instance of a dedicated Narasimha shrine among this directory's European entries, most of which center on Krishna, Rama, Shiva, or Devi forms rather than this particular Vishnu avatar.

The temple is one of several Hindu temples that have been established in Warsaw alongside Poland's older, ISKCON-founded rural communities such as New Shantipur (Czarnów, 1980) and New Ramana Reti (Mysiadło, near Warsaw, 1989), both already documented elsewhere in this directory, reflecting a more recent diversification of organized Hindu devotional life in the Polish capital beyond ISKCON's Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition. Beyond its 2009 founding date, city, and sponsoring foundation, further detail on the temple's address, building, and congregation could not be confirmed in the source available for this entry.
