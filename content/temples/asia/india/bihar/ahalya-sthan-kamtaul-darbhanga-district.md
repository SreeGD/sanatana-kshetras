The Ramayana's Bala Kanda tells of Ahalya, wife of the sage Gautama, who was cursed to become stone after the god Indra deceived her in her husband's absence; the curse held until Rama, traveling with the sage Vishwamitra toward Mithila, touched the stone and restored her to human form. The site identified with that episode in Bihar's Mithila region is Ahalya Sthan (also spelled Ahilya Asthan), located in Ward 11 of Kamtaul, in Darbhanga district.

According to the source consulted for this entry, the present temple was built between 1662 and 1682 CE under two rulers named Maharaja Chhatra Singh and Maharaja Rudra Singh, and is locally described as the first Ram-Janaki temple built in India — a claim this entry reports but has not been able to independently corroborate beyond the single source that states it. Ahalya Sthan is counted today among Darbhanga district's notable pilgrimage and tourist destinations, alongside the nearby Gautam Ashram associated with the same story. No festival calendar, darshan-hours schedule, or further architectural detail was found in the sources consulted, so those specifics are omitted here pending better documentation.
