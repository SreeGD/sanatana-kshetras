Hastinapur, on the old course of the Ganga in what is today Meerut district, is remembered as the Kuru capital of the Mahabharata — the seat from which the Kauravas and Pandavas ruled before the great war that gives the epic its name. Amid the ruins of that ancient city stands the Pandeshwar Mahadev Temple, a Shiva shrine on a hillock traditionally identified as the place where the Kaurava and Pandava princes received their education in the Vedas and Puranas. Devotional tradition credits Yudhishthira, the eldest of the five Pandava brothers, with establishing the shivalinga here and praying to Shiva for victory before the war began — a legendary founding account rather than a dated historical record. A temple to the goddess Kali and several Hindu ashramas share the same hillock among the ruins, and nearby, on an old ravine along the Ganga's bank, stands a separate Karna Temple, whose own Shivling is traditionally said to have been established by Karna.

Hastinapur's Mahabharata associations exist alongside a second, independent layer of sacred geography: the town is also one of the most important pilgrimage centers in Jainism, traditionally held to be the birthplace of several Tirthankaras, with its own large Jain temple complex (including the Digamber Jain Bada Mandir and the Jambudweep complex) drawing substantial pilgrim traffic in its own right. The Pandeshwar Mahadev Temple is a distinct, Hindu, Shaiva site within the same historic township, not part of that Jain complex, and this entry concerns only the former.
