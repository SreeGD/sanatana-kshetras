Tamil Hindu indentured laborers and their descendants maintained a well-attended temple on the Umbilo River in Durban, known as the First River temple (Shri Ambalavaanar Alayam, honoring Shiva in his form as Ambalavaanar, "Lord of the [cosmic dance] hall," i.e. Nataraja), by as early as 1875, making it one of the earliest documented Hindu places of worship in colonial Natal alongside the wider wave of Tamil temple-building that followed the start of Indian indenture in 1860. Severe flooding in 1905 damaged the original riverside structure, but the community saved the temple's deity images and other relocatable fittings beforehand and re-established worship nearby at a newly built Second River temple.

That successor Second River temple is recorded as having remained in continuous use for more than a century since, making it, alongside other Durban-area Tamil Shaiva temples founded in the same indenture-era decades, part of the long unbroken thread of organized Hindu worship among South Africa's Indian-origin community on the KwaZulu-Natal coast. Its history of flood damage and careful relocation of sacred images also illustrates how several of Durban's oldest Hindu institutions survived not by remaining fixed in a single building, but by preserving continuity of worship and consecrated images across a change of site.
