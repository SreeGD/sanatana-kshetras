Kalpeshwar is dedicated to Shiva in the form of his matted locks (jata), the fifth and final body-part manifestation of the Panch Kedar legend. Tradition holds that after the Pandavas' victory at Kurukshetra, seeking to atone for killing their kin, they pursued Shiva through the Garhwal Himalayas as he fled in the form of a bull; when Bhima caught hold of the bull, it is said to have plunged into the earth and resurfaced in five places as five parts of its body, with Kalpeshwar marking the hair. As with the other Panch Kedar sites, no independently documented founder or construction date survives outside this tradition. The shrine itself is unusual among the five: a small stone sanctuary reached through a natural cave passage, set in dense forest in the Urgam Valley beside the Kalpganga river.

Kalpeshwar's most distinctive feature is practical rather than legendary: alone among the Panch Kedar shrines, it remains open and accessible to pilgrims throughout the year, since heavy snow does not close off its lower-altitude approach the way it does at Kedarnath, Tungnath, Rudranath, and Madhyamaheshwar. Road access along the Rishikesh-Badrinath highway now reaches close to the Urgam Valley, and the final approach from Devgram village has been shortened to a brief walk, a significant change from the longer forest trek historically required from Helang.
