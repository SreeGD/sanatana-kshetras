## Origin and legend

The Thrikkodithanam Mahavishnu Temple enshrines Vishnu as Arputha Narayanan (also called Amirutha Narayanan), a roughly six-foot standing image in Vishvarupa (cosmic) form facing east, with consort Lakshmi worshipped as Karpagavalli. Local tradition holds that the image was consecrated by Sahadeva, the youngest of the five Pandava brothers, as one of five neighbouring shrines the Pandavas are said to have founded along the Pamba river, alongside Thrichittat, Puliyoor, Aranmula and Thiruvanvandoor.

## Historical significance

The temple's Kerala-style architecture includes a two-story gopuram and a rectangular sanctum raised on a five-stepped platform, with wood and stucco carvings depicting scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata and murals dated to the early 18th century. A notable feature is the Kazhuvetti Kallu, a granite marker traditionally understood to depict punishment for temple desecration and bribery -- a reminder of the temple's role in enforcing customary temple law. Women's ritual dance (Kutakkuttu) is performed here as part of temple worship, and a monthly Sravana Deepam festival of lights continues to draw devotees.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thrikkodithanam is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. Nammalvar performed mangalasasanam (verse-glorification) of the shrine, and it is accordingly included among the 108 Divya Desams recorded in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham.
