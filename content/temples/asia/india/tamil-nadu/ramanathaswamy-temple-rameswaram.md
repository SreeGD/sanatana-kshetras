One of the twelve Jyotirlingas and one of the Char Dham of Hindu pilgrimage, Ramanathaswamy is traditionally held to enshrine a linga installed by Rama himself (with Hanuman's help) to atone for killing the Brahmin Ravana, on his return from Lanka. The temple's present form was substantially built up over centuries: expanded under the Pandyas in the 12th century, its sanctum renovated in the 14th-15th century by Jaffna Kingdom rulers using stone shipped from Koneswaram temple, and its famous outermost pillared corridor (roughly 1,170-1,200 meters around, with over 1,200 pillars) constructed under the Setupati (Ramnad) ruler Muthuramalinga Setupati in the late 18th century.
