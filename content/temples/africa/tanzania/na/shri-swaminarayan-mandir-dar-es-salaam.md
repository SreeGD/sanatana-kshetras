Dar es Salaam's Shri Swaminarayan Mandir on Morogoro Road anchors a dense cluster of Hindu temples in the Tanzanian capital's city center, a concentration significant enough that the local street has since been renamed Pramukh Swami Street in recognition of the Swaminarayan community's presence there. The temple serves a Hindu community that is overwhelmingly of Gujarati descent, tracing back both to Indian Ocean trading families who had connections to the East African coast for centuries and, more directly, to the waves of Gujarati migrants who settled in colonial Tanganyika (now mainland Tanzania) from the late nineteenth century onward.

The Swaminarayan tradition itself became firmly rooted across East Africa, including Tanzania, through the twentieth century, with Wikipedia's history of Hinduism in the country describing it as well established by 1950 on the strength of its social, cultural, and temple-building work. Alongside its Dar es Salaam mandir, the same Swaminarayan community built additional temples in Zanzibar, Arusha, Moshi, and Mwanza, making it one of the most widely distributed Hindu traditions in the country.
