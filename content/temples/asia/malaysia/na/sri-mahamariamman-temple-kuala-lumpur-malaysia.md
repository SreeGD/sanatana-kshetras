Sri Mahamariamman Temple began in 1873 as a private family shrine established by K. Thamboosamy Pillai, a Tamil community leader who would later, in 1891, go on to establish the better-known Murugan temple at Batu Caves. Originally sited near Kuala Lumpur's railway station, the shrine moved to its present location on what is now Jalan Tun H.S. Lee in 1885, and the Pillai family opened it to public worship in the late 1920s. It is generally regarded as the oldest functioning Hindu temple in Malaysia and one of the country's most prominent, funded in part by its long-standing reputation as among the wealthiest Hindu temple trusts in the region.

Built in South Indian Dravidian style, the temple's most striking feature is a five-tiered gopuram completed in 1972, rising about 22.9 meters and covered in roughly 228 sculpted figures of deities. It follows the Hindu custom of periodic reconsecration (kumbhabhishekam) approximately every twelve years to renew the sanctity of its shrines. Each year during Thaipusam, the temple serves as the ceremonial starting point for the procession that carries Lord Murugan's image, accompanied by a silver chariot built in 1983, from central Kuala Lumpur to Batu Caves — making it a central node, alongside Batu Caves itself, in Malaysia's largest annual Hindu festival.
