Built entirely of white marble on a raised platform in Jaipur's Tilak Nagar neighborhood near Moti Dungari hill, the Birla Mandir is one of several temples funded across India by the Birla industrial family and today stands as one of Jaipur's most recognizable modern religious landmarks. The B.M. Birla Foundation began construction in 1977 under the direction of Ramanuj Das and Ghanshyam Birla, with Rukmani Devi Birla and Braj Mohan Birla named as the temple's founders, and the completed temple opened to the public on 22 February 1988.

The main sanctum enshrines Lakshmi and Vishnu worshipped together as Lakshmi-Narayan, with Ganesh also honored within the complex; the temple's structure is organized into a sanctum, tower, main hall, and entrance beneath three towers said to represent India's three main faiths, its stained-glass windows depicting scenes from Hindu tradition and its marble surfaces carved with mythological reliefs. In keeping with the ecumenical character the Birla family favored across its temple-building projects, the outer walls also carry depictions of figures revered in other faiths, including Christ, the Virgin Mary, and St. Peter alongside Buddha, Confucius, and Socrates. Local tradition holds that a Maharaja of Jaipur sold the family the land for the temple for a token sum of just one rupee, though this remains a popularly repeated story rather than a documented transaction. Raised on its platform above the surrounding streets, the temple becomes especially prominent after dark, when floodlighting sets it apart in the Jaipur skyline; the complex also houses the underground B.M. Birla Family Museum and the Sri and Smt. G.P. Birla Gallery. Diwali and Janmashtami are the temple's principal festival occasions.
