Small and easy to overlook amid Gurugram's high-rises, this temple in the Bhim Nagar area of Sector 6 is remarkable mainly for who it is dedicated to: Dronacharya, the martial and Vedic instructor of both the Pandava and Kaurava princes in the Mahabharata, rather than to a deity in the conventional sense. Sources describe it as one of only a handful of temples anywhere in India built specifically to honor this figure, reflecting a distinctly local tradition that credits Gurugram's very name — Guru's village — to Dronacharya having lived and taught in the area, a popular etymology that this entry treats as tradition rather than settled linguistic history.

The temple itself has a documented, if modest, history: it was built in 1872 by a devotee named Singha Bharat, and has grown only slightly since — from a single room to two — remaining under the care of five local families rather than any larger trust. It draws some additional footfall from pilgrims visiting the far larger and better-known Sheetla Mata Mandir nearby, whose own founding legend, involving Dronacharya's wife, is closely intertwined with this site's identity.
