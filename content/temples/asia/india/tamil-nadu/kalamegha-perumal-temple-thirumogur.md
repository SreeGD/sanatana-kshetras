Thirumogur, a village on the northeastern outskirts of Madurai, takes its name from a striking episode in Vaishnava legend: to defeat the demon Bhasmasura, Vishnu is said to have assumed the enchanting form of Mohini, and the place where he "lured" (moham) the demon into destruction became Thirumohur, or Thirumogur. The presiding deity, standing and bearing Vishnu's five weapons, is worshipped here as Kalamegha Perumal, "the lord dark as a monsoon cloud."

The temple is glorified in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham by Nammalvar and Thirumangai Alvar, situating it among the Pandya Nadu Divya Desams sung by the poet-saints between the 6th and 9th centuries CE. An inscription recorded in the early 20th century attributes a major building phase, dated to 1259 CE, to a chieftain called Kaala Maegam (also known as Kaangaeyan) during the reign of the Pandya king Sadaiya Varman Sundara Pandiyan, a rare instance among the smaller Divya Desams of a named historical patron tied directly to the presiding deity's own title. The Madurai Nayak rulers, and later the Marudu brothers of Sivaganga, are credited with subsequent additions to the shrine.

Today the temple's Vaikasi Brahmotsavam and Panguni Thirukalyanam remain its principal annual observances, alongside Vaikuntha Ekadasi and Gajendra Moksham festivals, drawing pilgrims from greater Madurai to a shrine whose founding legend, historical patronage, and Alvar hymns together span the full range of the site's documented and traditional history.
