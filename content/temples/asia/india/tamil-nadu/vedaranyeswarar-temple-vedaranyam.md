The Vedaranyeswarar Temple, on the Tamil Nadu coast near Point Calimere in Nagapattinam district, is a Paadal Petra Sthalam praised by all three principal Nayanar poet-saints — Appar, Thirugnanasambandar, and Sundarar — and is noted in some accounts as the only Shiva temple to be mentioned across all seven Thirumurais of the Tamil Shaiva canon. Shiva is worshipped here as Vedaranyeswarar, represented in the sanctum by an emerald lingam known locally as the Maragatha lingam. Devotional tradition holds that the Nayanar saints visited during the reign of the 7th-century Pallava king Mahendravarman I, though the earliest surviving inscriptional evidence documented comes from the later Chola period, leaving the temple's precise founding date unsettled.

The complex is laid out across three prakarams behind twin five-tiered gopurams facing east and west, with a temple tank named Manikarnika. Vedaranyam's coastal, forested setting — the name itself evokes a Vedic forest — has long made the temple and its surrounding tract, including the adjoining Point Calimere wildlife sanctuary, a site of both pilgrimage and ecological significance; the temple's own festival calendar centers on an annual Brahmotsavam and paired observances during the Tamil months of Adi and Thai.
