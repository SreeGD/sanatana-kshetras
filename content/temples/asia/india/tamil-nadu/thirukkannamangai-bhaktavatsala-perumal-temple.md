The Bhaktavatsala Perumal Temple at Thirukkannamangai holds a special place in Sri Vaishnava devotional practice: it is glorified by an unusually generous ten consecutive pasurams — the entire final decad of Thirumangai Alvar's Periya Thirumozhi — traditionally believed to confer moksha (liberation) upon those who recite them. The presiding deity, Bhaktavatsala Perumal ("the lord affectionate to his devotees"), stands facing east in the gesture of blessing, with his consort worshipped as Kannamangai Nachiyar.

According to the temple's sthala purana, Lakshmi performed penance at a spot known as Lakshmi Vanam here in order to win Vishnu as her husband, and their divine wedding is said to have taken place at this very site — the origin of the deity's name. The temple is also venerated as a Chandra Dosha Parihara Sthalam, a site for relief from astrological afflictions associated with the moon, tied to a legend of the moon-god Chandra being freed from a curse here; the temple's sacred tank is separately said to have formed from water that fell from Brahma's ritual vessel while he worshipped Vishnu in his dwarf (Vamana) form.

Historically, three Chola-period inscriptions survive at the temple, and its construction is generally attributed to the late 9th century CE under the Medieval Cholas, with later additions credited to the Thanjavur Nayaks. Sri Vaishnava tradition further holds that the 10th-century devotional figure Tirukanna Mankaiyantan, a disciple of the acharya Nathamuni, was born at this site, and that the composer Muthuswami Dikshitar later wrote verses in praise of the deity — both traditional attributions rather than independently verified historical facts. The temple's principal living festival, a ten-day Brahmotsavam, is celebrated each year in the Tamil month of Chittirai.
