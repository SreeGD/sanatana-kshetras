Siddhesvara Dham, on Solophok hill a short distance from Namchi in South Sikkim district, is a large modern pilgrimage complex built around a 108-foot (33-meter) statue of Shiva, one of the tallest Shiva statues in the eastern Himalayan region. Alongside it stands an 18-foot statue of Kirateshwar — Shiva in the hunter-form he is said to have taken in the Mahabharata's Kirata-Arjuna episode, the same form venerated at Sikkim's older Kirateshwar Mahadev Temple at Legship in West Sikkim — along with scale-model replicas of India's four Char Dham pilgrimage sites (Badrinath, Dwarka, Puri, and Rameswaram) and of the twelve Jyotirlingas, the pan-Indian network of Shiva's most sacred lingam shrines. The effect is to compress much of subcontinental Hindu pilgrimage geography into a single accessible hilltop complex for pilgrims who may not otherwise be able to travel to each individual site.

Unlike most entries in this directory, Siddhesvara Dham is not built around an ancient legend specific to this hill; its significance rests instead on its role as a deliberately constructed, ecumenical devotional and tourism destination reflecting Sikkim's comparatively recent and still-growing Hindu temple architecture. The precise year of its construction or inauguration was not confirmed in the source consulted for this entry and should be treated as an open question for further verification; what is documented is its current function as an active and popular pilgrimage site in South Sikkim.
