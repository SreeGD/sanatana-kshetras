Montreal's Hare Krishna temple traces to March 1967, when Kirtanananda Dasa and Janus Dambergs (later Janardana Dasa), a French-speaking university student, established a center in the city on the personal instruction of A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, founder of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON). By the temple's own account it became the third city in the world outside India — after New York and San Francisco — to house a dedicated Krishna temple, making it one of the movement's earliest and most historically significant outposts. Prabhupada himself visited Montreal twice: an extended three-month stay from August to October 1968, and a single-day visit in the summer of 1975.

Today the temple operates at 1626 Boulevard Pie-IX, where the presiding deities are Sri Sri Radha Manohara, alongside a Govinda's-branded gift shop and vegetarian food counter typical of ISKCON centers worldwide. As with all ISKCON temples, it belongs to the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition and continues Prabhupada's program of public kirtan, scriptural teaching, and prasadam distribution for Montreal's Hindu and broader spiritual community.
