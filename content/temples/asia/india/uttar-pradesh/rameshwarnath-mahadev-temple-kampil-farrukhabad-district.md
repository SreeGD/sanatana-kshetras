Kampil, a small town in Farrukhabad district of Uttar Pradesh, is identified with ancient Kampilya, capital of the Southern Panchala kingdom ruled by King Drupada in the Mahabharata. Tradition holds that the sacrificial altar, the Yajna Kunda, from which Drupada's daughter Draupadi is said to have emerged from the fire of a great sacrifice, still stands at Kampil, along with a nearby hermitage associated with a sage named Kampila. This Mahabharata-era identity -- as the traditional site of Draupadi's birth and, per wider epic tradition, of her swayamvara -- is well documented as belonging to the place itself, though the sources consulted do not name a specific standing temple dedicated to that history; a mound in the town also carries time-eroded Shivlings from a destroyed ancient temple that local legend attributes to the sage Agastya.

The one specifically named, currently-standing Hindu temple documented for Kampil is the Rameshwarnath Mahadeva Temple, dedicated to Shiva, whose own founding legend belongs to a different epic altogether: by local tradition it is attributed to Shatrughna, the youngest of Rama's three brothers, who is said to have brought back and installed here a Shiva lingam that had been worshipped by Sita during her captivity in the Ashoka Vatika in Lanka. This is a devotional attribution rather than a dated historical record, and it is distinct from, not a retelling of, the town's separate Panchala/Draupadi associations.

Kampil is also one of the most important pilgrimage centers in Jainism, traditionally held to be the birthplace of Vimalnath, the 13th Tirthankara, with its own Digambar and Shwetambar Jain temple complexes housing idols reckoned to be roughly 2,600 years old. The Rameshwarnath Mahadeva Temple is a distinct, Hindu, Shaiva site within the same historic town, not part of that Jain heritage, and this entry concerns only the former.
