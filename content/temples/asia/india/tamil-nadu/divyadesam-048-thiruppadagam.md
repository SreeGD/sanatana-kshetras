Thiruppadagam, within the great temple-city of Kanchipuram in Thondai Nadu, is venerated as one of the 108 Divya Desams of Lord Vishnu hymned in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The presiding deity here is the celebrated Pandava Doothar, the Lord enshrined in his role as the messenger and envoy of the Pandavas. According to tradition, this form commemorates Sri Krishna's mission to the court of the Kauravas on behalf of the Pandavas, when he went as a peace ambassador seeking to avert the great war, and the Lord is remembered here in that gracious office of advocate for his devotees. The Moolavar is famed as one of the largest seated images among the Divya Desams, a vast and majestic form of the Lord seated in his royal embassy, which draws the wonder and devotion of pilgrims. The exceptional sanctity of Thiruppadagam is marked by the number of Alwars who sang its praise: among the three foremost Mudhal Alwars, both Bhoothath Alwar and Pey Alwar hymned this shrine, as did the philosopher-saint Thirumazhisai Alwar and the prolific Thirumangai Alwar. This rich confluence of Alwar pasurams underscores its eminence within the corpus and among the Divya Desams of Kanchipuram. To meditate upon Pandava Doothar is to recall the Lord who lowers himself to serve and intercede for those who love him, and Thiruppadagam stands as the abode where this tender truth of his condescension is enshrined for the worship of the faithful.

## Festivals

Pandava Doodha Perumal at Thiruppadagam, where the Lord is revered in His towering seated form as the divine envoy of the Pandavas, traditionally observes the Srivaishnava festival cycle. The annual Brahmotsavam, with the Lord borne on the customary vahanas, is the chief celebration. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the Margazhi month is kept with the recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, and Pavithrotsavam renews the year's worship. As one of the Kanchipuram Divya Desams, it shares in the town's renowned observances such as Garuda Sevai and the chanting of the Tiruvaimozhi, together with Pongal-day offerings and float processions where held, all traditionally observed with devotion.

## Location & Access

The temple is situated at Thiruppadagam within Kanchipuram, in the old Thondai Nadu region of Tamil Nadu. Kanchipuram is an accessible temple town connected by road and rail and lies within easy reach of Chennai, the nearest major city and transport hub. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours, closing for a period at midday.
