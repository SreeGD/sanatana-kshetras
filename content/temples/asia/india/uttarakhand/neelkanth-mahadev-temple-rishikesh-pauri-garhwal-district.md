Neelkanth Mahadev takes its name from the Puranic account of the Samudra Manthan, the churning of the cosmic ocean by gods and demons in search of amrita, the nectar of immortality. Among the things the churning brought up was Halahala, a poison potent enough to destroy creation; to save the universe, Shiva drank it himself, and his throat turned blue as a result — nila kantha, "the blue-throated one." Tradition holds that Shiva then withdrew to this hillside above Rishikesh and meditated beneath a tree for a vast span of time, popularly given as 60,000 years, to absorb the poison's effects, and that the site has been sacred to him ever since.

The temple stands about 32 km from Rishikesh in Pauri Garhwal district, its tiered gopura-style tower showing Dravidian architectural influence unusual for the Garhwal Himalayas, with carvings of the Samudra Manthan story worked into the shikhara. It draws large crowds on Maha Shivaratri and throughout the Shravan month, when Kanwar pilgrims carrying Ganga water on foot from Haridwar make it a stop on their route. No construction date for the present structure survives in the source checked here, so its architectural history is not established in this entry.
