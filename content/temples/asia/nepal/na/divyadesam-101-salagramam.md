Thiruchaalagiraamam, identified with the sacred site of Salagramam, is counted among the 108 Divya Desams as one of the venerated northern abodes of Vishnu, lying in the high Himalayan region beyond the Tamil country. The presiding deity is worshipped as Mukti Narayanar, the Lord who bestows liberation, the very name proclaiming this as a place sanctified for the granting of moksha to His devotees. The kshetra takes its name from the salagrama, the naturally sacred stone revered in the Srivaishnava tradition as a self-manifest form of Vishnu, and the region near the source of its holy river is held to be the abode from which such sacred stones arise. According to tradition, the Lord is present here in a form especially associated with the deliverance of souls, and the place is approached by pilgrims as a remote yet profoundly auspicious destination. Periyalwar and Thirumangai Alwar sang of this northern shrine in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and through their hymns the distant Himalayan abode entered the sacred corpus revered by the Srivaishnava community. As one of the Divya Desams set far in the Vada Nadu, Thiruchaalagiraamam, with its presiding Mukti Narayanar, embodies the tradition's teaching that the grace of liberation flows from the Lord wherever He chooses to abide, drawing the faithful even to the snow-bound heights of the north in search of His redeeming presence.
