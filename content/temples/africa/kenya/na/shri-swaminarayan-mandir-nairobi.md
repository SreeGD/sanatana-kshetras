Nairobi's Shri Swaminarayan Mandir serves as the East African Swaminarayan Sanstha's (EASS) principal temple in Kenya's capital, one of the country's more than fifteen documented Hindu temples and among several Swaminarayan-tradition mandirs that Gujarati Hindu communities established across East Africa during the twentieth century. The temple's congregation descends largely from Gujarati families who arrived in Kenya from the late 1800s through the early 1900s, many originally brought by British colonial authorities as laborers on the Uganda-Kenya railway before settling permanently and building trading and religious institutions across the country.

As with Swaminarayan mandirs elsewhere, the Nairobi temple centers its worship on murtis of Swaminarayan (Bhagwan Swaminarayan, born Ghanshyam Pande, 1781-1830) alongside associated saints of the sampradaya, and functions as both a place of daily worship and a hub for Nairobi's Gujarati Hindu community's cultural and social life, particularly around Diwali and the Annakut (Hindu New Year) celebrations.
