Sri Deepaprakasar Perumal Temple, known by the names Tiruththanka and Thoopul after its locality in Kanchipuram, enshrines Vishnu as Deepaprakasar ("the one who is light itself"), also called Vilakoli Perumal in Tamil (vilakku-oli, "the glow of a lamp"). Temple legend holds that Vishnu manifested here as a radiant light to dispel darkness and grant a devotee's wish, giving the deity his distinctive name. The temple is one of the 108 Divya Desams, glorified by Thirumangai Alvar in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham.

The site holds special significance for Sri Vaishnavas as the birthplace (avatara sthalam) of the philosopher-poet Vedanta Desika (traditionally dated 1268–1369 CE), one of the most influential acharyas of the Vadakalai school of Sri Vaishnavism, who is consequently also known as "Thoopul Vedanta Desikan." A dedicated shrine to Vedanta Desika stands within the temple complex alongside shrines for Lakshmi-Hayagriva, Andal, and the Alvars.

Architecturally the temple has a three-tiered rajagopuram and follows Dravidian conventions; it was expanded during the Medieval Chola and Vijayanagara periods, and carries inscriptions dating to the reign of Rajaraja Chola III (circa 1223 CE). Its most prominent living festival is the annual Brahmotsavam in the Tamil month of Puratasi, during which the festival image of Vedanta Desika is ceremonially carried to the nearby Varadaraja Perumal Temple, symbolically linking the two shrines and commemorating the acharya's devotion to Kanchipuram's presiding deity.
