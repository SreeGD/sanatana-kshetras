Julfa Mata's temple at Nangal, close to the Punjab-Himachal Pradesh border, is counted among the 51 Shakti Peethas — the network of sites across the subcontinent tradition associates with the scattering of the goddess Sati's body after her death and Shiva's grief-stricken wandering with her remains. Here the temple's name itself encodes the local tradition: "julfa" means hair in the regional language, and this is held to be the spot where a lock of the goddess's hair is said to have fallen.

Unlike the temple's founding legend, its present-day character is straightforwardly documented: it is an active shrine with a resident priest, an image of the goddess in the main sanctum, an adjoining shrine to Shiva, and a sacred peepal tree where devotees tie threads seeking blessings — a common feature at regional Shakti shrines across North India. Devotees visit with offerings of sweets, puffed rice, coconut, and flowers, with Navratri and the monsoon month of Sawan drawing particularly heavy attendance. No construction date or founder is documented in the sources available for this entry, so the temple's institutional history prior to its current active life remains unclear.
