The Avinashiappar Temple, in the town of Avinashi in Tiruppur district, is counted among the seven Paadal Petra Sthalams of the Kongu Nadu region per Wikipedia's regional enumeration of the canon, making it this directory's representative of a part of Tamil Nadu with comparatively thin English-language temple documentation. Shiva is worshipped here as Avinashiappar, "the indestructible one," also known as Avinashilingeshwarar; the temple and town are also referred to by the older names Tiruppukkozhiyur and, in inscriptions, "Dakshina Varanasi" (the Varanasi of the South). Construction is attributed to the Pandya king Sundarapandiya, and the site carries an associated tradition connecting it to the Nayanar saint Sundaramoorthy (Sundarar).

The temple is a protected monument under the Archaeological Survey of India, and is also cited in the Tirumantiram of Tirumular, the Tiruppugazh of Arunagirinathar, and Manikkavasakar's Tiruvasakam — though this entry notes, rather than asserts as certain, its specific Tevaram authorship, since the temple's own Wikipedia article does not itself name a Tevaram verse or author. Its best-known living tradition is the annual Chittirai Brahmotsavam, when a wooden processional chariot — reckoned the second-largest in India after Tiruvarur's — is hauled through the town; the chariot procession was historically observed over ten days and is now typically held over two. The temple calendar also includes an Arubathi Moovar (63 Nayanmaars) Ula procession honouring the Nayanar saints.
