Unlike the diaspora community temples elsewhere in this directory, Pura Tri Hita Karana sits inside a public park: the Balinese Garden within Berlin's Gärten der Welt (Gardens of the World) complex in the eastern district of Marzahn. The garden, one of several themed international gardens added to the park over the 2000s, opened on 18 December 2003 and includes a functioning Hindu temple built in the pura style of Bali, Indonesia — complete with the split candi bentar gateways, multi-tiered meru shrines, and thatched or tiled roofs characteristic of Balinese temple architecture. It is cited as one of only a handful of Balinese-style Hindu temples built outside Indonesia.

The temple serves Berlin's small Balinese Hindu community, whose Agama Hindu Dharma tradition blends Shaiva philosophical roots with distinctly Balinese village, ancestral, and nature-worship practices — placing it outside the mainstream Sanskritic sects more typical of temples elsewhere in this directory. Because it functions inside a municipal garden rather than as a standalone community-run institution, its access, opening hours, and ceremonial calendar are tied to the park's own seasonal operation, and detailed information about its founding congregation and maintaining organization is thinner in public sources than for most other temples in this directory.
