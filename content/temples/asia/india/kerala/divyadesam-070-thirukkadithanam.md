The Adbhuta Narayanan Temple at Thirukkadithanam, in the Malai Nadu (Kerala) country, enshrines the Lord Vishnu as Adbhuta Narayanan, the "Wonderful One," and is counted among the 108 Divya Desams revered in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The presiding deity is worshipped here in a serene, gracious form, and the shrine is reckoned among the cluster of sacred abodes lying along the Pamba river region of Kerala, several of which are associated in tradition with the five Pandava brothers, who are held to have installed and worshipped Vishnu at these places during their forest sojourn. Thirukkadithanam is traditionally linked in this manner with the worship of the Lord, the name Adbhuta Narayanan itself evoking the marvellous and grace-filled aspect of the Supreme One who dwells here for the redemption of His devotees. The glory of this Divya Desam was sung by Nammalwar, foremost of the Alwars, in his Tiruvaaymozhi, and by Thirumangai Alwar, the prince among the poet-saints, whose hymns secure the temple's place in the canonical corpus of the Tamil Prabandham. To be praised by these Alwars marks Thirukkadithanam as a place of high sanctity, drawing pilgrims who seek the darshan of Adbhuta Narayanan. As with the other Malai Nadu shrines, the temple preserves the distinctive architectural and ritual character of Kerala's Vaishnava tradition, while its standing as a Divya Desam binds it to the wider devotional world of the Alwars and the Sri Vaishnava sampradaya.
