Ghatagaon, in Odisha's Keonjhar (Kendujhar) district near the Baitarani river, is home to Maa Tarini, a goddess built in Kalinga-style temple architecture and popularly regarded across Odisha as the presiding deity of Shakti and Tantra worship in the state. Her image is distinctive: a red face with large eyes and a single central mark doing double duty as nose and tilak, the red coloring traditionally linked in local tradition to iron-oxide pigments long used by the region's tribal communities — a detail suggesting roots in tribal or folk devotion that were later absorbed into the wider Shakta tradition, which this entry reflects by tagging both `shakta` and `folk_regional`.

Devotional tradition, set "near about 1475 CE," recounts that King Purushottama Deva of Kalinga sent his general Govinda Bhanja marching toward Kanchi at the head of an army after a dispute over a marriage proposal to a South Indian princess; near the present temple site, the general's horse is said to have refused to move further, understood as a sign that the goddess herself wished to remain there rather than accompany the campaign. This story survives mainly through devotional poetic works about the goddess rather than independent historical record, and the source consulted for this entry explicitly notes that historians still debate the exact sequence of events for that reason — this entry accordingly treats the date and the specific royal association as tradition rather than a settled founding date. (Purushottama Deva is separately known to history as a Gajapati-dynasty ruler of Odisha, though the source used here does not itself draw that connection.)

The temple remains a major, actively worshipped Shakti pilgrimage site today, with a festival calendar including the April Chaiti and Patua Yatras and the Ashadhi Parba. Large-animal sacrifice, once part of temple practice, is now banned, though goat sacrifice reportedly continues at some of the temple's more remote associated shrines.
