Garhmukteshwar, a Ganga-bank town in Hapur district, Uttar Pradesh, takes its very name from a temple: Mukteshwar Mahadeva, a Shiva shrine whose worship here is bound up with that of the river goddess Ganga herself, honored locally across four temples rather than a single structure. The town's roots reach deep into epic tradition -- it is traditionally linked to ancient Hastinapur, capital of the Pandavas, and is named in both the Bhagavata Purana and the Mahabharata -- and its long historical layering shows in other ways too: eighty sati pillars still mark the town, alongside a 13th-century mosque built under the Delhi Sultanate ruler Ghiyas-ud-din Balban, evidence of the site's continuous occupation and significance across centuries and communities.

Sitting about five kilometers from the Ganges and roughly a hundred kilometers from Delhi along National Highway 9, Garhmukteshwar remains an active riverside pilgrimage town distinct from the Ganga's much larger downstream centers at Haridwar, Prayagraj, and Varanasi already documented elsewhere in this directory -- a reminder that the river's sanctity is marked out at many points along its course, not only at its most famous ones.
