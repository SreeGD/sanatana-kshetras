The Virudhagiriswarar Temple in Virudhachalam, Cuddalore district, is a Paadal Petra Sthalam associated with the Nayanar saint Sundarar, who is recorded as having sung of the presiding deity here. Shiva is worshipped as Pazhamalai Nadhar, also known as Virudhagiriswarar, alongside his consort Viruthambigai Amman. Tradition credits the Chola queen Sembiyan Mahadevi, a noted temple patron of the 10th century, with commissioning the shrine, placing the temple within the broader Chola architectural period spanning roughly the 9th to 12th centuries.

Among the temple's notable features is a hundred-pillared mandapam carved to resemble a chariot, and a Vanni tree on the grounds said to be around 1,700 years old. The complex, spread across five gopurams in Dravidian style, remains an active site of worship under the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Board, and underwent a kumbabhishekam (temple consecration) as recently as February 2022. Its principal annual festival is the Brahmotsavam held during Masi Magam, in the Tamil month of Masi (February/March).
