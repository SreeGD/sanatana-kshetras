Bhavishya Badri — the "future Badri" — is one of the five Panch Badri shrines, set in dense forest at Subain (Subhain) village beyond Tapovan, on an old pilgrim route that once continued toward Mount Kailash and Lake Manasarovar along the Dhauli Ganga. Tradition holds that as the world declines, the mountains of Nara and Narayan will eventually close off the route to the main Badrinath temple, and worship of Vishnu will then shift permanently to this site, making it the Badrinath of a future age. A related tradition, tied to the nearby Narsingh Badri shrine at Joshimath, holds that a stone image's arm is slowly thinning over time and that its eventual disappearance will mark the transition. These are prophetic and devotional traditions rather than documented history; the deity currently worshipped at Bhavishya Badri itself is Narasimha, the lion-faced avatar of Vishnu.

Reaching the shrine requires a drive from Joshimath through Tapovan to the roadhead at Saldhar, followed by a forest trek of roughly six kilometers. Its remoteness and forest setting mean the temple sees far fewer pilgrims than Badrinath itself, and access is realistically confined to the same broad fair-weather window — May/June and September/October — that governs the rest of the region's high-altitude shrines.
