The Saranatha Perumal Temple at Thirucherai, on the Kumbakonam-Thiruvarur road, is traditionally described as the only Divya Desam depicting Vishnu with five consorts together — Sridevi, Bhudevi, Neeladevi, Mahalakshmi, and a fifth goddess named in different sources as Sara Nayagi or Saranayaki. The presiding deity, Saranathan, stands roughly twelve feet tall facing east, with the principal consort shrine dedicated to Saranayaki Thayar.

The temple carries an unusually rich cluster of legends. One holds that Brahma, before the onset of cosmic dissolution (Mahapralaya), used sand from this very place to fashion a protective vessel for the Vedas. Another, more central to the site's identity, tells of the river-goddess Kaveri performing penance here; Vishnu appeared to her — in some tellings as a child in her arms — and revealed his ten avatars, granting her precedence over even the Ganga among sacred rivers. This legend is commemorated by a rare dedicated shrine to Kaveri herself, depicted maternally with a child, and by the Thaipoosam festival, during which Saranathan is processed as a child in his mother Kaveri's arms in a decorated chariot. A separate foundational anecdote credits a royal minister, Narasa Boopalan, with diverting temple-construction stone originally intended for a project in Mannargudi in order to build this temple quickly and earn a king's pardon — a story with no inscriptional support.

On more solid historical ground, an inscription of the Chola king Parakesari Varman, dated to the early 10th century CE, documents patronage of the temple, and a later 18th-century Maratha-era inscription records further royal involvement; the temple's sculptural details, including crown styles showing Buddhist-tradition influence and Chola-period metalwork, attest to centuries of continuous artistic investment. The temple is glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by Thirumangai Alvar, and today is administered by the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Board. Its principal annual festival, a 12-day Brahmotsavam, is held in the Tamil month of Thai.
