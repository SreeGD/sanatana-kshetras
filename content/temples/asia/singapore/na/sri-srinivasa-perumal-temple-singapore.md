Sri Srinivasa Perumal Temple, in Singapore's Little India, traces its origins to land acquired for a Hindu temple in 1851, with construction completed in 1855. It was originally consecrated to Narasimha Perumal, the man-lion avatar of Vishnu, but on the advice of temple elders the principal deity was later changed to Srinivasa Perumal, a form of Vishnu also worshipped elsewhere as Venkateswara/Balaji; subsidiary shrines within the temple also honor Lakshmi, Andal, and Garuda. Its most striking feature, a five-tiered gopuram depicting scenes from Vishnu's various incarnations, was donated by the early Indian community leader and philanthropist P. Govindasamy Pillai and completed in 1966 at a reported cost of about S$300,000.

Gazetted a National Monument of Singapore on 10 November 1978, the temple remains an active center of Vaishnavite worship in the city-state. It is also the traditional starting point of Singapore's annual Thaipusam procession, in which devotees carry kavadi in fulfillment of vows on their walk to the Sri Thendayuthapani Temple.
