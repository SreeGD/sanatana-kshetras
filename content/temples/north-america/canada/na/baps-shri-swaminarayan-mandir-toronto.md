Built on a 7.3-hectare site in northwest Toronto, this mandir was raised in stages: a foundation stone ceremony in July 2000, an adjoining haveli (cultural and community hall) completed in 2004, and the main shikharbaddha temple itself consecrated in July 2007 following construction methods drawn from the Shilpa Shastras, the traditional Hindu architectural texts. As with other BAPS mandirs of this style, its stonework — tens of thousands of pieces of hand-carved marble, limestone, and sandstone — was carved by craftsmen in India and Turkey before being assembled on site without structural steel. At its 2007 opening it was described as the largest traditional Hindu mandir of its kind in Canada, and it has since become a landmark stop for visiting dignitaries, including a 2017 tenth-anniversary event attended by Prime Minister Justin Trudeau. The central shrine houses Bhagwan Swaminarayan alongside Gunatitanand Swami, with secondary shrines for Radha-Krishna, Sita-Rama, Shiva-Parvati, Ganesha, and Hanuman, serving the Greater Toronto Area's large Gujarati and broader Hindu community.
