Maluti, a village in Jharkhand's Dumka district near the West Bengal border, was once home to as many as 108 terracotta temples commissioned under the Baj Basanta ("Rai") dynasty -- local rulers whose rise to power is remembered through a folk legend in which the founding raja recovered a lost pet hawk belonging to the Bengal Sultan Alauddin Husain Shah and was rewarded with the kingdom around the turn of the 16th century. Roughly 72 of those temples survive standing today, built not in a single classical Indian architectural idiom but by specialist Bengali artisans working across at least five distinct terracotta styles -- a decorative tradition closely related to, though smaller-scale and less internationally known than, the terracotta temple heritage of Bishnupur in West Bengal, already documented elsewhere in this directory.

The dynasty's own chosen deity, Ma Mauliksha, presides over the cluster's principal shrine and remains in regular worship even as many of the surrounding subsidiary temples stand empty and increasingly fragile; one scholarly account traces her name and iconography to a Vajrayana Buddhist goddess called Pandora, later absorbed into Hindu tantric practice, though this layered history is noted here rather than independently verified. Maluti's terracotta cluster is also, unusually for an entry in this directory, as much a conservation story as a devotional one: the Global Heritage Fund listed it in 2010 among twelve sites worldwide judged nearest to irreparable loss for want of adequate management, a status that makes the temples that do survive here -- and the worship still conducted at Ma Mauliksha's shrine -- feel especially precarious.
