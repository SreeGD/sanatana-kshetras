Consecrated on June 20, 2010 in a ceremony drawing more than 10,000 devotees and using waters gathered from over 250 rivers around the world, the Sringeri Temple of Toronto is a rare North American temple dedicated principally to Goddess Sharadamba — a form of Saraswati, goddess of learning and the arts — rather than to one of the more commonly enshrined deities among diaspora temples. Then-Prime Minister Stephen Harper attended the consecration, calling the $11 million, 30,000-square-foot complex in Etobicoke "a testament to Canada's and India's proud traditions of pluralism."

The temple is modeled architecturally on the 8th-century Sringeri Sharadamba Temple in Karnataka, India — the seat of the first of the four cardinal mathas (monastic institutions) established by the philosopher-saint Adi Shankaracharya — and operates under the Sringeri Vidya Bharati Foundation (SVBF Canada), affiliated with the sitting Acharya of the Sringeri Sharada Peetham lineage. Its features include a 30-foot gopuram tower, an 18-foot mandapam hall, a ceremonial passageway depicting scenes from Adi Shankaracharya's life, eleven subsidiary shrines, an Adi Shankara museum, a library, and a Vedic-studies school offering free classes — making it as much an institutional seat of the Smarta/Advaita Vedanta tradition in Canada as a place of daily worship.
