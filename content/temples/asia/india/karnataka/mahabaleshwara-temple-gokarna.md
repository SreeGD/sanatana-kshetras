Mahabaleshwara Temple sits in the coastal pilgrim town of Gokarna in Karnataka's Uttara Kannada district, at a site Shaiva tradition regards as one of the most sacred in South India because of the object it enshrines: not a conventional carved lingam but a Pranalinga, or Atmalinga, a stone fixed into a pedestal with only a small aperture through which it can be viewed. Puranic legend holds that the demon king Ravana won this Atmalinga from Shiva through severe penance, on condition that it never touch the ground before reaching Lanka; Ganesha, disguised as a Brahmin boy, intercepted Ravana at Gokarna and, once handed the linga to hold during Ravana's evening rituals, set it down — after which it could not be lifted again, fixing Shiva's presence permanently at this spot.

The present temple structure is credited by tradition and by inscriptional/literary evidence to the Kadamba dynasty king Mayuravarma in the 4th century CE, raised over what was already regarded as a sanctified linga; the classical poet Kalidasa's reference to "the Lord of Gokarna" in his Raghuvamsha is cited as corroborating the site's importance by around that same era. Built in Dravidian style from granite, with gopura towers and a pradakshina path for circumambulation, the temple today draws its largest crowds at Maha Shivaratri, when a chariot bearing the deity is drawn in procession through Gokarna's streets — a festival that, alongside the temple's location on a scenic stretch of the Arabian Sea coast, has made Gokarna both a major Shaiva pilgrimage center and a well-known beach town in its own right.
