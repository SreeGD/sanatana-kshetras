The Gombeshwar Shiva Temple sits inside a set of natural caves at Nyali beach on Mombasa's coast, where several lingams - naturally occurring rock formations worshipped as manifestations of Shiva - are said to have formed. Local Hindu tradition holds that herders first discovered the site after noticing that one of their cows would wander to the same spot on the hills each day and release her milk there; puzzled, they consulted Hindu families in the area, and a subsequent dig at the spot is said to have uncovered the cave and its self-manifest lingam. Whatever the shrine's exact origins, it has become an established pilgrimage and worship site for Mombasa's Hindu community, run today under the umbrella of the Hindu Union of Mombasa, a community organization documented as having been founded in 1899 to serve the coastal city's Indian-origin Hindus.

Devotees visit the cave temple to offer milk to the lingam, a practice tied directly to the herding legend and to Shiva's broader iconographic association with cattle through his vehicle, Nandi the bull. The site remains active today alongside other Mombasa temples under Hindu Union management, including a separate Shivalaay shrine at Makadara, reflecting the long-established and still-active Hindu community along Kenya's coast.
