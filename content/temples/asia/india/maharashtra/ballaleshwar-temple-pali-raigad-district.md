Unique among the eight Ashtavinayak shrines for being named after a human devotee rather than a demon or epithet of Ganesha himself. Tradition holds that a boy named Ballal, absorbed in devotion to Ganesha to the point of neglecting his studies, was tied to a tree and abandoned in the forest by his exasperated father; Ganesha appeared, freed him, and — at Ballal's request — agreed to remain at the site permanently as Ballaleshwar. The temple faces east and is built largely of black stone.
