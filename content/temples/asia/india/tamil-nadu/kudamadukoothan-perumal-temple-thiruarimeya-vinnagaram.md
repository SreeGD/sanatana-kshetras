The name of this Thirunangur shrine, Thiruarimeya Vinnagaram, is explained locally as "the celestial abode where Hari (Ari) resides," and its presiding deity carries an equally distinctive name: Kudamadukoothan, "the one who dances the pot-dance." According to temple tradition, Vishnu revealed himself here performing the kuda koothu, a classical dance form in which the performer remains largely still while conveying meaning through expression and gesture, balancing or manipulating a pot as part of the performance - an unusual and specific iconographic identity among the Divya Desams. The deity is worshipped together with his consort, named in temple tradition as Amirtagadavalli.

This shrine's inclusion among the 108 Divya Desams rests on the mangalasasanam (verses of blessing) composed here by Thirumangai Alvar, part of his extended hymn-cycle across the Chola Nadu shrines preserved in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. As one of the eleven Thirunangur temples, it participates each year in the Thirumangai Alvar Mangalasasana Utsavam during the Tamil month of Thai, when its festival deity travels with the other ten in the Garudasevai procession, the collective celebration that unites the Thirunangur cluster.
