The Azhagiya Manavala Perumal Temple at Uraiyur, known in the Divya Prabandham as Thirukkozhi, stands in one of the oldest continuously inhabited urban sites in Tamil Nadu. Uraiyur was the ancient capital of the Sangam-era Cholas, and archaeological excavations here have uncovered Roman rouletted pottery and a dyeing vat from a 1st-to-4th-century-CE settlement layer — solid evidence of Uraiyur's importance as a trade and textile center in early historic South India, independent of the temple's own construction date. The presiding deity, Azhagiya Manavalan ("the beautiful bridegroom"), stands facing north, an orientation unusual among Divya Desams, said to signify his gaze toward Ranganatha at Srirangam a few kilometers away.

The temple's founding legend concerns the goddess Kamalavalli Nachiyar, said to have been born from a lotus and raised as a princess before choosing Vishnu as her consort; local tradition differs on the details — one telling names a childless Chola king who prayed for an heir and received Kamalavalli as a lotus-born daughter later given in marriage to Ranganatha, while another version names a different royal patron and has the princess choose Vishnu herself in a swayamvara. Whichever the precise telling, the marriage of Kamalavalli to the Lord of Srirangam is commemorated every year at the Panguni Uthiram festival, when the processional deity travels from Uraiyur to Srirangam in a re-enactment of the wedding journey — one of the most distinctive living rituals among the Chola Nadu Divya Desams. The goddess retains an unusual ritual prominence at this temple, including precedence in certain processions.

The temple is glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by Kulasekara Alvar and Thirumangai Alvar, and Uraiyur is also traditionally regarded as the birthplace of Thiruppaan Alvar, one of the twelve Alvar saints, though this is a distinct biographical tradition rather than a hymn of glorification. While the general antiquity of Uraiyur as a settlement is well documented archaeologically, the present temple structure's own construction history is less firmly dated in available sources, and is conventionally — though not conclusively — attributed to the Medieval Chola period with later renovations.
