Orchha's Ram Raja Temple is unusual among Rama temples for where it stands: not in a structure purpose-built as a temple, but inside what was once the royal palace (Ranivaas) of the Bundela kings, in the queen's own private quarters. Temple legend attributes this to Queen Ganesh Kunwari, also known as Kamla Devi, wife of the Bundela ruler Madhukar Shah (who reigned 1554-1592). She is said to have walked to Ayodhya and undertaken fasting and austerities on the banks of the Sarayu River to obtain a child form of Rama, then carried the deity back to Orchha over a journey of nearly nine months. According to the story, when a grand new Chaturbhuj temple was built nearby to house the deity, the image could not be moved from the queen's palace where it had first been installed — so the palace itself became, and remains, the temple.

That accident of devotion is why Ram Raja Temple carries a distinction found nowhere else in the Rama tradition: because the deity effectively "lives" in a royal residence, Rama is worshipped here not simply as god but as a reigning king, complete with a daily ceremonial guard-of-honour salute performed with the same protocol once due to Orchha's human rulers. The sanctum's image shows Rama seated with a sword and shield rather than his usual bow, flanked by Sita and Lakshmana, with Sugriva and Narasimha also present. The temple remains one of the region's most visited sites, particularly around Rama Navami, Makar Sankranti, and Vivaha Panchami (commemorating Rama and Sita's wedding), and devotees still look for a sandalwood mark said to appear on the deity's left toe, believed to grant wishes.
