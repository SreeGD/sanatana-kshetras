Naina Devi is worshipped on the shore of Naini Lake, the body of water that gives Nainital its name and, by tradition, its founding legend: when Shiva carried Sati's body across the sky in grief and Vishnu's discus divided it to end his destructive wandering, her eye (nain) is said to have fallen at this spot, making the lake itself one of the Shakti Peethas and the temple its point of worship.

The temple seen today is not the original structure. On 18 September 1880 a major landslide struck Nainital, killing roughly 151 people and destroying a number of buildings around the lake, including the temple that had stood there. It was rebuilt afterward on the lake's north shore, where it remains an active and prominent shrine in the town center, drawing its largest crowds each September for the Nanda Devi Mela around Nandashtami. No date survives in the source checked here for when the original, pre-1880 temple was first built.
