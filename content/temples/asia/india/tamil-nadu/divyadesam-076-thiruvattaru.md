The Adikesava Perumal Temple at Thiruvattaru (Thiruvattar), set amid the waters and greenery of the Malai Nadu region in present-day Kanyakumari district, Tamil Nadu (near, but not across, the Kerala border), is revered as one of the 108 Divya Desams, the sacred abodes of Lord Vishnu celebrated by the Alwars, and specifically as one of the historic thirteen Divya Desams of Malai Nadu. The presiding deity, the Moolavar, is Adikesava Perumal, who is enshrined in a majestic reclining posture (sayana thirukkolam), his vast form filling the sanctum so that devotees behold him through three doorways; his consort is worshipped here as Maragathavalli Thaayaar. The temple is bounded on three sides by rivers (the Kothai, Pahrali and Thamirabarani) and stands as one of the foremost Vishnu sanctuaries of the southern Malai Nadu, closely linked in tradition and proximity to the great Padmanabhaswamy shrine, and is traditionally styled "the Srirangam of the Chera Kingdom." According to temple inscriptions dated to 779 KE (Kollam Era), the shrine's founding is traditionally placed in the Treta Yuga and its consecration attributed to the sage Parasurama, with the temple also mentioned in the Padma Purana. Vijayanagara-era kings made further additions in the early 1500s, and the flag mast was renovated in 1071 KE by Travancore's Moolam Thirunal Rama Varma VI, reflecting the temple's historic role as the Rajya Temple and Bharadevatha (state) shrine of the erstwhile Travancore kingdom. The temple's Dravidian-Kerala fusion architecture features wooden pillars, doors and roofing, a thirty-foot-high fort wall, and an outer corridor of 224 stone pillars. Among the Alwar saints it is Nammalwar, the foremost of the Alwars and author of the Tiruvaymoli, who sang the glory of this divine abode in eleven pasurams of the sixth Thiruvaimozhi, enshrining it forever within the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Through his hymns the Lord of Thiruvattaru is established among the celebrated Divya Desams, drawing pilgrims who seek the grace of the reclining Perumal whose serene and immense form embodies the protective repose of the Supreme in the sanctified waters of this ancient land.

## Festivals

As a celebrated Divya Desam set amid the lush Malai Nadu country, Thiruvattaru traditionally observes the great Vaishnava festival cycle around its reclining Lord Adikesava Perumal. Vaikuntha Ekadasi in the Tamil month of Margazhi is among the holiest occasions, when devotees gather for special darshan and the Paramapada Vasal observance customary at major Perumal temples. Krishna Janmashtami is also observed with particular devotion. A principal annual Brahmotsavam is kept with grand processions of the utsava deity around the temple and town, accompanied by recitation of the Tamil Nalayira Divya Prabandham, alongside the customary Pavithrotsavam purification festival and a Float festival (Theppotsavam). Prasadams distinctive to the temple include paal payasam (milk kheer), aval (beaten rice) and appam.

## Location & Access

The temple stands in Thiruvattar village in the Kanniyakumari (Kanyakumari) district of the old Malai Nadu region of Tamil Nadu, near the river country at roughly 8°19'47"N, 77°15'57"E (8.330, 77.266 in decimal degrees per Wikipedia; this entry's dataset coordinates are close but not identical). It lies within reach of Nagercoil and the major hub of Kanniyakumari, with Thiruvananthapuram being the nearest large city and transport centre. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday closure.
