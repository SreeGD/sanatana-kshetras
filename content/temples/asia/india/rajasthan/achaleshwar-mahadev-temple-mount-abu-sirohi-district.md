Set just outside Achalgarh Fort on Mount Abu, this Shiva temple is unusual in that its sanctum centers on a toe-print rather than a linga, alongside a brass image of Nandi. Nearby, three stone buffalo figures stand around a small pond, a feature local pilgrimage tradition connects to a legend of buffalo-formed demons troubling a sacred tank until subdued by Shiva — an account that also explains "Achal," meaning immovable, though the fuller narrative was not independently confirmed in the sources checked here and should be read as oral tradition rather than settled history.

The fort at Achalgarh has roots going back to an earlier structure associated with the Paramara dynasty, and was substantially rebuilt and given its present name by Maharana Kumbha of Mewar, a prolific hill-fort builder, in around 1452 CE. A separate source gives 1412 as the temple's own construction date, a figure that sits awkwardly before Kumbha's reign even began and could not be reconciled with the 1452 fort-naming date within the material checked for this entry; the temple's precise founding chronology is therefore treated here as unresolved rather than settled.
