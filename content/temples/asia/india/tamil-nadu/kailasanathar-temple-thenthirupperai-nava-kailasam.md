Thenthirupperai, a village in the Thamirabarani river belt of Thoothukudi (Tuticorin) district near Alwarthirunagari, is the seventh of the nine Nava Kailasam temples and is associated with Mercury (Buthan) among the "planets" in that group's tradition — a distinct tradition from the separate Navagraha temple circuit near Kumbakonam further north in Tamil Nadu.

The presiding deity is worshipped here as Kailasanathar, in keeping with the naming shared by most temples in the group. As with the wider Nava Kailasam group, tradition holds that the nine sites trace to the sage Agastya's disciple Uromacha Munivar, instructed to set nine flowers afloat on the Thamirabarani and to install a Shiva lingam wherever each one came to rest, attaining liberation after venerating all nine.

This same village is also home to one of the 108 Divya Desams of the Sri Vaishnava tradition, the Magara Nedungkuzhaikkaadhar temple (recorded elsewhere in this directory), a pattern repeated at several other Nava Kailasam sites along the Thamirabarani where a Shiva and a Vishnu shrine of separate traditions share a village. This entry concerns only the Shiva Kailasanathar temple of the Nava Kailasam group; no source consulted suggests the two are the same shrine. Beyond the group-level listing, no independent source describing this Shiva temple's own history, architecture, or precise location could be located during research, so this entry is marked `pending`.
