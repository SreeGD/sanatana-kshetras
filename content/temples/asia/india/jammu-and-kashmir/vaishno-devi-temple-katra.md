Set in a cave shrine in the Trikuta hills, Vaishno Devi is among the most visited pilgrimage sites in India, drawing millions of devotees annually. The goddess is worshipped here in the form of three natural rock formations (pindies) representing Mahakali, Mahalakshmi, and Mahasaraswati. Local tradition credits a 10th-century Brahmin priest, Pandit Shridhar, with discovering the cave shrine, originally associated with a deity called "Trikuta Devi." The shrine's popularity has grown enormously since the Shri Mata Vaishno Devi Shrine Board was established in 1986, taking over administration from the hereditary Dharmarth Trust and modernizing pilgrim infrastructure; annual visitor numbers rose from roughly 900,000 in 1981 to over 9.5 million by 2023.
