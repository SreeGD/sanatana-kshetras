Sri Vrindavan Dham is the name used by the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON) community in Almaty, Kazakhstan's commercial capital, for its temple complex. The community's own website lists two locations under this community: a temple at Mereyy Street 1 in Almaty city, which publishes a full daily schedule of arati and classes, and a farm-based "Iskcon Temple" in Ptitsevodstvo village, Karasai District, in the surrounding Almaty Region.

The Karasai District site has a documented and difficult history. According to English-language Wikipedia articles sourced substantially from US State Department and OSCE-era reporting, Hare Krishna followers purchased land there in 1999 from a farmer who, it later emerged, did not hold clear title to it. Kazakhstan officially registered ISKCON as a religious organization in 2002. Beginning around 2004, the Karasai district government pursued a campaign to reclaim the land, culminating in the demolition of thirteen homes at the commune on November 20, 2006, by riot police and bulldozers in sub-zero weather -- an action that international observers and Wikipedia's own account describe as bearing signs of religious targeting, though Kazakh officials characterized it purely as a land-title dispute. A further round of demolitions followed on June 15, 2007. Sources available to this entry do not confirm the community's precise legal or physical status in Karasai District in the years since, though the community's own current website continues to list an ISKCON temple presence at Ptitsevodstvo village in that same district, alongside the actively operating Mereyy Street temple in Almaty city.

Today, Hinduism in Kazakhstan is composed mainly of ISKCON's Gaudiya Vaishnava followers and a small diaspora Hindu population from India (801 Hindus recorded in the 2010 census). Wikipedia's "Freedom of religion in Kazakhstan" article notes that only two Hare Krishna communities remain officially registered nationally, in the capital Astana and in Almaty, each reported to have more than 50 members.
