Dang district, in the forested hill country of southern Gujarat, is traditionally identified with a stretch of the Dandakaranya through which Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana are said to have passed during their forest exile, on the way toward Nashik. At Subir, a small town on the Ahwa-Nawapur road about 27 km from the district headquarters at Ahwa, this tradition is localized at a site known as Shabari Dham (also written Shabri Dham) — named for Shabari, the tribal ascetic-devotee of the Ramayana who, in the Aranya Kanda, offers Rama and Lakshmana wild berries she had tasted herself so as to give them only the sweetest, an act of pure, unlettered devotion that the epic holds up as equal to any scholar's. Some tellings also have Shabari directing Rama from her ashram toward Sugriva and Hanuman at Pampa Sarovar; a namesake "Pampa Sarovar" stands alongside Shabari Dham at Subir today, though it is a local/regional counterpart rather than the more famous lake of that name near Hampi in Karnataka.

Wikipedia's coverage of this site is thin — a single listing among Dang district's "places of interest" rather than a dedicated article — so this entry is deliberately narrow: it confirms that a real, named pilgrimage site called Shabari Dham exists today at Subir and carries the traditional Shabari association, without asserting details (architecture, founding date, administering trust, festival calendar) that could not be verified from the sources consulted. Readers should also note this site has no connection to Sabarimala, the well-known Ayyappa temple in Kerala; the similar-sounding name is coincidental, and Shabari Dham commemorates a different figure from a different part of the Ramayana narrative entirely.
