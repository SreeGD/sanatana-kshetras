Bhairabkunda Shiva Mandir, in the far southeastern Bhutanese town of Jomotsangkha near the tri-junction of Bhutan, Assam, and Arunachal Pradesh, is described as the only Shakta Pitha in Bhutan — a status that makes it one of the most religiously significant Hindu sites in a country whose formal Hindu population is small but concentrated in exactly this southern border region. Tradition holds that the ascetic Bhairab Nath discovered a natural Shiva lingam here while meditating beside a sacred lake, or kunda, giving the site its name, and that it is counted among the traditionally enumerated Shakti Pithas marking where a fragment of the goddess Sati's body fell.

The original sacred lake was damaged when it burst during the powerful 1950 Assam-Tibet earthquake, and the present temple was rebuilt from 2002 to 2005 through contributions from the Bhutanese people, reflecting a community-driven revival of the site in the early 2000s. Today Bhairabkunda functions as an active shrine and a notable point of cross-border devotional and cultural exchange, drawing pilgrims from Bhutan, Assam, and Arunachal Pradesh each January for the Bhairabkunda Festival held around Makar Sankranti.
