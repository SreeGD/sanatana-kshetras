On Gujarat's Saurashtra coast, in the old Bhatia Bazar market quarter of Porbandar — a city historically known as "Sudamapuri" and traditionally held to be the birthplace of Sudama — stands the Sudama Mandir, one of the few temples in India dedicated to a mortal devotee rather than a deity. Sudama, a poor Brahmin, studied alongside Krishna and Balarama in their boyhood at sage Sandipani's ashram in Ujjain; years later, per the Bhagavata Purana, he travels to Dwarka to see his old friend, now a king, carrying nothing but a modest gift of flattened rice. Krishna receives him with overwhelming affection, and Sudama returns home to find his family's long poverty transformed into prosperity — a story treasured across Vaishnava tradition as an emblem of humble devotion outweighing material offering.

The temple's origins are traditionally dated to the 12th–13th century, with a significant renovation later carried out under Bhavsinhji Madhavsinhji of Porbandar; a separate tourism source instead dates the standing structure's construction to 1902–1907, a discrepancy this entry notes rather than resolves. The temple today has a light-pink exterior with marble pillars and an intricately carved shikhara, centers on a statue depicting Krishna embracing Sudama, and includes an adjoining stepwell amid its grounds. The complex also holds a monument to Shri Ram Devji Jethwa, a ruler of the Jethwa dynasty that historically governed the Porbandar region. This site is distinct from Sandipani Ashram in Ujjain, the shared gurukul where Krishna, Balarama, and Sudama studied together, which is catalogued separately in this directory; the Porbandar temple instead marks Sudama's own traditional hometown and his story of reunion with Krishna.
