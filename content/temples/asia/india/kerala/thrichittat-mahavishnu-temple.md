## Origin and legend

The Thrichittat (or Thiruchenkundroor) Maha Vishnu Temple enshrines Vishnu as Imayavarappan (also rendered Imayavarappa Perumal). Local legend attributes the temple's founding to Yudhishthira, eldest of the Pandava brothers of the Mahabharata, as one of five sibling shrines built by the five brothers along the banks of the Pamba river in the Chengannur area, alongside Puliyoor (Bhima), Aranmula (Arjuna), Thiruvanvandoor (Nakula) and Thrikkodithanam (Sahadeva).

## Historical significance

Built in the Kerala style of temple architecture and administered by the Travancore Devaswom Board, the temple's ten-day annual festival runs from Atham to Thiruvonam in the Malayalam month of Meenam. Since 1957 it has also served as the starting point for the Gosala Krishna Temple festival procession, featuring 25 caparisoned elephants, linking Thrichittat to Thiruvanvandoor's own Gosala Krishna shrine in the shared devotional geography of the Pamba valley's Pandava-temple cluster.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thrichittat is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. It is glorified by Nammalvar, the 8th-9th century Vaishnava saint-poet, in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, placing it among the 108 Divya Desams "seen and sung" by the Alvars.
