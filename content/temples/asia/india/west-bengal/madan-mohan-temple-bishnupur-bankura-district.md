The Madan Mohan Temple is one of the best-known members of Bishnupur's celebrated cluster of terracotta temples, built in 1694 CE (recorded locally as year 1000 of the Mallabda era) by King Durjan Singha Dev of the Malla dynasty, whose Vaishnavite rulers — beginning with Bir Hambir in the late 16th century — made Bishnupur one of Bengal's principal centers of temple architecture and culture. Dedicated to Madanmohan, a form of Krishna, the temple follows the eka-ratna variant of Bengal's distinctive Ratna style: a square base rising to a single domed tower, its curved cornice borrowed from the thatched-roof "chala" huts of rural Bengal and rendered permanently in brick and laterite. Its walls are covered in finely worked terracotta plaques portraying Krishna-lila episodes, the ten avatars of Vishnu, and other Puranic scenes — the hallmark decorative technique that distinguishes Bishnupur's temples from the stone-built temple traditions found elsewhere in India.

Unlike some other shrines in the Bishnupur cluster, the Madan Mohan Temple is today maintained purely as a protected heritage monument by the Archaeological Survey of India (Monument of National Importance N-WB-15), with no regular worship currently conducted there; visitors come primarily to see its architecture and terracotta work rather than for darshan. The wider Bishnupur terracotta-temple cluster, of which this is one of the finest examples, has been on UNESCO's World Heritage Tentative List since 1997.
