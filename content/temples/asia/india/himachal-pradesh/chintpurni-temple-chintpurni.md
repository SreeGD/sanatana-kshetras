Chintpurni is one of five Shakti Peethas within present-day Himachal Pradesh and one of the most visited Devi shrines in the western Himalayan foothills, dedicated to the goddess in her fierce Chhinnamasta form. Temple tradition credits its founding to a devotee named Mai Das, though no confirmed date survives for when the shrine was first established or built up into its present form. What is documented is the site's long life as a working pilgrimage destination in its own right: for generations the temple kept genealogical registers recording visiting pilgrims' names, native villages, and the purpose of their visit, records later microfilmed by the Genealogical Society of Utah and still used today for family-history research. As with the other Shakti Peethas, the temple's mythological anchor is the story of Sati's dismembered body falling to earth in 51 places after Vishnu cut it apart to end Shiva's grief-stricken wandering; which specific part is said to have fallen at Chintpurni is not consistently recorded across sources, so it is left unspecified here rather than guessed.
