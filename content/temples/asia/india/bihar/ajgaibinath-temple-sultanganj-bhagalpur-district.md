Ajgaibinath Temple stands on a rocky island in the middle of the Ganga at Sultanganj, on the river's southern bank in Bihar's Bhagalpur district. Architecturally a Nagara-shikhara shrine to Shiva, its present form dates to a 19th-century renovation carried out under Rani Kalavati of the local royal family, though the temple's own devotional roots as a riverside Shiva shrine are considerably older; the sources consulted for this entry, however, do not preserve a documented founding narrative earlier than that renovation.

The temple's real significance lies less in its own architecture than in its position as the starting line of one of eastern India's largest annual pilgrimages: during the Shravan month, hundreds of thousands of barefoot Kanwariya pilgrims gather at Ajgaibinath to draw sacred Ganga water directly from the river beside the temple, then carry it in kanwars (shoulder-borne containers) over roughly 105 km to the Baba Baidyanath Temple at Deoghar in neighbouring Jharkhand -- one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and already documented in this directory, along with the nearby Basukinath Temple that many of the same pilgrims visit afterward. Ajgaibinath and Deoghar are thus two ends of a single living pilgrimage corridor, the Shravani Mela / Kanwar Yatra, that spans the Bihar-Jharkhand border and draws some of the largest annual crowds of any Shaiva observance in the country.
