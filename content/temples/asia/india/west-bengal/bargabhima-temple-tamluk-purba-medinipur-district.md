Bargabhima Temple, on the bank of the Rupnarayan river in Tamluk, Purba Medinipur district, is identified with the Vibhash Shakta Pitha, one of Bengal's 51 Shakti Peethas. Tradition holds that the left ankle of Sati fell here after her body was cut apart; the presiding goddess is worshipped as Kapalini, in her fierce Bhimarupa aspect, and is popularly known simply as Bargabhima. Tamluk itself carries older layers of religious memory beyond the Shakti Peetha tradition — the town is mentioned in the Mahabharata in connection with Bhima, and the site's character is described as blending Bengali Hindu and Buddhist influences, reflecting the region's long, layered religious history.

The present temple structure was built by the Tamluk Raj family after an earlier temple was destroyed during medieval Islamic-era incursions into the region, though no specific rebuilding date could be confirmed in the sources checked; the West Bengal government has since recognized the site as a Heritage Site. Daily worship here includes the distinctive local custom of offering non-vegetarian prasad, such as snakehead murrel fish, alongside more conventional observances at Durga Puja, Pahela Baishakh, and Kali Puja. The temple also holds a place in Bengal's modern political history: the revolutionary Khudiram Bosu is recorded as having worshipped here, and the site is remembered as a place where many Midnapore freedom fighters took oaths to pursue armed resistance against British rule.
