Sri Pavalavanna Perumal Temple, at Thiru Pavalavannam close to the heart of Kanchipuram, enshrines Vishnu in a coral-red hue — Pavalavannar, "the Lord of coral complexion" — alongside his consort Pavalavalli Thayar. Temple tradition traces the name to the Treta Yuga, when Vishnu is said to have taken on this luminous coral color to appear before the sage Naimisaranya, granting him darshan in a form distinct from the more commonly depicted dark (Neelamega) or golden hues of Vishnu seen at neighboring Kanchipuram shrines, and lending this temple its own distinctive place within the town's sacred geography.

The shrine is counted among the 108 Divya Desams through the hymns of three Alvars — Peyalvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, and Thirumangai Alvar — preserved in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, situating it firmly within the earliest layer of Tamil Vaishnava devotional literature alongside its more prominent neighbors. Its Dravidian-style structure, seven-tiered rajagopuram, and temple tank (Chakra Theertham) are attributed in tradition to Chola-era construction, with inscriptions recording endowments from the reigns of Rajadhiraja Chola and Kulothunga Chola I, and further embellishment under the Vijayanagara kings who patronized much of Kanchipuram's sacred architecture in the 15th and 16th centuries.

Located only a short walk from Kanchipuram's railway station and bus stand, Pavalavanna Perumal temple remains an easily accessible stop for pilgrims tracing the town's dense cluster of Divya Desams, and continues to observe a full annual festival calendar — including its Vaikasi Brahmotsavam, Pavitrotsavam, and Vaikunta Ekadashi — that keeps it an active center of Sri Vaishnava worship.
