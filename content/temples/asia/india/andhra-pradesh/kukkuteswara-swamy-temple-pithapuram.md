Pithapuram's Kukkuteswara Swamy Temple is a joint Shiva-Shakti sacred complex: its presiding deity is a swayambhu (self-manifested) Shiva lingam known as Kukkuteswara, while the same complex houses the shrine of Puruhutika Devi, one of the eighteen Maha Shakti Peethas of pan-Indian Shakta tradition. That dual identity — a major Shaiva shrine and a Shakti Peetha sharing one temple precinct — is why this entry records both Shaiva and Shakta traditions rather than treating it as a purely Shakta site. Tradition holds that a portion of Sati's body, most commonly identified as the hip, fell here after her self-immolation and dismemberment during Daksha's sacrifice, consecrating Puruhutika Devi's shrine as one of the network of Shakti Peethas that spans the subcontinent; as with the wider legend, this is devotional narrative rather than an independently dated event.

Unlike some of the better-documented Andhra temples, no accessible source gives a founding date, patron dynasty, or inscriptional history for either the Shiva sanctum or the Devi shrine at Pithapuram, so the site's age is asserted here only in the general sense implied by its long-standing status as a named Maha Shakti Peetha, not as a dated architectural fact. It is worth noting that Pithapuram is a separate sacred site from the nearby Pancharama Kshetra temples such as Draksharamam — the two are sometimes confused because both lie in the same coastal Godavari delta region and both carry major Shakti Peetha associations, but Pithapuram's Kukkuteswara/Puruhutika complex is not counted among the five Pancharama Shiva temples. The temple today functions as an active place of daily worship, drawing its largest crowds during Maha Shivaratri and Navaratri.
