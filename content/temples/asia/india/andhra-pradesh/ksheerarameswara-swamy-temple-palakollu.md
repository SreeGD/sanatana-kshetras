Ksheerarama, the Ksheera Ramalingeswara Swamy Temple at Palakollu in West Godavari district, is one of the five Pancharama Kshetras and is best known for its unusually tall, milky-white Shivalinga -- a color and character that local tradition links directly to the temple's name, "ksheera" meaning milk. Like its sister Pancharama shrines, Ksheerarama shares the group's common origin legend: the demon Tarakasura's invincible Atma-linga, broken apart by Kartikeya's agneyastra on Vishnu's instruction, is said to have fragmented into five pieces consecrated at five separate sites. A further piece of temple lore holds that a single day spent at Ksheerarama carries the same spiritual merit as a full year spent at Varanasi -- devotional tradition rather than a documented claim. The presiding deity, Ksheera Ramalingeswara, is worshipped here alongside Parvathi, and the sanctum is built so the tall lingam can be viewed from windows on all four sides.

Available sources attribute the temple's origin to the Chalukya period, in the 9th century during the reign of Chalukya Bheema, with later building phases recorded in stages: the prakara (enclosure) credited to one Sri Velupathi in the 10th century, the gopuram built under the direction of Alladu Reddy in the 14th century, and a Kalyana Mantapam together with a shrine to Ashta Bhuja Lakshmi Narayanaswamy added in the 17th century. None of these century attributions, however, come with a specific inscription number or dated year in the source consulted, so they are treated here as approximate rather than settled the way Amararama's dated inscriptions are. What is well documented is the temple's scale and protected status: its tower rises about 120 feet across nine tiers, among the tallest of the Pancharama gopurams, its mandapa holds 72 black-stone pillars, and the wider complex houses subsidiary shrines to Gokarneswara, Ganesha, Kartikeya, Vishnu, Surya, Durga, Brahma, Saraswati, Bhairava, Nataraja, and other deities. The temple is recognized as a centrally protected monument of national importance, and the Gostani river, which flows through Palakollu before joining the Godavari near Narsapur, adds to the site's sacred-geography significance. Today it remains an active place of worship, with Maha Shivaratri its largest annual occasion.
