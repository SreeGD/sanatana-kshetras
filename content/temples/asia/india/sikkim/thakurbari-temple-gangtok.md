The Thakurbari Temple sits in the heart of Gangtok, Sikkim's capital, and is counted among the oldest Hindu temples in the state — itself a reminder of how comparatively recent organized Hindu temple worship is in Sikkim, a Himalayan territory whose former ruling Namgyal dynasty and majority population were traditionally Buddhist. The temple was established in 1935 on land donated by the reigning Chogyal of Sikkim, a gesture that gave Gangtok's Hindu community, drawn substantially from Nepali-speaking settlers, a central place of worship in the town. Between 1945 and 1947 the original shrine was expanded into a larger temple complex, and further additions, including a multipurpose hall and library, were made around 2011.

Though its primary deity is Shiva, the temple functions as a general-purpose Hindu shrine said to house images of most of the major deities of the pan-Indian pantheon, rather than being built around a single site-specific legend or foundational story; no myth of divine origin attaches to it in the sources available for this entry. As of a 2015 report the complex remained incomplete, still short of the funds needed to finish construction — a detail that has not been independently re-verified here and should be treated as dated rather than current information. What is well documented is the temple's ongoing role as a central gathering point for Gangtok's Hindu community, especially during Maha Shivaratri and Bala Chaturdashi.
