The Adi Jagannatha Perumal Temple at Thiruppullani, in the Pandya Nadu country near the coast of Ramanathapuram, enshrines the Lord as Adi Jagannatha, here revered in a special reclining form as Darbhasayana Rama, the Lord who lay upon a bed of darbha (kusa) grass. It is counted among the 108 Divya Desams sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. According to the sthala purana, this is the sacred shore associated with the Ramayana, where Sri Rama, seeking passage across the ocean to Lanka, lay in penance upon a seat of darbha grass facing the sea to petition Varuna, the lord of the waters, to grant a way across. Tradition therefore venerates Thiruppullani as the place where the Lord prepared for the building of the great causeway and the rescue of Sita, and pilgrims associate the locale with Rama's resolve and grace. The temple's glory was sung by Thirumangai Alwar, whose Tamil verses in the Divya Prabandham enshrine this abode among the sacred shrines of the Pandya region. The reclining and the standing forms of the Lord here, together with the strong Ramayana association, give Thiruppullani a distinctive place among the southern Divya Desams, drawing devotees who come to worship the Lord who, though the sovereign of all worlds, humbled himself upon a bed of grass for the sake of dharma.

## Festivals

At this coastal shrine of Adi Jagannatha Perumal at Thiruppullani, the devotional calendar follows the well-attested Srivaishnava cycle. An annual Brahmotsavam is traditionally celebrated with great fervour, the utsava deity taken in procession on various vahanas through the temple streets. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the Tamil month of Margazhi is observed with the chanting of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitations, and the opening of the Paramapada Vasal for devotees. Pavithrotsavam, performed for the ritual purification of the year's worship, and the Float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank are also traditionally kept. The temple's strong association with Sri Rama, who is said to have rested here on the darbha grass before crossing to Lanka, lends Ramayana-related observances a special devotional resonance.

## Location & Access

The temple stands at Thiruppullani, a coastal village in the Pandya Nadu region of Ramanathapuram district, Tamil Nadu, a short distance from Ramanathapuram town and on the route toward Rameswaram. Ramanathapuram serves as the nearest town with road and rail links, while Madurai is the larger city and transport hub for the area.
