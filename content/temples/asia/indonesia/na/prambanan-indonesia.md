Built in the mid-9th century CE by the Hindu Sanjaya dynasty of the Mataram Kingdom, Prambanan is the largest Hindu temple complex in Indonesia and one of the largest in Southeast Asia, its central compound dominated by three towering shrines to Shiva, Brahma, and Vishnu. Largely abandoned after the kingdom's political center shifted and following earthquake damage over the centuries, it was rediscovered and restored beginning in the colonial era and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site; unlike Angkor Wat, some sections still see periodic Hindu ritual use by Indonesia's Hindu (largely Balinese-descended) minority.
