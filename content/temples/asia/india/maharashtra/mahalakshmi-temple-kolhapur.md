Kolhapur's Mahalakshmi (locally, Ambabai) temple is counted among the three-and-a-half Shakti Peethas of Shakta tradition — a smaller, especially venerated subset of the wider network of 51 Peethas. Its founding, however, is one of the more genuinely disputed histories in this collection. One commonly cited account credits a Chalukya king named Karnadeva with building the temple in 634 CE; a different scholarly reading, advanced by historian Paul Dundas on the basis of the 12th-century Kannada poet Brahmashiva's writings, holds that the site began as a Jain temple dedicated to the eighth Tirthankara, Chandraprabha, and was only later converted into a Hindu shrine to the goddess amid medieval-era religious tensions. Neither claim is treated here as settled; both are recorded because they come from named, traceable sources rather than because one can be confidently preferred over the other.

What is clearer is the temple's present form and devotional life: the goddess is worshipped as a roughly three-foot, black-stone image weighing around 40 kg, crowned with a five-headed serpent and holding a citron (matulinga), mace, shield, and drinking bowl, with a carved Sri Yantra on an interior wall and a stone lion as her mount. The temple's best-known modern observance is the Kirnotsav, when for a few days around late January/early February and again in early-to-mid November, the setting sun is positioned so its rays fall directly on the idol — an alignment that draws large crowds specifically to witness it, alongside the temple's regular Navratri observances.
