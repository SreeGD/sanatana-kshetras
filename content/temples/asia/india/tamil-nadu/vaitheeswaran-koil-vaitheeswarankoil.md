Vaitheeswaran Koil, in Mayiladuthurai district, is among the most distinctive of Tamil Nadu's Shaiva pilgrimage sites: a temple built around Shiva's role as a healer, worshipped here as Vaidyanathar — from "vaidya" (physician) and "ishvara" (lord) — and sought out by devotees for relief from illness as much as for planetary affliction. Devotional legend anchors this healing identity in several overlapping stories: that Angaraka, the deity of Mars, was himself cured of leprosy at this spot by Shiva, which is why the temple also serves as the Mars shrine within Tamil Nadu's nine-temple Navagraha circuit; that Shiva tended Subrahmanya's wounds here after his battle with the demon Surapadman; and that Rama, Lakshmana, and the Saptarishis worshipped at the site during their exile as told in the Ramayana. The temple's sacred tank, Siddhamirtham, is likewise regarded as having curative water, and devotees continue to bring salt as an offering here in the belief that it aids healing.

Hymned in the 7th century by the Nayanar saints Thirunavukkarasar and Sambandar, Vaitheeswaran Koil carries Paadal Petra Sthalam status, though no source consulted could establish precisely when the temple itself was first built; the earliest solid evidence found is inscriptional activity from the reign of the Chola emperor Kulottunga Chola I in the late 11th and early 12th centuries, with later Chola, Vijayanagara-era Nayak, and Maratha patrons all credited with expansions. Built in Dravidian style behind a five-tiered gopuram, the temple today draws its heaviest crowds on Tuesdays, the day astrologically associated with Mars, and during its Brahmotsavam and Kanda Sashti festivals.
