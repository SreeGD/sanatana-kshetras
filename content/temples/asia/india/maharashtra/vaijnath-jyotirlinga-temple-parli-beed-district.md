Parli Vaijnath, in Maharashtra's Beed district, is counted as the fifth of the twelve Jyotirlingas, the sites where Shiva is held to have manifested as a self-formed column of light. The temple standing today is built of black stone in the Hemadpanthi style, roughly 75-80 feet high with a brass-plated doorway on its eastern face; Wikipedia's article records a renovation in 1706 and further restoration work credited to the Maratha queen Ahilyabai Holkar later in the 18th century, though no original construction date for the site is established in the source checked.

Shaiva tradition more broadly associates the Vaidyanath Jyotirlingas with a legend of the demon-king Ravana attempting to carry a linga of Shiva to Lanka and being persuaded, or tricked, into setting it down on earth instead — a devotional account rather than documented history. This entry does not assert that this legend specifically and exclusively belongs to the Parli site, because the identity of "the" Vaidyanath Jyotirlinga is itself a genuine, long-running point of disagreement across traditions, most notably between this temple and the Baidyanath Dham temple at Deoghar, Jharkhand. Parli is followed here as it appears in the standard twelve-Jyotirlinga listing, without taking a position on that wider dispute.

The temple is administered today by the Vaijnath Devasthan Trust and remains an active pilgrimage site, drawing its largest crowds during Maha Shivaratri.
