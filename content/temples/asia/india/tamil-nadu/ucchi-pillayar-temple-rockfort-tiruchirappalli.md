Rising abruptly from the middle of Tiruchirappalli, the Rockfort is among the oldest exposed rock formations on earth and, since at least the seventh century CE, a site of continuous Hindu worship: rock-cut work at the hill began under the Pallava dynasty, with the temples reaching their present form under the Nayaks of Madurai during the era of Vijayanagara rule. Three shrines occupy the hill in sequence — the Manikka Vinayagar temple at the base, the Thayumanavar (Shiva) temple partway up, and, at the very summit, the Ucchi Pillayar Temple dedicated to Ganesha, reached by a long, steep flight of rock-cut steps that has become one of the most recognizable pilgrim climbs in Tamil Nadu.

Devotional tradition ties the hill directly into the larger legend of Srirangam, visible from the summit a short distance away: when the demon king Ravana's brother Vibhishana was carrying the Ranganathaswamy processional idol toward Lanka, Ganesha is said to have intervened, in some versions of the story tricking Vibhishana into setting the idol down at Srirangam, and this rock is where that intervention, or Ganesha's subsequent repose, is traditionally located — giving the summit deity his name, Ucchi Pillaiyar, "Ganesha of the summit." Today the temple remains an active pilgrimage site and one of Tiruchirappalli's most visible civic landmarks, its silhouette overlooking the Kaveri delta city much as it has for well over a thousand years.
