Sthalasayana Perumal at Thirukkadalmallai, the celebrated coastal town of Mahabalipuram in Thondai Nadu, is reckoned among the 108 Divya Desams extolled in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The presiding deity (Moolavar), Sthalasayana Perumal, is distinguished by his reclining posture directly upon the ground (sthala), without the customary serpent couch of Adisesha, a rare and revered form of the Lord; his consort here is Nilamangai Thayaar, a form of Lakshmi. The deity's hand is held in Ahvahanahasta, the beckoning gesture inviting devotees toward him. According to the sthala purana, the sage Pundarika undertook intense devotion at this shore, seeking to offer lotuses to the Lord and labouring to empty the sea so as to reach him; moved by this devotion, Vishnu is said to have appeared and reclined upon the bare earth before his devotee, whence the name Sthalasayana, the one who reposes on the ground. The kshetra is twice blessed in the Prabandham, sung both by Bhoothath Alwar, one of the three Mudhal Alwars who inaugurated the Alwar tradition and who is himself believed to have been born here, and by Thirumangai Alwar, whose abundant pasurams glorify this seaside abode.

The temple's core is traditionally traced to the Pallava period, with subsequent additions and endowments recorded under the medieval Cholas, the Vijayanagara kings, and the Madurai Nayaks; inscriptions in the temple document grants made during the reigns of the Chola emperor Rajendra I (11th century) and Vikrama Chola (1118-35 CE). Built in the Pallavan/Dravidian architectural idiom, the temple's seven-tiered rajagopuram was constructed in the 15th-16th century. It is administered today by the Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments (HR&CE) Board of the Government of Tamil Nadu. Mahabalipuram itself is famed as an ancient Pallava port and a seat of sculptural splendour, and the Divya Desam stands amid this heritage as a place of singular sanctity. Pilgrims come to behold the Lord in his ground-reclining form and to recall the devotion of the sage who, by his unwavering faith, drew the Lord to rest upon the very land of Thirukkadalmallai.

## Festivals

The most prominent annual observance is the Bhoothath Alvar Avatara festival, marking the birth of the Alvar traditionally held to have been born at this kshetra, celebrated in the Tamil month of Aipasi (October-November). Alongside this, the shrine of Sthalasayana Perumal traditionally observes the annual Brahmotsavam with the customary processions, vahana sevai and the temple car festival drawing pilgrims to this ancient coastal town. Vaikunta Ekadasi is kept with devotion during Margazhi, when the Paramapada Vasal is opened for darshan and the Tiruvaymoli is recited through the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu observances. Pavithrotsavam for ritual purification and the Float festival (Theppotsavam) are also followed in keeping with Srivaishnava practice. Sri Jayanthi, Sri Rama Navami, Navaratri and the recitation festivals honouring the Alvars complete the wider Vaishnava festival cycle.

## Location & Access

The temple stands in Mahabalipuram, the historic seaside town known as Thirukkadalmallai, on the East Coast Road within Chengalpattu district. It lies south of Chennai, the nearest major city and transport hub, and close to Mahabalipuram's celebrated shore monuments. The temple is open from approximately 6:00 AM to 12:00 PM and 3:00 PM to 8:30 PM. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
