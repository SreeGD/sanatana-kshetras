One of the Shakti Peethas and among the most important Devi temples of South India, Kamakshi Amman (also known by the temple name Kamakoti Nayaki Kovil) is traditionally associated with Adi Shankaracharya, who is held by tradition to have installed the Sri Chakra before the goddess and established worship according to the Shaktha tradition, moderating an earlier, more fierce (ugra) form of the deity into her present benign form. The temple's Dravidian-style complex spans about 5 acres, with an east-facing gopuram, a gold-plated vimana over the sanctum, and its own temple tank; because the goddess is worshipped here as supreme and self-sufficient, no separate shrine is maintained for Shiva (present in the wider complex as Ekambareswara) within the Utsava Kamakshi section. The temple was further modified in the 14th and 17th centuries, on top of whichever earlier construction phase (Pallava or Chola) originally established it. It is administered today by the Kanchi Kamakoti Peetham under its Shankaracharya (Peetadipathi).

## Festivals

The temple's single most important festival is the annual Mahotsavam in the Tamil month of Masi (mid-February to mid-March), culminating in Maasi Pooram, and including a chariot (Ther) festival and a float (Theppam) festival. Navaratri (September-October) is also a major occasion, alongside Panguni Uthiram, the Aadi festival, Aippasi Pooram, Sankara Jayanthi, and Vasanta Utsavam.
