Parshuram Kund, on the Lohit River in Arunachal Pradesh's Lohit district, is one of the northeasternmost major Hindu pilgrimage sites in India, drawing tens of thousands of devotees each Makar Sankranti (mid-January) for a ritual bath believed to wash away sin. The site is named for and dedicated to Parashurama, the axe-wielding sixth avatar of Vishnu, and the sanctity of the spot rests on the legend that he came here to cleanse himself of the sin of matricide after beheading his mother Renuka on his father Jamadagni's order — his axe, said to have stuck fast to his hand, is described as finally falling away in these waters. A shrine to Renuka herself, and a separate Dashavatar temple honoring all ten avatars of Vishnu, stand alongside the main Parashurama shrine at the complex today.

The physical kund pilgrims visit is not, in fact, ancient in its present form: the original pool was completely submerged by the severe 1950 Assam earthquake, and the roughly circular arrangement of boulders in the riverbed that forms today's kund emerged afterward. Organized promotion of the site as a pilgrimage and tourist destination dates only from the 1970s, driven substantially by pilgrims from neighboring Assam. No historical record establishes when a built shrine first stood at this location, so its status here is best understood as a site of very old legendary association with a comparatively recent, earthquake-reshaped physical form and modern-era temple structures.
