The International Society for Krishna Consciousness was founded in New York City on 13 July 1966 by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, who had arrived in the United States from India in September 1965 at age 69, initially with few resources. A storefront at 26 Second Avenue in Manhattan's Lower East Side served as his first center, from which the movement's now-global network of temples grew; ISKCON is credited with introducing large-scale public kirtan (call-and-response chanting) and the Ratha Yatra festival to a wide Western public for the first time.
