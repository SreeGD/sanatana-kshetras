On the old course of the Ganga about 25 km north of Kanpur, the riverside town of Bithoor is traditionally identified as the site of sage Valmiki's forest ashram. Devotional tradition holds that after Rama sent the pregnant Sita away, she found refuge here, gave birth to and raised the twins Lava and Kusha, and that it was at this ashram that Valmiki composed the Ramayana. Bithoor's Valmiki Ashram stands today as the town's central landmark commemorating this narrative, and pilgrims visiting it typically also take in a cluster of related sites nearby: a Sita Temple said to mark the spot where she "left for her heavenly abode by entering into the earth," and separately-named Ram Janki and Lav-Kush temples.

Alongside the ashram, Brahmavart Ghat is described as the holiest of Bithoor's riverbank ghats, where devotees bathe and pray at an altar; it holds a Shiva-linga worshipped as Brahmeshwar Mahadeva and a horseshoe nail embedded in its steps, popularly said to belong to the horse of Brahma's Ashwamedha Yajna performed here — the origin of the name "Brahmavart" ("the place of Brahma"). Nearby Patthar Ghat has its own Shiva temple with a kasauti-stone linga, and Dhruva Teela is associated with the child-devotee Dhruva's penance. This entry is entirely distinct from ISKCON Kanpur (the Sri Sri Radha Madhava Temple), a separate, unrelated 20th-century institution that happens to stand nearby on Bithoor Road but carries no Ramayana-era association of its own.
