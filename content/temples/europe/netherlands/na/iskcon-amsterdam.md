ISKCON has maintained a presence in the Netherlands since 1970, with centers historically recorded in Amsterdam and eight other Dutch cities, and Henk Keilman serving as chairman of the Dutch ISKCON Foundation in the early 1980s. Rather than concentrating around one single flagship temple, the Dutch Hare Krishna community has relocated many times over the decades - from an original Bijlmer temple through the Red Light District and a succession of canal-side and other addresses - and has long been closely intertwined with Belgium's Radhadesh community at Château de Petite Somme, which Dutch devotees played a leading role in establishing; Amsterdam's own most visible yearly public expression is a Harinam kirtan procession through the city center on King's Day (Koningsdag).

The current Amsterdam temple, named Gaura Dhama, opened at Lizzy Ansinghstraat 80 on 19 September 2015, following a stretch of over two decades (roughly 1992-2015) at a previous address on Van Hilligaertstraat. As of this writing, the community's current organizational website is running an active fundraising campaign for yet another new, purpose-built Amsterdam temple, which suggests the Dutch ISKCON presence today may continue to be more provisional in its physical footprint than the well-established rural temple-and-farm communities found in neighboring countries such as Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, and Spain. This entry should be treated as provisional pending direct confirmation of the temple's current status.
