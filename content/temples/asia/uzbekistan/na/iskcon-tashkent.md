ISKCON Tashkent is a currently active International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON) center in Tashkent, the capital of Uzbekistan. Its own official website and social media accounts (Telegram, Instagram, YouTube) document an ongoing program of Sunday gatherings, kirtan (devotional chanting), Bhagavad-gita study, arati (ritual worship of Krishna), prasada (sanctified food) distribution, and festival celebrations, consistent with a genuinely operating Gaudiya Vaishnava community rather than a purely historical or nominal registration.

Official Uzbek government records, as reflected in English Wikipedia's "Religion in Uzbekistan" article, list "Krishna Consciousness" as one registered religious organization in the country as of mid-2019 -- the same single group referenced in Wikipedia's "Hinduism in Uzbekistan" article. Uzbekistan's Hindu population is otherwise very small: the American Religious Data Archive (ARDA) recorded 734 Hindus in the country in 2010, rising to 895 by 2020.

No specific street address, GPS coordinates, or fixed daily darshan schedule for the Tashkent center could be confirmed from the sources available for this research pass; prospective visitors are directed to the center's own contact channels. Separately, research for this entry also examined whether any historic Hindu temple survives from Uzbekistan's Silk Road-era Indian trading communities (for example, the medieval Multani merchant presence documented in Bukhara), but found no evidence of a surviving or specifically documented Hindu temple structure from that period -- only a general historical reference to Multan merchants owning land in the city, without further detail on any place of worship.
