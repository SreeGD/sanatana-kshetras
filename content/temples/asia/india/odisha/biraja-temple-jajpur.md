Jajpur, on the Vaitarani river in coastal Odisha, is identified in tantric literature — notably the Tantra Cudamani — as the ancient Viraja Kshetra, the site where Sati's navel fell after her body was cut apart, making the goddess here Biraja (or Virajā) one of the more textually well-attested Shakti Peethas on the specific-body-part tradition. The temple's two-armed image departs from the many-armed Durga iconography common elsewhere: she is shown spearing the demon Mahishasura through the chest while pulling his tail, one foot planted on her lion mount and the other on the demon himself. The site's sanctity is affirmed across several tantric and Puranic sources, including the Brahmayamala Tantra and the Skanda Purana, and the philosopher Adi Shankara names the goddess here as Girija in his hymn to the eighteen Shakti Peethas.

The present temple structure is generally dated to around the 11th century CE, though the source consulted for this entry does not identify a specific royal patron or dynasty responsible for its construction — a gap worth noting given Jajpur's long history as an early capital of the Odisha region under several successive dynasties. Today the temple observes a full calendar of Odishan Shakta festivals, including a sixteen-day Durga Puja, its own Ratha Yatra with the Simhadhwaja flag, and the regional Raja Parva, alongside Navratri.
