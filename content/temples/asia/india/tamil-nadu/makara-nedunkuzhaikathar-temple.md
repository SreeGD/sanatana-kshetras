Thenthiruperai, one of the nine Nava Tirupathi villages on the Thamirabarani river in Thoothukudi district, is the seat of Vishnu worshipped as Makara Nedunkuzhaikathar ("the one wearing the long fish-shaped earrings"), with his consort Lakshmi honoured here as Thiruperai Nachiyar. The temple is traditionally assigned as the Venus (Sukra) sthalam among the nine Nava Tirupathi shrines, each of which is associated with one of the nine grahas of Hindu astrology.

The temple's name and founding legend are linked to Bhudevi, the earth goddess, who is said to have performed penance on the banks of the Thamirabarani at this spot and, in the course of her devotions, discovered a pair of makara-shaped (fish/crocodile-form) earrings, which she offered to Vishnu. Pleased with her devotion, Vishnu accepted the ornaments and wore them, restoring Bhudevi to her true form; he came to be known thereafter as Makara Nedunkuzhaikathar, "he of the long makara earrings," and the village as Thenthiruperai (the southern Thiruperai, distinguishing it from a namesake shrine elsewhere).

The shrine's inclusion among the Nava Tirupathi is documented both in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, through Nammalvar's Tiruvaimozhi verses composed for the nine river-bank temples as a set, and in the Navatirupati Mahatmiyam section of the Brahmanda Purana, which narrates the sthala puranas of all nine shrines together. Later devotional literature, including eighteenth- and nineteenth-century Tamil works such as the 108 Tirupati Antati, continued to celebrate the Nava Tirupathi as a fixed pilgrimage circuit, of which this temple has remained an integral part.
