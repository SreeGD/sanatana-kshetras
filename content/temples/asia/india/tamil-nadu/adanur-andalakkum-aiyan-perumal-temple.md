The Andalakkum Aiyan Perumal Temple at Adanur, near Swamimalai, enshrines Vishnu in a distinctive reclining (Bhujanga Sayana) form facing east: the deity rests his head on a marakkal (a traditional grain-measure) and holds a palm-leaf manuscript (olai chuvadi) and stylus (ezhuthaani) — an iconography that gives the temple its name, Andalakkum Aiyan, "the lord who measures/accounts for" the deeds of souls. The temple carries the additional epithet "Agni Shapa Vimochana Sthalam," commemorating a legend in which the fire-god Agni was released here from a curse arising from the sin of killing a Brahmin.

The temple's founding legend is drawn from the story of the Churning of the Ocean (Samudra Manthana): the divine cow Kamadhenu, asked to prove her worth, failed a test of pride, while Lakshmi humbly offered only a Tulasi leaf and was honored above her — an episode said to explain the deity's "measuring" iconography here, weighing devotion rather than material offering. Other traditions associated with the site include a penance performed by Indra and a visit by Hanuman en route to Lanka, said to have left his footprints nearby.

The temple is glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by one pasuram of Thirumangai Alvar; a further attribution to Kulasekhara Alvar appears in one source but is not corroborated elsewhere. It is traditionally associated with the Chola king Aditya I as an early patron, though this is not backed by a cited inscription in available sources. Today the temple is administered by the Ahobila Mutt, a monastic institution of the Vadakalai branch of Sri Vaishnavism — a well-documented present-day affiliation. Its major festivals include an annual Brahmotsavam in Vaikasi and Vaikuntha Ekadashi observances during Margazhi.
