Thiruthuvarakai, the Dwaraka of the north, stands among the 108 Divya Desams celebrated by the Alwars, where the Lord is worshipped as Kalyana Narayanar. Located in the Vada Nadu, the northern region, on the western seashore, Dwaraka is revered in tradition as the golden city of Sri Krishna, the kingdom he established after departing from Mathura. According to the puranic tradition, having led the Yadava clan to this coastal stronghold, Krishna reigned here as a king and householder, and it was at Dwaraka that he wedded his consorts; hence the presiding deity is hailed as Kalyana Narayanar, the Lord of the auspicious marriage. Dwaraka is honoured among the great holy cities of the land and is intimately bound to the close of Krishna's earthly sojourn. This abode was sung by several of the Alwars: Thirumazhisai Alwar and Nammalwar, foremost among the saint-poets, together with Periyalwar and his daughter Andal, whose hearts overflowed with love for Krishna, and Thirumangai Alwar, the prolific pilgrim of the shrines. Through their hymns in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, the distant western seat of Krishna's royal lila was drawn into the devotional vision of the Tamil Vaishnava tradition, so that worshippers might glorify the Lord both as the cowherd of Gokul and as the crowned sovereign and bridegroom of Dwaraka.

## Festivals

As one of Sri Krishna's principal abodes, this Dwarka kshetram observes Sri Krishna Janmashtami as its foremost festival, marking the Lord's birth with abhishekam, adornment and night-long devotion. The Braj and Gujarat traditions surrounding Krishna as king of Dwarka are recalled through the year, and the great pilgrim seasons draw large gatherings to the seaside shrine. In keeping with Srivaishnava observance the temple honours the wider Vishnu festival cycle, including a Brahmotsavam, Vaikunta Ekadasi during Margazhi, and Pavithrotsavam. Festival days unfold with processions, recitation of sacred verse and special offerings, celebrating the Lord whom the Azhwars revered as Kalyana Narayanar, the Lord of auspicious marriage.

## Location & Access

The temple is at Dwarka (Thiruthuvarakai) on the western coast of Gujarat, a celebrated pilgrim town at the edge of the Arabian Sea in the northern Vada Nadu tradition. Dwarka has its own railway station on the western line, with Jamnagar and Rajkot the nearer cities and airports serving the region. Like most Vishnu temples it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours, with a break during the day.
