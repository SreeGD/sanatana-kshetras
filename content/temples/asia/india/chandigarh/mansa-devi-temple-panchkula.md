The Mata Mansa Devi Mandir, dedicated to the goddess Mansa Devi as a form of Shakti, was built between 1811 and 1815 by Maharaja Gopal Das Singh of the princely state of Mani Majra (enthroned 1783), in the Shivalik foothills of what is now Panchkula district, Haryana. The temple this entry describes is not physically inside the Chandigarh Union Territory — it sits about 10 km away, near Bilaspur village — but it is the closest major, well-documented Hindu pilgrimage site to the planned city, and is treated here as the region's principal Shakti shrine for that reason.

The complex grew over time into three linked temples, the original Mansa Devi shrine being the oldest; a Shivalaya was added in 1840 by Maharaja Karam Singh of Patiala, decorated with wall paintings whose surviving panels are said to date to around that period. After Indian independence the site fell into disrepair under fragmented private management, until the Haryana state government established the Shri Mata Mansa Devi Shrine Board by legislative act in 1991 to restore and administer it. The complex today spans roughly 100 acres and draws several thousand devotees on an ordinary day, with numbers swelling considerably during the Chaitra and Sharad Navratri festivals.
