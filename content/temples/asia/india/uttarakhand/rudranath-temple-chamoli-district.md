Set high in the Garhwal Himalayas between the Mandakini and Alaknanda valleys, Rudranath is dedicated to Shiva in the form of his face (mukh), one of five body-part manifestations at the heart of the Panch Kedar legend. According to that tradition, the Pandavas, seeking to atone for the killing of kin at Kurukshetra, pursued Shiva through the Garhwal mountains as he fled in the form of a bull to avoid granting them forgiveness; when Bhima finally caught hold of the bull, it is said to have plunged into the earth and resurfaced in five places as five parts of its body, with Rudranath marking the face. As with the other Panch Kedar sites, no independently documented founder or construction date survives outside this tradition, and the temple's natural rock sanctuary sits among a cluster of small subsidiary shrines and sacred kunds (ponds) at the site.

Rudranath is commonly regarded as the most physically demanding of the five Panch Kedar pilgrimages. The motorable road ends at Sagar village beyond Gopeshwar, after which pilgrims face a multi-day trek of roughly 17-20 km through dense oak and rhododendron forest, past shepherd settlements, before reaching the shrine's high meadows; longer alternate approaches run via Mandal and the Anusuya Devi Temple, or from Joshimath. Like its sister shrines, Rudranath closes for the winter under heavy snow, and its deity is ceremonially carried in procession down to the Gopinath Mandir in Gopeshwar until the shrine reopens with the broader Char Dham/Panch Kedar season in late spring.
