Inis Rath is a small private island in Lower Lough Erne, County Fermanagh, Northern Ireland, centered on a Victorian mansion originally built for the Cavendish Butler family and later used as a hunting lodge by Lord Erne. Hare Krishna devotees acquired the island in the early 1980s — Wikipedia's own account gives dates ranging from 1982 to the mid-1980s — and converted the mansion into a monastic ISKCON community, one of only three ISKCON centres on the island of Ireland, the others being in Dublin and Belfast. As a Gaudiya Vaishnava community in the tradition established in the West by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, its worship centers on Radha and Krishna.

The community was drawn into national controversy in 1987, when a Dublin man alleged on RTÉ radio that he had been kidnapped and forcibly drugged on the island; footage later surfaced placing him at a public protest during the very period he claimed to have been held captive, discrediting the allegation. Financial pressures nearly brought the community to an end in the early 2000s, when the island was listed for sale through Sotheby's for £950,000 before residents chose to remain and continue. Today Inis Rath supports roughly 15 permanent residents on a 22-acre nature reserve that is open to the public, and since 2022 it has also given sanctuary to Hare Krishna refugees displaced by the war in Ukraine — a continuation, more than four decades on, of the island's original role as a place of refuge and religious community.
