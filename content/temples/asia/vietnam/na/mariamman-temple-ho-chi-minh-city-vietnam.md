The Mariamman Temple in Ho Chi Minh City's central Ben Thanh area, near the city's famous Ben Thanh Market, was established in the late 19th century by Palaniappa Chettiar and the Nagarathar (Chettiar) trading community, South Indian Tamil merchants who settled in French colonial Saigon. Dedicated principally to the goddess Mariamman, the temple also houses images of Shiva, Vishnu, Brahma, Kali, and Parvati, along with subsidiary depictions of Lakshmi and Murugan, behind a roughly 12-meter Dravidian-style Raja-gopuram entrance tower and a large mandapam hall — making it one of the most architecturally distinctive Hindu sites in mainland Southeast Asia's Indochina region.

While the temple's resident Tamil Hindu community has shrunk over the decades to around fifty families, it remains an active and, notably, widely popular devotional destination in Vietnam's largest city: the great majority of its daily visitors today are Vietnamese or Sino-Vietnamese residents who have come to believe in Mariamman's spiritual power, treating the temple as a place to seek blessings and fortune, well beyond its original Tamil Hindu congregation. This makes it a distinctive civic landmark in Ho Chi Minh City, illustrating how a Hindu temple built for a diaspora trading community can become woven into the wider religious life of its host city.
