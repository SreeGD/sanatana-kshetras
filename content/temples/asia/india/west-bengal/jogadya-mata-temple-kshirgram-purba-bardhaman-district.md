Jogadya Mata Temple, in the village of Kshirgram (also spelled Khirogram) in Purba Bardhaman district, is one of Bengal's 51 Shakti Peethas, marking the spot where tradition holds a fragment of the big toe of Sati's right leg fell to earth. The goddess is worshipped here as Jogadya (Yogadya) in her fierce ugra-chandi aspect, with a secondary shrine on the grounds dedicated to Shiva in his local form as Kshireswar.

An unusual detail recorded in local tradition is that the site was for a time worshipped without any physical idol at all, until a ruler remembered as King Haridutta reportedly received a vision instructing him to commission a stone image of the goddess in her ten-armed Singhabahini form. When that image was later damaged, it was replaced by an artisan named Nabin Bhaskar of nearby Dainhat, with funding from the Maharaja of Bardhaman — a detail that at least places the replacement within the era of the Bardhaman Raj, though no precise date for either the original vision or the later replacement could be confirmed in the sources checked. Today the temple's largest gathering is its annual fair held around Vaisakha Sankranti in mid-April, when the wider Kshirgram community and regional pilgrims converge on the site.
