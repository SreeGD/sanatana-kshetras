The Valvil Ramar Perumal Temple at Pullabhoothangudi is unusual among the 108 Divya Desams for enshrining Vishnu as Rama in his reclining (Bhujanga Sayanam) form — and, according to temple tradition, as the only Divya Desam depicting Rama with four arms, two of which hold the conch and discus more usually associated with Vishnu's own form. The temple's name, Valvil ("mighty bow"), refers to Rama's bow, and the place name Pullabhoothangudi is traditionally said to derive from pul ("bird") and kudi ("dwelling"), commemorating the temple's central legend.

That legend holds that Rama and Lakshmana performed the last rites of Jatayu, the vulture-king who gave his life fighting Ravana to protect Sita, at this very spot. Because Sita herself was in captivity in Lanka at the time, tradition holds that the earth-goddess Bhoomadevi appeared in her place to assist — which is why the temple's consort is worshipped not as Sita directly but as Bhoomadevi, under the names Potramaraiyaal or Hemambujavalli. This distinctive theological detail sets the temple apart from other Rama shrines among the Divya Desams.

The temple is glorified in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by ten verses of Thirumangai Alvar (Periya Thirumozhi 1348-1357); one source additionally credits Kulasekhara Alvar, though this is not corroborated elsewhere and is treated here as unconfirmed. On the historical side, one temple-history source cites a 14th-century Pandya-period inscription recording a land grant under Maravarman Kulasekaran II, though this has not been independently verified against a formal epigraphical record. The temple's major annual observance is Vaikuntha Ekadashi during Margazhi, alongside a Brahmotsavam in the month of Panguni.
