Thirukkannapuram, a village just outside Nagapattinam on the Kaveri delta, holds an outsized place in Sri Vaishnava devotional literature: after Srirangam itself, no other Divya Desam receives as many verses in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Five of the twelve Alvars - Periyalvar, Andal, Kulasekara Alvar, Nammalvar, and Thirumangai Alvar - sang here, praising the reclining Vishnu of this shrine as Neelamegha Perumal, "the Lord dark as a rain-cloud," with his consort worshipped as Thirukkannapura Nayagi.

The temple's festival name, Sowriraja Perumal, comes from a well-loved local legend: a temple priest, having sold the sacred hair (used to make garlands) meant for the deity to a customer in need and unable to produce it for the day's service, was saved from disgrace when Vishnu himself appeared wearing a wig (sowri in Tamil) so that the ritual could proceed unblemished, a story that is still recounted during the temple's Vaikasi Brahmotsavam, when the marriage of the Lord to Padmasini is ceremonially re-enacted over twelve days. Thirukkannapuram is also counted among the Pancha Krishna Kshetrams, a set of five temples along the Kaveri delta associated with Krishna-Vishnu worship, and is generally regarded as foremost among them. The present structure, built up under the Cholas with later Thanjavur Nayak-period additions, stands as one of the more historically layered shrines of this stretch of the delta, though a fully documented inscriptional history was not available in the sources consulted for this entry.
