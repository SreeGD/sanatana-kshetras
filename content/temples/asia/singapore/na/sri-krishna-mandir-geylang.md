Singapore has a distinctive relationship with the Hare Krishna movement compared to most countries in this directory: the Singapore government banned the International Society for Krishna Consciousness by name in the 1970s, barring foreign ISKCON monks (and Founder-Acharya A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada himself) from entering the country and refusing the society's attempts to register under the ISKCON name. Rather than disappearing, the local Gaudiya Vaishnava community adapted by registering under different names, continuing devotional practice at what is known today as the Sri Krishna Mandir in Geylang, alongside a related congregation, the Gita Reading Society, meeting at the Gauranga Centre in Serangoon.

Sources consulted differ on whether Singapore's formal ban on ISKCON specifically remains in force today or has effectively lapsed in practice, but they agree that an active Hare Krishna congregation continues to worship, chant, and mark festivals such as Janmashtami at the Geylang temple. This entry documents that congregation, understood as part of the same Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition founded by Prabhupada even though it does not operate under the ISKCON name itself.
