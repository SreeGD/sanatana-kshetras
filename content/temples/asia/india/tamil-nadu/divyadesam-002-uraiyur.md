The temple of Azhagiya Manavalan at Uraiyur, within present-day Tiruchirappalli (Trichy) city, is counted among the 108 Divya Desams of the Srivaishnava tradition. The presiding deity, the Moolavar, is worshipped as Azhagiya Manavala Perumal, the beautiful bridegroom, a name that evokes the Lord in his aspect as the divine consort. Uraiyur itself has a documented ancient history distinct from the temple's own uncertain founding date: it was the Chola capital under King Karikala before the 1st century CE, is named in the Mauryan emperor Ashoka's inscriptions in connection with the Cholas, and only lost its administrative prominence when the later Imperial Cholas relocated their capital to Thanjavur; the name Uraiyur itself means simply "the residence" in Tamil. Tradition holds the temple's goddess, Kamalavalli Nachiyar, to have been a Chola princess found in a lotus who became the bride of the Lord of Srirangam; Uraiyur is thus revered as a Nachiyar sthala, intimately linked to the divine marriage celebrated at nearby Srirangam, and is noted among Divya Desam scholarship as one of the comparatively few sites where the goddess receives particular prominence alongside the Lord. Uraiyur is also associated in tradition with the birthplace of Thiruppaan Alwar, though the temple's own mangalasasanam hymn is credited to a different Alwar, Thirumangai. Through Thirumangai Alwar's praise, this abode of the beautiful bridegroom is enshrined among the sung temples of the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and pilgrims who tread the holy circuit of the Kaveri delta honour Uraiyur as a place of bridal devotion, where the soul, like the bride, seeks union with the Lord.

## Festivals

At Uraiyur, the shrine of Azhagiya Manavalan and his consort Kamalavalli Nachiyar, the festival cycle follows the wider Srivaishnava tradition. An annual Brahmotsavam with processions of the utsava deity on various vahanas is traditionally observed, as is the Vaikunta Ekadasi season with recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham. The temple is especially associated with Kamalavalli Nachiyar, said to have been discovered in a lotus by the Chola king, and her dedication to the Lord is commemorated with particular devotion. Pavithrotsavam and other seasonal observances common to Perumal temples, together with daily and monthly worship on auspicious days, mark the year here.

## Location & Access

The temple lies at Uraiyur, an ancient Chola capital now a part of Tiruchirappalli (Trichy) city in the Chola Nadu region of Tamil Nadu, near coordinates 10.83 N, 78.67 E. As Uraiyur sits within Trichy itself, the city's railway junction, bus stands, and airport serve as the nearest transport hubs. Like most Vishnu temples, it generally observes morning and evening darshan hours with a midday break.
