Pushkar is one of the very few places in the world with a temple dedicated to Brahma, the creator god who is otherwise honored mainly as one figure within the Trimurti rather than as an object of standalone worship. Tradition explains the rarity through a Puranic story: Brahma performed a yajna beside Pushkar Lake, and when his consort Savitri arrived late and found he had married Gayatri to complete the ritual on time, her curse restricted his direct worship to this one site. Whatever the deeper truth of that story, the practical effect is real — pilgrims travel from across India specifically because a Brahma shrine open to devotees is otherwise nearly impossible to find.

The temple's present core is usually dated to around the 14th century, with later repairs and additions attributed to Maharaja Jawat Raj of Ratlam; a separate strand of tradition credits the philosopher Adi Shankara with renovating and reconsecrating an earlier shrine on the site in the 8th century, though the two claims sit awkwardly together and neither is treated here as settled fact. The sanctum today is administered by ascetics of the Sanyasi sect, and unusually, married householders are not permitted to enter and worship the deity directly — a restriction devotees connect back to Savitri's curse. The temple's ritual calendar peaks at Kartik Poornima, when pilgrims bathe in the lake for merit at the same time Pushkar hosts its famous camel fair.
