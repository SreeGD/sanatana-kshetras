Thiruthalaivillimangalam, in the Pandya country, is one of the celebrated 108 Divya Desams and is known as one of the two shrines that together form the Irattai Thirupathi, the 'twin sacred abodes,' a distinction by which two neighbouring temples are honoured as a single divine pair. Here the presiding Lord bears the name Aravinda Lochanan, the 'lotus-eyed one,' a name that lovingly recalls the beauty of Vishnu's eyes, soft and wide as the petals of the lotus, a form that has long captivated the devotion of the faithful. As an Irattai Thirupathi, this shrine is worshipped in close companionship with its paired Divya Desam, the two together drawing pilgrims who seek the darshan of both in one sacred sojourn. The glory of this place was sung by Nammalwar, the greatest of the Alwars, whose Tiruvaymoli enshrines Thalaivillimangalam within the Naalayira Divya Prabandham, and it is through his hymns that the temple holds its place among the abodes that the Alwars sanctified. Lying within the constellation of Divya Desams clustered near Nammalwar's native Thirukkurugur, the temple partakes of the intense, intimate devotion that the saint directed toward the Lord of these Pandya shrines. Tradition reveres Aravinda Lochanan as the Lord whose lotus gaze falls in compassion upon his devotees, and worship here, twinned with its companion abode, is held to bestow a fullness of grace. Thus the temple endures as a cherished link in the sacred geography sung by the Alwars, its sanctity affirmed by the verses of the Prabandham.

## Festivals

Thiruthalaivillimangalam, one of the twin shrines known as Irattai Tirupati and counted among the Nava Tirupati, traditionally observes the shared Nava Tirupati festival cycle in which the nine Tamiraparani Vishnu temples are venerated together. The annual Brahmotsavam is celebrated with great devotion, with ceremonial processions and the famed Garuda Sevai among its highlights. Vaikunta Ekadasi is kept with special reverence, accompanied by recitation of Nammalvar's Tiruvaymozhi, the alvar closely associated with this sacred land. Pavithrotsavam and a Theppotsavam on the temple tank are also part of the traditional calendar. The Tiruvadhyayana Utsavam, with its Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu recitations, along with Tamil New Year and Pongal observances, mark the devotional year at this Pandya Nadu temple.

## Location & Access

The temple is located at Thiruthalaivillimangalam in the Pandya Nadu region, near the Tamiraparani river in the Thoothukudi (Tuticorin) district of Tamil Nadu, and forms one half of the Irattai Tirupati within the Nava Tirupati group. Tirunelveli and Thoothukudi are the nearest major towns and transport hubs offering road and rail connections.
