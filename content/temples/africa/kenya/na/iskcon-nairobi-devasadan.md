Hare Krishna devotees became active in Nairobi and Mombasa from 1969 onward, during the same early-1970s wave of expansion that took the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON) to new centers worldwide following its founding in New York in 1966. The Nairobi group formalized itself as a registered religious and cultural society in October 1971, the same year its founder, A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, visited the city in person and addressed a large public gathering at the University of Nairobi's Taifa Hall. Prabhupada was reportedly denied entry to Kenya by immigration authorities in November 1973, before returning successfully to Nairobi in 1975, by which point the community's temple had grown enough to be given a formal name, Devasadan ("abode of the gods"), that November.

Nairobi's ISKCON center developed alongside a smaller sister community in Mombasa on the coast, both drawing on Kenya's substantial Indian-origin population for devotees and support. As with ISKCON centers elsewhere, the Nairobi temple's practice centers on the worship of Radha and Krishna and the congregational chanting (kirtan) that Prabhupada emphasized as the movement's core practice, alongside outreach and prasadam (sanctified food) distribution to the wider community.
