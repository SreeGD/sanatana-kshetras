Shree Ratneshwar Mahadev Temple is a historic underground cave shrine to Shiva near Clifton Beach in Karachi, reached by descending two staircases close to the city's Jehangir Kothari Parade into a six-level cave complex that includes a marble-floored courtyard and a freshwater spring. Local devotional tradition holds that Shiva himself once dwelt in the cave — reflected in a belief that his third eye watches over the adjacent sea to guard against flooding — and that the Sikh Guru Nanak meditated at the site; devotional sources also describe the temple as roughly 150 years old, though no independently documented founding date was available for this entry.

The temple remains an active pilgrimage site for Karachi's Hindu community, drawing its largest crowds — an estimated 25,000 pilgrims — for Maha Shivaratri, when devotees fast, hold nightlong prayers, ritually bathe the Shiva idol, and process to the sea. Nearby construction in 2014 caused structural damage to the cave complex, leading the Pakistan Hindu Panchayat to pursue Supreme Court intervention to secure the site's restoration, underscoring the pressures facing minority religious heritage sites amid Karachi's rapid urban development.
