## Origin and legend

The Thrikkakara Temple, also known as Thirukkaatkarai in older Tamil usage, enshrines Vishnu in his fifth avatar, Vamana the dwarf-brahmin, shown in the act of preparing to place his foot upon the Asura King Mahabali. The temple is traditionally identified with the legendary capital of Mahabali's kingdom and is the ritual and mythological heart of Onam, Kerala's harvest festival, which commemorates Mahabali's annual return to visit his former subjects after being sent by Vamana to rule the netherworld (Patala). The temple complex also houses subsidiary shrines to Bhagavati, Sasthavu, Gopalakrishna, Naga, Brahmarakshasa and Yakshi deities, alongside an adjoining Shiva temple, making the site sacred to both Vaishnava and Shaiva worshippers.

## Historical significance

The temple is reckoned to be around two thousand years old, and its records are cited as containing the earliest documented mention of the Onam festival, dated to 861 CE -- among the oldest attestations of the festival anywhere. Today the temple's ten-day Onam celebration draws over twenty thousand people for its communal feast, along with processions, ceremonial decoration of the deity (Chaarthu), and cultural performances including Kathakali and Panchavadyam. The temple is administered by the Travancore Devaswom Board.

## Connection to the Alvars and Divya Prabandham

Thrikkakara is one of the thirteen Malai Nadu Divya Desams. Nammalvar sang a pasuram on "Sri Kaatkarai Appan," suggesting the temple's existence and renown predate the 8th century CE, and this hymn places Thrikkakara among the 108 Divya Desams of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham.
