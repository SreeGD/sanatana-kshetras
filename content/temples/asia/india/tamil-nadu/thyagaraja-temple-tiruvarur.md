The Thyagaraja Temple at Tiruvarur is one of the largest and most historically layered Shiva temple complexes in the Kaveri delta, and among the most significant of the Paadal Petra Sthalams still uncovered in this directory. Shiva is worshipped here as Thyagarajaswami, also known as Vanmikinathar ("lord who emerged from an anthill"), alongside his consort Neelotpalambika, also revered as Kamalambika. Tradition places an original shrine here under the 7th-century Pallavas, but the surviving masonry structure is dated to the 9th-century Chola period, with inscriptions attesting to continued royal patronage under Rajendra Chola I in the early 11th century.

Beyond its architecture — a nearly 8.1-hectare complex ringed by nine gopurams and centered on an unusual standing (rather than seated) Nandi facing the sanctum — Tiruvarur occupies a special place in Tamil Shaiva devotional memory as the town most closely associated with the Nayanar saint Sundarar, whose eventful life, as told in the Periya Puranam, is largely set here. The temple's best-known living tradition is its annual chariot festival during the Tamil month of Chithirai (March/April), when a temple car reckoned among the largest of its kind — reportedly around 300 tonnes and nearly 29 metres tall — is hauled through the town by thousands of devotees.
