At Bangkok's Ratchaprasong intersection — already home to the world-famous Erawan Shrine, documented separately in this directory — stands a smaller shrine popularly known in English as the "Trimurti Shrine." For years it was assumed to depict the Hindu Trimurti, the Brahma-Vishnu-Shiva triad, but in 2017 the enshrined image was formally identified as Sadasiva, a five-faced form of Shiva, worshipped specifically as a god of love rather than as the three-fold creator-preserver-destroyer triad its popular name suggests.

The shrine draws especially large crowds of young Thai visitors praying for love and romantic success, a practice concentrated on Thursday nights around 9:30 p.m. and reaching its peak on Valentine's Day, when queues can stretch well beyond the shrine itself. It is one of several Hindu-derived shrines clustered at Ratchaprasong — nicknamed the "Intersection of Gods" — alongside dedications to Lakshmi, Parvati, Indra, and two forms of Vishnu/Narayana, reflecting the same syncretic blend of Hindu iconography and Thai folk-devotional practice found at the neighboring Erawan Shrine.
