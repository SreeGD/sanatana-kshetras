Tara Tarini stands on the Kumari Hills overlooking the Rushikulya river in Ganjam district, near the city of Brahmapur, and is unusual among the Shakti Peethas for enshrining the Goddess as a twin form rather than a single icon — two stone faces, adorned and paired with movable brass heads (Chalanti Pratima), representing Tara and Tarini together. Tradition holds that this is where Sati's breasts fell after her body was cut apart, and the site is popularly counted among a set of four especially revered Adi Shakti Peethas alongside Kamakhya, Vimala, and Kalighat — though, as with most Shakti Peetha lore, this is devotional geography rather than documented chronology. No source consulted for this entry names a specific king, dynasty, or century for the shrine's original construction; the temple's own tradition attributes an early structure to unnamed rulers of ancient Kalinga, which should be read as an assertion of antiquity rather than a verified date.

What can be dated with more confidence is a 17th-century renovation carried out under a patron named Basupraharaj, and the temple's continued active life since — today overseen by a dedicated Tara Tarini Development Board, with a ropeway now supplementing the traditional hill climb for pilgrims. The shrine also unexpectedly houses an image of the Buddha, whose place in an otherwise Shakta sanctum is not explained in the sources reviewed and may reflect the region's older, layered religious history. Worship continues year-round, but the temple is busiest during the Tuesdays of Chaitra Jatra and during the monthly Sankranti Mela, when pilgrims from across coastal Odisha converge on the hilltop.
