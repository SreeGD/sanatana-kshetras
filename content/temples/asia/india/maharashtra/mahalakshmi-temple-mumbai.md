Built in 1831 by the Hindu merchant Dhakji Dadaji, this Mahalaxmi-neighborhood temple on Bhulabhai Desai Road, near the Haji Ali causeway in South Mumbai, is distinct from the older, separately documented Mahalakshmi (Ambabai) temple in Kolhapur — the two share a name but not a history. It enshrines the Tridevi as three separate images side by side: Mahakali, Mahalakshmi, and Mahasaraswati, representing power, wealth, and knowledge respectively. Administered by the Shri Mahalaxmi Temple Trust, it is among Mumbai's most visited temples, especially around Navratri and Diwali's Lakshmi Puja, alongside the Maharashtrian new year Gudi Padwa.

A popular account holds that the temple's idols were recovered from the sea by a British engineer building the causeway connecting Worli to Mahalaxmi, after the goddess appeared to him in a dream; this origin story is part of the temple's devotional lore but was not independently confirmed in the historical source consulted for this entry, and is therefore not asserted as documented fact here.
