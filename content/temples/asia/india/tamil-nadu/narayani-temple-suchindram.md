Suchindram, near Kanyakumari at the southern tip of Tamil Nadu, is dominated by the Thanumalayan Temple, a large complex whose presiding deity is a single composite image representing the Hindu trinity — Shiva (Sthanu), Vishnu (Mal), and Brahma (Ayan) together — housed beneath a 134-foot entrance tower and surrounded by roughly thirty shrines, including a notable set of musical pillars and monumental Hanuman images. Within this constellation of shrines, Shakti Peetha tradition places a Narayani Devi shrine, held to be where Sati's upper teeth fell after her body was cut apart, making Suchindram one of the network of Shakti Peethas that extends across the subcontinent. This entry must be honest about a real limitation in the sourcing: while the wider temple complex is very well documented, the Narayani shrine specifically, and its Shakti Peetha status, appear only in a general reference list of the Shakti Peethas rather than in the Thanumalayan Temple complex's own dedicated historical account, which does not mention a Narayani shrine by name among its many subsidiary shrines. The Shakti Peetha identification is therefore presented here as a devotional tradition attached to the site rather than as an independently documented fact about a specific shrine within the complex.

What is well established, by contrast, is the antiquity and continuous development of the wider Suchindram temple complex within which this tradition situates the Narayani shrine: a masonry core dating to the 9th-century Chola period, inscriptions from the reign of the Chola king Parantaka I in the early 10th century, substantial later additions under the Madurai Nayak rulers in the 17th century and under the Travancore royal house, and an 1875 renovation funded through a public lottery scheme. The complex remains a major active pilgrimage center today, administered by the Tamil Nadu government's Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department, with its largest crowds gathering for the ten-day Margazhi car festival in December-January and, for the Devi tradition specifically, during Navaratri.
