Established in 1998, the New Navadvip Temple in Wrocław, in Poland's Lower Silesian Voivodeship, is named for Navadvip (Nabadwip), the birthplace of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu in West Bengal — a naming convention shared by New Vraja-dhama-style ISKCON communities worldwide that evoke the sacred geography of Gaudiya Vaishnavism's founding saint. It is the third of Poland's three main ISKCON temples identified by Wikipedia's overview of Hinduism in Poland, alongside the pioneering New Shantipur Temple in Czarnów (founded 1980) and the New Ramana Reti Temple in Mysiadło, near Warsaw (founded 1989), both already documented elsewhere in this directory.

As at its sister temples, worship at New Navadvip centers on the Pancha Tattva in the paired form of Gaura-Nitai — Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and Nityananda — reflecting the missionary lineage of A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, who brought Krishna consciousness to the West. Its establishment in Wrocław, one of Poland's largest cities, in 1998 marked ISKCON's continued expansion beyond its original rural Silesian farm community nearly two decades after Poland's first Hindu temple was founded. Beyond its founding date, city, and deity dedication, further detail on the temple's founders, building, and current congregation could not be confirmed in the sources available for this entry.
