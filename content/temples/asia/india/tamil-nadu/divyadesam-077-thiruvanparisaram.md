The Thiruvaazhmaarban Temple at Thiruvanparisaram, in the Malai Nadu region bordering the southern Tamil and Kerala country, is honoured as one of the 108 Divya Desams sung in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. The presiding deity, the Moolavar, bears the evocative name Thiruvaazhmaarban, 'the Lord upon whose sacred chest resides Sri Mahalakshmi,' for the goddess of fortune is ever borne upon his breast, signifying that grace and prosperity flow inseparably from him. The Lord is enshrined here in a standing posture, granting darshan to his devotees with the assurance of the divine consort eternally at his side. According to tradition, the very name of the place, Thiruvanparisaram, is taken to convey the bestowing of boons and blessings, the Lord here being approached as the giver who withholds nothing from those who surrender. This sacred abode was sung by Nammalwar, the most exalted of the Alwar saints, whose verses in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham secure for Thiruvanparisaram its enduring place among the divine shrines of the Lord. As one of the Divya Desams of the Malai Nadu, the temple forms part of the small but cherished cluster of southern abodes that the tradition holds dear, and pilgrims journeying through this region come to behold the Lord whose very form proclaims the union of Vishnu and Lakshmi, the eternal source of auspiciousness and the refuge of all who seek him.

## Festivals

At Thiruvanparisaram, sacred to Lord Thiruvaazhmaarban, the traditional Srivaishnava festival calendar shapes the temple's devotional year. The annual Brahmotsavam is the foremost celebration, marked by daily processions of the utsava murti, ceremonial vahanas, and the chanting of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham by Araiyars and devotees. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi draws large gatherings for special darshan, following the cherished custom observed across Perumal temples. Pavithrotsavam, the festival of ritual purification and renewal, and a Float festival (Theppotsavam) on the temple tank are also traditionally kept where the sthala custom allows. Krishna Janmashtami, Sri Rama Navami, Navaratri and the Tamil New Year are observed with abhishekam and special offerings, sustaining the temple's living tradition of worship through the seasons.
