Bhabanipur, a temple complex roughly 33 km from Bogura town in northwestern Bangladesh, is counted among the network of Shakti Peethas as the site where a part of the goddess Sati's body fell after her self-immolation and dismemberment — most commonly said locally to be her left anklet, an identification reflected in the name of the complex's sacred tank, Shakha-Pukur, or "conch-bangles pond." Other, less common accounts instead name the ribs of her left chest, her right eye, or her bedding as what fell here, and no source consulted resolves which version is oldest or most authoritative; this entry presents the anklet tradition as the most frequently cited without asserting it as the only one. The presiding goddess is worshipped as Aparna, with Shiva present in his Bhairava aspect as Vaman.

The complex today spans about four acres and is considerably more than a single shrine: alongside the main Devi temple stand four subsidiary Shiva temples, a Patal Bhairava Shiva temple, temples to Gopal and Bashudev, a Nat Mandir (dance/performance hall), and the Shakha-Pukur tank itself. No source consulted gives a founding date, patron, or ruling dynasty for the site, so its antiquity rests on regional tradition rather than documented history; what is on record is more recent institutional history, including a dedicated management and renovation committee that ran the complex from 1991 to 2010, and a 2007 incident in which a guest house then under construction on the grounds was destroyed. The temple remains an active center of daily and festival worship, with its largest crowds gathering for Durga Puja.
