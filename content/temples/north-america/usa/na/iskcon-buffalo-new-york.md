A Hare Krishna temple operated in Buffalo, New York from at least January 1969, closely tied to students at the State University of New York at Buffalo (SUNY Buffalo). Prahladananda Swami, then a SUNY Buffalo student, received initiation from A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada at the beginning of 1969 and moved into the Buffalo temple that January, taking on the roles of treasurer and head pujari and worshipping the temple's deities of Lord Jagannatha, Lord Baladeva, and Srimati Subhadra Devi. He organized daily harinama sankirtana and book distribution from the temple, including through severe Buffalo winters, before later helping to open ISKCON temples in Coconut Grove, Florida (Miami) and Houston, Texas. Bhakti Bhringa Govinda Swami likewise joined the movement in Buffalo in 1971, serving there and in Toronto as a cook and pujari before relocating to Vrindavan in 1977. A formal March 1975 Governing Body Commission resolution grouped Buffalo together with New Vrindaban (West Virginia), Cleveland, and Pittsburgh under a single zonal leader, confirming its standing as a recognized ISKCON temple through at least that year.

No source consulted documents when or why the Buffalo temple eventually closed, and no current address, website, or successor institution in Buffalo itself could be found; the nearest present-day related center, ISKCON of Niagara, operates independently in Niagara Falls, a separate city roughly 20 miles away, and does not describe itself as a continuation of the original Buffalo temple. This entry is therefore recorded as a historically significant but apparently discontinued ISKCON center, pending any evidence of a current or more recently active successor.
