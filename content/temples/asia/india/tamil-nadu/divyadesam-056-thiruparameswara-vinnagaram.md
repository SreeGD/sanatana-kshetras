The Vaikunta Perumal Temple at Thiruparameswara Vinnagaram, in Kanchipuram of Thondai Nadu, is revered as one of the 108 Divya Desams sung by the Alwars. The very name Vinnagaram signifies a celestial city or a temple of the heavens, and the Lord here is worshipped as Vaikunta Perumal, the master of Sri Vaikuntam, evoking for the devotee a foretaste of the Supreme Abode upon earth. The presiding Moolavar grants darshan in this sanctuary as a vision of the Lord who reigns in glory, and the shrine is held among the cluster of sacred abodes that make Kanchipuram a city dense with Divya Desams. This is among the best-documented and most architecturally significant of the Kanchipuram Divya Desams. Built by the Pallava emperor Nandivarman II Pallavamalla (scholarly dating ranges from around 690 CE to the late 8th century), the temple is laid out as a three-dimensional chakrabja-mandala (lotus-and-wheel design), its three-storied vimana rising in a stepped pyramid over a drainage moat symbolizing the Kshira Sagara, the primordial Ocean of Milk. Three sanctums, one on each floor, house Vishnu seated, reclining, and standing in turn -- access to the upper two is restricted, the first-floor reclining sanctum open to devotees only on Ekadashi days and the second-floor standing sanctum closed to the public entirely. An 813 CE inscription records a gift of a golden vessel weighing 10,000 kalanju from the Pallava king Dantivarman I. The temple's outer walls are famous for relief-sculpture panels narrating Pallava dynastic history -- a divine lineage traced from Brahma through Drona and Ashwatthama, historical kings including Mahendravarman I and Narasimhavarman I, battle scenes against the Chalukya king Pulakesin II, and the succession after Paramesvaravarman II's death -- alongside separate Mahabharata scenes. A local legend, which the sources themselves note diverges from the historical Nandivarman II attribution, holds that a child protected by both Shiva and Vishnu was raised to become the Pallava king Paramesvaravarman II and built the temple in his own name. The kshetra was hallowed by ten hymns from Thirumangai Alwar in the Periya Tirumoli, composed by around 790 CE, confirming its place within the canonical company of the Divya Desams. The temple is protected as an archaeological monument by the Archaeological Survey of India -- the only such ASI-protected status confirmed among this directory's wider Kanchipuram Divya Desam cluster -- and devotees making the round of the Divya Desams of Thondai Nadu come here to behold Vaikunta Perumal and to meditate upon the bliss of the heavenly city the Lord embodies.

## Festivals

The Vaikunta Perumal temple at Thiruparameswara Vinnagaram in Kanchipuram traditionally observes the Srivaishnava annual festival cycle. The yearly Brahmotsavam, marked by colourful processions of the utsava deity on successive mounts including the Garuda vahana, is the chief celebration. Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi is kept with special reverence, accompanied by the Adhyayana Utsavam during which the Nalayira Divya Prabandham is recited before the Lord. Pavithrotsavam is performed to sanctify the worship of the year, and Pongal, Tamil New Year, Sri Jayanthi and Rama Navami are observed. The festival days commemorating the Azhwars and Acharyas are also honoured here, in harmony with the devotional traditions of the ancient temple city of Kanchipuram.

## Location & Access

The Vaikunta Perumal temple lies in Kanchipuram, in the Thondai Nadu region of northern Tamil Nadu, close to the famed Kamakshi and Kailasanathar shrines of the temple town. Kanchipuram is well linked by road and rail, with Chennai serving as the nearest major city and transport hub. The temple generally observes morning and evening darshan hours like most Vishnu temples, closing for a period at midday. It is conveniently visited alongside the other Kanchipuram Divya Desams. Verify current darshan timings and travel details with the temple or official sources before visiting.
