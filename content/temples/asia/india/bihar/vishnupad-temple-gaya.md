Built on the bank of the Falgu river around a 40-centimeter footprint impression in solid rock, traditionally held to be Vishnu's own footprint, Vishnupad Temple is the principal site in India for shraddha (ancestral offering) rites, drawing pilgrims from across the country, especially during Pitru Paksha. Tradition holds Vishnu placed his foot on the head of the penitent demon Gayasura to subdue him and push him beneath the earth, in turn granting that offerings made here would nourish departed souls and aid their journey toward liberation. The temple's precise original construction date is unknown; the present structure was rebuilt in 1787 by Ahilyabai Holkar, the Maratha-era ruler of Indore who also rebuilt Somnath and other major temples (see that entry in this directory).
