Ireland's Hindu population grew rapidly in the early twenty-first century, reaching 33,043 residents in the 2022 census — up 131% since 2016 — with the community skewing young and professional (roughly half in professional occupations, including a notable share working as software developers). Among the small number of dedicated Hindu temples that emerged in Dublin to serve this growing population is the Ireland Ananda Sidhi Vinayaka Temple in Kingswood, Dublin 24, dedicated to Vinayaka (Ganesha) under his Siddhi Vinayaka aspect, the form widely invoked as remover of obstacles at the start of undertakings.

Beyond its listing among Dublin's active Hindu places of worship — alongside the Hare Krishna Cultural Centre in Dublin 1 and the Vedic Hindu Cultural Centre in Walkinstown — no independently documented history of this specific temple's founding or early years could be located. It is best understood as part of the broader wave of community-built Hindu institutions, including others in Letterkenny (Donegal) and Enfield (Meath), that accompanied Ireland's fast-growing Hindu population over the past two decades.
