Kalika Mata's shrine sits at the summit of Pavagadh Hill, roughly 760-830 meters up, inside what is now the Champaner-Pavagadh Archaeological Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site better known for the abandoned 16th-century Gujarat Sultanate capital at its base. The temple itself predates that city by centuries: the Kali yantra worshipped in the sanctum is generally dated to around the 10th-11th century, making it the oldest religious structure on the hill, and the site is counted among the Shakti Peethas — places where, in Puranic tradition, a fragment of the goddess Sati's dismembered body is said to have fallen; here, devotees hold it was the toe of her right foot.

The main shrine houses three images side by side — Mahakali at the center, flanked by Kali and Bahuchara Mata — reflecting Pavagadh's long history as a site of layered devotion; a nearby dargah associated with a Muslim saint, part of the hill's history under both Rajput and Sultanate rule, and an important complex of Jain temples dating to the 3rd century BCE Digambara tradition and the founding of the Achalgacch order in 1113 CE, coexist within the same hill complex, though not without occasional friction, as a 2024 incident of damage to Jain images on the approach stairway illustrated. Pavagadh remains one of Gujarat's most heavily visited pilgrimage and tourist destinations, with Navratri and the Chaitra fair drawing especially large crowds up the ropeway or forest path to the summit.
