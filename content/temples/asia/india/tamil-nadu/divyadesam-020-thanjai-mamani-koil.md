Thanjai Mamani Koil, the Great-Jewel Temple of Thanjavur, enshrines the Lord as Neelamega Perumal, He whose hue is that of the dark rain-laden cloud, and is venerated among the 108 Divya Desams of the Alwars. The name Mamani Koil, the temple of the great gem, signals the special esteem in which this abode is held within the Chola country, the heartland of Vaishnava devotion. The shrine carries a distinction shared by few, for its glory was sung by three of the great Alwars: Bhoothath Alwar, one of the earliest Mudhal Alwars whose mystic verses opened the way of the Prabandham; Nammalwar, the foremost of the saints whose Tiruvaymoli is honoured as the very essence of the Vedas in Tamil; and Thirumangai Alwar, the prolific singer of the Chola and coastal shrines. To be hymned by such a company of saints marks Thanjai Mamani Koil as a pre-eminent paadal petra sthalam, woven deeply into the four-thousand verses recited daily in the tradition. The Lord's name, Neelamega, the blue cloud, recalls the form of Vishnu that the Alwars never tired of praising, the dark and gracious refuge who rains down compassion upon those who take shelter in Him. According to tradition the place takes its identity from this jewel-like sanctity at the doorstep of the great Chola capital. Here the devout behold the cloud-hued Lord enshrined as the great gem of Thanjavur, and join their voices to those of the ancient saints in cherishing one of the celebrated abodes of the south.

## Festivals

Neelamega Perumal Temple at Thanjai Mamani Koil traditionally observes the annual Brahmotsavam, during which the Lord is taken in procession on a series of vahanas, with the Garuda Sevai forming a particularly venerated occasion. Vaikunta Ekadasi in the month of Margazhi is observed with great devotion, accompanied by the recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham through the Pagal-pathu and Ra-pathu, and devotees passing through the Paramapada Vasal where it is opened. The Pavithrotsavam of ritual purification and the Theppotsavam or Float festival upon the temple tank are also among the customary celebrations. The Tamil months of Purattasi and Margazhi, sacred to Vishnu, bring special abhishekams, alankarams and devotional recitation, while Sri Jayanthi and other Vaishnava festivals are kept according to traditional practice.

## Location & Access

The temple is situated at Thanjai Mamani Koil within Thanjavur in the Chola Nadu region of Tamil Nadu, the three shrines of this Divya Desam lying close together near the historic city. Thanjavur is well served by road and rail and is itself a major regional centre, with Tiruchirappalli nearby offering the closest large transport and air links. As with most Vishnu temples, darshan is generally available during morning and evening hours, with a closure through the middle of the day.
