Alarnath Temple (Alarnatha Mandira), in the town of Brahmagiri roughly 23 km from Puri, houses Alarnath Dev, a four-armed form of Vishnu, alongside a shrine to his consort Mahalakshmi. It is counted among the 108 Abhimana Kshethram of the Vaishnava tradition and is best known today as the destination pilgrims turn to during Anavasara — the roughly two-week period before Ratha Yatra each year when Jagannath's deity at the Puri temple is withdrawn from public darshan following his ceremonial "bathing festival," Snana Yatra.

Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, including the Chaitanya Charitamrita's account of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's travels, records Chaitanya visiting this temple during his years based at Puri, both en route to and from his pilgrimage through South India, and specifically during Anavasara periods when he could not see Jagannath. A stone preserved at the site is associated with his sankirtana (congregational chanting) there. The temple remains an active place of worship today, open daily from 6:00 AM to 9:30 PM, with a full daily bhoga (food offering) schedule that draws particular attention to its rice-pudding (kheeri) offering during the Anavasara season.
